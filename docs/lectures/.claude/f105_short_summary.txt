     1. Macro-economic environment

Central bank

v Monetary policy
     Ø Liquidity adjustment -> influence banking sector liquidity and ST rates
          § Open market controls -> buy/sells gov bills from banks, high/low sup, low/high rates
          § Non-market controls -> restricts ability to lend money
                · Min reserve ratios -> banks must hold more reserves
                     ¨ Fractional reserve system -> small reserves held, deposits to loans
                     ¨ Run on banks -> customers wanting deposits, banks call loans
                · Interest rate ceilings -> restricts ability to compete for deposits (inflow)
                · Lending type directives -> direct lending restrictions
     Ø Inflation targeting -> influences GDP & unemployment
          § QE -> increase money supply
                · Open market controls -> credit account ex-nihilo, buy gov or corp bonds or MBS
                · Non-market controls -> same as liquidity adjustment
          § Repo rate adjustment -> Lending rate to commercial banks
     Ø Forward guidance -> indication of how policy will change, reduce interest rate uncertainty
          § Influence LT interest rates
          § Influence inflation expectations
                · Influence currency strength -> weaker currency better for exports
                · Influence risk-free LT bond yields -> expectation theory

v Other interests x6 -> Banking reg, printing money, gov borrowing implementation, fin markets integrity,
     currency market intervention, tax

Taxation

v Post-tax returns -> maximise by minimising tax liability or deferring
     Ø Tax-specific factors -> total tax rate, tax split, timing, source deduction, reclaiming extent, offsetting

     Ø Other factors -> tax systems (exemption & allowances), asset rules, investor rules, investor financial

v Tax types
     Ø Income tax -> tax-free allowance, payable immediately
          § Earned income -> wages and salaries
                · Progressive system -> marginal tax rate increase as income increases
                · Regressive system -> marginal tax rates decrease as income increases
          § Unearned income -> investment returns
     Ø Capital gains tax -> tax-free allowance, payable on sale, inflation allowance, dependent on time asset

     Ø Total return tax -> CGT + income, valuations needed at sale, only marketable assets
     Ø Stamp duty -> purchase of asset
     Ø Withholding tax -> offshore assets, double taxation agreement

Behavioural finance

v Individual behaviour
     Ø Rationality -> maximising wealth/utility, extra return for extra risk, fully informed, ability to diversify,
          continuous review, security price = fundamental value, EMH assumption that enough investors are
          rational, market anomalies disappear
     Ø Prospect theory -> asymmetric response to losses vs gains, winning ­ risk averse, losing ­ risk seeking
          § Framing -> decision depends on framing of choices
          § Loss aversion -> more sensitive to losses than gain of same magnitude
          § Myopic loss aversion -> less risk averse as frequency of choice / reporting period increases
          § Mental accounting -> separate related decisions and not aggregate
     Ø Heuristics -> mental shortcut to make decisions
          § Anchoring -> small adjustments from initial arbitrary value
                · Belief preservation -> reluctant to change initial belief
                · Availability bias -> higher probabilities to more recent events, COVID
                · Representative bias -> higher probabilities on sample instead of whole distribution
          § Overconfidence -> overestimating outcomes
                · Hindsight bias -> believing current events were predictable
                · Confirmation bias -> looking for evidence that supports decision
                · Dislike of negative events -> underestimate probability of negative events
          § Optimism -> overestimating abilities
                · Self-serving bias -> credit positive outcomes only to themselves, blame negatives on external
          § Familiarity -> prefer familiar options over newer ones
                · Status quo bias -> stick with current situation
                · Herd behaviour -> mimic decisions by others
     Ø Effect of options -> additional to framing
          § Primary effect -> first option will be chosen
          § Recency effect -> last option will be chosen
          § Intermediate effect -> middle option will be chosen
          § Explicitly stated options -> encourages decision making
          § Multiple options -> discourages decision making
          § Regret aversion -> stay with current situation to avoid loss
          § Ambiguity aversion -> paying for more information to reduce uncertainty

     Ø Sentiment -> aggregate attitude, bullish or bearish, quantitative measures
          § Discount to NAV -> high sentiment yields narrow discount
          § Share volume -> high sentiment yields high turnover
          § IPO activity -> high sentiment yields high activity
          § Equity issue -> high sentiment yields high issue levels
          § Dividend premium -> high sentiment yields lower premiums, higher demand for growth shares
     Ø Cross sectional behaviour of returns -> market overaction in long run and under reaction in short run
          § Size -> small stocks outperform in recovery cycle and low sentiment
          § Stock types -> value stocks with low PE ratios outperform when sentiment is low
          § Cross-sectional momentum -> poor performing stock will continue to perform poorly
          § Time series momentum -> historical excess returns will positively predict future returns
     Ø Herding -> like herd behaviour, increases volatility, linked to regret aversion, thinking others are better
          informed, fund managers copy each other, FOMO, desire to conform
          § Noise trader -> influenced by ST news, creates volatility, drives herd behaviour
     Ø Mood based anomalies -> emotions and mood influence decisions, calendar effect, sunshine effect,
          sports results, aviation disasters

v Using futures

    Ø Hedging -> taking position in futures market, opposite to cash market, market risk reduced

    § Short hedge -> knowing you will sell in future, take short position, close out before maturity,

       protecting against fall in equity markets, protecting against rise in bond yields

    § Long hedge -> knowing you will buy in future, take long position, close out before maturity,

       protecting against rise in equity markets, protecting against fall in bond yields

    Ø Transition management -> liabilities/objectives change, portfolio not optimal, changing allocations

    will incur transaction costs (broker comm, BO spreads, tax liabilities, admin costs, pricing impacts),

    better to transact entire asset classes, index futures, synthetic holding, quicker, cheaper

    § SAA change -> buy/sell underlying over long period, favourable prices, careful analysis

    § TAA change -> long/short future over short period, no costs incurred, no CGT, illiquid assets useful

    § Policy switching -> long/short futures instead of buy/sell bonds

    § Stock selection profit consolidated -> remove P/L from sector, short index futures

    Ø Speculation -> hedge funds use

    Ø Arbitrage -> taking equal and opposite positions, risk-free profit, sell future and buy underlying
