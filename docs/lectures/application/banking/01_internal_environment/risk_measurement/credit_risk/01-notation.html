Notation

The symbol table for everything that follows. Every later credit-risk lecture (PD, LGD, EAD, A-IRB capital, IFRS 9 ECL) reuses these definitions, so this is the file to bookmark and the file to argue about first.

Credit risk, foundation file Reading time, 30 minutes Source: docs/wiki/.../credit_risk/03_notation.md
Step 1

The 30-second version

A credit-risk model is a sequence of probabilities, ratios, and indicators stitched together across two time axes (the loan's age and the calendar) and one population axis (the obligor). Almost every dispute in a modelling team is, on inspection, a dispute about what a symbol means. This lecture is the single place those definitions live.

Five families of symbol carry the weight. Identifiers and time distinguish a loan \(i\) from its obligor \(i'\), the loan's age \(t\) from the calendar date \(t'\), and the outcome horizon \(k\) from both. Delinquency mechanics turn raw days-past-due into the binary default indicator the regulator and the IFRS 9 stage transitions both depend on. The PD family covers the through-the-cycle, point-in-time, conditional, unconditional, cumulative, and lifetime variants, each with a different purpose. EAD and LGD turn a default event into a monetary loss. The capital objects at the top of the stack (WCDR, K, RWA, ECL, EL shortfall) consume all of the above.

Feynman test A credit model is a translation device. On one side: a borrower who pays or does not pay, an account balance that rises and falls, an economy that booms and slumps. On the other side: a single number called expected loss. Every symbol in this lecture is a step in that translation. The trick to reading any credit model is to keep asking, at every symbol, "is this about a single loan, a population of loans, or the economy as a whole?", and "is the time index the loan's age or the calendar date?". Get those two questions right and the algebra becomes mechanical.
Step 2

Why this matters

Inconsistent notation is one of the few sources of model error that survives every layer of validation. A documentation slip that swaps the order of two arguments will not fail a unit test, will not show up in a Q-Q plot, and will not break a backtest. It will only surface when a regulator asks a junior analyst to define a term and gets an answer that contradicts the model technical document. By then the audit trail is months deep and the rebuild is expensive.

The risk is not theoretical. The wiki notation file from which this lecture descends contains an outright typo (a cure indicator referencing an undefined symbol), an argument-order inconsistency that propagates through six downstream formulae, a cumulative-PD definition that double-counts the survival chain, and a Vasicek formula that works in two opposite directions depending on the unstated sign convention of the forward-looking index. Each is harmless in isolation. Combined into a full ECL or capital number, they shift results by tens of basis points and turn the audit report into a multi-day discussion.

The supervisor cares about this for a specific reason. The IRB use test under CRR Article 144, the IFRS 9 governance expectations under PRA SS3/24, and the broader BCBS 239 data lineage requirement all hinge on the bank being able to explain, end to end, what each number means. Notation is the bottom rung of that ladder. If the symbol table is wrong, every higher rung wobbles.

Step 3

The mental model

The single most useful picture in credit notation is the two-axis time diagram. A loan ages from \(t = 0\) at origination to maturity along its loan-term axis. Each loan also sits at a calendar date \(t'\). The outcome window \(k\) is a horizon (usually 12 months) measured forward from a chosen observation point on either axis. Most notation errors are time-axis confusions.

The second picture matters because the cumulative-PD formula is the one that goes wrong most often. The survival probability \(S_{i,t}\) is already a cumulative product across loan ages; the cumulative PD is its complement, not a product over an already-cumulative quantity. Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The two clocks are the source of half the bugs

Almost every credit-model defect that survives validation comes from a confusion between loan age \(t\) and calendar date \(t'\). The TTC PD lives on the calendar axis (averaged across cycles). The lifetime PD lives on the loan-term axis (summed across ages). The PiT PD sits at a single point on both. The FLI lives on the calendar axis only. When a model document writes "PD at time \(t\)" without saying which clock, a code review three years later cannot reconstruct the intent. Always carry both subscripts and never drop one to make a formula look cleaner.

Indicator brackets vs Iverson brackets vs predicate evaluation

This lecture uses \(\mathbf{1}[\cdot]\) (the indicator function returning 0 or 1) consistently. The wiki notation file used the Iverson bracket \([\cdot]\), which is the same object but visually collides with normal square brackets used for intervals such as \([t'_a, t'_b]\). The collision is harmless until a formula is read aloud or transcribed, at which point ambiguity creeps in. Pick a convention and stick to it.

The cumulative PD is the complement of the survival probability, full stop

The chain is: 1-period marginal PDs \(\to\) 1-period survival probabilities \(\to\) cumulative survival as a product \(\to\) cumulative PD as one minus that product. The sum-of-PDs form looks like the right answer at small PDs and breaks down quickly at larger ones. For a portfolio with monthly PDs of 1%, the 60-month cumulative PD by the sum form is 60% and by the correct form is \(1 - 0.99^{60} \approx 45\%\). The 15 percentage points are not a rounding error; they are a real provisioning gap.

The Vasicek sign convention

The Basel WCDR uses \(+\sqrt{\rho} \cdot N^{-1}(0.999)\) in the numerator. \(N^{-1}(0.999) \approx 3.09\) is positive, and the formula returns a conditional PD higher than the TTC. Plug in \(N^{-1}(0.5) = 0\) and you recover the TTC. The systematic factor in this convention is "positive = stress". If you adopt the academic convention where the systematic factor \(Z\) is "positive = good times", the sign flips to \(-\sqrt{\rho} \cdot Z\) and the calculation is identical. Both are correct; what is incorrect is failing to state which convention you have chosen. The wiki file did not, and the formula it presents is right if and only if the FLI is signed so that positive means stress. State the convention in the documentation, every time.

Argument order is documentation

Across this lecture, the convention is: outcome operators take the horizon \(k\) first, then the probation period \(p\), so \(D^{\*}_{i,t}(k, p)\). PD operators take the horizon \(k\) first, then any conditioning information, so \(\text{PD}^{\text{PiT}}_{i,t}(k, x_i, x_{i,t})\). This means the leftmost argument is always the time window. The wiki had \(D^{\*}\) sometimes with \(k\) first, sometimes \(p\) first; the inconsistency is harmless to a careful reader and toxic to a junior analyst.

EAD vs CCF vs EADF

Three closely related symbols. EAD is the currency amount at default. The Basel CCF is the share of the undrawn limit that is expected to be drawn before default and is the regulatory convention for off-balance-sheet credit lines. The EADF is the ratio of EAD over current balance and is the modelling convention preferred for revolving products where the undrawn limit is not a meaningful denominator (because limits float or are large relative to typical drawn balances). The two are not interchangeable, and a model document should say which one is being estimated.

LGD has more flavours than any other parameter

Workout LGD comes from observed recovery cash flows on closed workouts. Market LGD comes from secondary-market prices on defaulted debt. LRA LGD is the historical average across both. Downturn LGD adds a margin for adverse periods. PiT LGD adds the current-state adjustment for IFRS 9. ELBE is the best-estimate LGD on already-defaulted exposures. The Basel formula uses downturn LGD. The IFRS 9 formula uses PiT (or near-PiT) LGD. The accounting and regulatory LGDs for the same loan can differ by ten or twenty percentage points; both can be right.

Provisions vs EL: the regulatory-accounting bridge

The EL shortfall \(\max(0, \text{EL}^{\text{IRB}} - \text{Provisions})\) is the CRR Article 159 mechanic that connects the accounting and regulatory worlds. If IRB EL exceeds accounting provisions, the excess is deducted from CET1 (the loss is not yet recognised on the books, so capital absorbs it). If provisions exceed IRB EL, the excess can be added back to T2 capital up to a 0.6% of RWA cap. The mechanism is the reason IFRS 9 ECL and IRB EL must both be calculated and reconciled at every reporting date, even though they answer different questions.

Step 6

Test yourself

  1. State the difference between \(t\) and \(t'\). Which clock does the TTC PD live on, which clock does the lifetime PD live on, and which clock does the FLI live on?
  2. Write out the cumulative PD to loan age \(t\) in terms of 1-period conditional PiT PDs. State the small-PD approximation and give a worked example where the two forms differ by more than 10 percentage points.
  3. The Basel WCDR formula contains a term \(+\sqrt{\rho} \cdot N^{-1}(0.999)\). What is the sign convention that makes the plus sign correct, and what would have to change if you adopted the opposite convention?
  4. Define EAD, CCF, and EADF. Give an example product for which the CCF is the natural metric and one for which the EADF is the natural metric.
  5. Define workout LGD, market LGD, LRA LGD, downturn LGD, PiT LGD, and ELBE. Which of these enters the Basel K formula and which enters the IFRS 9 ECL formula?
  6. Explain the EL shortfall mechanic under CRR Article 159. When does the shortfall hit CET1, and when can excess provisions be added back to T2 capital?
  7. State the conditions on the calendar window \([t'_a, t'_b]\) under which the long-run average forward default rate is a reasonable estimator of the TTC PD.
  8. The default-outcome operator is \(D^{\*}_{i,t}(k, p)\). State which argument is the outcome horizon and which is the probation period, and explain why argument order is part of the model documentation rather than a stylistic choice.
