A-IRB regulatory capital

Thirty-two lectures covering the advanced internal ratings based approach end to end: the regulatory frame and the definition of default, the data foundation, then the full seven-stage modelling pipeline run three times over for PD, LGD, and EAD, and finally the margin of conservatism, the risk-weighted asset calculation, and the monitoring cycle. This is the deepest track in the whole course.

How this track is organised

The wiki organises A-IRB material in nine lifecycle folders. The lectures unroll those folders into a single flat numbered chain, because the lifecycle is genuinely sequential and a flat chain makes the dependencies visible. Lectures 1 to 5 set the frame: what regulatory capital is for, how expected and unexpected loss decompose, what counts as a default under CRR Article 178, how the nine stages fit together, and what the use test demands. Lectures 6 to 8 build the data foundation, because every parameter estimate downstream is only as good as the modelling reference dataset underneath it.

Lectures 9 to 29 are the heart of the track. The same seven-stage pipeline runs three times: concept, target and data, sampling and representativeness, feature engineering, risk differentiation, risk quantification, testing. Reading the PD block first is worth the effort even if your interest is LGD or EAD, because the PD block introduces the pipeline vocabulary and the later blocks are written as deltas against it. The LGD block's substantive difference is the three-component mixture; the EAD block's is the continuous target and the GLM that follows from it.

Lectures 30 to 32 close the loop. The margin of conservatism converts every acknowledged weakness in the preceding 29 lectures into an explicit capital add-on, the RWA lecture derives the Vasicek formula that turns the three parameters into a capital number, and the monitoring lecture defines the cadence that keeps the model fit for use between redevelopments.
