Data controls, governance and limitations

The wrapper around stage 1 of the A-IRB lifecycle. How the bank evidences that its data layer is controlled (BCBS 239, SS3/24), governed (ownership, lineage, metadata) and honest about its weaknesses (the data-limitations register, which feeds margin of conservatism category A).

Step 1

The 30-second version

The data layer needs three things on top of the technical pipeline. Controls are the automated and manual checks that catch breaks the moment they happen: file-transfer record counts, error-handling routines, periodic sample-accuracy testing, business-logic validation, and formal change control on any modification to mappings, rules or source systems. Governance assigns ownership and accountability: a single team (commonly Risk, Finance and Treasury, RFT) owns the end-to-end lineage, maintains the data dictionary, runs the governance forums and escalates material issues to the Model Risk Committee. Limitations is the honest list of what the data cannot tell you: exclusions, gaps, definitional inconsistencies, policy-driven shifts. The limitations register feeds directly into margin-of-conservatism category A and is one of the first documents the supervisor asks for.

The three together implement BCBS 239 principle 1 (governance), the PRA SS3/24 expectations on data quality (paragraphs 11.1 to 11.8), and CRR Article 144(1)(d) on the institution-wide risk-data infrastructure. If any of the three is missing, the model approval is at risk regardless of how strong the modelling itself is.

Feynman test The simplest way to picture this lecture is to imagine you are the supervisor walking into the bank for an IRB inspection. The model documentation has already been read. The questions you ask are: who owns this data? Who fixes it when it breaks? What checks catch a break before it reaches the model? And, crucially, what do you know is wrong with the data that you have decided to live with, and how have you compensated for it in the parameters? Controls answer "who catches breaks". Governance answers "who owns the data". Limitations answer "what is known to be imperfect". A bank that cannot answer all three loses the IRB benefit.
Step 2

Why this matters

The IRB approval programme typically costs a large bank between £20m and £80m. The biggest source of cost overrun is not the modelling but the data infrastructure. Many banks have discovered that their source-system data, when subjected to formal IRB-grade controls and governance for the first time, has structural issues that take 18 to 24 months and tens of millions of pounds to remediate. The controls, governance and limitations work in this lecture is not paperwork: it is the operating model that determines whether the IRB build is a 24-month or a 48-month project.

The supervisory pattern at SREP is also revealing. The first question is rarely "is your PD model accurate"; it is more often "show me your data-quality dashboard for the last quarter". A bank that can demonstrate a working control framework, named owners for every field, and a regularly-updated limitations register typically gets the benefit of the doubt on modelling questions. A bank that cannot demonstrate the control wrapper finds itself in remediation mode regardless of how good the underlying statistics are.

For F107 the controls and governance content is testable as a stand-alone, and the limitations register is the natural link to the MoC lecture later in the track. The exam pattern is to ask a question on data controls and a follow-on question on how that control framework supports the use test or the MoC overlay.

Step 3

The mental model

The control wrapper sits at three levels of the data layer. At the boundary (data entering the bank from source systems), file-transfer and error-handling controls catch volume and format breaks. Inside the pipeline, business-logic validation and change control catch definitional and processing breaks. At the output (model inputs leaving the data layer), periodic sample-accuracy testing reconciles the end-to-end transformation back to source. Governance and the limitations register sit alongside these three levels rather than inside any one of them: they describe the accountability and known-weakness state that the controls operate within.

The diagram makes one important point. Controls are reactive; they catch breaks. Governance is structural; it determines who acts when a control fires. The limitations register is forward-looking; it documents weaknesses the bank has decided to live with and compensate for through MoC. A bank that has all three operating in concert can demonstrate end-to-end data assurance. A bank with only one or two has a gap the supervisor will find.

Step 4

The detail

Data controls

The control framework comprises five specific control families. Each operates at a defined point in the pipeline and produces evidence the supervisor can inspect.

Control familyWhat it doesExample
File transfer controlsAutomated record-count and format validation on each ingestion of source data, with exception reporting where expected and received volumes divergeDaily bureau data load: expected volume 100K rows ± 5%. Actual 78K rows triggers an exception, blocks the downstream MRD refresh, and pages the on-call data-ops engineer.
Error handlingEmbedded routines that identify, log and escalate data anomalies or processing errors before they reach the model inputPandera schema validation in the DQA notebook; structured error log with severity, source field and resolution status.
Data accuracy checksPeriodic (typically quarterly) manual validation of a sample of records against the original source systemQuarterly sample of ~200 customer accounts: every key field compared to the source-system value at the same date, with reconciliation differences investigated and resolved.
Business-logic validationPeriodic validation of transformation rules, business filters and derivations against approved definitions and regulatory expectationsAnnual review of the delinquency-flag and transactor-indicator logic; confirmation that the DPD calculation still aligns with the CRR Article 178 definition.
Change controlFormal approval and testing of any change to data mappings, business rules or source systems before deploymentSource-system schema change: SQL diff reviewed, impact assessed on downstream MRD, regression-tested on a backfill, signed off by the data-governance lead before production deployment.

The control framework is anchored in three regulatory texts. BCBS 239 provides the principles (governance, accuracy, integrity, completeness, timeliness, reconciliation, distribution). PRA SS11/13 (now superseded by SS3/24) provided the UK-specific IRB data expectations. PA Directive D5/2017 (South Africa) provides the SARB framework for data management in banks. The five families above are the operational implementation of the requirements set in those texts.

Data governance

Governance assigns ownership and accountability. In most large banks the credit-risk data layer is owned by a Risk, Finance and Treasury (RFT) team, who hold the end-to-end accountability and ensure adherence to the firm's data-management policy. The five governance pillars are:

  • Data source ownership. RFT maintains up-to-date documentation of every source system that feeds the MRD, including data dictionaries, transformation logic and named ownership contacts. A new source system cannot be added to the MRD pipeline without going through the RFT onboarding process first.
  • Data lineage and traceability. End-to-end documentation traces every data element from original source system through to model input table. Every transformation and aggregation is identified, so any model output can be traced back to its source-system inputs.
  • Metadata management. Every key data element is catalogued with a defined business description, data-quality threshold, and control owner. Metadata is treated as a first-class deliverable, not a by-product.
  • Governance oversight. Data-quality metrics and control outcomes are reviewed in regular RFT Data Governance Forums. Material issues escalate to the Model Risk Committee for resolution.
  • Regulatory compliance. The governance process ensures all data used in credit-risk models remains compliant with PRA and ECB (or local equivalent) expectations on data lineage, accuracy and use-test alignment.

The governance design pattern that has emerged across well-run banks is the three-lines-of-defence mapping. First line: RFT operates the pipeline and the controls. Second line: model validation and operational risk independently challenge the data, with the validation team running an annual independent review. Third line: internal audit gives a third opinion at lower frequency, typically every 2 to 3 years. The supervisor inspects the work of all three lines but treats the first-line evidence as the primary input.

Data limitations

The limitations register is the bank's honest catalogue of what the data cannot tell you. Eight categories of limitation recur across portfolios. Each entry includes the description of the limitation, the affected scope, the materiality assessment, and the compensating action (typically an MoC uplift, an exclusion, or a conservative parameter assumption).

LimitationTypical exampleCompensating action
Non-representative period exclusionsCOVID-19 lockdown period (2020 Q2 to 2021 Q1) excluded from the LRA window because government support distorted default ratesMoC category A uplift; sensitivity analysis with and without the period; explicit disclosure in development document
Limited internal historyProduct launched in 2019 has only 5 years of internal data, below the regulatory minimum for some applicationsExternal benchmarking, MoC uplift, conservative LRA assumption
Segment-specific data gapNew portfolio acquired in 2025 lacks sufficient historical performance dataExcluded from main model development; rated using benchmarking or conservative assumptions; reviewed at next redevelopment cycle
Inconsistent DoD implementationWrite-off logic or forbearance flagging changed operationally in 2021, creating discontinuity in the target variableRe-derivation of historical default flag using current logic where possible; MoC uplift for the residual inconsistency
Data migrations and system changesBureau data gap in 2022 during a vendor migration; truncated behavioural histories for some accountsExclusion of affected periods or accounts; imputation of missing fields with documented assumptions
Sparse observations for rare eventsDefault rate in a specific segment is too low to support a fully granular PD modelSegment pooling, regularisation, or use of expert overlay; documented limitation on model granularity
Incomplete external dataBureau coverage is high but not 100%; some applicants have thin or no bureau history"No bureau" treated as a feature; conservative PD assumption for thin-file accounts
Policy-driven shiftsCredit-score cut-off raised in 2023, changing portfolio composition; new affordability rules in 2024 changed approval profileCohort analysis to disentangle policy effect from economic effect; MoC uplift if the disentanglement is incomplete

The limitations register has three formal touch-points each year. The development team updates it at every model build. The validation team independently reviews it at every tollgate. The governance forum approves the MoC uplifts that compensate for the material limitations. The register travels with the model into the SREP package, and the supervisor reads it before reading the model documentation.

From limitations to MoC category A

The relationship between this lecture and the later MoC lecture is direct. CRR Article 179(1)(f) and EBA/GL/2017/16 require that "PD estimates capture the long-run average default rate, and reflect all relevant available information and add a margin of conservatism related to the expected range of estimation errors". The MoC framework operationalises this requirement by splitting the uplift into three categories: A for data and information deficiencies, B for methodological weaknesses, and C for general estimation error. Each item on the limitations register that is judged material maps onto a category-A uplift in the MoC calculation.

The exam-worthy detail is that the same limitations register feeds both the modelling team (who use it to design the MoC) and the validation team (who use it to challenge the MoC). Banks that try to keep parallel limitations registers for the two teams fail consistency tests at validation. The single register, owned by the development team and reviewed by validation, is the regulatory expectation.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The honest limitations register is one of the strongest signals a bank can send to its supervisor. Banks that submit IRB packages with a brief or perfunctory limitations register are signalling either that they have not done the work, or that they are unwilling to acknowledge their data weaknesses; both readings are unfavourable. Banks that submit comprehensive registers with explicit, quantified compensating MoC uplifts are signalling competence and discipline; the supervisor reads this as the absence of red flags, which speeds approval.

The second insight is that controls and governance compound. A bank with strong controls but weak governance will catch breaks but not act on them; the data dashboard glows red and nothing happens. A bank with strong governance but weak controls will act decisively on the breaks it knows about but will miss the ones it cannot see; the action is loud but partial. Only the combination delivers full assurance, and the combination is hard to build because the two require different organisational cultures. Strong controls reward automation, instrumentation and discipline. Strong governance rewards seniority, accountability and cross-functional authority. Building both in the same team takes years.

The third insight is structural. The data layer is the place where the bank's modelling and IT functions meet, and they often have competing priorities (modellers want stability, IT wants change). The RFT team exists at this boundary explicitly to manage the tension. Banks that try to embed data governance inside either the modelling function or the IT function find that the other side is unwilling to defer; only an independent team with explicit authority over both sides can hold the boundary. The location of the data-governance function on the org chart is therefore a structural decision, not an administrative one.
