Application scoring

Five lectures on the stage where risk is chosen rather than measured. Everything else in the credit risk collection estimates the risk of exposures that already exist; underwriting decides what gets written. The track runs from the qualitative frameworks that structured lending judgement before models existed, through the application scorecard and the seven-step decision chain it sits inside, to the behavioural scorecard that takes over once an account has performance, and closes with the data foundations and modelling techniques.

How this track is organised

The wiki holds seven notes across three lifecycle folders, weighted heavily towards the introduction. The lectures follow that weighting: three on the conceptual frame, where the source is strongest, and two on data and modelling, where it is thinner and the lectures lean on the A-IRB chain for the machinery that carries over.

The organising distinction across the whole track is between the two scorecards. An application scorecard uses only what is known at the point of application and is the only estimate available on day one. A behavioural scorecard uses observed account performance and refreshes monthly. They are complements rather than substitutes: the application score sets the origination reference point that IFRS 9 staging compares against, and the behavioural score keeps the risk estimate current for capital and provisioning.

Read the A-IRB capital track for the modelling pipeline in full, particularly lecture 9 on rating philosophies and lecture 13 on logistic regression and segmentation. The IFRS 9 track supplies the staging mechanics that consume the origination PD.

Track contents

  • Built1 — Underwriting: the front and back book, willingness against ability, qualitative factors and where they are strongest, specialised lending, the five Cs and CAMPARI with the observation that Margin is a return factor rather than a risk factor, internal against external ratings, and a quantification of what subjectivity costs in discrimination, consistency and bias
  • Built2 — Application scorecards: what a scorecard is and is not, the seven-step decision chain, the band table checked six ways, a pricing curve derived from a RAROC hurdle rather than asserted, why retail capital rises far less than retail risk, the PD hierarchy for IRB and IFRS 9, and the finding that banding concentrates cliff edges rather than removing them
  • Built3 — Behavioural scorecards: the five-phase handover, the blend weight derived by inverse-variance weighting and compared against the practitioner ramp, the blend as a volatility control with a symmetric cost, the six variants and their defining triples, why the missing unlikeness-to-pay component hits provisions ten times harder than capital, and the consolidation of six variants into four modelling problems
  • Built4 — Data and sampling: reject inference quantified in both directions, why the two biases flatter opposite things, what statistical augmentation can and cannot do, the selection-bias arithmetic that puts a number on what a holdout buys, and the design effect that turns twelve panel rows into 1.6 observations
  • Built5 — Modelling: the scaling and why the score range is decoration, what the points-to-double-odds actually doubles, why weight-of-evidence coding removes the linearity limitation before the model is fitted, the resampling result showing the intercept moves and nothing else, what the actuary-against-nurse check is really testing, and a closing synthesis of where the collection's errors were found

Source material

Lectures are built from docs/wiki/application/banking/01_internal_environment/risk_measurement/credit_risk/application_scoring/, whose seven notes run to around 410 lines. The introduction notes on underwriting, application scorecards and behavioural scorecards are substantive; the feature engineering and modelling notes are shorter and are supplemented from the A-IRB chain and from published supervisory material, including the EBA guidelines on PD and LGD estimation for the requirement that estimates reflect current risk characteristics.
