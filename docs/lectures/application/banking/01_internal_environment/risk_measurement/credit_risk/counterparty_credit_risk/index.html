Counterparty credit risk

Two lectures on the credit risk that lives in the trading book. Everything in the A-IRB and IFRS 9 tracks assumed a unilateral exposure whose size was close to observable. Here the exposure is bilateral, has no fixed sign, moves with the market, and is measured at the level of a legal netting agreement rather than a trade. The probability of default and the loss given default are borrowed from machinery already built. The exposure is the whole subject.

How this track is organised

The wiki splits this topic in two and the split is a good one, so the lectures follow it. The first note covers the regulatory frame: the trading book boundary, which assets carry counterparty credit risk, the netting and hedging set constructs, and the two capital charges with their standardised and internal-model variants. The second covers the mathematics the capital formulas consume: the expected exposure hierarchy, market-implied default probabilities from credit spreads, Monte Carlo simulation and wrong-way risk.

This is an F107 priority gap area, and unusually the constraint is not source material. Both wiki notes are substantive. The work is separating what they get right from two places where they slip, both of which are identified in the lectures and both of which would cost marks if carried into an exam.

Read the A-IRB capital track first for the banking book contrast, and market risk for the trading book context. Lecture 6 of the market risk track covers CVA as a pricing adjustment; the treatment here is of CVA as a capital charge, and the two are complements rather than duplicates.

Track contents

  • Built1 — Context and capital: bilateral exposure and what follows from it, counterparty against settlement risk, the trading book boundary, netting sets as legal constructs and hedging sets as computational ones, SA-CCR and the internal model method with the alpha and its 1.2 own-estimate floor, and the three CVA approaches with BA-CVA worked. Includes a closed-form result for the netting benefit and a self-contained proof that the source's full BA-CVA formula has dropped a term.
  • Built2 — Counterparty exposures: the four exposure metrics in order with a correction to the source's labelling, survival curves and hazard bootstrapping, the computational bill of Monte Carlo, the alpha inverted into the exposure-default correlation it assumes, and the credit triangle as an identification problem
