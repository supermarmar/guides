Credit concentration risk

Three lectures on the risk Pillar 1 assumes away. The IRB capital formula has a closed form because it treats the portfolio as infinitely granular and driven by a single systematic factor, and both assumptions are violated by real books. Concentration risk is the measured size of those violations, and because it has no prescribed formula it is one of the few places where an internal modelling judgement becomes a binding capital requirement directly.

How this track is organised

The wiki splits the topic three ways and the split follows the structure of the problem. The first note sets the frame: economic capital, how it differs from regulatory capital, and where the concentration add-on sits in the pipeline from parameter models through the ICAAP to the Pillar 2 requirement. The second covers single-name concentration, which violates the granularity assumption. The third covers sector concentration, which violates the single-factor assumption, and treats geographic concentration as another dimension of the same model.

Two of the three assumptions in that sentence are testable by simulation rather than assertion, and the lectures test them. The first lecture measures how wrong infinite granularity is at portfolio sizes from five obligors to five thousand, which turns out to settle both why the adjustment matters and why Basel moved it out of Pillar 1.

Read the A-IRB capital track first, particularly lecture 2 on the loss distribution and lecture 31 on the risk weight function, since this track measures the error in that formula rather than replacing it. The risk management track covers economic capital, the ICAAP and the SREP as processes.

Track contents

  • Built1 — Context: the ASRF model and its two assumptions, economic capital and the three dimensions on which it differs, the granularity gap measured by simulation from five obligors to five thousand, the three kinds of concentration, the pipeline to the Pillar 2 requirement, and why Pillar 1's bias reverses direction between granular and concentrated portfolios
  • Built2 — Single-name concentration: the granularity adjustment and its lineage, the Herfindahl index verified against the source, the true add-on simulated and found twenty-two times the index-scaled figure, why the index ratio uses the wrong benchmark and diverges asymptotically, and the large exposures regime read as an implicit granularity control
  • Built3 — Sector concentration: the multi-factor decomposition and why it nests the framework it corrects, the three-sector add-on verified to two decimal places, the convexity in correlation measured, the four calibration routes with their weaknesses, and the finding that summing per-sector capital overstates the add-on by 151 per cent
