IFRS 9 impairments

Twelve lectures on expected credit loss modelling under IFRS 9. The track is written as a set of deltas against the A-IRB capital track rather than a repeat of it: point-in-time rather than through-the-cycle, forward-looking scenarios rather than a downturn anchor, best estimate rather than a margin of conservatism, monthly rather than annual cadence, and an auditor rather than a supervisor as the primary challenger.

How this track is organised

The wiki organises IFRS 9 material in eight lifecycle folders. As with A-IRB capital, the lectures unroll those into a flat numbered chain. The sequence is deliberately shorter than the 32-lecture A-IRB chain because the modelling mechanics overlap heavily: the seven-stage pipeline, the feature engineering discipline, and the testing philosophy all carry over. Rather than restate them, each lecture here names its A-IRB analogue and then spends its length on what IFRS 9 does differently.

Lectures 1 to 4 set the accounting frame and the data foundation. Lectures 5 to 7 cover the three parameter models, where the substantive differences are the lifetime extension of PD through migration matrices, the removal of the downturn add-on and indirect costs from LGD, and the amortisation and prepayment treatment in EAD. Lectures 8 and 9 cover the two components with no A-IRB counterpart at all: forward-looking information and the significant-increase-in-credit-risk test. Lecture 10 aggregates everything into the staged ECL number, and lectures 11 and 12 close with testing and the monthly monitoring cycle.

Read the A-IRB capital track first if you can. This track assumes it.

Parameter models

  • Built5 — PD modelling: the four candidate methodologies, the empirical term structure estimator worked through, why the reference period makes the estimate point-in-time at every horizon, segmentation and its three tests, and the re-default consequence for the LGD model
  • Built6 — LGD modelling: the decomposition into probability of write-off given default and the two conditional severities, why the cure severity carries the larger derivative, the completion threshold and the width of its censoring bound, the three-part decomposition of the gap to the A-IRB parameter, and the eight per cent error in averaging parameters before multiplying
  • Built7 — EAD modelling: why IFRS 9 never defines the parameter, the amortisation profile and the prepayment overlay, the two channels by which prepayment reduces the provision and their relative size, the revolver carve-out in paragraph 5.5.20, the pipeline conversion factor tested for materiality two ways, and prepayment's credit selectivity

IFRS 9-specific components

  • Built8 — Forward-looking information: the four-step pipeline, when the intercept check holds and when it fails, the gap between the baseline and the weighted PD decomposed, quadrature nodes that close the discrete-versus-stochastic choice, the lifetime factor that does not average out, and the under-dispersion identity that no correlation test can see
  • Built9 — SICR: the PD comparison approach and its decision function, the four threshold families and the crossover between relative and absolute rules, why the absolute floor is structural rather than cosmetic, the roll-rate quota that inverts the procyclicality, the delinquency-based definition with its three parameters, and why prevalence rather than the cost ratio sets the stage 2 book

Aggregation, testing, monitoring

  • Built10 — ECL: the assembly and what the stage actually changes, the default window against the loss window, the staging cliff, the interest revenue switch in stage 3, the provision matrix, the three-factor covariance decomposition, and the one asset class that can carry a negative allowance
  • Built11 — Testing: three tiers of battery, the twelve stage transfer metrics grouped by what each detects, the four evaluation factors for a staging definition, why discrimination and calibration select different thresholds, and oscillation priced into the impairment charge
  • Built12 — Monitoring: the IFRS 7 35H reconciliation as the anchoring artefact, the four sources of movement, the IAS 8 change-in-estimate against error distinction, the auditor's three questions under ISA 540, why the impairment charge is half noise, and a synthesis of the seven errors the track identified
