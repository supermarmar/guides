Risk measurement, phase 3

Other Pillar 2A risks

The risks a bank must capitalise that have no Pillar 1 formula and no single measurement discipline: pension obligation risk, model risk, business model and strategic risk, reputational risk and legal risk. Two lectures. The first is a genuine quantitative exercise and is the most actuarial topic in the banking track; the second is a set of judgemental charges where the honest answer is that the number is argued rather than calculated.

How this track is organised

Two lectures matching the wiki's own division, and the division is meaningful rather than administrative. Pension obligation risk is quantifiable: it has a defined liability, a stress specification and a formula, and the lecture works one through. The remaining Pillar 2A risks do not, and the second lecture is explicit that a model risk charge expressed as a percentage uplift, or a business model risk charge expressed in basis points of risk-weighted assets, is a judgement given numerical form rather than a measurement.

Status badges show what is built (built) or planned. Both wiki notes are short, so both lectures extend them.

Lectures

The quantifiable one

  • built01. Pension obligation risk — why only defined benefit schemes create the exposure, the liability's three sensitivities, the stressed deficit formula, the asymmetry by which deficits consume capital while surpluses do not supply it, the two tests a mitigation must pass, and a worked stress showing the charge is essentially a charge for the scheme's asset-liability mismatch.

The judgemental ones

  • built02. Model, business model, reputational and legal risk — the percentage uplift formula and the identity that it covers a proportional understatement equal to its own multiplier, the margin of conservatism boundary, what each of business model risk's three assessment methods cannot detect, the reputational transmission chain, and the standard a judgemental capital number should meet. Closes with the three patterns that recur across the whole risk measurement track.

Why this is the most actuarial corner of the banking track

Pension obligation risk is an asset-liability management problem on a defined benefit scheme, with liabilities sensitive to a discount rate, to inflation and to longevity. That is the actuarial problem, unmodified, appearing inside a bank's capital assessment. An actuary reading lecture 1 will find nothing methodologically new and one thing genuinely surprising, which is the asymmetric regulatory treatment: a deficit must be covered from common equity and a surplus is not admitted as capital at all.

The lecture also demonstrates something the wiki's formula does not make obvious. The stressed deficit is driven far more by the scheme's asset allocation than by the size of the existing deficit, so the Pillar 2A charge is best understood as a charge for the mismatch rather than for the deficit, and it can be very largely removed by changing the mismatch.
