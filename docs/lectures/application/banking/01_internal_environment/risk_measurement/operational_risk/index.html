Operational risk

The risk of loss from inadequate or failed internal processes, people, and systems, or from external events. This track covers the sources and event taxonomy of operational risk, loss data collection and modelling, the three Basel II capital approaches (BIA, TSA, AMA), the Basel III Standardised Measurement Approach that replaced them, the anti-money laundering and know-your-customer framework, and the modern cyber risk and operational resilience supervisory regime built around BCBS d516 and the SARB / FSCA Joint Standard.

How this track is organised

The wiki under docs/wiki/application/banking/01_internal_environment/risk_measurement/operational_risk/ organises content in five files moving from taxonomy through measurement to capital to compliance. The lectures follow that sequence directly and add a sixth lecture covering cyber risk and operational resilience, drawing on supervisory sources (BCBS d516, PRA SS1/21, SARB Joint Standard) rather than wiki material. Lectures 1 and 2 build the conceptual and data foundation: what operational risk is, how it is categorised, and how loss data is collected and modelled. Lectures 3 and 4 cover two generations of capital calculation: the Basel II approaches introduced in 2004 and the Basel III Standardised Measurement Approach that replaced them in 2023. Lecture 5 treats anti-money laundering and know-your-customer requirements as a stand-alone compliance topic with direct connections to Pillar 2 conduct risk and, for ASSA candidates, to South African regulatory requirements under FICA. Lecture 6 covers cyber risk and operational resilience as the modern supervisory frontier, including the BCBS d516 Principles for Operational Resilience and the SARB / FSCA Joint Standard on Cybersecurity and Cyber Resilience.
