Behavioural modelling of cash flows

The discipline that underwrites every metric in the liquidity track. Contractual and behavioural tenor diverge in two directions: deposits stickier than contract, loans shorter than contract. The job of behavioural modelling is to put numbers on both sides of that gap.

Step 1

The 30-second version

Every liquidity metric in the previous four lectures depends on cash-flow assumptions. Behavioural modelling is the discipline that sets those assumptions. The central observation is that contractual tenor and behavioural tenor diverge, in opposite directions for assets and liabilities.

On the liability side, demand deposits and instant-access savings accounts have a contractual tenor of "now" but a behavioural tenor that can run to multiple years if balances are persistently stable. On the asset side, residential mortgages have a contractual tenor of 20 to 30 years but a behavioural tenor of 6 to 7 years on average, because customers prepay, refinance, or move home. The job of behavioural modelling is to quantify both gaps using historical observation supplemented by stress-period evidence.

Feynman test The behaviouralisation exercise is an actuarial experience investigation by another name. Group accounts into cohorts (by product, by opening date, by customer type), plot the aggregate balance over time, and observe what actually happens. The behavioural tenor is then the tenor that best fits the observed run-off, with a conservatism overlay that recognises future stresses may not look like past ones. An actuary moving into bank liquidity work will find the framework familiar immediately.
Step 2

Why this matters

Pure liquidity stress events are rare, and many senior managers have never experienced one. The 2007 to 2009 stress and the brief 2023 stresses (SVB, Credit Suisse) are the main events the current generation of treasury teams has lived through, with a thin scattering of country-specific events in between. The historical record for calibrating behavioural assumptions is consequently shallow.

Conservatism and prudence are therefore the appropriate approach for both liability strategy and liquidity risk management. The candidate should not assume that statistical observation of past behaviour is a reliable guide to future behaviour in a stress event of a type not seen in the data. Every behavioural assumption needs a sense check that asks "what would this number have been in October 2008", and where the answer is "much worse", the assumption is too generous.

Three nuances are worth flagging up front:

  • Digital and mobile accounts can behave very differently from branch or postal accounts, particularly for younger demographics. The behavioural tenor of a digital current account may be materially shorter than that of a branch-opened current account in the same product class.
  • In markets such as South Africa, very low-income customers may withdraw entire salary payments in cash on pay day, creating predictable but concentrated outflow events that look stable on a monthly snapshot but extreme on a daily snapshot.
  • The optimum liability strategy combines conservatism on the central assumption with supplementary forward-looking stress scenarios, both internally calibrated and run through the LCR / NSFR / CFP framework.
Step 3

The mental model, two ALM gap views

All liquidity management requires a bank to model both contractual and behavioural tenor profiles and the resulting asset-liability gap profiles. Assets and liabilities may exhibit behavioural characteristics that are materially different from their underlying contractual characteristics.

  • Liabilities (call accounts, demand deposits) have short contractual tenors but may exhibit stable, long-dated behavioural tenors if customers leave balances untouched.
  • Assets (residential mortgages with 20 to 30 year contractual terms) typically repay earlier than contractual maturity due to prepayment behaviour, producing a shorter behavioural tenor.

Understanding both sides of this distinction drives more informed decisions on funding, liquidity management, and fund transfer pricing (FTP), which is applied to behavioural (or expected) tenors rather than contractual tenors. The two views look as follows.

Step 4

Modelling demand deposits

The behavioural tenor of contractually short-dated deposits (call accounts, current accounts, instant-access savings) is set through statistical observation. The general procedure is:

  1. Observe month-end spot and average balances over time, grouped by product type (retail current accounts, corporate operational accounts, savings accounts, etc.).
  2. Note behaviour at expected outflow points (month-end, quarter-end, tax dates, payday).
  3. Note behaviour at times of stress (the interbank market freeze of October 2008 following the Lehman bankruptcy is the canonical observation in the modern dataset).

The Matz criteria

To better understand the stickiness of specific deposit pools, each liability should be assessed against the following criteria, originally proposed by Leonard Matz in his work on liquidity risk modelling. These eight questions are the standard checklist:

CriterionWhy it matters
Is the deposit insured?Insured retail deposits are by far the stickiest, because the depositor has no incentive to move on a confidence shock. The 3% LCR factor for insured deposits embeds this.
Is the deposit secured?A secured deposit (where the bank has pledged collateral) is operationally harder to move and consequently stickier.
Are the funds controlled by the owner?Funds with restricted access (escrow, regulatory deposits, trust-account balances) are stickier than freely-controllable balances.
Does the customer have other relationships with the bank?A customer with a mortgage, credit card, and current account is much stickier than a standalone deposit relationship.
Is there internet access enabling rapid transfer?Digital-channel deposits move materially faster than branch-only deposits, particularly in retail. The 2023 SVB run was the dramatic demonstration.
Is the depositor a net borrower?Net borrowers (more loans than deposits) cannot move funds out without an offsetting move, so their deposits are operationally sticky.
Type of counterparty, level of financial sophistication?Retail customers are stickier than corporate treasurers, who are stickier than money market funds. Financial sophistication is the gradient.
Direct depositor or via a third party?Deposits placed via an intermediary platform (a deposit aggregator, a savings comparison site) are less sticky than direct relationships, because the intermediary actively chases rate.

Stable aggregate balances observed across a sufficiently long time period (including at least one stress period) support the assignment of a longer behavioural tenor, typically with a haircut applied. A common modelling output is something like "90% of money transmission account balances treated as 5-year tenor", which means 90% of the aggregate balance is funded on a behavioural 5-year basis, with the remaining 10% treated as overnight.

The conservative cap

Hard cap on behavioural tenor Regardless of what historical statistics suggest, the behavioural tenor for demand deposits (current accounts, instant-access accounts) should not be set longer than 3 years, and certainly not longer than 5 years. The reason is not statistical but epistemic: the nature of future liquidity stress events may differ materially from historical observations, and treating a 10-year observed stability as a 10-year behavioural tenor is a forecast that has never been tested in a stress that long.

Multiple uses exist for the deposit behavioural analysis. The full list includes: FTP setting (long-behavioural deposits earn a higher FTP credit); calculation of an internal LCR view that may be more conservative than the regulatory LCR; determination of an appropriate LAB size in proportion to behavioural-short balances; informing customer pricing strategy where the bank prices LCR-friendly behavioural tenors more attractively to encourage migration.

Step 5

Modelling prepayment behaviour

Asset behavioural profiles, where assets repay earlier than contractual maturity, are obtained by combining historical observation with prepayment assumptions. The most important asset class is the residential mortgage book, where the historical average life is typically 6 to 7 years against a contractual term of 20 or 30 years. The same 6 to 7 year behavioural tenor is used for FTP purposes regardless of the contractual term, on the principle that the FTP system should price the actual funding requirement, not the headline contract.

Three drivers of prepayment

  • Refinancing as rates fall, customers refinance into lower-rate mortgages and the original loan prepays.
  • Moving home as customers move, the original mortgage is repaid from the sale proceeds. This driver is roughly rate-insensitive and produces the baseline 4 to 5 year refinance/move cycle.
  • Lump-sum prepayments as customers receive bonuses, inheritances, or other lump sums, they pay down mortgage balances.

Undrawn facilities and revolving credit

The post-2008 lesson For undrawn facilities and revolving credit facilities, best practice is to treat the asset as running to its full contractual tenor for FTP purposes. This is conservative but necessary, because back-up and other liquidity lines provided to customers are likely to be drawn precisely when the bank is seeking to preserve liquidity, i.e. during a funding crisis. Prior to the 2008 crisis, commitment lines typically attracted only a flat standing charge (e.g. 10-20 bps). Post-crisis, these assets are treated as running to full contractual maturity (whether drawn or not), with the appropriate liquidity premium charged at origination to reflect the embedded liquidity risk.
Step 6

Modelling contingency funding obligations

Contingent, off-balance-sheet, and collateral obligations also generate term funding requirements. The principal challenge is understanding the tenor characteristics of these cash flows, which by definition are uncertain.

Derivatives and CSA arrangements

Interbank derivatives trading takes place under the Credit Support Annex (CSA) of the standard ISDA contract. Under a two-way CSA, the mark-to-market value of each derivative is exchanged as cash collateral. In theory, a perfectly hedged book has netted zero collateral flows because what a bank posts on the negative leg it receives from the hedge counterparty.

However, many counterparties (corporates, sovereign authorities, debt management offices, central banks) do not sign CSA agreements. Under this one-way CSA arrangement, the bank must post cash collateral when the mark-to-market is negative to that counterparty, but receives nothing when the mark-to-market is positive. This creates a structural funding requirement that depends on market moves rather than on contractual cash flows.

The bank must apply the appropriate tenor liquidity premium to the net mark-to-market of all uncollateralised derivatives, based on the time-bucketed net cash-flow profile of those positions. The result feeds into the NSFR (uncollateralised derivative receivables attract a 100% RSF plus 10% of derivatives payable) and into the bank's own structural liquidity model. The lesson from 2008 was that one-way CSA collateral was a much larger structural funding requirement than pre-crisis banks had assumed, particularly in stressed market conditions when both market moves and collateral calls amplify simultaneously.

Step 7

The behaviouralisation exercise

The behaviouralisation exercise is the formal process by which a bank sets behavioural tenors for both assets and liabilities. The methodology is analogous to an actuarial experience investigation: use historical data to set credible behavioural assumptions, then overlay judgment about how future experience may differ.

For deposits (liabilities)

  1. Group accounts by cohort: by opening date, by product, and by customer type. The cohort definition determines how confounders are controlled. A "retail current account opened in 2020" cohort is much cleaner than an unsegmented retail current account population.
  2. Plot aggregate balances over time for each cohort. The chart should run from cohort inception to today, with clear markers for stress periods.
  3. Set the behavioural tenor based on actual observation of balance run-off, noting outliers and stress-period behaviour. The tenor is the duration over which a defined proportion of the initial balance survives.

For loans (assets)

  1. Group loan cohorts by product category (residential mortgage, personal loan, credit card, corporate term loan) and inception period (typically annual or quarterly vintages).
  2. Observe month-end spot balances to construct run-off profiles. Repayments typically occur towards month-end, so the month-end spot is the most informative observation.
  3. Calculate run-off rates for each repayment month; use the run-off rates to derive the behavioural tenor based on the observed run-off profile.

For revolving products (credit cards, overdrafts), it is common practice to additionally calculate the stable portion of outstanding balances. This is the core balance that is persistently utilised rather than transient, and it is treated as a term loan for FTP and liquidity-management purposes even though the contractual structure is a revolving facility.

Presentation and judgment

Results are typically presented graphically by cohort to allow visual identification of stable cores and to support conservative but credible tenor assignments. A bank should not simply accept a deposit pool as very long-dated solely because statistics suggest it. The tenor assumption must incorporate judgment about how future stresses may differ from the historical observation period, with the conservative cap from section 4 (3-year soft, 5-year hard) applied as the upper bound.

Actuarial parallel Every step of this process has a direct analogue in actuarial experience investigation. Cohort definition is risk-class segmentation. Plotting balances over time is the lapse-rate study. Run-off rate calculation is the calculation of an annual decrement. The conservative cap is the prudence margin. The judgment overlay is the actuary's view of how the future may differ from the past. The candidate moving from credit-risk modelling or insurance-modelling into bank liquidity work should expect the methods to feel immediately familiar, with the vocabulary translated.
Step 8

What the behavioural model is for

Behavioural assumptions feed every liquidity metric and every funds-transfer-price calculation. The main consumers are:

UseHow behavioural assumptions feed in
FTP systemEach product is priced internally on its behavioural tenor. A 25-year mortgage book is funded internally at the 6-7 year rate; a behavioural-5-year deposit earns the 5-year rate as an FTP credit. Without behavioural assumptions, the FTP system cannot match assets and liabilities by tenor at all.
Internal LCR viewThe regulatory LCR uses prescribed outflow factors. The internal LCR view replaces those with bank-specific behavioural assumptions and produces a more accurate (often more conservative) view of the bank's actual 30-day position.
LAB sizingThe size of the liquid asset buffer is calibrated to the bank's behavioural cash-flow profile under stress, not to a prescriptive schedule. Behavioural modelling of deposit run-off and asset prepayment under stress drives the buffer size.
Customer pricing strategyWhere behavioural analysis shows that a particular product behaves much better or worse than the regulatory factors imply, the bank can adjust pricing to encourage migration into the better-behaving products and to discourage flow into the worse-behaving ones.
NSFR >1yr classificationThe NSFR places liabilities with residual maturity above one year at 100% ASF. Where behavioural tenor exceeds one year, some jurisdictions allow the deposit to be classified at the higher ASF factor; the behavioural model is the evidence base for that classification.
Step 9

Synthesis and exam tips

Behavioural modelling sits underneath every metric in the liquidity track. The candidate who can articulate the two-direction divergence (deposits stickier than contract, loans shorter than contract), the Matz criteria as a stickiness checklist, the 3-year and 5-year hard caps for demand deposit tenors, and the actuarial parallel for the behaviouralisation exercise has the conceptual frame to answer almost any behavioural-modelling question.

Exam tip The most common F107 trap is the assumption that the regulatory LCR factors and NSFR factors are themselves the bank's behavioural assumptions. They are not. The regulatory factors are a prescribed minimum that all banks apply uniformly. The bank's own behavioural model produces a different, bank-specific set of assumptions that may be more or less conservative than the prescribed factors. The relationship is that the regulatory framework forces a minimum level of conservatism on every bank, but the bank's internal management uses its own behavioural model for FTP, internal LCR, and LAB sizing. Both views coexist; neither replaces the other.
