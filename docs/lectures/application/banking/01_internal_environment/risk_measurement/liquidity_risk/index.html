Risk measurement, phase 3

Liquidity risk

The risk that broke first in 2008 and broke fastest in 2023. Liquidity risk is governed today by two Basel III metrics, the liquidity coverage ratio for the next 30 days and the net stable funding ratio for the next year, sitting on top of a layer of bank-internal management metrics. The wiki source under docs/wiki/.../liquidity_risk/ is mirrored one-to-one, with three F107 priority gaps (intraday liquidity, formal stress testing scenarios, contingency funding plan) extended inside the relevant lectures.

How this track is organised

Five lectures, in the order the wiki places them. The shape of the track is bottom-up: bank-internal short-term measures first, the regulatory short-term metric (the LCR) second, bank-internal structural measures third, the regulatory structural metric (the NSFR) fourth, and the behavioural-modelling discipline that underwrites every one of them last.

Status badges show what is built (built) or planned. F107 gap extensions are flagged inside each lecture with a gap-flag callout, and a matching <!-- GAP --> marker is added to the wiki at the point where the missing coverage should later be backfilled.

Lectures

Bank-internal short-term metrics

  • built01. Short-term metrics — loan-to-deposit ratio, 1-week and 1-month liquidity ratios, the cumulative liquidity model, and the liquid asset buffer. F107 extension: intraday liquidity and the seven BCBS 248 supervisory tools.

Regulatory short-term metric

  • built02. Liquidity coverage ratio — HQLA over 30-day net stressed outflows, with the seven stress drivers, the full counterparty outflow schedule, the four deposit liquidity value drivers, and South Africa's no-deposit-insurance complication. F107 extension: liquidity stress testing scenarios beyond the LCR.

Bank-internal structural metrics

  • built03. Long-term metrics — liquidity risk factor (maturity transformation snapshot), concentration report, inter-entity lending report. F107 extension: the contingency funding plan.

Regulatory structural metric

  • built04. Net stable funding ratio — available stable funding factors, required stable funding factors, the worked example, improvement levers, and the South African 35% ZAR dispensation. Includes the LCR-NSFR interaction analysis.

The discipline that underwrites all of the above

  • built05. Behavioural modelling — contractual versus behavioural tenor, the two ALM gaps, the Matz criteria for deposit stickiness, prepayment modelling, the one-way CSA funding requirement, and the behaviouralisation exercise as an actuarial experience investigation.

Why this is the next track after credit risk

Credit risk is the largest exposure on most balance sheets, but liquidity risk is the fastest. A solvent bank with a liquidity squeeze is dead in a week, sometimes in a weekend, as Northern Rock in 2007, Lehman in 2008, and Silicon Valley Bank in 2023 each demonstrated in turn. The LCR exists because of Northern Rock and Lehman; the question for the 2020s is whether 30 days of HQLA is still the right buffer when a deposit run can complete in 24 hours.

For F107 the liquidity area is broadly well covered in the wiki, with the exception of intraday liquidity, the formal stress testing scenarios that sit alongside the LCR scenario, and the contingency funding plan document itself. Those three gaps are filled inside the lectures below and flagged for later backfill in the wiki.
