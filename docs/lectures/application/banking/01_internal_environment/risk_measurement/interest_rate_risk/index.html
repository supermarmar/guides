Risk measurement, phase 3

Interest rate risk in the banking book

The structural risk every deposit-taking institution carries and the only material risk type the Basel Committee deliberately declined to put in Pillar 1. IRRBB is measured from two perspectives that can point in opposite directions, earnings and economic value, and it rests more heavily on behavioural assumptions than any other quantified risk. Five lectures, built on five substantial wiki notes and extended with the published BCBS standard.

How this track is organised

The order follows the wiki and it is the right order. Lecture 1 establishes where the risk comes from, because IRRBB is unusual in having four distinct sources of mismatch and four distinct forms of risk, and confusing them is the commonest error. Lecture 2 covers the yield curves and benchmark rates that everything else is measured against, which matters more since the transition away from interbank offered rates. Lectures 3 and 4 give the two measurement perspectives, earnings first because it is what management actually watches and economic value second because it is what the supervisory test uses. Lecture 5 assembles the supervisory framework.

All five lectures are built. Where a lecture goes beyond the wiki it is flagged with a Beyond the wiki callout naming the published source, and where it corrects the wiki it says so explicitly. Two corrections are recorded: lecture 1 untangles the outlier test, which the wiki states by combining the 2016 threshold with the 2004 shock, and lecture 4 records an arithmetic error in the wiki's PV01 table. The wiki's repricing gap and NII sensitivity examples were verified cell by cell and are correct.

Lectures

Where the risk comes from

  • built01. Sources and forms of IRRBB — the two effects, the four sources of structural mismatch, and the four forms of risk: gap, option (automatic and behavioural), basis and yield curve. Asset-sensitive against liability-sensitive balance sheets, the South African prime-versus-JIBAR structure, and the naked hedge problem. F107 extension: why the Committee kept IRRBB in Pillar 2, and a correction to the wiki's outlier test.

What it is measured against

  • built02. Yield curves and benchmark rates — the five curve shapes and four shape hypotheses, the three construction methods and why only one suits economic value work, the LIBOR mechanism and its manipulation, and the argument that the transition to risk-free rates removed a natural hedge against a bank's own funding stress. F107 extension: the ZARONIA reform, which the wiki does not mention.

The two measurement perspectives

  • built03. Net interest income and net interest margin — the three-layer decomposition from NII to risk-adjusted NII with the wiki's worked example verified, what NIM can and cannot measure, net interest spread, the term liquidity premium, and NII through the cycle. F107 extension: the deposit repricing lag treated as a written option and unified with lecture 1's floor risk, plus the NII-versus-EVE divergence.
  • built04. Economic value of equity measurement — the measurement chain from gap analysis through PV01 and bucketed PV01 to EVE and earnings at risk, the six-versus-two scenario asymmetry, the equity treatment problem, and the earnings-against-value trade-off. Includes a reproducible PV01 and ΔEVE calculation taken through to the outlier ratio, and a correction to an arithmetic error in the wiki's PV01 table.

The supervisory framework

  • built05. BCBS 368 and the supervisory framework — the twelve principles, nine addressed to banks and three to supervisors, the shock calibration by currency, the caps the standardised framework places on behavioural assumptions, the disclosure regime, and the outlier test as a screening device rather than a capital charge.

Why IRRBB is worth an actuary's attention

Of all the risk types in this collection, IRRBB is the one whose methodology most closely resembles actuarial work. It is a present-value problem solved with duration and cash flow projection, its hardest inputs are behavioural assumptions about how customers exercise options, and those assumptions are established by experience investigation and monitored for drift. A modeller who has calibrated lapse rates on a life book will recognise deposit stickiness and mortgage prepayment immediately, including the awkward fact that the behaviour changes in exactly the conditions where the model matters.

It is also the risk type where the two standard measures disagree most usefully. A balance sheet can be positioned to gain from rising rates in earnings terms while losing economic value, because the earnings measure looks one or two years out and the value measure discounts everything. Holding both perspectives simultaneously, and knowing which one a given decision should be judged against, is the substance of asset and liability management.

Source material

The wiki source is docs/wiki/application/banking/01_internal_environment/risk_measurement/interest_rate_risk/, five notes running to roughly 450 lines: context and the Pillar 2A framing, sources of IRRBB, yield curves and benchmarks, NII and NIM, and IRRBB measurement. This is among the better-covered areas in the wiki, so the lectures mirror it closely and extend rather than replace.

The published source used throughout is the Basel Committee's Interest rate risk in the banking book standard (BCBS 368, April 2016), which replaced the 2004 Principles for the management and supervision of interest rate risk. Lecture 5 treats it directly and the earlier lectures cite it where they go beyond the wiki.

Sister tracks: market risk for the trading book side of the boundary and for the hedging instruments, liquidity risk for the behavioural modelling discipline that IRRBB shares, and economic capital for the Pillar 2A context.
