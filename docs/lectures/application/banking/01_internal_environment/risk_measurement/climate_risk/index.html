Risk measurement, phase 3

Climate risk

A risk a bank experiences almost entirely through its customers rather than directly, on a horizon longer than any measurement window the bank possesses, with no agreed method for adjusting the parameters it would have to change. Two lectures: what climate risk is and how it transmits into the existing risk types, then the modelling approach and scenario apparatus. The wiki is unusually well developed here at 214 lines.

How this track is organised

Two lectures, following the wiki's own division. Lecture 1 establishes the physical facts, the disclosure frameworks and the transmission mechanism, and its organising insight is that banks are not directly exposed: the exposure runs through clients and counterparties, which makes climate risk a transmission and data problem before it is a modelling one. Lecture 2 turns to the modelling approach, the scenario apparatus that the published pathways supply, and what can and cannot currently be estimated.

Why this sits slightly apart from the other risk types

Every other track in this folder measures a risk the bank runs. Climate risk is different in three structural ways, and the lectures are organised around them. It is transmitted, arriving through customers rather than through the bank's own positions, so measuring it requires data about counterparties that banks have not historically collected. Its horizon exceeds every measurement window the bank has, being decades where Pillar 1 is one year and a stress test is three to five. And there is no agreed method, which the wiki states plainly: no benchmark or best practice exists for adjusting probabilities of default or loss given default for climate.

That combination makes it the one risk in this collection where an honest treatment has to spend as much time on what cannot be done as on what can. The lectures do so, which is why they read differently from the credit or market risk material.
