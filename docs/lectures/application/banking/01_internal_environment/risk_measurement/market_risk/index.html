Risk measurement, phase 3

Market risk

The risk of loss from movements in market prices, and the one area of Basel where the risk measure itself was replaced rather than recalibrated. Six lectures run from the trading book boundary through the failure of value at risk as a coherent measure, to the two capital methodologies the Fundamental Review of the Trading Book left in place. An F107 priority gap: the wiki source is three files and the lectures go substantially beyond it using the published Basel standards.

How this track is organised

The order is conceptual rather than regulatory. Lecture 1 establishes what is in scope, because the trading book boundary is where the framework's history and most of its abuse live. Lectures 2 and 3 develop the risk measures, and they are the theoretical core: the reason FRTB moved from value at risk to expected shortfall is a mathematical property that can be demonstrated in nine lines of arithmetic, and lecture 2 demonstrates it. Lectures 4 and 5 then give the two capital methodologies, standardised and internal models. Lecture 6 covers valuation adjustments, which sit at the boundary between market and counterparty credit risk and belong to neither cleanly.

All six lectures are built. Where a lecture goes beyond the wiki, and most of them do here, it is flagged with a Beyond the wiki callout naming the published source used. Lecture 6 additionally records two corrections to the wiki: the conflation of SA-CCR with KVA, and the multi-curve discussion's reliance on LIBOR, which has since ceased.

Lectures

Scope

  • built01. The trading book and the boundary — what market risk capital applies to, the intent-based test and why it failed, FRTB's presumptive lists, the reclassification rules, the desk structure, and internal risk transfers. F107 extension: the entire boundary framework, which the wiki mentions in one line.

The risk measures

  • built02. Value at risk and coherent risk measures — the four axioms from Artzner and colleagues, VaR's definition and its uses, back-testing and VaR breaks, and a worked counterexample in which VaR reports that diversifying two bonds increased risk. F107 extension: the coherence proof made concrete, and the elliptical-distribution condition.
  • built03. Expected shortfall and the move to tail measures — ES as the coherent repair, the computed 97.5 per cent calibration equivalence with 99 per cent VaR, the shortfall-to-quantile ratio across Student-t degrees of freedom, the five liquidity horizon buckets, stressed calibration with a reduced set of risk factors, non-modellable risk factors, and the elicitability problem that makes ES hard to back-test.

Capital: the two methodologies

  • built04. FRTB standardised approach — the three components, seven risk classes, the two-level quadratic aggregation, the three prescribed correlation scenarios, and worked bucket-level examples for both the sensitivities-based method and default risk capital using the standard's own risk weights and correlation function.
  • built05. FRTB internal models approach — the three eligibility gates, the back-testing thresholds with their computed false-failure rates, profit and loss attribution and its traffic-light zones, the capital assembly with its constrained and unconstrained expected shortfall blend, and why so few banks are adopting it.

The boundary with counterparty credit

  • built06. CVA, XVA and proprietary trading — CVA as a credit exposure marked to market, the full XVA family with the FVA controversy, the 2020 CVA capital revision, the DVA prudential filter, and the Volcker versus ring-fencing comparison as regulatory design. Includes two corrections to the wiki.

Why this is an F107 priority gap

Market risk is one of the areas the root CLAUDE.md flags as materially weaker than credit risk in this collection, and the wiki bears that out: three files covering the capital framework, the risk measures, and a short note on proprietary trading and XVA. What is missing is the boundary framework, the FRTB detail on both the sensitivities-based method and the internal models approach, and the whole treatment of non-modellable risk factors.

For an actuary coming from credit risk the conceptual distance is smaller than it looks. A trading desk's expected shortfall at a ten-day horizon and a downturn LGD are both tail statistics estimated from a sample that may not contain the event being estimated, and the arguments about window selection, representativeness and back-testing transfer directly. What does not transfer is the horizon: credit risk models are calibrated over years and market risk models over days, which changes almost everything about the data problem.

Source material

The wiki source is docs/wiki/application/banking/01_internal_environment/risk_measurement/market_risk/: 01-context.md on the capital framework, 02-models.md on the risk measures, and 03-proprietary_trading_xva.md. Each lecture cites the wiki file it draws on and names the published standard where it goes further.

The published sources used across the track are the Basel Committee's market risk standard, Minimum capital requirements for market risk (BCBS d457, January 2019, revising d352 of January 2016), the Basel Framework's MAR chapters that consolidate it, and Artzner, Delbaen, Eber and Heath's 1999 paper on coherent measures of risk for the theory in lectures 2 and 3.
