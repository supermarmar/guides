Banking, internal environment

Risk measurement

Quantification methodology for each material risk a bank carries. Credit risk first, because it is the largest exposure on most bank balance sheets and the area where the modelling discipline has been pushed hardest. The other risk types follow, each with its own arc from concepts to capital. The series sits underneath the broader risk management framework already covered: this is the "how do we measure it" layer.

How this track is organised

Each risk type lives in its own folder with a flat numbered sequence of lectures and a landing page. The progression inside each is the same: foundations, then framework, then specialised topics. Lectures are not a one-to-one mapping of the wiki notes: where the wiki organises content by model development lifecycle, the lectures reorder into the path a learner actually takes.

Status badges show what is built (built), planned, or a gap where the source wiki is itself a stub.

Every risk type in phase 3 is now built: market risk, operational risk, liquidity risk, IRRBB, stress testing, climate risk and the other Pillar 2A risks. Credit risk, the deepest track at 55 lectures, is now complete: notation, A-IRB capital, IFRS 9 impairments, counterparty credit risk, concentration risk and application scoring. Three patterns recur across the completed folders and are drawn together in the closing lecture of other Pillar 2A risks: one-sided constraint on assumptions, systematic distrust of modelled correlation, and a boundary with accounting recognition that neither framework will draw for the other.

Phase 2 — credit risk

Fifty-five lectures, complete

  • builtCredit risk landing — notation, the 32-lecture A-IRB chain, the 12-lecture IFRS 9 chain, counterparty credit risk, concentration risk and application scoring. All built.

The largest and deepest track. Foundations establish the language of credit losses; A-IRB and IFRS 9 are then taught as separate frameworks, each with its own PD, LGD, EAD split, because the calibration assumptions and horizons differ materially. Adjacent topics close out: application scoring, counterparty credit risk, concentration risk.

Phase 3 — other risk types

Market risk

F107 priority gap: the lectures go substantially beyond the three wiki files, using the published Basel market risk standard.

Operational risk

  • builtOperational risk landing — six lectures: taxonomy and sources, loss data, the BIA, TSA and AMA approaches, SMA capital, AML and KYC, and cyber resilience. All built.

Liquidity risk

  • builtLiquidity risk landing — five lectures: short-term metrics, LCR, long-term metrics, NSFR, behavioural modelling. F107 gap extensions on intraday liquidity (BCBS 248), liquidity stress testing scenarios, and the contingency funding plan.

Interest rate risk in the banking book

  • builtIRRBB landing — five lectures: sources and forms, yield curves and benchmarks, NII and NIM, EVE measurement, and the BCBS 368 framework. All built. The wiki is well developed here (five notes, roughly 450 lines), so the lectures mirror it and extend rather than replace.

Climate risk

  • builtClimate risk landing — two lectures: context, disclosure and transmission, then the modelling approach. All built. The wiki is well developed here at 214 lines, so the lectures mirror it and update where the institutional landscape has moved.

Stress testing

  • builtStress testing landing — three lectures: Pillar 2B and the capital planning buffer, the named supervisory programmes, and reverse stress testing. All built. The largest F107 gap in the collection: the wiki holds a single 37-line note, so lectures 2 and 3 rest entirely on published supervisory material.

Other risks

  • builtOther Pillar 2A risks landing — two lectures: pension obligation risk, then model, business model, reputational and legal risk. All built. Pension risk is the most directly actuarial topic in the banking track, and lecture 2 closes the whole risk measurement series.
