Risk management

The process by which a bank decides how much risk it wants, finds the risk it already has, puts a number on it, and does something about it. Eight lectures run the full loop: the governance architecture and the three lines of defence, the four process steps (appetite, identification, measurement, mitigation), and the capital adequacy dialogue that sits on top (economic capital, ICAAP, SREP).

How this track is organised

Risk management is a loop, not a list, so the lectures are sequenced the way the loop actually runs. Lecture 1 sets the governance frame: who owns risk, who controls it, who audits it, and why those have to be three different sets of people. Lectures 2 to 5 walk the four process steps in order, because each one depends on the previous: you cannot identify risk against an appetite that has not been written, you cannot measure a risk you have not named, and you cannot choose a mitigation for a risk you have not sized. Lectures 6 to 8 then move up a level to the capital question. Economic capital is the bank's own answer to how much capital the measured risk profile demands, the ICAAP is the document in which the bank argues that answer, and the SREP is the supervisor's binding verdict on it.

This track is distinct from the risk measurement track. Risk management is the process and the governance; risk measurement is the quantification methodology for each individual risk type. Lecture 4 here is the bridge: it sets out the general measurement problem, and the risk measurement track then solves it eight times over, once per risk type.
