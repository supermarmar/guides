Applied track, ASSA F102 companion

Life insurance

The second applied track, and the one that puts the actuarial fundamentals to work. Banking taught risk measurement through the Basel lens, where risk type is the organising axis. Life insurance runs along a different spine: a policy is sold, priced, underwritten, reserved for, monitored, and eventually paid or surrendered, and every actuarial judgement attaches to a point on that lifecycle. Sixty-four lectures across six modules, covering all thirty-six chapters of the ASSA F102 syllabus.

How this track is organised

The top two levels mirror the banking track. Internal environment is the insurer itself: what it sells, how it prices, how it manages risk, how it values what it owes, and how it invests. External environment is everything the insurer operates inside: distribution, the economy, the legal and regulatory regime, tax, and professional guidance.

Below that the structure diverges, deliberately. Banking splits risk measurement by risk type because Basel does. F102 does not think that way, so risk_measurement/ here is organised by what the actuary actually produces: an asset share, a bonus declaration, a reserve, a solvency capital requirement, an embedded value, a surrender value, an analysis of surplus. That keeps each syllabus chapter contiguous, so the revision order matches the reading order.

The six modules

Internal environment overview, five lectures

  • 10 of 64 builtInternal environment landing — the business model, the financial statements, capital and new business strain, stakeholders, and the policy lifecycle that maps the rest of the track

Read this first. It answers the question the rest of the track assumes: what is a life insurer, what does its balance sheet look like, and why does writing profitable business consume capital before it releases any.

Products, eight lectures

  • 3 of 8 builtProducts landing — the product framework, the four product bases, assurances, annuities, income protection, critical illness, long-term care, group business

Descriptive, and the shortest route into the exam's language. Every later module refers back to a product by name and expects you to know its benefit shape, its customer need, and which of the insurer's risks it concentrates.

Pricing, nine lectures

  • plannedPricing landing — product design, models and model applications, setting assumptions, unit pricing, actuarial funding, the cost of guarantees and options, health and care pricing

The technical core. The mathematics of profit testing already sits in the fundamentals track; what is new here is the judgement layer on top: which assumption, set how, for what purpose, with how much margin.

Risk management, seven lectures

  • plannedRisk management landing — risk sources, new business and other risks, aggregation and credit failure, underwriting, reinsurance types, reinsurance use, further risk management

Identification and mitigation rather than quantification. Underwriting and reinsurance are the two levers a life insurer pulls hardest, and both carry more exam weight than their page count suggests.

Risk measurement, twenty-four lectures

  • plannedRisk measurement landing — asset shares, with-profits surplus distribution, reserving, capital and embedded value, policyholder values, experience analysis

The largest module and the one that rewards the most time. The sequence is weighted by where the marks are lost, not by chapter count: with-profits distribution takes six lectures and policyholder values five, eleven between them for four chapters, because asset shares, smoothing, bonus declaration and the alteration methods have no analogue in banking or in general insurance and there is no transferable intuition to fall back on. Reserving and capital take eight for two chapters and will feel more familiar to anyone who has built regulatory capital models, though the horizon, the margin philosophy and the treatment of future premiums all differ.

Investment, four lectures

  • plannedInvestment landing — investment principles, asset classes, asset-liability matching, investment strategy and ALM models

Short, and easy to under-prepare. The exam asks it as an application question: given these liabilities, justify this asset mix, and say what breaks if you get it wrong.

External environment, seven lectures

  • plannedExternal environment landing — the general business environment, distribution channels, the economy, regulation, accounting, tax, professional guidance

Two syllabus chapters stretched over seven lectures, because the regulatory and accounting layers need more than the summary gives them. South African solvency regulation and IFRS 17 are both material to the exam and neither is covered in the source notes.

What is deliberately not here

The mathematics lives in actuarial science and is not re-derived. This track links to it rather than repeating it, which is why sixty-four lectures can cover thirty-six chapters without becoming a textbook, and why the pages that remain can be spent on judgement rather than derivation.

Source material

The primary source is a set of four ASSA F102 summaries, all sharing the same thirty-six chapter spine. Each lecture cites its chapter in the header, in the form Source: ASSA F102 Summary, Ch 23. Unlike the banking track there is no wiki mirror under docs/wiki/, so the chapter reference is the citation.

Where a lecture goes beyond the summaries it is badged as a gap and names its external source. Those sources are the Insurance Act 18 of 2017 and the Prudential Authority's prudential standards for South African solvency regulation, IFRS 17 for the accounting environment, the Solvency II Directive and EIOPA technical specifications as the comparator for the value-at-risk capital framing, the CFO Forum market consistent embedded value principles, and the standard academic texts: Dickson, Hardy and Waters on life contingent risks, Booth and Chadburn on modern actuarial practice, Hardy on investment guarantees, and Macdonald, Richards and Currie on mortality modelling.

Regulatory detail is not duplicated here. The exam framing lives in the external environment module; the standard-by-standard treatment belongs in the regulation track.
