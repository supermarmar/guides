Life insurance, internal environment

Pricing

Nine lectures covering F102 chapters 16 to 22, 27 and 28. The arithmetic of projecting a policy's cashflows and discounting them to a profit measure already sits in profit testing. What this module adds is everything the arithmetic cannot tell you: which model to build, which assumption to set, how much margin to hold in it, for what purpose, and what to charge for a guarantee the policyholder does not know they have.

How this module is organised

Four parts. Design comes first because it constrains everything downstream: a product whose guarantees cannot be hedged should not reach the pricing model at all. Models and their applications come second, assumptions third, and the specialist topics last.

The syllabus order is reversed in one place. It introduces unit pricing at chapter 16 and actuarial funding at chapter 17, ahead of the general modelling material. Both are mechanical, both apply only to unit-linked business, and both make more sense after the general model and assumption framework is in place, so they sit in part three here.

Status badges show what is built (built) or planned. Planned lectures show their intended filename.

Part 1: design

  • planned01. Product design 01-product_design.html — the design checklist in full: financing requirement, onerousness of guarantees, risk characteristics, competitiveness, cross-subsidies, consistency with the existing range, marketability, profitability and profit sensitivity; and the trade-offs between them (Ch 20)

The exam asks this as a discursive question, almost always framed as "an insurer is considering launching X, discuss the considerations". The marks are in the breadth of the checklist and in tailoring each item to the specific product, not in the depth of any one item.

Part 2: models

  • planned02. Models 02-models.html — what a good actuarial model requires, deterministic against stochastic, when the extra cost of a stochastic model is justified, and how a stochastic model is calibrated (Ch 18)
  • planned03. Model applications 03-model_applications.html — model points and how to choose them, pricing and profitability assessment, solvency projection, and sensitivity testing as the main defence against assumption error (Ch 19)

The profit vector, profit signature, net present value and internal rate of return machinery is in profit testing, and reserves inside a profit test are in reserves under profit testing. These two lectures use that machinery rather than deriving it, and concentrate on the judgement: how many model points, which profit criterion, what to shock and by how much.

Part 3: assumptions

  • planned04. Setting assumptions 04-setting_assumptions.html — the general methodology and the requirement of internal consistency, then each assumption in turn: mortality, morbidity, expenses, inflation, investment return, withdrawals, and the risk discount rate; plus risk margins and the choice of profit criterion (Ch 21)
  • planned05. Assumption purposes 05-assumption_purposes.html — why the same contract needs several different bases: market-consistent against best-estimate against prudent, pricing against reserving, and what changes between them (Ch 22)

Consistency is the trap. An assumption set that is individually defensible can be jointly incoherent, most obviously when inflation and investment return are set independently, or when a mortality improvement assumption is used for an annuity and its mirror image is not used for the assurance book. Chapter 21 lists the consistency requirements explicitly and the exam tests them.

Unit-linked mechanics

  • planned06. Unit pricing 06-unit_pricing.html — the creation and cancellation price basis, appropriation and expropriation, the direction of the offer and bid adjustment, and the risks unit pricing itself introduces (Ch 16)
  • planned07. Actuarial funding 07-actuarial_funding.html — what it is, when it is permitted, how the reduced unit holding is calculated, what it does to the cashflow pattern, and the capital relief it buys against the risks it creates (Ch 17)

The fund mechanics, allocation rates, bid-offer spread and annual management charge are covered in contingencies. These lectures pick up where that stops: the pricing basis for creating and cancelling units, and the capital engineering that actuarial funding performs on a capital-units contract.

Part 4: specialist pricing

  • planned08. Guarantees and options 08-guarantees_and_options.html — investment guarantees and what makes them expensive, the option pricing and hedging approach, stochastic simulation where hedging is impractical, and mortality options priced on the north American method (Ch 27)
  • planned09. Health and care pricing 09-health_and_care_pricing.html — the income protection multiple-state model, the inception and disabled-life-annuity decomposition, and the critical illness inception model (Ch 28)

Lecture 08 is where financial engineering pays off. A guaranteed annuity rate is a receiver swaption written to a policyholder who paid nothing for it, and a maturity guarantee on a unit-linked contract is a put on the fund. The lecture makes those identifications explicitly, then covers why an insurer might still choose stochastic simulation with a reserve over a hedge, and what Hardy's Investment Guarantees adds on the calibration of the underlying real-world model.

Lecture 09 rests on multiple-state models. The transition intensities and Thiele's equation are already established there; what is new is the choice of state space for a real income protection contract, and the data problems that make the inception plus annuity decomposition more practical than a full multiple-state fit.
