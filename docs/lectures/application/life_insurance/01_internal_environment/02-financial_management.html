A reconciling analysis of surplus is not a correct one, it is a consistent one

The summary instructs the actuary to derive the analysis components and total them so that they match the actual surplus. They always will, under any ordering. But the components themselves are not unique: on the illustrative year the expense component is -525.0000 if expenses are analysed first and -550.0000 if interest is, a difference of 25.0000, which is exactly the expense deviation of 500.0000 times the interest deviation of 0.0500. The interaction term goes entirely to whichever assumption is analysed second, so the choice of order is a choice about who gets blamed.

Step 1

The 30-second version

Reading a life insurer's accounts means answering two questions the statements do not answer themselves: where did the surplus come from, and does its existence mean anything. The summary supplies the machinery for both, an analysis of surplus by assumption and a total that everything must reconcile to. Four things about that machinery are computable, and three of them qualify claims the summary makes flatly.

Start with the reconciliation. The summary gives total surplus as (A1 - A0) - (V1 - V0) and instructs the actuary to total the components so they match it. Every ordering does match, at 200.0000 on the illustrative year. But the components differ: the expense component is -525.0000 or -550.0000 depending on whether expenses or interest is analysed first, and the 25.0000 difference is exactly the product of the two deviations. Mortality escapes it, because it enters the fund as a subtraction of claims rather than as a rate applied to the fund, so an additively acting assumption is uniquely attributable and a multiplicatively acting one is not.

Then the sign. The summary says positive surplus should arise under a supervisory basis and that a realistic basis should show equal negative and positive surplus. Run a book whose experience is exactly on the best estimate and the realistic basis gives exactly zero every year while the supervisory basis gives a positive figure every year, totalling 150.0000, which is exactly the margin held at the start. A supervisory surplus is the unwinding of a deliberate understatement, so its sign identifies the basis rather than the performance.

Then new business, which the summary says reduces surplus on a prudent basis and increases it on a realistic one. Both are the strain identity with different reserves in it: the effect falls from 200.0000 to -400.0000 as the basis strengthens and crosses zero at a reserve of 400.0000, exactly the premium less the initial expense. The summary's further point that regular premium business is the worse case follows immediately, since only the first year's premium arrives against the whole initial expense.

Finally the measures. The summary says embedded value is a better remuneration measure than surplus arising because it does not fall with new business strain, which is true: the static manager beats the growing one on surplus and loses on embedded value. But the summary also says embedded value takes credit for future profits, and the growing manager's advantage is exactly the extra policies times the excess of assumed profit over strain, vanishing where the two are equal. Surplus penalises real growth and embedded value rewards assumed growth, so neither measure is neutral.

Step 2

Why this matters

The order-dependence result matters because an analysis of surplus is used to allocate responsibility. If the expense component is a management performance measure and the interest component is an investment performance measure, then the order in which the analysis is run moves money between two departments' scorecards without anybody choosing to. The reconciliation check the summary insists on gives no protection here, because every ordering reconciles. What is needed is a stated convention, applied consistently, and an understanding that the residual interaction has been assigned by that convention rather than measured.

The sign result matters because it stops a common misreading. A supervisory surplus emerging year after year is not evidence that the business is profitable, and its absence is not evidence that the business is failing. On a realistic basis the opposite holds: a persistent one-sided surplus is direct evidence that the basis is wrong, which is why the summary tells the actuary to change the assumptions when experience deviates too much. The same number carries diagnostic information on one basis and none on the other, so the first question about any surplus figure is which basis produced it.

The new business result matters because it means an insurer can choose whether growth looks good or bad in its own accounts, within the range of defensible reserving bases. That is not fraud, it is the consequence of the strain identity, and it is why supervisors specify the reserving basis rather than leaving it to the reporting entity. It also explains why a company under pressure to show surplus has an incentive to slow new business, which is the opposite of what a long-term owner would want.

The measures result matters because remuneration follows measurement. A management team paid on surplus arising will under-write new business; one paid on embedded value will over-write it and will lean on the assumptions that value it. The summary's recommendation of embedded value is right about the first failure and silent about the second, and the practical answer is neither measure alone but the reconciliation the summary itself prescribes elsewhere: identify every contribution to the change, and separate experience from assumption changes before anybody is paid.

The Feynman version. You had a good year and you want to know why. You expected to spend two hundred on running costs, earn five per cent, and pay a thousand in claims. What actually happened is you spent seven hundred, earned ten per cent, and paid only eight hundred. Net, you are two hundred better off. Now explain that to the board.

Here is the trap. You overspent by five hundred, but you were earning ten per cent on the money instead of five, so overspending cost you a bit more than five hundred, because the money you wasted would have earned the higher rate. Or: you earned an extra five per cent, but on a smaller pot because you had overspent, so the interest gain is a bit less than it looks. Both stories are true. Which one you tell depends on which effect you write down first, and the difference is exactly five hundred times five per cent, twenty-five pounds, handed to whichever department goes second in the report.

The total is always two hundred. That part is honest arithmetic. The split between the expenses team and the investment team is a filing convention, and everybody in the room should know that before the bonuses are set.

Step 3

The mental model

Hold four pictures. The first is a total that is robust sitting on components that are not. Any decomposition of a change into contributions from several variables has interaction terms, and a sequential attribution assigns each interaction to whichever variable comes later. So the sum is a fact and the parts are a convention. That is true of an analysis of surplus, of a variance attribution on an investment portfolio, and of any profit-and-loss walk between two periods.

The second is a margin unwinding. A prudent reserve is a deliberate understatement of what the insurer expects to keep, and it releases as the policy runs off. So a supervisory surplus is a schedule rather than a result, and it appears whether or not anything good has happened. The realistic basis strips that schedule out, which is what makes it diagnostic and what makes a one-sided realistic surplus a signal.

The third is one inequality read from two sides. New business adds to surplus when the reserve it establishes is less than the premium net of the initial expense, and subtracts when it is more. The summary's separate statements about prudent and realistic bases, and about regular and single premiums, are all positions on that one comparison, which is the strain identity from the product framework wearing different clothes.

The fourth is two measures with opposite blind spots. Surplus arising counts money received and charges the cost of growth; embedded value counts money expected and credits the value of growth. Each is a defensible view of the same business and each rewards a different distortion. The pair is only safe when the movement between periods is decomposed, which is the same technique as the first picture applied to a different quantity.

Step 4

The detail

4.1 The total reconciles, but the components are not unique

The summary defines the total surplus over a year as (A1 - A0) - (V1 - V0): the growth in assets less the growth in reserves. Under the analysis of surplus it lists four assumptions to test, mortality, expense, withdrawal and interest, and under a data valuation check it instructs the actuary to derive the components and total them so that they match the actual surplus. Take a year in which three assumptions all came out differently from expectation.

   the outcome        expenses   interest   mortality   fund at the   surplus against
                      paid       earned     rate        year end      expectation
   as expected             200.0000     0.0500      0.0100    10550.0000                -
   as it happened          700.0000     0.1000      0.0080    10750.0000         200.0000

A fund of 10000 with 1200 of premiums, 100 lives at a 1000 sum assured. Expenses overran by 500, interest beat the assumption by 0.0500, and mortality came in light. All invented for illustration.

The fund ended at 10750.0000 against an expected 10550.0000, so the surplus to explain is 200.0000. Now decompose it. Take the three assumptions one at a time, each measured on what is left of the basis after the previous steps, which is how an analysis of surplus is built.

   order of analysis            expense     interest    mortality   components   matches the
                                component   component   component   total        actual surplus?
   expenses, interest, mortality   -525.0000    525.0000    200.0000     200.0000   yes
   interest, expenses, mortality   -550.0000    550.0000    200.0000     200.0000   yes
   mortality, interest, expenses   -550.0000    550.0000    200.0000     200.0000   yes

The same year analysed in three different orders. Every ordering reconciles to the actual surplus; the components differ.

Every ordering totals to 200.0000, so the summary's reconciliation check passes in all of them. But the expense component is -525.0000 if expenses are taken first and -550.0000 if interest is, a difference of 25.0000. That is exactly the interaction term, the expense deviation of 500.0000 times the interest deviation of 0.0500, and it is attributed entirely to whichever of the two is analysed second. So a reconciling analysis of surplus is not a correct one, it is a consistent one, and the choice of order is a choice about who gets blamed.

And mortality escapes the problem for a structural reason worth knowing. It enters the fund as a subtraction of claims rather than as a rate applied to the fund, so it has no interaction with the interest rate and its component is identical in every ordering. An assumption that acts additively is uniquely attributable; one that acts multiplicatively is not. That gives a quick test for any decomposition: identify which variables multiply together in the underlying relationship, because those are the ones whose attribution depends on convention.

One boundary. The illustration has three assumptions and one significant interaction, so the discrepancy is a single clean product. With four assumptions, as the summary's list has, there are six pairwise interactions plus higher-order terms, and the attribution differences compound. The direction of the result is unaffected: the total always reconciles and the parts never uniquely do.

4.2 The sign of the surplus identifies the basis

The summary makes two claims side by side under the financial effect of assumptions: positive surplus should arise under a supervisory basis, and under an internal realistic basis one should expect to see equal negative and positive surplus. Run a book whose experience is exactly on the best estimate and the reason becomes visible.

   year   best estimate   supervisory   margin      surplus on the   surplus on the
          reserve         reserve       held        realistic basis  supervisory basis
   1             820.0000      943.0000    123.0000           0.0000           27.0000
   2             630.0000      724.5000     94.5000           0.0000           28.5000
   3             430.0000      494.5000     64.5000           0.0000           30.0000
   4             220.0000      253.0000     33.0000           0.0000           31.5000
   5               0.0000        0.0000      0.0000           0.0000           33.0000

A best estimate reserve running off from 1000 to zero, with a 0.1500 prudential margin in the supervisory basis. Experience is exactly on the best estimate throughout.

On a realistic basis the surplus is exactly zero every year, because the reserve moves by exactly what experience delivers. On a supervisory basis it is positive every year, and the total released over the term is 150.0000, exactly the margin held at the start. So a supervisory surplus is not a profit, it is the unwinding of a deliberate understatement, and its sign tells you which basis you are reading rather than how the business did. The summary's two claims are therefore one claim about prudence.

Which is exactly why the summary says to change the pricing or valuation assumptions if experience deviates too much. On a realistic basis a persistent one-sided surplus is evidence that the basis is wrong, because the expected surplus is zero. On a supervisory basis a positive surplus is evidence of nothing at all, because it was designed in. The diagnostic value of the number depends entirely on which basis produced it, which is a good reason for an insurer to run both and a bad reason to report only the one that flatters.

The same distinction sits underneath the summary's remarks on surplus distribution. It says the contribution method of distributing surplus is very similar to an analysis of surplus of assumptions under a supervisory basis, and that the revalorisation method cannot distribute a non-recurring component. Both remarks follow from what a supervisory surplus is: if the surplus arising is largely released margin, then a distribution method built on it is really distributing prudence, and a method that cannot isolate one-off items will distribute them as though they recurred.

4.3 New business changes the sign of its own effect

The summary says new business affects surplus negatively in the inception year under a prudent supervisory basis, because the premium is insufficient to cover all the initial expenses, and positively under a realistic basis, because lower reserves are held and there is less new business strain. It adds that regular premium business leads to a larger reduction. All three statements are the strain identity with different reserves in it.

   the reserving basis   reserve   initial   premium   effect on   does new business
                         held      expense   received  surplus     add to surplus?

   realistic, fuller       350.0000  600.0000 1000.0000     50.0000   yes
   the crossover           400.0000  600.0000 1000.0000      0.0000   exactly neutral
   supervisory             550.0000  600.0000 1000.0000   -150.0000   NO

A 1000 annual premium against 600 of initial expense, with the reserve varying by basis. Invented for illustration.

The effect falls from 200.0000 to -400.0000 as the basis strengthens, crossing zero at a reserve of 400.0000, which is exactly the premium less the initial expense. So the summary's two statements about new business are not two facts, they are one inequality read from either side, and the only question is whether the reserve exceeds the first year's spare premium.

And the regular premium point follows immediately, because only the first year's premium arrives against the whole of the initial expense.

   premium pattern      premium in   initial   supervisory   effect on   the summary's
                        year one     expense   reserve       surplus     ranking

A regular premium contract reduces surplus by 150.0000 and a single premium one adds 13850.0000, on the same reserve and the same expense. That is the summary's larger reduction for regular premium business, and it is the same premium frequency effect that dominated the five capital factors in the product framework, where frequency alone changed the sign of the strain.

4.4 Surplus and embedded value are gameable in opposite directions

Under the analysis of embedded value the summary says the measure provides data for executive remuneration, because embedded value is a better measure than surplus arising since it does not decrease by new business strain. Two lines later, under information for accounts, it says embedded value is good for rapidly expanding companies and takes credit for future profits. Put both remarks together.

   the manager        policies   surplus arising   embedded value   which manager wins
                      written    for the year      added            on each measure?
   runs a static book      4.0000         3800.0000        2000.0000   -
   grows the book hard    20.0000        -1000.0000       10000.0000   -

A strain of 300 per policy written against 800 of present value of future profits per policy, on a base surplus of 5000. Invented for illustration.

On surplus arising the static manager shows 3800.0000 against -1000.0000, and on embedded value added the growing manager shows 10000.0000 against 2000.0000. The business is identical and the ranking reverses, which is the summary's point and is correct as far as it goes. But the second remark matters, because embedded value depends on an assumed margin nobody has earned yet.

   assumed future profit   EV added by the   EV added by the   which manager   is the ranking
   per policy              static manager    growing manager   wins?           safe?
   800.0000                        2000.0000        10000.0000   growing         yes
   500.0000                         800.0000         4000.0000   growing         yes
   350.0000                         200.0000         1000.0000   growing         yes
   300.0000                           0.0000            0.0000   level           NO
   200.0000                        -400.0000        -2000.0000   static          yes

The same two managers valued at five different assumptions about future profit per policy, against an unchanged strain of 300.

The growing manager's advantage falls from 8000.0000 to -1600.0000 as the assumed future profit falls from 800.0000 to 200.0000, and it is exactly the difference in policies written times the excess of assumed profit over strain. So it vanishes precisely where the two are equal, at 300.0000, and reverses below it. Which means the ranking of the two managers is decided by an assumption about profits not yet earned rather than by anything either of them did.

An observation is recorded against the summary here. Its claim that embedded value is a better remuneration measure is half right. Surplus arising penalises real growth, which is the failure the summary identifies. Embedded value rewards assumed growth, which is the failure it describes two lines later without connecting the two. The examinable position is that neither measure is neutral, and that they distort in opposite directions: one discourages writing business and the other encourages valuing it optimistically.

And the summary supplies the resolution itself without connecting it. It instructs the actuary to reconcile successive embedded values by identifying every contribution to the change, and separately notes that worse experience raises the present value of future profits while lowering free assets. An analysis that separates those two movements is exactly what stops an assumption change being reported as performance. So the safeguard for the measure is the same technique as section 4.1's analysis of surplus, applied to a different quantity, and neither works without a stated convention for the interactions.

4.5 What the expense analysis is actually apportioning

The summary's expense analysis opens with one line that decides the whole exercise: commission is excluded since it will be known with certainty. Everything that follows is about apportioning costs that are not attributable to any one policy, which is why it spends so long on notional rent, timesheets, floor space and computer usage. It then offers three drivers for the resulting unit cost.

   the driver the expense is   basis of        expense per unit   what the summary says
   proportional to            apportionment   of that driver     about it
   the number of contracts          500.0000       200.00000000   MOST COMMON
   the amount of premium         600000.0000         0.16666667   an alternative
   the amount of benefit       20000000.0000         0.00500000   underwriting expense uses this

A total expense of 100000 spread over 500 policies, 600000 of premium or 20000000 of benefit. Invented for illustration.

The same 100000 of expense is 200.00000000 per policy, 0.16666667 per unit of premium or 0.00500000 per unit of benefit. Those are not three estimates of one number, they are three different pricing structures, and choosing between them decides which policies subsidise which.

   a policy with               expense charged   expense charged   expense charged   moving to a premium
                               per policy        per premium       per benefit       basis would
   a small premium and benefit             200.0000           66.6667          100.0000   help this policy
   an average premium and benefit          200.0000          200.0000          200.0000   leave it unchanged
   a large premium and benefit             200.0000          666.6667          750.0000   hurt this policy

Three policies charged on each of the three bases. The per-policy charge is identical for all three by construction, so the last column asks who gains if the basis changes.

A per-policy charge is 200.0000 whatever the policy, so a small policy pays 0.5000 of its premium in expenses and a large one 0.0500. A premium-based charge reverses that entirely. The summary calls the per-contract basis the most common and not the most correct, which is precisely the right word: apportionment is a decision about cross-subsidy dressed as a measurement, and there is no basis that avoids making it.

The summary's other expense notes are all attempts to reduce the arbitrariness rather than remove it. Notional rent should be charged where the insurer owns its premises, because the alternative is to treat owned space as free and understate the cost of whichever department occupies it. Salary costs split three ways according to whether staff contribute to one expense, to several by timesheet, or to overheads. And investment expenses may be allowed for as a reduction in yield or as a percentage of reserves, which are again two different structures rather than two estimates.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The habit worth installing first is to ask, of any decomposition, which variables multiply. Wherever two drivers multiply in the underlying relationship, their interaction has to be assigned by convention, and a sequential attribution hands it to whichever goes second. That applies to an analysis of surplus, to performance attribution on an investment portfolio, to a variance analysis in a bank's net interest income, and to any profit walk between two periods. The reconciliation is not the check people think it is, because it passes under every convention.

The second habit is to establish the basis before interpreting the number. A supervisory surplus is a schedule of released prudence; a realistic surplus is a measurement of experience. The same figure is uninformative on one basis and diagnostic on the other, and confusing them produces both false comfort and false alarm. The general form of the question is: what would this number be if nothing had happened? Where the answer is not zero, the number is not a measurement.

The third habit is to treat any accounting choice that changes the sign of an activity as a governance question rather than a technical one. An insurer that can make its own growth appear as a cost or a benefit by moving between defensible reserving bases has discretion over the story its accounts tell, and the response is not better judgement but an externally specified basis. That is why supervisors prescribe reserving rather than reviewing it, and the same logic sits behind prescribed risk weights in banking.

The fourth habit is to ask what a performance measure penalises before adopting it. Surplus arising penalises writing business; embedded value penalises pessimism about it. Both are real distortions, neither is a scandal, and the fix is never a single better number. It is a decomposition that separates experience from assumption changes and from volume, reported alongside whichever headline measure is used. Any incentive scheme paid on an undecomposed measure will buy the distortion that measure contains.

The last habit is to notice when an apportionment is being presented as a measurement. Expense allocation, transfer pricing between a bank's divisions, and the assignment of overheads to product lines are all decisions about cross-subsidy, and the choice of driver decides who pays. There is no neutral answer to find, only a defensible one to state, and the useful discipline is to compute the alternative and see who gains. If nobody can say who is subsidised under the chosen basis, the basis has not been understood.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The income statement and balance sheet this lecture reads are set out in the business model, which also established that increases in reserves and solvency capital are income statement costs and that free assets are a geared residual. The strain identity that section 4.3 varies, with its five capital factors and the dominance of premium frequency, is in the product framework. The sum at risk in the summary's mortality surplus formula is defined and computed in assurances. The bonus and surplus distribution methods section 4.2 refers to are in product bases and surplus distribution. And the reserving bases that section 4.2 contrasts are the prudent and realistic constructions developed properly in reserves and profit testing, where the emergence of surplus from a prudent basis is derived rather than illustrated.

Where this goes next. Overview lecture 3 is the capital hinge, taking new business strain from the per-policy identity to the portfolio question: how much capital a given growth plan needs, over what period it returns, and how that interacts with the solvency requirement. Lecture 4 develops the stakeholder conflicts, where section 4.4's remuneration problem becomes a governance question about who sets the measure. Lecture 5 maps the policy lifecycle onto the sub-modules. Beyond the overview, the reserving bases section 4.2 contrasts are constructed in the risk measurement module, which also owns embedded value properly; the expense assumptions section 4.5 apportions are set in the pricing module; and the monitoring cycle that produces an analysis of surplus each year belongs with experience analysis in risk measurement.

Where the bank sits opposite. Section 4.1's order-dependence is the same problem a bank meets in decomposing net interest income into volume, rate and mix effects: the interactions have to be assigned by convention, and a walk that reconciles is not thereby correct. Section 4.2's released-margin surplus is the banking analogue of provision releases: a bank that provisioned prudently reports profit as loans perform, and the profit measures the earlier prudence rather than the current year, which is why the IFRS 9 staging and coverage disclosures in impairment modelling separate the two. Section 4.3's sign flip is the provisioning parallel exactly: a growing loan book charges impairment on origination, so growth depresses reported profit under an expected loss standard and would not under an incurred loss one. And section 4.5's apportionment problem is funds transfer pricing, where the choice of basis decides which business unit looks profitable, developed in net interest income and margin.
