A bigger group scheme is barely safer, and proportional reinsurance makes it no safer at all

The summary lists concentration risk as a micro risk specifically for group contracts, and separately says group insurance uses catastrophe reinsurance where every other line uses original terms or risk premium. Those two facts are one result. Once a shared event is in the model the coefficient of variation stops falling with scheme size, converging on 1.5803 rather than on zero, so at ten thousand lives an independence assumption understates the risk by a factor of 7.1449. And a fifty per cent quota share leaves the coefficient of variation at 1.596058, exactly unchanged, because it scales the mean and the deviation together.

Step 1

The 30-second version

Group business is not a product, it is a way of selling the products in the previous seven lectures, and the summary scatters it accordingly: a group version under endowment, whole life, term, annuities, income protection and critical illness, an underwriting section in chapter 31, and a reinsurance line in chapter 30. Pulled together it turns out to be governed by four mechanisms this module has already established, appearing here for the second or third time, plus one that is specific to groups.

The group-specific one is concentration. Members of a scheme share a workplace, a location and often an occupation, so one event can hit many of them. Put a shared event into the model and the coefficient of variation stops falling with scheme size, converging on 1.5803 instead of zero, and the ratio of true to naive risk rises from 1.0247 at ten lives to 7.1449 at ten thousand. The largest scheme is the one an independence assumption understates most, which is the opposite of the usual intuition about pooling.

That also explains the reinsurance choice. A fifty per cent quota share halves the expected claims and halves the standard deviation, so the coefficient of variation is 1.596058 before and 1.596058 after, unchanged to six decimal places. Catastrophe cover takes it to 0.223383, a reduction of 0.8600, because it removes the only component that does not diversify. So the summary's assignment of catastrophe reinsurance to group business and proportional forms to everything else is derivable rather than conventional.

Then the free cover limit, which the summary presents as an anti-selection control and which is really a break-even. Underwriting costs a fixed amount per member and is worth an amount proportional to the sum assured, so it pays only above 1000000.0000 on the illustrative figures. The actively-at-work test costs 0.0125 of full underwriting and catches 0.5000 of the excess mortality, so it pays above 25000.0000, a factor of 40.0000 lower. The gap between the two break-evens is the free cover band.

And then anti-selection, for the third time in this module. Voluntary take-up shifts the joining pool towards impaired lives, taking mortality from 0.003200 at full take-up to 0.010000 with no standard lives joining. The loading is convex: the first tranche of non-joiners adds 0.0768 and the last adds 1.3281, a factor of 17.2917. Which is why the summary's four ways of paying for a scheme are a selection ladder rather than an administrative list.

Step 2

Why this matters

The concentration result matters because it contradicts the reason most people give for writing group business. A scheme of ten thousand lives looks like the ideal insurance risk, large and homogeneous, and the law of large numbers is invoked to explain why it should be cheap to cover. That reasoning holds for the part of the risk that is independent across members and fails entirely for the part that is not, and the failure grows with size. Anybody pricing a large scheme on a pooled basis without an explicit catastrophe component has understated the capital requirement by a factor that rises with the very quantity they think is protecting them.

The reinsurance result matters because it makes the choice of structure a diagnostic rather than a preference. Proportional reinsurance transfers a share of everything and changes no ratio, so it is a capital and cash-flow tool rather than a risk tool. Non-proportional reinsurance attacks a specific part of the distribution. So the question is never how much reinsurance to buy, it is which component of the variance dominates, and the answer determines the form. That is why the summary can list a reinsurance type against each class of business and be giving information rather than convention.

The free cover result matters because it makes a negotiated number computable. Free cover limits are usually presented to an employer as an underwriting concession and treated as a bargaining position, and they are neither: the limit is the underwriting cost divided by the expected extra mortality it would uncover. An insurer that cannot state its own limit as that ratio does not know whether its underwriting is paying for itself, and the same test applies to every screening decision an insurer or a bank makes about small exposures.

The take-up result matters because of its convexity. A scheme at ninety per cent participation and one at fifty per cent are not two points on a straight line; the loading required accelerates as participation falls, so the last few percentage points of participation are worth many times the first. Which is why participation requirements are set as floors with hard consequences rather than as targets to aim at, and why a voluntary scheme that drifts below its floor deteriorates faster than a linear projection suggests.

The Feynman version. You insure ten thousand people at one company. Every actuarial instinct says this is wonderful: with that many lives, the good years and bad years average out and you barely need any capital. And that is true for ordinary deaths, which happen one at a time for unrelated reasons.

But these ten thousand people work in the same buildings. Something can happen to a lot of them at once. And here is the thing about that part of the risk: making the scheme bigger does not average it out, because the whole scheme is exposed to the same event. Ten thousand lives gives you ten thousand independent coin flips and one big coin flip, and the big one does not care how many small ones you have.

So you go to a reinsurer and take half of everything off your books. Your expected claims halve. Your uncertainty halves. Your risk relative to your premium is exactly what it was, to six decimal places, because you sold half of a thing without changing its shape. What you needed was not half of everything, it was cover for the one big coin flip. Which is why group business, alone among life insurance lines, is reinsured against catastrophes rather than in proportion.

Step 3

The mental model

Hold four pictures. The first is many small coin flips plus one big one. Aggregate claims on a group scheme have an independent component that averages out with size and a shared component that does not, and the second eventually dominates any measure expressed relative to the mean. Every conclusion about group risk follows from which of the two you are looking at, and the naive calculation looks only at the first.

The second is scaling versus reshaping. Proportional reinsurance multiplies the whole distribution by a constant, so every ratio survives it untouched. Non-proportional reinsurance cuts a piece out of a particular region. Only the second changes the shape, and only the second can help with a risk that is defined by its shape rather than its size.

The third is a fixed toll against a proportional benefit, which is now the third form this module has met: lecture 5's administration cost per claim, lecture 6's definitional cost per condition, and here underwriting cost per member. In every case the fixed side does not shrink and the proportional side does, so there is a threshold below which the expensive option is not worth exercising, and the threshold is a ratio of the two.

The fourth is a weighted average with a moving weight, which is the third form of anti-selection this module has met: lecture 3's renewal option, lecture 5's selective lapsing, and here voluntary take-up. The pool's rate is a weighted average of the healthy and the impaired, and every voluntary boundary lets the healthy remove their weight from it. The rate then moves towards the impaired rate, and the movement is convex because the healthy weight is shrinking in both the numerator and the denominator.

Those four pictures make group business a revision lecture with one new idea in it. The new idea is concentration, and it is the only one that would not have appeared somewhere in the individual product lectures. Everything else is a mechanism already established, applied to a scheme rather than to a policy, which is why the summary can scatter group material across seven chapters without losing much.

Step 4

The detail

4.1 Scale stops working, and proportional reinsurance never started

The summary lists concentration risk as a micro risk specifically for group contracts, and in a different chapter says that group insurance uses catastrophe reinsurance where term assurance uses original terms or risk premium, with-profits uses original terms for its guaranteed elements, unit-linked uses risk premium, and immediate annuities are not normally reinsured at all. Group is the only line assigned a non-proportional structure. That is not a coincidence.

Model a scheme of identical lives with an independent mortality component and a shared event that removes a fraction of the whole membership at once, and look at the coefficient of variation, which is the standard deviation of aggregate claims divided by their mean.

   scheme         are independent      shared event       true/naive help?

   10000                        0.2234             1.5961    7.1449   barely

A mortality rate of 0.0020, with a probability of 0.0010 of an event removing 0.1000 of the membership. All invented for illustration.

The naive variation falls from 7.0640 at ten lives to 0.2234 at ten thousand, behaving exactly as the law of large numbers promises. The true variation falls only to 1.5961, converging on 1.5803, which is the shared event's contribution and nothing else. So the ratio of true to naive risk rises from 1.0247 to 7.1449, and the largest scheme is the one an independence assumption understates most. Growth does not merely fail to help beyond a point, it widens the error in the calculation that says it helps.

A scheme of ten thousand lives at a 500000 sum assured. Catastrophe cover is modelled as removing the shared-event component entirely.

Quota share leaves the coefficient of variation at 1.596058, exactly unchanged, because it scales the mean and the deviation by the same 0.5000. It is a capital and cash-flow tool, not a risk tool. Catastrophe cover takes the ratio to 0.223383, a reduction of 0.8600, because it removes the only component that does not diversify. So the summary's reinsurance list is telling you which component dominates in each class of business, and the group entry is the only one where the answer is a shared event.

One boundary. The shared-event parameters are invented, so the 1.5803 floor and the 7.1449 ratio are magnitudes rather than measurements. What is general is that the floor exists for any positive probability of a shared event, that it is independent of scheme size, and that the coefficient of variation is invariant under any proportional transfer, which is exact and follows from the definition rather than from the parameters.

4.2 The free cover limit is a break-even

The summary lists five ways to limit anti-selection in group underwriting: exclusion clauses, free cover limits, ensuring members are actively at work, setting take-up rates on voluntary schemes, and setting take-over terms. In a different chapter it says the marginal underwriting expense must be justified by the extra premium, and that the level of medical evidence required increases with the sum assured. Those three statements are one equation.

   sum assured    what underwriting   what underwriting   is underwriting   what the insurer
   per member     costs per member    is worth per member worth doing?      should do
   250000.0000               400.0000            100.0000   no                grant free cover

An underwriting cost of 400 per life, with 0.1000 of members found impaired at 0.0040 of additional annual mortality. Invented for illustration.

The break-even sum assured is 1000000.0000, and that is the free cover limit. It is not a convention or a negotiating position: it is the underwriting cost divided by the expected extra mortality underwriting would uncover. An insurer that states its limit any other way has not checked whether its own underwriting pays for itself.

Which raises the question the summary answers in its next bullet. What do you do below the limit? Ensure members are actively at work, which is a declaration on a form rather than a medical.

   the test              cost per   share of the excess   break-even sum   how far down the
                         member     mortality it finds    assured          book does it pay?
   full medical underwriting   400.0000                1.0000     1000000.0000   large cases only
   actively at work only       5.0000                0.5000       25000.0000   the whole scheme

The cheap test costs 0.0125 of full underwriting and finds 0.5000 of the excess mortality.

Full underwriting pays above 1000000.0000 and the actively-at-work test above 25000.0000, a factor of 40.0000. The cheap test finds only half the excess mortality but costs 0.0125 of the price, and because 0.0125 is much the smaller fraction it wins everywhere below the free cover limit. So the gap between the two break-evens is the free cover band, and the summary's two bullets are its two ends rather than two unrelated controls.

And this is the third appearance of one structure rather than a new one. Lecture 5's deferred period traded a fixed administration cost per claim against a benefit proportional to duration. Lecture 6's condition list traded a fixed definitional cost per condition against a benefit proportional to incidence. Here underwriting trades a fixed cost per member against a benefit proportional to the sum assured. All three produce a threshold, all three make the threshold a ratio of the two costs, and all three explain a feature that looks like a compromise and is the efficient design.

4.3 Voluntary take-up is anti-selection for the third time

The summary lists setting take-up rates on voluntary schemes among its anti-selection controls, and in chapter 1 lists four ways a group scheme can be paid for: the employer pays, costs are shared, the employee pays, or the arrangement is linked to a club membership as an affinity group. Those two lists are the same axis, because who pays determines whether joining is a choice.

At full take-up the pool is the population, at 0.003200. With no standard lives joining it is the impaired rate of 0.010000, a multiple of 3.1250. That is the same mechanism as lecture 3's renewal option and lecture 5's selective lapsing, its third appearance in eight lectures. And the loading is convex: the first tranche of non-joiners adds 0.0768 to the loading and the last adds 1.3281, a factor of 17.2917, so participation is worth most where it is already highest.

A minimum take-up of 0.7500 requires 0.7059 of standard lives to join and caps the pool's mortality at 0.003600, a multiple of 1.1250 rather than 3.1250. So the summary's take-up bullet is a price control expressed as a participation rule, and the convexity is why it works as a floor: the rule binds in the region where each additional non-joiner is cheap, and prevents entry into the region where they become expensive.

So the four ways of paying are a selection ladder. An employer-paid scheme is compulsory and carries no selection at all, which is why it needs no take-up rule. An affinity scheme is voluntary with no employment screen, which is why it needs the most. That also explains the summary's otherwise puzzling remark that group critical illness is usually provided to employees who cannot afford income protection or for whom it is unavailable: the employer is paying, so the selection that would wreck a voluntary version of the same cover is absent, and lives who could not buy the product individually become insurable in a scheme.

4.4 The continuation option is selected twice over

The summary gives group term assurance a continuation option following cessation of employment, and gives group income protection one under which the policy is taken out as an individual policy and can be carried on after leaving the job. Both are exercised by leavers, and leavers are selected twice: once on why they left and again on whether they exercise.

   the population          standard lives   impaired lives   mortality    multiple of the
                           in it            in it            of the group active members
   active members                     0.8500           0.1500     0.003200          1.0000
   all leavers                        0.1020           0.0450     0.004449          1.3903
   those who exercise                 0.0102           0.0360     0.008234          2.5731

Standard lives leaving at 0.1200 a year and impaired at 0.3000, then exercising at 0.1000 and 0.8000 respectively. Invented for illustration.

Active members run at 0.003200, leavers at 0.004449 and exercisers at 0.008234, which is 2.5731 times the active rate. The two stages multiply: 1.3903 from why they left and a further 1.8507 from who exercises. An insurer that prices the option on the active membership has understated it by the product of the two, not by either one, and pricing it on the leaver population alone still leaves most of the error in place.

And no exclusion clause reaches it, which is why the summary lists exclusions and continuation options in different places. An exclusion narrows what is covered; the continuation option's problem is who is covered, and the selection happens through a decision the member makes after leaving, when the insurer has no further underwriting right. The only available response is a loading collected at inception from the whole scheme, exactly as in lecture 3's renewable term assurance, which means the members who never leave pay for the option the leavers exercise.

The summary's other group income protection features are worth reading in the same light. Benefits may be based on net or gross salary, with easier administration on gross, and may additionally cover pension and state welfare contributions for both employer and employee. Each of those widens the benefit without widening the selection, because none of them is a choice the member makes. The continuation option is the only feature in the list that hands the member an election, and it is the only one carrying anti-selection.

4.5 Only a large scheme is rewarded for its own experience

Experience rating is market practice rather than a heading in this part of the summary, and it follows directly from credibility theory, which is standard actuarial material: the blended rate is Z × own experience + (1 - Z) × book rate, with Z = n / (n + k) in the Buhlmann form. Put numbers on it for a scheme whose own claim rate is half the book's.

   life-years of   credibility   weight on   blended rate as   discount the scheme
   own experience  factor Z      the book    a share of book   actually receives
   100                  0.047619    0.952381          0.976190              0.0238
   500                  0.200000    0.800000          0.900000              0.1000
   2000                 0.500000    0.500000          0.750000              0.2500
   10000                0.833333    0.166667          0.583333              0.4167

A credibility constant of 2000 life-years against a scheme running at 0.5000 of the book rate. The constant is invented; the blending formula is standard.

A scheme running at half the book rate receives a 0.0238 discount at a hundred life-years and 0.4167 at ten thousand, a factor of 17.5000 for identical experience. At the exposure that equals the credibility constant it receives exactly half the discount its own experience would justify, which is what the constant means. So the incentive to manage workplace health is proportional to scheme size, and a small employer is priced on other employers' experience almost entirely.

And that is why the largest employers leave the insured market. At a hundred thousand life-years the credibility factor reaches 0.980392, so the book rate contributes 0.019608 of the price. At that point an insurer is charging administration and capital costs to confirm a number the employer's own data already determines, and self-insurance or a captive becomes the cheaper way to hold the same risk. The insured group market is therefore a middle-sized market by arithmetic rather than by preference: too small to be credible on their own at one end, too credible to need an insurer at the other. That is a commercial fact the summary's product list does not state and every group actuary works inside.

One qualification. Credibility rewards good experience and penalises bad, so the argument runs both ways: a large scheme with poor experience is also priced on it, and cannot hide inside a book. Which means the self-insurance decision is not simply about avoiding an insurer's margin, it is about whether the employer would rather hold its own risk at its own price than pool at a blended one, and the answer depends on which side of the book rate its experience sits.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The habit worth installing is to decompose a variance before invoking the law of large numbers. Pooling works on the independent component and does nothing whatever to the shared one, so a statement that a portfolio is large enough to be safe is incomplete until it says which component is being discussed. This lecture is the third time that decomposition has decided a question in this module, after lecture 5's morbidity guarantee and lecture 6's diagnostic drift, and in all three the intuition that scale solves the problem was not merely optimistic but pointed the wrong way.

The second habit is to read a reinsurance or hedging structure as a claim about which part of a distribution matters. Proportional cover changes no ratio and is therefore about capital and cash flow; non-proportional cover changes the shape and is therefore about risk. Anybody who buys the first hoping for the second has spent money to no effect, and the test is arithmetic: compute the coefficient of variation before and after. If it has not moved, the structure was not a risk transfer.

The third habit is to insist that every threshold in a contract be stated as the ratio it is. Free cover limits, deferred periods, minimum claim sizes and condition lists are all fixed costs divided by proportional benefits, and expressing them that way converts a negotiated number into a checkable one. It also reveals when a threshold has drifted away from its own logic, which happens whenever the fixed cost changes and the threshold does not.

The fourth habit is to look for the election. Every feature in a group arrangement that the member chooses is selected against, and every feature the employer chooses on their behalf is not. That single test sorts the summary's long lists of group features into the ones that need a loading and the ones that do not, and it generalises: benefits paid on gross rather than net salary carry no selection, while an option to continue after leaving carries a great deal. Ask who decides, and the anti-selection question answers itself.

The last habit is to notice when a market's size distribution is determined by a formula rather than by taste. Credibility explains why the insured group market has a middle: below a certain exposure an employer's own experience carries no weight, and above another its own experience carries almost all of it, so the insurer's value is highest in between. Any market where the product is essentially information about risk will have that shape, which is why the same argument explains the boundaries of the rated bond market and of external credit assessment generally.

Connections

What this assumes. Every mechanism in this lecture except concentration has appeared earlier in the module. The anti-selection identity of section 4.3 is lecture 3's renewal option, in assurances, where the required loading equalled the proportional deterioration exactly. The fixed-cost-per-event structure of section 4.2 is lecture 5's deferred period, in income protection, which also supplied the selective lapsing result and the split between diversifiable and systematic uncertainty that section 4.1 reuses. Lecture 6's condition list, in critical illness, is the same structure again. The group versions of the products themselves are in assurances, annuities and long-term care, and the underlying bases in product bases. Section 4.5's blending formula is developed properly in credibility theory and empirical Bayes credibility, where the Buhlmann constant is derived rather than assumed.

Where this goes next. This closes the products module. The pricing module takes the same eight lectures and asks what premium each contract needs, which turns section 4.2's break-even into a proper expense analysis and section 4.5's blending into a rating structure. The investment module starts from the liabilities these products create and works outwards to the assets, where the concentration result matters again because a scheme's assets and its members' employer can be the same exposure. The risk management and risk measurement modules take up reserving, capital and the reinsurance decision, where section 4.1's variance decomposition becomes the basis for choosing a retention rather than an illustration of why the choice matters.

Where the bank sits opposite. Section 4.1 is the concentration problem a bank knows as single-name and sector risk: a portfolio of loans to one industry has an independent default component and a shared one, the second does not diversify, and the capital treatment has to model it explicitly. That machinery is in credit concentration risk, and the correlation parameter in the regulatory formula exists for exactly the reason the floor in this lecture exists. Section 4.1's reinsurance result also has a direct analogue: a bank that sells a pro-rata participation in a loan portfolio changes its exposure and not its risk profile, while a first-loss tranche or a credit default swap on the tail changes the shape, which is the securitisation and risk transfer material in the same module. Section 4.2's screening break-even is the small-exposure question a bank answers with automated scoring rather than manual assessment, in application scoring, where the cost of a credit decision against the size of the facility sets the same kind of limit. And section 4.5's credibility argument explains the boundary of external credit assessment, since a borrower with enough of its own history does not need a rating agency's book, which is in ratings agencies.

Experience rating in section 4.5 is market practice rather than a heading in those chapters, and it is grounded in the Buhlmann credibility form, which is standard actuarial material covered in the fundamentals track; that section is labelled accordingly rather than presented as syllabus content. No observation is recorded against the summary in this lecture. Its assignment of catastrophe reinsurance to group business turns out to be derivable from the variance decomposition rather than conventional, its free cover limit turns out to be a computable break-even it has already given the ingredients for in another chapter, and its four funding models turn out to be a selection ladder. Everything numerical is invented for illustration: an underwriting cost of 400 per life against 0.1000 of members impaired at 0.0040 of extra mortality, an actively-at-work declaration costing 5.0 and finding 0.5000 of the excess, a membership 0.8500 standard at 0.0020 and 0.1500 impaired at 0.0100, withdrawal rates of 0.1200 and 0.3000 with exercise rates of 0.1000 and 0.8000, a 500000 sum assured with a 0.0010 chance of an event removing 0.1000 of a scheme, and a credibility constant of 2000 life-years against a scheme at 0.5000 of the book rate. Twenty-four claims and counterexamples were encoded as assertions, all twenty-four passed, and none can pass without the computation. Two claims in a first run were algebraic identities that would have held for any inputs: one restated that a loading equals a proportional deterioration, which is true by definition, and was replaced by an assertion that the loading is convex in the fall in take-up, with the first increment at 0.0768 and the last at 1.3281; the other compared the credibility constant with itself, and was replaced by an assertion that the discount at that exposure is exactly half the discount the scheme's own experience would justify. Every headline finding states the region in which it holds: the invariance of the coefficient of variation under proportional reinsurance is exact and follows from the definition, holding for any retention; the existence of a floor holds for any positive shared-event probability and is independent of scheme size, with 1.5803 and the 7.1449 ratio specific to the illustrative parameters; both screening break-evens are exact ratios of cost to expected detected mortality; the take-up convexity holds for any two-class membership in which the impaired always join; and the credibility results are exact given the Buhlmann form, with the 17.5000 factor specific to the assumed constant.
