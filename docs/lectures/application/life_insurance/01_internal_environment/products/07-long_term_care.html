Lectures / Life insurance / Internal environment / Products

The unit-linked contract runs out of money at the age the claims arrive

The summary warns that a unit-linked long-term care fund may be exhausted by rising risk charges and that the cover will lapse. It does not say when. On the illustrative figures the fund empties at age 86, and 0.3453 of expected claims fall at or after that age, more than the whole of the first fifteen years of cover. The same rising incidence that empties the fund is the reason to hold the cover, so the two are one curve. And the summary's middle guarantee, protection from age 90, is worth exactly nothing here, because the failure precedes the protection.

Step 1

The 30-second version

Long-term care is the hardest product on this syllabus, and the reason is that it combines every difficulty the previous three lectures introduced separately. It pays a continuing benefit over an uncertain duration, like income protection. It turns on a written definition an insurer cannot fully control, like critical illness. It runs on lives whose survival is the exposure, like an annuity. The summary's own description adds the sentence that makes all three worse: the target market is people who are not going to get better.

Start with the unit-linked contract, because the summary states its failure mode and stops short of quantifying it. Risk charges rise with incidence and the fund grows with markets, so the two compound at different rates and the crossing is arithmetic rather than bad luck. The fund empties at age 86, and 0.3453 of expected claims fall at or after that age, which is more than the whole of the first fifteen years of cover. A better investment return defers the failure without removing it: at 0.0800 the fund lasts to 91 and still loses 0.1593 of claims.

Then the benefit form. The summary lists advantages and disadvantages for indemnity and cash benefits as though they were separate items. They are one transfer. The cash contract saves 75000.0000, exactly the loading on the cost uncertainty, and the policyholder's expected shortfall on the bad side is 59841.3421, a ratio of 0.7979. So cash is genuinely good value for someone who can absorb the tail and genuinely poor value for someone who cannot, and the summary's phrase about misleading expectations names the second group.

Then the cost side, where the arithmetic runs lecture 5's escalation identity backwards. Care is labour-intensive with little productivity growth, so its price tracks wages rather than consumer prices, which is Baumol's cost disease. A benefit indexed at 0.0300 against costs rising at 0.0500 covers 0.6807 of the cost after twenty years and halves after 36.0425. Lecture 5 had the benefit outrunning the salary and producing over-insurance; here the cost outruns the benefit and produces under-insurance. Same ratio, same test, opposite failure.

Finally the claim definition, which is why this product resists the discipline that worked for critical illness. Tightening from one activity of daily living to five cuts the activities route by 0.9333 and the total by only 0.8224, because mental impairment overrides the activities test entirely. The override's share of claims rises from 0.0132 to 0.6296 across that range, so the route the insurer cannot tighten grows in importance exactly as it tightens the one it can.

Step 2

Why this matters

The unit-linked result matters because it describes a product that fails silently and on schedule. For twenty-five years the policyholder sees a fund that is larger than the premium paid, and there is no point at which ordinary experience signals what is coming. Then the charges overtake the growth, the fund drains in a few years, and the cover ends at the age when the risk is heaviest. Anybody assessing such a contract should project the charge and the fund to the end of the table rather than to a policy anniversary, and should treat the guarantee level as the product's defining feature rather than as an option on it.

The indemnity and cash result matters because it converts a list of six bullet points into a single question about the buyer. The summary presents choice, known amounts, lower margins, windfalls, family exploitation and misleading expectations as six independent considerations, and they collapse into one: has the cost uncertainty been transferred, and can this particular buyer carry it? A pensioner with substantial assets can and should take the cash discount. A pensioner without them is buying a discount they cannot afford to use, and no amount of disclosure changes the arithmetic.

The cost inflation result matters because it is the most reliable adverse trend in the whole product. Investment returns are uncertain, incidence is uncertain, and the wage premium in service costs is close to structural, because it follows from care being labour-intensive rather than from anything an insurer or a government decides. So a level or price-indexed cash benefit erodes predictably, and the erosion is computable at the point of sale rather than discovered afterwards. Anybody selling one should quote the covered share at twenty years alongside the benefit at outset.

The definition result matters because it marks the limit of a technique. Lecture 6 showed that narrowing a critical illness condition list buys a premium guarantee cheaply, because the rare conditions cost little and their definitions are the volatile part. That technique does not transfer here. The activities of daily living can be tightened, the mental impairment override cannot, and tightening one raises the share of claims arriving through the other. An insurer that responds to poor long-term care experience by tightening the activities test is chasing a shrinking part of its own problem.

The Feynman version. You give an insurer a hundred thousand at sixty. They invest it, and every month they take out a slice to pay for care cover. When you are sixty the slice is tiny, because almost nobody needs care at sixty. When you are eighty-five the slice is enormous, because plenty of people do. Meanwhile the pot grows at whatever markets give you, six per cent say. But the slice grows at eleven per cent, because that is how fast the chance of needing care climbs with age.

Six against eleven has only one ending. The pot gets bigger for about twelve years, then flattens, then falls off a cliff, and it is empty at eighty-six. Which is roughly the age you were buying the cover for. You have paid for twenty-six years of protection you did not need in order to have none in the years you did.

And nothing warns you. At eighty your pot is still worth more than you put in. The failure is not a market crash or a scandal, it is two compound growth rates that were never going to meet, agreed in a contract you signed when you were sixty and could not have projected in your head.

Step 3

The mental model

Hold five pictures. The first is two compound curves crossing. Any arrangement where a charge grows at one rate and a fund at a lower one has a guaranteed ending, and the only question a higher return settles is whether the crossing falls inside a human lifetime. That picture applies to unit-linked care cover, to any charge-deducting contract on a rising hazard, and to a pension in drawdown against rising withdrawals.

The second is a discount and its consequence separated by decades. A cash benefit is cheaper than an indemnity benefit by exactly the loading on the cost uncertainty, and the buyer meets the other side of that trade at the claim rather than at the sale. Every argument about cash versus indemnity is an argument about whether the buyer will still be able to bear what they agreed to bear.

The third is a ratio of two growing quantities, which is lecture 5's picture reused with the numerator and denominator swapped. There the benefit grew faster than the salary and produced over-insurance. Here the cost grows faster than the benefit and produces under-insurance. One test, applied in both directions, catches both failures, and the test is simply whether the two growth rates match.

The fourth is a threshold with an unpriced quantity on one side. Informal care looks free because the carer's foregone earnings appear on nobody's account, and it creates value only while those earnings fall short of the formal cost. The summary's cultural variables move families across that line rather than changing their preferences, which is why insurance demand rises with participation in paid work.

The fifth is two doors into one room. A claim can arrive through the activities test or through the mental impairment override, and only one door has a handle the insurer can turn. Narrowing the first raises the share arriving through the second, so the achievable saving has a floor. Whenever a definition has an override clause, the override sets the limit of what definitional tightening can do.

Step 4

The detail

4.1 The fund empties at the age the claims arrive

The summary's unit-linked section carries a warning it does not quantify: as the policyholder ages the risk charges increase, investment growth may not match them, so effectively no charges will be paid and the cover will lapse. Take a single premium of 100000 at age 60 into a fund growing at 0.0600, with monthly risk charges buying a 1000000 care package on an incidence rising at 0.1100 a year.

The fund is exhausted at age 86. For the first twenty years nothing looks wrong: at 80 the fund is still 114854.0821 against a 100000.0000 premium, so a policyholder checking a statement sees a pot larger than the money they put in. Then the charge, compounding at 0.1100, overtakes the fund, compounding at 0.0600, and the balance drains in six years.

Now ask when the claims arrive. The same rising incidence that empties the fund is the reason to hold the cover, so the two are the same curve.

The contract dies immediately after its busiest decade, and the policyholder has paid for twenty-six years of protection against a risk that had not yet arrived in order to have none in the years when it had.

A higher return shrinks the lost share and does not change the structure, because incidence compounds at 0.1100 and no credible fund matches that. The only question a better return settles is whether the race is lost inside a human lifetime. Which is why the summary lists three guarantee levels rather than treating the unguaranteed version as a default with a caveat.

   the guarantee level      what the insurer takes on   share of claims   is the cover fit

The three levels the summary describes, valued against the same claim distribution.

The middle option covers 0.6547, which is the same number as no guarantee at all. It is worth exactly nothing here, because the fund is empty at 86 and the protection only begins at 90. A guarantee that starts after the failure is not a partial guarantee, it is none, and whether it binds depends on a race the policyholder cannot observe and the insurer sets the odds of by choosing the charging basis. So the three levels are not a ladder of prudence: two are products and one is a bet.

One boundary. The incidence and mortality curves are invented, so age 86 and the 0.3453 share are magnitudes rather than forecasts. What is general is that the charge and the fund compound at different rates whenever incidence grows faster than markets, which is true of any care-cover charging structure at advanced ages, and that the protection-age option is worth nothing whenever exhaustion precedes it, which is a condition an insurer can test at the point of design.

So cash is genuinely good value for a policyholder who can absorb the tail and genuinely poor value for one who cannot, and the summary's phrase, misleading policyholder expectations, names the second group rather than describing a mis-sale. The disclosure question and the affordability question are different, and only the second one is answerable at the point of sale.

One observation is recorded against the summary here. Among the advantages of cash benefits it says the number and amount of claims are known. Only the amount is known. The number depends on incidence, which rises from 0.002000 at age 60 to 0.045785 at 90, a factor of 22.8923, and which the summary itself lists as a data credibility micro risk two sections earlier. The examinable position is that a cash benefit removes uncertainty in the severity of each claim and leaves the frequency untouched, which is the same distinction lecture 5 drew between the two halves of a deferred period's saving.

4.3 Care cost inflation runs the drift identity in reverse

Care is a labour-intensive service with little scope for productivity growth, so its price tracks wages rather than general prices. That is Baumol's cost disease, described in Baumol and Bowen's 1966 study of the performing arts and since applied across personal services, health and education: where output per worker cannot rise but wages must keep pace with the rest of the economy, unit costs rise relative to the general price level. It is the most reliable adverse trend in this product.

A benefit escalating at 0.0300 with consumer prices against care costs escalating at 0.0500 with wages. Both rates invented for illustration.

The covered share falls to 0.6807 after twenty years and halves after 36.0425, and the halving time is a closed form in the two escalation rates alone, independent of the benefit level. This is lecture 5's escalation identity with the sign reversed. There the benefit outran the salary and produced over-insurance, and the summary's remedy was a maximum ratio. Here the cost outruns the benefit and produces under-insurance, and there is no remedy inside the contract at all. One ratio of two growing quantities, two opposite failures, and the same single test.

Which is what the summary means by misleading policyholder expectations, a phrase it lists among the disadvantages of cash benefits without connecting it to escalation. The expectation is not misleading at outset, when the benefit covers the cost exactly. It becomes misleading by arithmetic, on a schedule fixed at the point of sale, and the seller could quote the twenty-year covered share as easily as the first-year benefit.

Now informal care, which the summary says is provided by a spouse or children usually over 60, with an indirect cost in lost economic activity, and whose supply depends on cultural traditions, the proportion of women working, family dispersion and family structures. That indirect cost is unpriced rather than absent, so there is a threshold.

The threshold is exactly the formal care cost. Below it family care creates value; above it family care destroys value while still looking free, because the output lost appears on nobody's account. So the summary's list of cultural variables, and particularly the proportion of women working, acts through arithmetic rather than through taste: rising earnings push families across a line, informal supply falls, and insurance demand rises. That mechanism is also why the demand for this product is highest in exactly the economies that can most easily afford to supply it.

4.4 A benefit that rises with deepening incapacity is largely self-funding

The summary's immediate needs product is, in its own words, an impaired-life annuity bought with a single premium by someone already in care, and it adds that the level of benefit may increase with deepening incapacity. That clause sounds expensive and is mostly not, because the trigger that raises the benefit is the same deterioration that shortens the period it is paid for. Measure the escalation against a like-for-like baseline.

A 200000 benefit escalating at 0.0500, discounted at 0.0500, with claimant mortality of 0.1500 rising by 0.0400 a year where incapacity deepens. Each escalation cost is measured against its own level-benefit baseline.

Escalation costs 281554.3302 on a constant-mortality life and 83931.2898 when the trigger also raises mortality, so 0.7019 of the escalation is self-funding. It is not free, which the fourth row shows and which matters, because the natural conclusion from a two-row comparison would be that the clause costs nothing. It costs about three tenths of what a plain escalation costs, and that is why an insurer can offer it as a feature rather than as an expensive option.

And the same mechanism explains a capital note that otherwise reads oddly. The summary says long-term care reserves resemble an endowment for the pre-funded version or whole life for immediate needs, then adds that the lack of a death benefit can lower the reserve. On a deteriorating life with no death benefit, worsening experience reduces the liability, so the reserve falls as the policyholder's health does. That is the opposite of every other contract in this module, where deterioration raises the reserve, and it is worth stating explicitly because it inverts the usual direction of prudence.

This is lecture 4's impaired-life result applied within a single contract rather than across two classes of annuitant. There, pricing the impaired life separately removed a cross-subsidy between two groups. Here, a benefit that tracks deterioration is self-funding within one life, and both follow from the same fact: the payment period and the payment size move in opposite directions when health is the driver of both.

4.5 The override caps what tightening the definition can save

The summary gives five activities of daily living as the claim definition, feeding, dressing, washing, toileting and mobility, and adds that mental impairment overrides cognitive impairment even where the insured can perform them. That override is a second route to a claim, and it puts a floor under the claim rate that tightening cannot breach.

Tightening from one activity to five cuts the activities route by 0.9333 and the total by only 0.8224, because the override is unaffected by the threshold. The override's share of all claims rises from 0.0132 to 0.6296 across that range, a factor of nearly fifty, so the route the insurer cannot tighten becomes more important exactly as it tightens the one it can.

That is the structural reason long-term care resists the discipline that worked in lecture 6, where narrowing a critical illness condition list genuinely bought a premium guarantee. There every route to a claim ran through a named condition, so removing conditions removed routes. Here one route is defined by function and another by cognition, and the second is deliberately written to survive the first. The summary's own phrasing, that mental impairment overrides cognitive impairment even if the insured is able to perform the activities, is the clause that creates the floor.

It also explains why the summary lists data credibility as a distinct micro risk rather than folding it into morbidity. Two routes with different drivers need two sets of data, one on functional decline and one on cognitive decline, and neither has the volume of a mortality table. An insurer that has calibrated its activities incidence well may still have no view at all on the route that will eventually supply most of its claims.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The habit worth installing is to project any charge-deducting arrangement to the end of the mortality table rather than to a policy anniversary or a planning horizon. Two quantities compounding at different rates always cross, and the crossing is invisible for as long as the level difference exceeds the accumulated rate difference. Unit-linked care cover is the clearest case, but the same test applies to a drawdown pension against rising withdrawals, to a fee-deducting wrapper against a shrinking fund, and to any structure where the deduction is a function of age.

The second habit is to ask, of any cheaper product, what was transferred to buy the discount and whether the buyer can carry it. The cash benefit's saving is exactly the loading on the cost uncertainty, so nothing was created and nothing was lost, only moved. That framing turns most product comparisons into a single question about the counterparty rather than a balancing of listed features, and it is usually answerable. It also identifies the cases where a cheaper product is genuinely better, which are the ones where the buyer is a better holder of the risk than the insurer.

The third habit is to check the escalation basis against the basis of whatever the benefit is meant to buy. Lecture 5 and this lecture are the same identity in opposite directions, and between them they cover both failures: a benefit indexed faster than its reference produces over-insurance and moral hazard, and one indexed slower produces under-insurance and disappointed expectations. Neither is a risk in the ordinary sense, because both are determined at outset by two chosen numbers. Compute the twenty-year ratio before signing, always.

The fourth habit is to look for unpriced quantities before concluding that something is free. Informal care costs nothing in cash and a great deal in foregone output, and the threshold where it stops making sense is exactly the formal cost. The general point is that a resource with no market price is not a resource with no cost, and the decisions built on treating it as free will be systematically wrong in one direction. That applies to family care, to internal staff time in a bank, and to any capacity that appears in no budget line.

The last habit is to identify which part of a definition you control before proposing to tighten it. An override clause, a legal minimum, a regulatory floor or a court's interpretation all set limits on what definitional discipline can achieve, and tightening the controllable part raises the share of claims arriving through the part you cannot touch. Long-term care is the case here; the banking equivalent is a covenant package where one covenant is negotiable and another is set by statute, and tightening the first simply concentrates the outcome on the second.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The escalation identity section 4.3 runs in reverse, and the frequency-against-severity distinction section 4.2's observation depends on, are both in income protection. The definitional discipline that section 4.5 shows does not transfer, and the parameter uncertainty behind a premium guarantee, are in critical illness. Section 4.4 is annuities applied within one contract rather than across two classes, and the impaired-life result there is the direct antecedent. The unit-linked mechanics section 4.1 projects, with charges deducted from a fund, are in product bases and surplus distribution, and the reserve and strain concepts are in the product framework. The multiple-state machinery behind a healthy-impaired-dead process with two routes into claim is in multiple state models.

Where the bank sits opposite. Section 4.1's two compound curves are the structure of a bank's interest-only mortgage against a repayment vehicle, where the debt is fixed and the vehicle is expected to grow into it, and the failure is invisible until close to redemption; the same shortfall arithmetic drives the affordability and forbearance treatment in A-IRB capital modelling. Section 4.2's transfer of cost uncertainty for a discount is exactly the choice between a fixed and a floating rate loan, where the cheaper option at outset is the one that leaves the borrower holding the rate risk, and the mechanics are in interest rate risk in the banking book. Section 4.3's Baumol mechanism is a real-economy input to any long-horizon credit assessment, and the labour-cost channel is developed in economics. Section 4.5's uncontrollable route has a direct lending counterpart in statutory protections a bank cannot contract out of: tightening the negotiable covenants concentrates the outcome on the ones set by law, which is the same floor under the same kind of tightening.

One observation is recorded against the summary, in section 4.2: it lists the number and amount of claims as known under a cash benefit, and only the amount is known, since the number depends on an incidence the summary itself flags as a data credibility risk. Everything else it states is correct, including the unit-linked lapse warning, which section 4.1 shows to be a central projection rather than a tail risk, and its decision to describe three guarantee levels rather than treating the unguaranteed version as a default. Twenty-six claims and counterexamples were encoded as assertions, all twenty-six passed, and none can pass without the computation. A first run made three errors that the assertions caught: it summed incidence rates and labelled the result a probability, giving band shares that exceeded one and an age-band structure that never straddled the exhaustion age; it asserted that a higher investment return only defers the failure, which is false at a 0.1200 return inside the projection horizon, so the claim now reports the lost share at four returns and states the mechanism instead; and it compared an escalating benefit on a deteriorating life against a level benefit on a healthy one, confounding two changes and producing a self-funding share above one, so a fourth row was added to measure each escalation against its own baseline. Every headline finding states the region in which it holds: the exhaustion result is general wherever incidence compounds faster than the fund, with age 86 and the 0.3453 share specific to the illustrative curves; the equality between the cash discount and the cost-uncertainty loading is exact by construction and holds for any loading factor; the halving time depends only on the two escalation rates and not on the benefit level; the informal care threshold is exactly the formal cost for any earnings level; the escalation is 0.7019 self-funding on these mortality assumptions and is never free; and the override floor exists for any positive override rate, with the 0.6296 share specific to the illustrative overlap.
