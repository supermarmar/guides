Risk measurement

Twenty-four lectures covering F102 chapters 8 to 10, 23 to 26, 32 and 35. The largest module in the track by a wide margin, and the one where the marks are. Every lecture here answers the same shape of question: what number goes on the balance sheet, in the bonus declaration, or in the surrender value quotation, and what would make it wrong.

How this module is organised

By deliverable, not by risk type. The banking equivalent splits into credit, market, operational, liquidity and interest rate risk because Basel does, and each folder then runs its own arc from concept to capital. F102 has no such taxonomy. Its measurement chapters are organised around what the actuary produces, so the folders here are asset shares, with-profits distribution, reserving, capital, policyholder values, and experience analysis.

Read them in that order. The asset share is the foundation: it is the retrospective accumulation that with-profits bonus policy is judged against, that surrender values are anchored to, and that alterations must not breach. Nothing later in the module makes full sense without it.

The lecture count is weighted towards where the marks are lost rather than towards the number of syllabus chapters. With-profits distribution and policyholder values get eleven lectures between them for four chapters, because neither has any analogue in banking, in general insurance, or in the pure mathematics, so there is no transferable intuition to compress against. Reserving and capital get eight for two chapters, and they will feel more familiar to anyone who has built regulatory capital models.

Foundation

  • planned01. Asset shares, the concept 01-asset_shares_concept.html — what an asset share is and what it is not, why the concept exists at all, how it develops over a policy's life, and its three roles: the yardstick for bonus policy, the anchor for surrender values, and the constraint on alterations (Ch 8)
  • planned02. Asset shares, the calculation 02-asset_shares_calculation.html — the accumulation item by item, the treatment of each cashflow including tax, expenses, the cost of guarantees and the charge for capital, and the judgements that make two offices' asset shares differ for identical policies (Ch 8)

Bookmark these two. The asset share is the single most reused object in the module and the concept with no counterpart anywhere else in the lecture library. It is not a reserve, it is not a surrender value, and it is not an embedded value; it is the accumulated premiums less the accumulated outgo, at actual earned rates, for a specified group of policies. It gets two lectures because the concept and the arithmetic fail in different ways: the concept is misunderstood, and the calculation is where the discretionary items hide.

The five sub-modules

With-profits, six lectures

  • plannedWith-profits landing — where surplus comes from, conventional and accumulating additions to benefits taken separately, revalorisation and the contribution method, choosing a bonus method, and declaring one (Ch 9, 10)

The hardest sub-module in the track, and the most heavily weighted relative to its two chapters. Nothing transfers: smoothing, the ownership of the estate, and the tension between guarantee and discretion are peculiar to with-profits business. Conventional and accumulating with-profits get a lecture each rather than being treated as variants of one idea, because the guarantee bites at different points and an office running both is running two risk profiles under one label.

Reserving, four lectures

  • plannedReserving landing — the purposes a reserve serves, gross and net premium valuation compared, unit-linked reserves including the negative non-unit reserve problem, and market-consistent valuation (Ch 23)

Capital, four lectures

  • plannedCapital landing — the principles behind supervisory reserves and capital, the solvency capital requirement, the value-at-risk approach with the passive and active valuation alternatives, and embedded value (Ch 24)

The module that will feel most familiar. A one-year value-at-risk capital requirement at a high confidence level is structurally the same idea as the Basel capital formula, and the lectures make the comparison explicit rather than pretending the two worlds are unrelated.

Policyholder values, five lectures

  • plannedPolicyholder values landing — surrender value principles then methods, paid-up values, the proportionate paid-up value and equating policy values methods, and the selection and profitability consequences (Ch 25, 26)

Weighted well above its two chapters, for three reasons. Alterations are examined regularly, the two methods give different answers to the same question, and the selection effects that follow an alteration are the part candidates routinely miss. The sub-module is also where the actuarial judgement meets the fairness obligation most directly.

Experience analysis, three lectures

  • plannedExperience analysis landing — policy data checks, monitoring experience by decrement and by expense category, and the analysis of surplus and of embedded value movement (Ch 32, 35)

The feedback loop. Everything pricing assumed gets tested here, and the results feed back into the next assumption review. The analysis of surplus is the reconciliation that proves the whole apparatus hangs together.

What is deliberately not here

Prospective and retrospective reserves, the equation of value, and the relationship between the two are established in actuarial mathematics. Reserves inside a profit test, including zeroisation of negative reserves, are in reserves under profit testing. Reversionary and terminal bonus arithmetic is in variable benefits. This module assumes all of it and spends its pages on the judgement instead.

Source material

ASSA F102 Summary, chapters 8 to 10, 23 to 26, 32 and 35. The capital sub-module carries gap extensions from the Solvency II Directive and EIOPA technical specifications for the value-at-risk framing, from the South African prudential standards for the local regime, and from the CFO Forum market consistent embedded value principles for the embedded value lecture.
