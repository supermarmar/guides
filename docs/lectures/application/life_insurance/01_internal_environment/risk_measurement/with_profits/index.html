Risk measurement, life insurance

With-profits surplus distribution

Six lectures covering F102 chapters 9 and 10. A with-profits policyholder buys a share of the office's surplus without knowing in advance what that share will be. Everything difficult about the product follows from that sentence: how surplus is identified, how much of it is distributed now rather than later, in what form, and who arbitrates between generations of policyholders when the answer favours one over another.

How this sub-module is organised

Two chapters over six lectures, the highest ratio in the track. That is deliberate. This is the material with no analogue anywhere else in the lecture library: nothing in banking, in general insurance, or in the pure mathematics prepares you for smoothing, for the estate, or for the tension between a guarantee and a discretion. There is no transferable intuition to compress against, so the sequence is built long and slow.

Lecture 01 establishes where surplus comes from. Lectures 02 and 03 then take the additions-to-benefits mechanism in its two forms, conventional and accumulating, one lecture each rather than crammed together, because the two behave differently enough that treating them as variants of one idea is the mistake. Lecture 04 covers the two non-British mechanisms. Lectures 05 and 06 are the decisions: which mechanism an office adopts, and then what it declares each year.

Read the asset shares lectures first. Bonus policy is judged by whether the total payout tracks the asset share, so the asset share is the yardstick that makes this whole sub-module measurable rather than arbitrary.

Additions to benefits

The mechanism used across the English-speaking world, and the one the exam assumes unless it says otherwise.

  • planned02. Conventional with-profits 02-conventional_with_profits.html — reversionary bonus in its simple, compound and super-compound forms, terminal bonus as the shock absorber, what each does to the guaranteed benefit, and the irreversibility that makes a reversionary declaration a permanent commitment (Ch 9)
  • planned03. Accumulating with-profits 03-accumulating_with_profits.html — the unitised and non-unitised forms, regular and final bonus, the market value reduction and when it may be applied, and how the accumulating structure changes the office's exposure relative to conventional business (Ch 9)
Why these are separate lectures

A conventional contract has a guaranteed sum assured that reversionary bonus increases. An accumulating contract has an account balance that regular bonus credits. Both are additions to benefits, and the resemblance stops there. The guarantee bites at different points, the surrender basis differs, the market value reduction exists in one and not the other, and an office running both is running two different risk profiles under one label.

The alternatives

  • planned04. Revalorisation and contribution 04-revalorisation_and_contribution.html — the continental European revalorisation method and the north American contribution method, how each identifies and allocates surplus, and the advantages and drawbacks of both (Ch 10)

Examined less often than additions to benefits, but examined as a comparison, which means the marks come from being able to say what each method does differently rather than from reciting either in isolation.

The trade-off the exam wants

A reversionary bonus increases a guaranteed benefit, so declaring one is irreversible and raises the office's liability permanently. A terminal bonus is not guaranteed, so it can be cut. An office that has been generous with reversionary bonuses has spent its flexibility and must meet the guarantee from a fund it can no longer manage freely. That trade-off between generosity now and freedom later is the substance of chapter 10, and it is the point on which discursive answers earn or lose most of their marks.

What is deliberately not here

The arithmetic of reversionary and terminal bonus accumulation, and the valuation of a contract carrying declared bonuses, is in variable benefits. These six lectures assume it and spend their pages on the policy question instead.
