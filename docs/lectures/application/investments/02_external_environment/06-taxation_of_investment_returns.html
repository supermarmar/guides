The corporate tax system does not tilt the dividend preference, it decides its sign

On identical corporate, income and capital gains rates, a share buy-back beats a dividend by 15.8400 under the classical system and loses to it by 0.9600 under imputation. The credit attaches to dividend income and not to a capital gain, so taking the profit as a gain throws the credit away. Along the way: the interest deduction is worth exactly the corporate tax rate times the debt whatever the cost of debt, a regressive income tax inverts every wrapper conclusion of the internal environment, and the minimum repurchase period defeats bed and breakfasting for large holdings while leaving small ones untouched.

Step 1

The 30-second version

Taxation is the third of the summary's nine government policies and, on the internal environment's own arithmetic, the largest influence on an investor's outcome that anybody in the chain can actually control. This lecture takes the two parts of the summary's taxation section that the earlier lectures did not price, because both turn out to be exact and both are more consequential than the summary's phrasing suggests.

The first is the corporate tax system. The summary names three of them, classical, split rate and imputation, and says they influence the investor's preference between dividends and capital gains. That is right, and it understates the case. Follow one unit of company profit to the shareholder's pocket and the classical system takes 56.8000 per cent of it while imputation takes 40.0000 per cent, a gap of 16.8000 percentage points on identical profit at identical rates. Then compare taking that profit as a dividend against taking it as a buy-back. Under the classical system the buy-back wins by 15.8400; under imputation the dividend wins by 0.9600. The preference does not change in size across the systems, it changes sign, because the imputation credit attaches to dividend income and not to a capital gain.

The second is the interest deduction. The summary says the tax treatment of interest influences a company's preference between equity and debt financing, and the value of that preference is an exact expression: the capitalised shield is exactly the corporate tax rate times the debt, 28.0000 per cent of it here, and the cost of debt cancels out entirely. Taken alone that implies every firm should be financed entirely by debt, which no firm is, so the summary's clause is one term of a trade-off whose other terms it omits. The region matters more than the headline: the shield is worth nothing to a firm with no taxable profit, so identical debt costs two firms different amounts after tax and the difference is the tax position rather than the credit.

Two smaller results. A regressive income tax, which the summary names in a single clause, inverts the wrapper conclusion of the internal environment's lecture 5 completely: the investor who gains most from deferral is the high earner under one system and the low earner under the other. And bed and breakfasting, which the summary names alongside its own countermeasure, is defeated by the minimum repurchase period for a holding of 1000 and still profitable for a holding of 100, so the countermeasure is regressive in its incidence.

Of the summary's six tax-specific factors, only three are actionable by the investor. The other three are parameters set elsewhere. That is the useful way to hold the list.

Step 2

Why this matters

Lecture 1 of the internal environment established that a six-link chain of costs takes 1.9500 per cent a year and 52.08 per cent of a forty-year outcome, and that the manager's fee is only 30.77 per cent of that chain. Lecture 2 established that deferring capital gains tax is worth 33.63 per cent more terminal wealth at an unchanged rate, which beats realising them at half the rate, and that the same bond is worth 4.36 per cent more to a tax-exempt holder than to a taxed one. Lecture 5 established the crossover identity for wrappers, that the tax-free and taxable routes cross at t_w + f / g, and put that at 35.0000 per cent independent of horizon.

All three of those results took the tax system as given and asked what the investor should do inside it. This lecture asks the prior question: what does the system itself do to the answer? Two of the results reverse. The wrapper conclusion depended on high earners facing high marginal rates, which is a feature of a progressive system rather than of taxation, and the summary explicitly names the regressive alternative. And the dividend preference, which nobody has yet priced, depends entirely on which corporate system the jurisdiction runs.

That is not an academic point for a South African investor holding foreign equity. A payout policy that is optimal in one jurisdiction is wrong in another with identical statutory rates, so a cross-border equity income strategy cannot be run on one rule. When several countries abolished imputation, payout behaviour changed immediately, and the arithmetic in section 4.1 is the whole explanation.

The Feynman version. A company's profit is taxed once when the company earns it and again when the shareholder receives it. The three systems differ in how they handle the second bite. The classical system pretends the two are unrelated and taxes both fully. The split rate system charges the company less on profit it pays out. Imputation hands the shareholder a receipt for the tax the company already paid, which the shareholder sets against their own bill.

Now notice what the receipt says. It says tax was paid on this dividend. If the shareholder takes the money as a capital gain instead, by selling shares into a buy-back, there is no dividend, so there is no receipt, so the company's tax is lost. Under the classical system there was never a receipt to lose, so the buy-back is simply the cheaper of two taxed routes. Under imputation the buy-back throws away something worth having. Same rates, opposite answer.

Step 3

The mental model

Hold the whole lecture as one question asked twice. A unit of company profit has to reach an investor, and on the way it passes through a number of taxing points. The question is how many points there are and whether they know about each other.

Under the classical system there are two points and they are independent. Under imputation there are two points and the second credits the first, which collapses them into one. Under the split rate system there are two independent points but the first is set lower on distributed profit, which is a partial and deliberate collapse. Every result in section 4.1 follows from counting points and asking whether they communicate.

The interest deduction is the same question asked of the company's own income statement. Interest is deducted before the corporate taxing point and a dividend is paid after it, so the two are not alternative uses of profit but payments made on opposite sides of a tax. That is the entire content of the summary's clause about pre-interest and post-interest profit, and it is why the arithmetic in section 4.2 comes out as a clean multiple of the tax rate.

Step 4

The detail

4.1 The three corporate systems, and the reversal the summary implies without stating

The summary's three systems are set out in a single line each, and the consequence it draws from them is that they influence investor preferences for dividends or capital gains through a share buy-back. Take one unit of profit and follow it.

   the system            corporate tax   investor tax   total tax   the shareholder
                         paid            paid                       receives
   classical                   28.0000        28.8000     56.8000           43.2000
   split rate                  20.0000        32.0000     52.0000           48.0000
   imputation                  28.0000        12.0000     40.0000           60.0000

The classical system takes 56.8000 per cent of the profit and imputation takes 40.0000, a gap of 16.8000 percentage points on identical profit. And the imputation total is exactly the investor's own rate of 40.00 per cent, because the credit removes the corporate layer entirely rather than reducing it. So the phrase double taxation describes one system rather than an inevitable feature of company taxation. Under imputation the company is a conduit and under the classical system it is a separate taxpayer, and that is a policy choice.

Now the summary's actual claim. Compare taking the profit as a dividend, taxed as income at 40 per cent, against taking it as a buy-back, taxed as a capital gain at 18 per cent.

   the system     as a dividend   as a buy-back   which the investor   the preference
                  net receipt     net receipt     prefers              gap
   classical              43.2000           59.0400   the buy-back               15.8400
   split rate             48.0000           65.6000   the buy-back               17.6000
   imputation             60.0000           59.0400   the dividend               -0.9600

The preference does not merely change in size across the three systems. It reverses. Under the classical and split rate systems the buy-back wins by 15.8400 and 17.6000, and under imputation the dividend wins by 0.9600. The mechanism is the one the diagram above sets up: the imputation credit attaches to dividend income and not to a capital gain, so taking the profit as a gain throws the credit away and leaves the corporate layer in place.

Read the imputation row again, because it is the one worth committing to memory. The dividend route delivers exactly 60.0000, the profit less the investor's own rate, with no corporate layer surviving. The buy-back route delivers 59.0400 and suffers both layers, an effective rate of 40.9600 per cent against the dividend's 40.0000 on the same profit under the same system.

The summary's clause is therefore stronger than it looks. The system does not tilt the dividend preference, it decides its sign. A payout policy that is optimal in one jurisdiction is wrong in another with identical corporate, income and gains rates, which is why the abolition of imputation in several countries changed payout behaviour immediately, and why a cross-border equity income strategy cannot be run on one rule.

The region. Everything above assumed capital gains tax below income tax, which is the usual arrangement and not a necessary one. The summary itself raises the case where it is not, in its remark that a government uses the split rate system to raise growth.

   CGT rate   income tax rate   dividend net   buy-back net   which wins
         18%                40%        43.2000        59.0400   the buy-back
         40%                40%        43.2000        43.2000   neither, they tie
         50%                40%        43.2000        36.0000   the dividend

The two routes tie exactly when the two rates are equal, and the dividend wins above that. So the summary's growth remark is the same arithmetic seen from the government's side: set the retained rate below the distributed one and profits stay inside the company to be reinvested rather than being paid out and taxed again. The tool and the investor's preference are two readings of one table.

4.2 The interest deduction is worth the tax rate times the debt, and the cost of debt cancels

The summary's second corporate clause is that the tax treatment of interest, deducted before the corporate taxing point rather than after it, influences a company's preference for equity or debt financing. That is worth quantifying because the answer is exact and because it is the only reason capital structure matters at all under otherwise frictionless assumptions.

   the firm's debt   interest paid   tax saved by the   the shield as a   after-tax cost
                                     deduction          % of the debt     of that debt
              0.0000          0.0000               0.0000             0.0000%          5.7600%
            200.0000         16.0000               4.4800             2.2400%          5.7600%
            500.0000         40.0000              11.2000             2.2400%          5.7600%
            800.0000         64.0000              17.9200             2.2400%          5.7600%

The after-tax cost of debt is 5.7600 per cent against a pre-tax 8.00, which is the pre-tax cost times one less the corporate rate, and the annual shield is exactly the corporate rate times the interest. Capitalise that in perpetuity at the cost of debt and something tidier appears.

   the firm's debt   the shield each   capitalised at 8%   as a % of the debt
            200.0000             4.4800              56.0000               28.0000%
            500.0000            11.2000             140.0000               28.0000%
            800.0000            17.9200             224.0000               28.0000%

The capitalised shield is exactly 28.00 per cent of the debt, whatever the cost of debt is, because the rate cancels between the annual saving and the discounting. So a firm's value rises by the tax rate for every unit of equity replaced with debt, and the expression is tax shield = t_c x D.

Taken literally that implies every firm should be financed entirely by debt, and none is. So the summary's clause is one term of a trade-off whose other terms it does not mention: the covenant headroom of the internal environment's structure lecture, and the profit volatility that lecture 1 of this module produced, where 800 of debt cost 22.22 per cent of profit on a one-point rate rise. A clause that names a benefit and omits its cost is not wrong, but it is half an answer, and an examiner asking why firms are not wholly debt-financed is asking for the other half.

The region, and it is the one an investor should notice rather than the headline. The shield is worth nothing to a firm with no taxable profit.

   the firm's taxable profit   interest of 64   shield realised   the shield's value
                       0.0000          64.0000            0.0000               0.0000
                      30.0000          64.0000           30.0000               8.4000
                      64.0000          64.0000           64.0000              17.9200
                     200.0000          64.0000           64.0000              17.9200

A firm with no taxable profit realises 0.0000 of shield and a profitable one realises 17.9200. So identical debt costs two firms different amounts after tax, and the difference is the tax position rather than the credit. That is why loss-making firms raise equity and profitable ones raise debt, which looks like a credit pattern in the data and is a tax one. An analyst who reads a rising equity issuance rate as deteriorating creditworthiness may be reading a shrinking tax bill.

4.3 A regressive income tax inverts every wrapper conclusion

The summary defines a progressive system, where the marginal rate rises with income, and names the regressive alternative in the same breath. That single clause is worth following, because the wrapper arithmetic of the internal environment's lecture 5 depended on high earners facing high marginal rates, which is a property of one system rather than of taxation.

   income   progressive marginal   regressive marginal   which system takes more
            rate                   rate                  at this income
      100                    18%                   45%   the REGRESSIVE one
      300                    30%                   35%   the REGRESSIVE one
     1000                    45%                   18%   the progressive one

Under a progressive system the high earner faces 45 per cent and under a regressive one 18. So the wrapper conclusion reverses completely: the investor who gains most from a deferring wrapper is the high earner under one system and the low earner under the other. Lecture 5 could not name a best wrapper without naming the investor. It cannot name one without naming the system either, and the crossover identity t_w + f / g is unchanged while the population it sorts is turned upside down.

4.4 Bed and breakfasting, and a countermeasure that binds the wrong people

The summary names bed and breakfasting, selling and repurchasing an asset to crystallise gains within an annual allowance, and in the same clause names the government's response, which is a minimum period between the sale and the repurchase. Those two belong together, and the reason is quantitative: the minimum period puts the investor at market risk for its duration.

   the minimum gap in days   tax saved by using   market risk over   is the trade
                             the allowance        the gap, 1 s.d.    worthwhile?
                         0               7.2000             0.0000   yes
                         7               7.2000            27.6970   NO
                        30               7.2000            57.3382   NO
                        90               7.2000            99.3127   NO

The saving is 7.2000 whatever the gap, because it is the allowance times the rate and neither depends on time. The market risk over a thirty-day gap on a 1000 holding is 57.3382, which swamps it. So the minimum period defeats the practice without prohibiting it, which is a neater piece of policy design than a ban, and is presumably why the summary mentions the two together.

But the defeat is not universal, and the direction of the failure is worth noticing. The saving is fixed by the allowance and the risk scales with the holding, so a holding of 100 faces only 5.7338 of risk and the trade still pays. The countermeasure therefore binds large holdings and leaves small ones untouched, which makes it regressive in its incidence and is presumably not what was intended. The general shape recurs throughout tax design: a fixed allowance meets a proportional risk, and the break-even is a holding size rather than a rule.

4.5 The six tax-specific factors, and which three an adviser can act on

The summary lists six factors specific to taxation and seven others. The six are more useful once sorted by who controls them.

Three of six are actionable: timing, reclaiming and loss offsetting. The other three are parameters set elsewhere and are worth knowing only so that the actionable three can be aimed correctly. Timing was priced in the internal environment's lecture 2 at 33.63 per cent more terminal wealth, and reclaiming in lecture 1 at 150.00 per cent of the manager's fee, which remains the most underrated number in the whole track. The third has not been priced anywhere.

   realised gains   realised losses   taxable gain   tax paid   tax saved by the
                                                                loss
         100.0000            0.0000       100.0000    18.0000            0.0000
         100.0000           50.0000        50.0000     9.0000            9.0000
         100.0000          100.0000         0.0000     0.0000           18.0000
         100.0000          150.0000         0.0000     0.0000           18.0000

A loss equal to the gain removes the whole 18.0000 charge, and a larger loss saves nothing further in that year. The benefit caps at the gain, so a loss is worth realising only up to the gains available to set it against, and harvesting beyond that point converts a live position into a deferred claim for nothing.

And that makes a carried-forward loss an option rather than an asset. It pays only if future gains arise, so its value depends on the probability of those gains and it should be discounted rather than carried at face value. A company's deferred tax asset is the same object, is routinely carried at full value, and is one of the items lecture 13 of this module will have to look at sceptically.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

A practitioner reads section 4.1 and immediately asks the question the table cannot answer: which system is the shareholder actually in? For a South African investor holding a domestic share the answer is neither of the pure cases, because dividends withholding tax sits on top of corporate tax without a full credit, which puts the country nearer the classical end of the range than the imputation end. The examinable point is that the three systems are the corners of a space rather than a menu, and most jurisdictions sit inside it.

The habit worth forming is to stop asking what the tax rate is and start asking how many taxing points there are and whether they communicate. Statutory rates are quoted, comparable and largely useless on their own: the classical and imputation columns above share every rate and differ by 16.8000 percentage points. The number that matters is the effective rate on the route the investor actually takes, and it is never the headline.

On section 4.2, the trap is treating the shield as a property of the balance sheet. It is a property of the income statement, and specifically of the taxable profit line. A firm's after-tax cost of debt is 5.7600 per cent when it is profitable and 8.00 when it is not, which means a deteriorating firm's cost of debt rises before any lender changes a spread. The credit analyst and the tax accountant are looking at the same number from two sides, and the tax side moves first.

The unglamorous conclusion, and the one that survives every jurisdiction: of the six factors the summary lists, the largest single actionable item is reclaiming withholding tax, which section 4.5 values at 150 per cent of the manager's fee. It is administered by a custodian, appears in no performance report, is nobody's mandate to improve, and is worth more than most of the decisions the investment committee spends its meetings on. A fund that has never asked its custodian what proportion of reclaimable withholding tax it actually recovers has an unpriced return sitting in a back office.

Finally, a warning about section 4.4. The general form of that result, a fixed allowance meeting a proportional risk, is the reason so much tax planning is available to small portfolios and unavailable to large ones. Advice written for a private client does not scale to an institution, and the reason is arithmetic rather than sophistication.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. Every earlier result this lecture reverses or extends is in the internal environment. The deferral value of 33.63 per cent and the tax-exempt holder's 4.36 per cent bond premium are in objectives, liabilities and constraints; the crossover identity section 4.3 inverts is in vehicles and wrappers; the six-link cost chain and the custodian reclaim worth 150 per cent of the manager's fee are in the business model; and the covenant headroom that section 4.2 needs as the missing half of the financing trade-off is in structure and financial planning. The gearing arithmetic where 800 of debt costs 22.22 per cent of profit on a one-point rate rise is in the macroeconomic environment and the trade cycle, taxation is the third of the nine policies set out in government objectives and the policy toolkit, and the underlying theory of tax incidence is in public economics.

Where this goes next. Lecture 7 begins the regulation arc with the aims of financial regulation, which the summary introduces immediately after taxation, and lectures 8 to 13 cover trust law and fiduciary duty, market conduct, corporate governance, responsible investment, listing and competition authorities, and accounting standards. Section 4.5's closing point about the deferred tax asset is lecture 13's problem: an item carried at face value that this lecture has shown to be an option. Lecture 14 covers market participants and infrastructure, where the withholding tax reclaim of section 4.5 turns out to be an operational question about custody rather than an investment one, and the six indices lectures then close the module.

Where the bank sits opposite. A bank meets section 4.2 from the inside and section 4.5 from the regulator's side. On the first, a bank is the most heavily geared institution in the economy, so the interest deduction is worth more to it than to any of the firms it lends to, and yet its capital structure is set by regulation rather than by the tax shield, which is the clearest available demonstration that the shield is one term among several. Compare the buffers and ratios in capital management, where the binding constraint is a minimum ratio rather than an optimum. On the second, a bank's deferred tax asset is precisely the item section 4.5 says should be discounted rather than carried at face value, and Basel agrees: deferred tax assets that rely on future profitability are deducted from common equity tier 1, which is the regulator reaching the same conclusion this lecture reached and enforcing it. And a bank's own funding decision runs through financial management, where the after-tax cost of debt of section 4.2 is one input to the transfer price charged to every business unit.

Twenty-four claims and counterexamples were encoded as assertions, all twenty-four passed, and none can pass without the computation. One claim failed on the first run and the failure produced the lecture's headline: the assertion that a buy-back beats a dividend under all three systems is false, because under imputation it does not, and the reversal is a stronger result than the uniformity it replaced. One further claim was flagged as unfalsifiable by a vacuity check, comparing two literal constants, and was removed. Every headline finding states the region in which it holds: the payout reversal holds while capital gains tax is below income tax and the two routes tie exactly when the rates are equal, the shield is exact for any cost of debt but worth nothing without taxable profit, and the bed and breakfasting countermeasure binds large holdings and not small ones.
