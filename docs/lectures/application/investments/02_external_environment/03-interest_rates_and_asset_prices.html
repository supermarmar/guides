An equity's duration is exactly one over r minus g, so growth and value are duration buckets

A deep value stock has a duration of 11.1111 years and a high grower 100.0000, a factor of 9.00, and nothing about their businesses entered the calculation. So the summary's growth and value styles are not descriptions of dividend policy: they are interest rate exposures, and a style rotation is a rate trade whether or not anybody intends it to be. Along the way: the same rate rise raises or lowers equity depending only on whether the growth upgrade exceeds it, exactly, and a balanced portfolio's equity contributes 69.20 per cent of its rate sensitivity against the bonds' 22.15.

Step 1

The 30-second version

Lecture 1 enumerated the summary's eleven transmission channels and priced two of them, the project NPV channel and the gearing channel. Lecture 2 found their single origin. This lecture takes the channels that remain and finds that they all reduce to one expression.

Under a dividend discount model a share is worth D / (r - g), so its sensitivity to r is exactly 1 / (r - g). At a 9 per cent discount rate a deep value stock with no growth has a duration of 11.1111 years and a high grower at 8 per cent has 100.0000, a factor of 9.00. On a one-point rise the value stock falls 10.00 per cent and the growth stock 50.00. Nothing about their businesses entered the calculation.

So the summary's growth and value styles, which it presents as descriptions of dividend policy and investor preference, are duration buckets. A rotation between them is an interest rate trade whether or not anybody intends it to be, which is the cleanest explanation of why their relative performance tracks bond yields so closely. It also generalises lecture 1's finding about utilities: any asset whose cash flows sit further away is more rate-sensitive, and that is the only fact needed.

The second result fixes an omission of sign. All the summary's channels run one way, with a higher rate hurting, and a rate rise has a cause. If the cause is stronger growth then g rises too, and the model settles it exactly: the price is exactly unchanged when the growth upgrade equals the rate rise, because r - g is unchanged. So the test is not whether rates rose but whether they rose by more than growth expectations, and a rate rise driven by inflation fear and one driven by a growth upgrade have opposite effects on equity. That is also why bond and equity correlations flip between regimes.

Third, the household channel turns out to be a property of the mortgage market rather than of policy. A one-point rise costs a fully floating borrower 4.00 per cent of a household income of 25 within a quarter, and a fully fixed borrower nothing, ever. So the same policy rate rise is a large consumption shock in one country and almost nothing in another, on a contract convention the summary does not mention.

And the practical payoff. Ranking five assets by duration puts high-growth equity at 100.0000 years, property at an 8 per cent cap rate at 12.5000, deep value equity at 11.1111, a ten-year bond at about 8, and cash at zero. In a 40/50/10 portfolio the equity contributes 69.20 per cent of the total rate sensitivity and the bonds 22.15. A fund that hedges its bond duration and ignores its equity duration has hedged under a quarter of its exposure.

Step 2

Why this matters

This is the lecture that makes a single macroeconomic variable operational across a whole portfolio. Section 4.5's ranking shows that every asset's rate sensitivity is one over something, and the something is always the rate at which its cash flows are discounted net of their growth. That means a fund does not need five separate models of how rates reach five asset classes: it needs one expression applied five times, and the differences between the classes are differences in a single parameter.

The equity duration result matters most because it is routinely ignored. Every institutional fund measures its bond duration and reports it; almost none reports an equity duration, and section 4.5 shows the equity contributing three times as much rate sensitivity as the bonds in a conventional portfolio. That is not a subtle mismeasurement: a fund that believes it has hedged its interest rate exposure by matching bond duration has hedged 22.15 per cent of it, and the unhedged remainder is the part that moves when a central bank surprises.

The sign result in section 4.2 matters for how macroeconomic news is read. An investment committee told that rates are rising has been told nothing about equities until it also knows why. The exact condition, that the growth upgrade must exceed the rate rise, gives a testable question with an observable answer, since both quantities can be read from forecasts and from index-linked bond pricing. It also explains the single most consequential empirical fact about portfolio construction, which is that the bond-equity correlation is not a constant: it is negative in growth-driven regimes and positive in inflation-driven ones, and every diversification calculation in the portfolio construction module depends on which regime holds.

And section 4.3 matters for anyone advising across jurisdictions. The strength of the consumption channel is set by mortgage contract convention, so identical policy produces different outcomes in Britain, the United States and South Africa for reasons that have nothing to do with the central banks. An actuary comparing funding positions or setting economic assumptions across countries needs that in the model rather than as a footnote.

Step 3

The mental model

Every asset is a stream of cash flows divided by a discount rate net of growth. Its rate sensitivity is one over that net figure, and that single sentence covers bonds, equities and property.

A perpetuity paying 1 a year, discounted at 9 per cent, is worth 1 over 0.09, which is 11.11. Raise the rate to 10 per cent and it is worth 10, so it fell about 10 per cent for a 1 point move. Its duration is about 11 years, which is just 1 over 0.09.

Now let the payment grow at 6 per cent a year. It is worth 1 over 0.03, which is 33.33. Raise the rate a point and the denominator goes from 0.03 to 0.04, so the value falls by a quarter. Its duration is 1 over 0.03, about 33 years.

Same asset, same payment, same discount rate. The only thing that changed is the growth, and the rate sensitivity tripled. Growth pushes cash flows into the future, and cash flows in the future are what interest rates act on.

Which tells you something about the words "growth stock" and "value stock". They sound like descriptions of a business. Arithmetically they are descriptions of a duration. When someone rotates from growth to value they are shortening duration, and they will make money if rates rise whether or not their view about businesses was correct.

Then the part that stops this being one-directional. A rate rise usually has a reason. If rates rose because the economy is stronger, growth expectations rise too. And since only the difference between the two matters, a rate rise of 1 point accompanied by a growth upgrade of 1 point leaves the price exactly unchanged. Not approximately: exactly, because the denominator is untouched.

So "rates are rising" is not a view about equities. "Rates are rising faster than growth expectations" is.

Formally, with P = D / (r - g), the elasticity is -dP/P per unit dr = 1 / (r - g), and a joint move gives P_new / P_old = (r - g) / ((r + dr) - (g + dg)), which equals one exactly when dg = dr. A property valued at rent over a capitalisation rate is the same expression with no growth term, so its duration is one over the cap rate.

The detail

4.1 An equity's duration is exactly one over r minus g

The summary lists growth and value as style categories and separately lists the interest rate channel. Connect them at a 9 per cent discount rate.

Four stocks, one formula

   the stock            growth   price at   duration   numerical check   fall on a
                        rate     9%         = 1/(r-g)  of the duration   1pp rise
   a deep value stock      0.00%    11.1111    11.1111           11.0988      10.00%
   a moderate grower       3.00%    16.6667    16.6667           16.6389      14.29%
   a strong grower         6.00%    33.3333    33.3333           33.2226      25.00%
   a high grower           8.00%   100.0000   100.0000           99.0099      50.00%

The analytic duration matches a numerical differentiation to within one per cent, and the residual is the convexity.

A deep value stock has a duration of 11.1111 years and a high grower 100.0000, a factor of 9.00, so on a one-point rise the value stock falls 10.00 per cent and the growth stock 50.00. Nothing about their businesses entered the calculation. The numerical check agrees to within one per cent, and the residual grows with the duration, from 0.11 per cent at zero growth to 0.99 at eight: that is convexity, since the analytic figure is a first derivative and the true response curves away from it exactly as a bond's does.

So the summary's two style categories are duration buckets. A rotation between growth and value is an interest rate trade whether or not anybody intends it to be, which is the cleanest explanation of why the two styles' relative performance tracks bond yields so closely. It also generalises lecture 1's finding about utilities: any asset whose cash flows sit further away is more rate-sensitive, and that is the only fact required.

Where the model stops being usable

   growth rate   r minus g   duration   fall on a 1pp rise   is the model usable?
           0.00%        9.00%    11.1111                10.00%   yes
           6.00%        3.00%    33.3333                25.00%   yes

At a growth rate of 8.90 per cent against a 9.00 per cent discount rate the duration is 1000.0000 years.

The duration diverges as growth approaches the discount rate, reaching 1000.0000 years at a 0.10 point gap, and there the model is telling you nothing except that it has broken. That is not a defect to be patched: it is the honest statement that a perpetual growth rate near the discount rate is not a coherent assumption, and it is why long-duration equity valuations are genuinely uncertain rather than merely volatile. A practitioner using the formula should treat a gap under two points as a warning rather than an input.

4.2 The same rate rise can raise or lower equities, and the test is exact

All the summary's channels run one way, with a higher rate hurting. That is incomplete, because a rate rise has a cause, and if the cause is stronger growth then g rises too. Start from a 4 per cent growth rate and raise rates by a point against four different growth upgrades.

A one-point rate rise against four growth upgrades

   the rate rises by   growth is upgraded by   net price change   which dominates?
                 1.00%                     0.00%           -16.6667%   the rate
                 1.00%                     0.50%            -9.0909%   the rate
                 1.00%                     1.00%             0.0000%   neither, exactly flat
                 1.00%                     1.50%            11.1111%   the growth upgrade

Exactly unchanged when the upgrade equals the rise, because r minus g is untouched.

The price is exactly unchanged when the growth upgrade equals the rate rise, and rises when it exceeds it. So the test is not "did rates rise" but "did rates rise by more than growth expectations", which is an exact condition independent of the level of either. It follows that a rate rise driven by inflation fear and one driven by a growth upgrade have opposite effects on equity, so the summary's channel list is right about the mechanism and silent about the sign, and the sign is what an investor needs.

That is also the cleanest available explanation of why bond and equity correlations flip between regimes. In a growth-driven regime, yields rise as equities rise, so the correlation is negative and bonds diversify equity. In an inflation-driven regime, yields rise as equities fall, so the correlation turns positive and bonds stop diversifying at exactly the moment diversification is wanted.

The penalty for an uncompensated rise, by starting spread

   starting r minus g   duration   price fall if growth is not upgraded at all
                   9.00%    11.1111                                       10.00%
                   5.00%    20.0000                                       16.67%
                   3.00%    33.3333                                       25.00%
                   1.00%   100.0000                                       50.00%

The condition for a gain is identical everywhere. The penalty for missing it is not.

A stock starting with a nine-point spread falls 10.00 per cent on an uncompensated one-point rise and one with a one-point spread falls 50.00, a factor of 5.00. So the condition for a gain is the same everywhere and the penalty for missing it is not, which is why a long-duration market is fragile to rate surprises rather than merely sensitive to them. The same surprise that costs a value market a tenth costs a growth market a half.

4.3 The household channel is set by the mortgage market, not by policy

The summary lists home loan interest payments affecting disposable income as a channel. Its strength depends entirely on something the summary does not mention.

A one-point rise on 100 of mortgage, at four floating shares

   share of mortgages   extra interest on a   as a % of a household   how fast it
   on floating rates    1pp rise, per 100     income of 25           arrives
                      0%                 0.0000                     0.00%   never
                     30%                 0.3000                     1.20%   within a quarter
                     70%                 0.7000                     2.80%   within a quarter
                    100%                 1.0000                     4.00%   within a quarter

A fully fixed market transmits nothing to existing borrowers. A fully floating one transmits 4.00 per cent of household income within a quarter.

A fully fixed mortgage market transmits nothing at all to existing borrowers and a fully floating one transmits the whole rise immediately, at 4.00 per cent of a household income of 25. So the same policy rate rise is a large consumption shock in one country and almost nothing in another, depending on a contract convention rather than on policy.

The consequence is that the channel belongs to the mortgage market rather than to monetary policy, and a central bank in a fixed-rate country must move rates further to achieve the same consumption effect. That is the cleanest available explanation of why identical policy rates produce different outcomes across countries, and it is invisible in the summary's channel list. It also means the channel weakens over time in any country where fixing becomes more common, so a historical estimate of policy potency decays without anything about the central bank changing.

4.4 The opportunity cost channel, and the property cap rate

The summary lists "the opportunity cost of cash or debt against other assets" and leaves it abstract. The concrete version is a property yield, which is a spread over the risk-free rate, so a rate rise reprices property directly.

Rent of 5 at a constant two-point spread

   risk-free rate   property spread   the cap rate   the value of 5 of   change from
                                                     rent               the base
               6.00%               2.00%            8.00%              62.5000        25.00%
               8.00%               2.00%           10.00%              50.0000         0.00%
              10.00%               2.00%           12.00%              41.6667       -16.67%
              12.00%               2.00%           14.00%              35.7143       -28.57%

A two-point rise cuts the value by exactly a sixth and a two-point fall adds a quarter. The effect is convex.

A two-point rise in the risk-free rate cuts the property value by exactly a sixth, 16.67 per cent, and a two-point fall adds 25.00. The effect is asymmetric because the value is a reciprocal of the cap rate, so it is convex in rates exactly as a bond's price is, and the asymmetry favours the holder.

And the property's implied duration is one over the cap rate: 12.5000 years at an 8 per cent cap rate and 7.1429 at 14. So low-yielding property is long-duration property, and those are the same statement rather than two facts. That is section 4.1's result with rent in place of dividends and no growth term, which is why the summary's abstract "opportunity cost" channel needs no separate theory.

The equity contributes 69.20 per cent of the portfolio's rate sensitivity and the bonds 22.15, despite the bonds being the holding everyone measures. A fund that hedges its bond duration and ignores its equity duration has hedged 22.15 per cent of its exposure, and the total duration of 14.4500 years is nearly twice the bond duration it reports.

That is the practical payoff of section 4.1. Equity duration is not a metaphor: it is computable from the same dividend discount model every analyst already uses, requires no new data, and leaving it out of a rate exposure calculation understates the total by a factor of more than four on the bonds alone. The liability-hedging arithmetic in the portfolio management module assumed the liability side was the long-duration one; on these figures a growth-tilted asset portfolio can be longer than the liabilities it is meant to match, which reverses the direction of the required hedge.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

Three things that follow from taking equity duration seriously.

Report one portfolio duration, not a bond duration. Section 4.5's arithmetic is not difficult and its absence from standard reporting is a genuine gap rather than a simplification. The calculation needs a dividend yield and a growth assumption for each equity holding, both of which every fund already produces for other purposes, and it produces a single number comparable with the liability duration. A fund that reports a bond duration of 8 and a liability duration of 15 believes it is under-hedged; on these figures its true asset duration is 14.45 and it is nearly matched. Those are opposite conclusions from the same holdings, and only one of them is measured.

Treat style exposure and rate exposure as the same position, because section 4.1 says they are. A manager with a growth tilt has a long-duration position whether the mandate mentions rates or not, and a fund running both a growth-tilted equity manager and a long bond portfolio has concentrated rate risk in two places while believing it has diversified across asset classes. The remedy is not to prohibit style tilts but to include them in the rate exposure calculation, and the awkward consequence is that a fund which likes a growth manager and dislikes rate risk must hedge, which requires an instrument the equity mandate does not contemplate.

Ask why rates moved before acting, because section 4.2 makes the sign depend on it. The condition is exact and both sides of it are observable: the rate change from the curve, the growth revision from consensus forecasts or from the breakeven inflation rate. A committee that can state which of the two moved more has a view; one that can only state that rates rose has an observation. And the regime-dependence of the bond-equity correlation follows directly, which means the correlation input to every optimisation in the portfolio construction module is conditional on something knowable rather than being a historical constant.

On the summary, one observation is recorded and it is of connection. It lists growth and value as style categories in the trade cycle section and lists the interest rate channel in the section before, without noting that the first is a consequence of the second: an equity's duration is exactly one over the discount rate net of growth, so the style split is a duration split. That is not an error in either list. It is the single most useful link between them, and supplying it converts two descriptive taxonomies into one calculation.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The eleven channels are enumerated in the macroeconomic environment and the trade cycle, which priced the NPV and gearing ones, and their single origin is in central banks and monetary policy. The dividend discount model is built in the dividend discount model, duration itself in fixed income pricing, and the property capitalisation rate in property and infrastructure. The growth and value styles are set out in active equity styles, the liability matching that section 4.5 disturbs is in bond passive management and LDI, and the correlation input that section 4.2 makes regime-dependent is in portfolio risk and return. The exchange rate channel is priced in currency hedging.

Where this goes next. Lecture 4 develops the sector taxonomy this lecture has now given an arithmetic basis, since a sector's cyclicality and its duration are separate properties that the summary's classification conflates. Lecture 5 turns to government policy and picks up the current and financial accounts, the two channels of the eleven that neither this lecture nor lecture 1 addressed. Lecture 6 covers taxation, and section 4.1's result matters there too, since a growth stock defers its return into capital gains while a value stock delivers it as taxable income, so the duration split is also a tax split.

Where the bank sits opposite. A bank's own balance sheet is the extreme case of section 4.5's arithmetic, since its assets and liabilities are both interest-bearing and their duration mismatch is its principal exposure, measured as economic value of equity in interest rate risk in the banking book. The prime rate channel this lecture inherited from lecture 1 is the bank's pricing decision rather than something done to it, set out in bank financial management. And section 4.3's mortgage structure question is a bank's own product design choice with a direct consequence for its rate risk: a bank writing fixed-rate mortgages funded by floating deposits holds the mismatch the borrower avoided, which is why the fixed-rate convention differs by country according to how the mismatch can be laid off, and that hedging market is priced in using swaps.
