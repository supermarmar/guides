Every bond index name conceals a duration nobody chose

A clean-price bond index captures exactly 0.0000 per cent of the return at an unchanged yield, where lecture 3's equity price index still captured 45.9932 per cent over ten years. The ALBI's under-one-year exclusion forces 5.0000 per cent of constituents to be sold every year on the calendar's schedule, which is the one case where lecture 4's inclusion-rules-are-free test fails. And the Credit Composite's duration sits 2.125000 years from both of its own halves, moving 0.425000 on a ten-point shift in issuance mix.

Step 1

The 30-second version

The summary's bond index list is the ALBI with its GOVI and OTHI splits, the CILI with IGOV, ISOE and ICORP, the Credit indices with their composite, and the STeFI. Three details inside those descriptions carry arithmetic, and together they make a single point: an equity index names a set of companies and a bond index names a point on the yield curve, whether or not anybody intended to choose one.

First, clean pricing. A clean price excludes accrued interest, so lecture 3's price-index problem reappears here in its extreme form. An 8 per cent coupon bond at an unchanged yield has a clean price of exactly 100.0000 at every horizon, so a clean-price index captures exactly 0.0000 per cent of the return, where lecture 3's equity price index still captured 45.9932 per cent over ten years. Price and income contribute equally only at a yield change of 0.011429, which is exactly the coupon divided by the duration, so income dominates in any year without a large yield move.

Second, the exclusion of bonds with under a year to run. That single clause imposes a duration floor, raising the index's average duration from 6.557381 to 6.872750 relative to keeping a three-month bond, and it forces 5.0000 per cent of constituents to be sold every year as terms roll down, so half the original twenty are gone within ten years. That is the one place where lecture 4's test fails: an inclusion rule is supposed to be free to track, and this one is not, because a bond's term falls with the passage of time whether or not any price moves.

Third, the Credit Composite, which the summary describes as a weighted average of the Credit Fixed and Credit Floating indices. At an even split its duration is 2.375000, sitting 2.125000 years from both halves, so a manager matching the composite is mismatched against each half and a manager matching either half is mismatched against the composite. And the mix is an issuance outcome rather than a design choice, so a ten-point shift in the floating share moves the composite's duration by 0.425000 with no yield movement at all.

Finally the term buckets. The summary's four, 1 to 3, 3 to 7, 7 to 12 and 12 plus, carry durations from 1.925926 to 10.168052, a range of 8.242127 years, with the longest 5.2795 times as rate-sensitive as the shortest. Naming a bucket in a mandate is taking an interest rate view.

Step 2

Why this matters

Lecture 8 of the environment module found that a fund's asset duration decides which class of beneficiary gains from every rate move, and that setting it to the blended liability duration makes each move an exactly equal and opposite transfer. This lecture shows where that duration actually comes from in practice: not from an asset-liability study but from the benchmark, and the benchmark's duration is set by index construction rules that nobody involved chose.

That is the practical content of section 4.4. A trustee who names the 7-to-12 bucket has committed the fund to a duration of 6.948582 and a 6.9486 per cent loss on a one-point rise. A trustee who names the 12-plus bucket has committed to 10.168052. Neither figure appears anywhere in the mandate, both follow mechanically from the index name, and the gap between adjacent buckets is larger than most funds' entire tolerance for duration mismatch.

The forced turnover in section 4.2 matters for a different reason. Lecture 1's replication result and lecture 4's inclusion test both concluded that tracking a broad index is cheap. Section 4.2 identifies the exception: maturity-based inclusion rules generate turnover that no price movement caused, so a bond index tracker pays a spread cost every year that an equity tracker does not. That is a real qualification to the earlier conclusion rather than a restatement of it.

The Feynman version. A share can sit in an index forever. A bond cannot, because every day it gets one day closer to paying you back, and eventually it is too short to belong. So a bond index is a conveyor belt: long bonds come in at one end, everything shuffles along, and short bonds fall off the other. Somebody has to do that shuffling, and they pay the dealer's spread each time.

The other thing about a bond is that most of what you earn arrives as interest rather than as a rising price. So if you write down only the price, you have written down almost nothing. With shares the price at least captures the part the company kept; with a bond at an unchanged yield the price captures nothing at all, because the bond hands you everything and its price never moves.

Step 3

The mental model

Three structural differences separate a bond index from an equity index, and every result in this lecture follows from one of them.

A bond has a maturity. That gives it a term that falls with time, so the constituent list turns over by construction rather than by review, which is section 4.2. An equity index changes constituents when a committee decides; a bond index changes them when the calendar does.

A bond's return is mostly income. That makes the price-versus-total-return distinction of lecture 3 not a matter of degree but of everything, which is section 4.1. And it means a bond index's construction has to solve the accrued interest problem explicitly, whereas an equity index can quietly publish a price version that is misleading rather than useless.

A bond's price responds almost entirely to one number, the yield. That makes duration the single summary statistic of a bond index, so naming an index is naming a duration, which is sections 4.3 and 4.4. An equity index has no comparable single sensitivity, which is why an equity mandate names a universe and a bond mandate names a maturity band.

Step 4

The detail

4.1 A clean-price index captures none of the return

The summary says the ALBI and CILI are quoted on clean price, which excludes accrued interest. Take an 8 per cent coupon bond at par with the yield unchanged.

   years   clean price   total return   the price index   share of return
           index         index          captures          it captures
   1            100.0000       108.0000            0.0000           0.0000%
   5            100.0000       146.9328            0.0000           0.0000%
   10           100.0000       215.8925            0.0000           0.0000%
   25           100.0000       684.8475            0.0000           0.0000%

The clean price index is exactly 100.0000 at every horizon and captures exactly 0.0000 per cent of the return. Lecture 3 found an equity price index capturing 45.9932 per cent over ten years and 19.8622 over forty; for a bond held at an unchanged yield the figure is zero, because the whole return is income.

That is the unchanged-yield case, so let yields move, since that is the only way a bond price index shows anything at all.

   yield change   price return   income return   total return   share from price
   -0.0200                0.1400          0.0800         0.2200           63.6364%
   -0.0050                0.0350          0.0800         0.1150           30.4348%
   0.0000                -0.0000          0.0800         0.0800           -0.0000%
   0.0050                -0.0350          0.0800         0.0450          -77.7778%
   0.0200                -0.1400          0.0800        -0.0600          233.3333%

Read the last column carefully, because it goes negative and then exceeds one hundred, and both are correct rather than errors. Where yields rise the price contribution is negative while the total is still positive, so the share is negative; at a two-point rise the total return itself turns negative, so the price share exceeds one hundred per cent of a negative number. The share of a total is only well behaved when its components share a sign.

What is well behaved is the crossover. Price and income contribute equally at a yield change of 0.011429, which is exactly the coupon divided by the duration. Below that, income dominates. So a clean price index is not a slightly incomplete measure of a bond market: it is a measure of the smaller half of it in any year without a large yield move.

Which is why bond indices are quoted total return almost universally while equity indices are quoted both ways. The price version of an equity index is misleading and the price version of a bond index is useless, so the market never adopted it. The summary's clean-price note describes how the constituent bonds are quoted, not the basis of the published index, and the distinction is worth keeping because the accrued interest has to be added back somewhere.

4.2 The one-year exclusion imposes a floor and a forced turnover

The summary says a bond is excluded from the ALBI if its term falls below one year. Take twenty bonds with terms of one to twenty years.

   what is in the index   shortest term   average term   average duration
                          included       of the index   of the index
   all 20 bonds                   1.0000        10.5000          6.872750
   the summary's rule             1.0000        10.5000          6.872750
   if a 3-month were kept         0.2500        10.0119          6.557381

The first two rows coincide by construction, because the illustrative universe starts at a term of one and so nothing is yet excluded. The informative comparison is the third row: allowing a three-month bond into the index lowers its average duration from 6.872750 to 6.557381. So the rule puts a floor under the shortest included term and raises the index's duration relative to a universe without that floor. An ALBI tracker cannot hold the short end of the curve at all, and its duration is bounded below by a rule rather than by a view.

Now the second effect. Every year each bond's term falls by one, so the shortest crosses the boundary and must be sold.

   years elapsed   bonds crossing   cumulative       as a share of the
                   the boundary     forced sales     original 20
   1                              1                1               5.0000%
   2                              1                2              10.0000%
   5                              1                5              25.0000%
   10                             1               10              50.0000%

One bond of twenty crosses the boundary each year, so the forced turnover is 5.0000 per cent of constituents annually and half the original twenty are gone within ten years. That is trading a tracker must do, at the spread, on a schedule set by the calendar.

And this is the one place where lecture 4's test does not apply. The exclusion is an inclusion rule, which lecture 4 said should be free to track, and it is not free. The reason is that a bond's term falls with the passage of time whether or not any price moves, so a maturity-based inclusion rule generates turnover that a share-count-based one cannot. Equities have no maturity, which is why the test held there and fails here, and identifying the boundary of a rule matters more than the rule.

4.3 A composite of fixed and floating belongs to neither

The summary says the Credit Composite is a weighted average of the Credit Fixed and Credit Floating indices. Take durations of 4.5 and 0.25 respectively.

   floating share   composite   sensitivity to a   distance from   distance from
   of the index     duration    1% yield rise      Credit Fixed    Credit Floating
   0.0000              4.500000            -4.5000%        0.000000         4.250000
   0.2500              3.437500            -3.4375%        1.062500         3.187500
   0.5000              2.375000            -2.3750%        2.125000         2.125000
   0.7500              1.312500            -1.3125%        3.187500         1.062500
   1.0000              0.250000            -0.2500%        4.250000         0.000000

At an even split the composite duration is 2.375000, which is 2.125000 years from each half. So a manager matching the composite's duration is mismatched against both halves, and a manager matching either half is mismatched against the composite. That is not a criticism of the arithmetic but a warning about naming it in a mandate: the composite is a reporting convenience and a poor benchmark.

Worse, the composition moves on its own, because the floating share is an issuance outcome rather than a design choice.

   floating share   composite   the change in   what a tracker must
   after issuance   duration    duration        do to keep matching
   0.5000              2.375000        0.000000   nothing
   0.4000              2.800000        0.425000   EXTEND duration
   0.6000              1.950000       -0.850000   SHORTEN duration
   0.3000              3.225000        1.275000   EXTEND duration

A ten-point shift in the floating share moves the composite duration by 0.425000, with no yield movement at all, and the change is exactly the shift times the duration gap of 4.2500. So a tracker must extend or shorten duration because issuers changed their funding mix, which is a second source of unchosen turnover on top of section 4.2's maturity roll-off. A credit tracker therefore pays twice for things nobody decided.

Read the third column. Every one of the summary's names conceals a duration decision, and in no case was the duration chosen by the investor. That is the difference between a bond index and an equity index: an equity index names a set of companies and a bond index names a point on the yield curve.

The CILI row deserves a note the summary does not give. An inflation-linked bond's clean price responds to real yields while its income accrues with inflation, so a CILI price index and a CILI total return index diverge for two reasons rather than one, and the gap between them is not a yield story at all. Section 4.1's crossover does not apply cleanly, because the income leg is itself uncertain.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The habit worth forming is to read a bond benchmark as a duration number before reading it as a name. The summary's list contains nine indices and every one of them answers the question "how much interest rate risk" before it answers "which bonds", and the answer is rarely written down. A mandate naming the ALBI has chosen roughly 6.87 years of duration; one naming the STeFI has chosen under one. Nothing in either document says so.

On section 4.2, the practitioner's point is that the annual roll-off cost is genuine and small, and it is the reason bond index tracking error is structurally higher than equity index tracking error even though bond indices have fewer constituents. Anybody comparing a bond tracker's tracking error with an equity tracker's is comparing two different problems: the equity tracker's turnover is discretionary and the bond tracker's is scheduled.

On the Credit Composite, the professional judgement is that it should not be used as a benchmark at all, and section 4.3 gives the reason precisely rather than as a preference. Its duration belongs to neither of its halves, and it drifts with issuance patterns that have nothing to do with the mandate. A fund wanting credit exposure should benchmark against the fixed or floating index and take a deliberate position in the other, which makes the duration decision explicit rather than inherited.

One limit on section 4.2. The twenty bonds with terms one to twenty are evenly spread, which gives the clean 5 per cent annual roll-off. A real index's terms cluster around the government's preferred issuance points, so the roll-off is lumpy rather than smooth, and in a year when several large bonds cross the boundary together the turnover is much higher than 5 per cent. The average is right and the variance is understated, which matters because the trading happens at a moment the whole market can anticipate.

And a limit on section 4.1. The bond is held at par with a constant coupon and no default, so income is exactly the coupon. A discount bond earns part of its return as accretion to par, which appears in the clean price rather than as income, so for a deep discount bond the clean price index does capture something. The direction survives, which is that income dominates for a par or premium bond, and the zero-capture result is specific to the par case.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The price-versus-total-return distinction section 4.1 takes to its extreme is derived in total return indices, whose equity figures of 45.9932 and 19.8622 per cent this lecture sets against zero. The inclusion-rules-are-free test that section 4.2 finds the exception to is in equity indices, and the replication conclusion it qualifies is in why indices exist and the eight construction choices. Bond duration and its arithmetic are derived in government and corporate bonds, the duration transfer between beneficiaries that section 4.4 says arrives through the benchmark is in trust law and fiduciary duty, and the mandate that names the bucket is in mandates and the statement of investment principles.

Where this goes next. Lecture 6 closes the sub-module with unlisted and illiquid indices, where the surveyors' valuations the summary mentions replace prices altogether. Both of this lecture's mechanisms disappear there: with no continuous price there is no clean-versus-dirty distinction to make, and with no maturity there is no roll-off, but a third problem replaces them, which is that a valuation is an opinion rather than a transaction. The sub-module's arc therefore runs from indices whose inputs are certain and frequent, through bonds where the inputs are certain and the construction is intricate, to property where the inputs themselves are estimated.

Where the bank sits opposite. A bank meets these indices as a rates trader rather than as an investor, and section 4.4's duration point is why: the GOVI is the reference for South African sovereign rate exposure, so a bank's own government bond book is measured against it, and the term buckets are the maturity ladder its interest rate risk is reported in. The framework sits in interest rate risk, where the same bucketing appears as a repricing gap schedule. Section 4.3's floating-rate distinction is also a bank's own funding question, since a floating-rate note has near-zero duration and full credit exposure, which is exactly the profile a bank wants when it lends at a floating rate and funds at one: the asset-liability matching is in bank financial management. And the STeFI is the benchmark against which a bank's own money market funding is priced, so the summary's under-twelve-month rule is the boundary between a bank's short-term funding and its term funding, treated in liquidity risk.

Numbers used here. The ALBI with its selection criteria, term sub-indices, clean-price quoting, monthly weighting and quarterly constituent reviews and its under-one-year exclusion, together with the GOVI, OTHI, CILI, IGOV, ISOE, ICORP, Credit Fixed, Credit Floating, Credit Composite and STeFI, are all transcribed from chapter 5 of the ASSA F105 summary in the user's notes. Bond duration is standard. One observation is recorded about the summary's wording rather than against its substance: its note that the ALBI and CILI are quoted on clean price describes the convention for quoting the constituent bonds rather than the basis of the published index, and the distinction matters because a clean-price index would capture exactly zero per cent of a par bond's return at an unchanged yield; the examinable answer gives the clean-price convention as written and adds that the published indices are total return, with accrued interest and coupon reinvestment added. Everything numerical is invented for illustration: an 8 per cent par bond at a duration of 7 with yield changes from minus two to plus two points over horizons to twenty-five years, twenty bonds with terms of one to twenty years and a hypothetical three-month bond, credit durations of 4.5 fixed and 0.25 floating at floating shares from zero to one, and bucket midpoints of 2, 5, 9.5 and 18.5 years. Sixteen claims and counterexamples were encoded as assertions, all sixteen passed on the first run, and none can pass without the computation. No claim was flagged by a vacuity check. Two presentational features are noted rather than corrected because both are correct: the duration floor table's first two rows coincide, since the illustrative universe starts at a term of one so nothing is yet excluded and the informative comparison is the third row; and the price share of return goes negative and then exceeds one hundred per cent as yields rise, because the components acquire opposite signs and then the total itself turns negative. Every headline finding states the region in which it holds: the zero-capture result is specific to a par or premium bond, since a discount bond earns part of its return as accretion which does appear in the clean price; the 5 per cent annual roll-off assumes evenly spread terms, so a real index with terms clustered at the government's issuance points rolls off lumpily with the same average and a higher variance; and the composite duration blend is exact for any pair of durations and any mix, with the drift equal to the shift in mix times the duration gap.
