Investments, external environment

Indices

Six lectures on how a market gets summarised into one number, and what that number quietly assumes. An index looks like an observation of the market but is a construction with at least eight design choices baked into it, and a manager benchmarked against one inherits every choice. The arithmetic in lectures 2 and 3 is where the exam concentrates, because it is the part that cannot be waffled.

How to use this

Lectures 1 to 3 are cumulative and build the machinery. Lectures 4 to 6 are catalogues of real index families and can be read as reference. Status badges show what is built (built), planned, or a gap.

Course sequence

How an index is made

  • builtLecture 1: why indices exist and the eight construction choices. A broad index turns out to be easy to replicate, and for an exact reason: weight falls as one over rank while spread rises only as the square root, so the hundredth stock’s spread is exactly ten times the largest’s and its weighted cost contribution exactly a tenth. On spread costs alone full replication wins at 0.1433 per cent a year against 1.4754 for the top ten, which contradicts the usual intuition and shows sampling must be driven by a cost that does not shrink with the position. Adding a per-holding administrative charge makes the optimum interior: a hundred-million fund replicates fully at 2,000 a year per line and holds twenty at 40,000, covering 69.3557 per cent, so sampling is a fund-size decision rather than an index-breadth one. The summary calls an unweighted index unsuitable for the second-best reason: the decisive objection is that non-cap weights cannot be maintained without trading, at a drag exactly proportional to cross-sectional dispersion and worth 0.1149 per cent a year at 8 per cent monthly, while a cap-weighted holding left alone tracks its index to fifteen decimal places. The same argument sets the hurdle for every alternative weighting. And the five purposes are not five uses of one index: two want maximum coverage and two actively do not, so a market needs a measurement index and an investable one
  • builtLecture 2: index arithmetic and chain-linking. A geometric index falls 13.3975 per cent in a market where the average stock did not move: one stock up 50 per cent and one down 50 leaves the arithmetic index at exactly 100.000000 and takes the geometric to 86.602540, the square root of three quarters. The gap is half the variance of the price relatives, second order and confirmed at 1.000025 times exact at a 1 per cent spread, so the bias grows with volatility and compounds: at a constant 20 per cent annual spread the geometric index reaches 60.0331 after twenty-five years while the arithmetic index is flat at 100.0000 throughout, making the entire 39.9669 per cent shortfall construction bias. A separate and decisive objection is that no portfolio earns a cross-sectional geometric mean, so the construction is not the performance of anything holdable. On the divisor: a rights issue at the market price makes the index jump from 100.000000 to 115.625000 if the divisor is left alone, a spurious 15.6250 per cent exactly equal to the rise in market capitalisation, while rebasing to 37.0000 holds the level and leaves a subsequent 10 per cent price rise moving it by exactly 10 per cent. So the divisor is a running record of every corporate action absorbed, and an index level is not reconstructible from prices alone. One of the six chain-link triggers is the signal and five are noise, with the rights issue hardest because it moves the share count and the price together, the loss on old shares exactly cancelling the gain on new
  • builtLecture 3: total return indices. The bigger finding is not the level but the composition. Four companies with an identical 9 per cent total return all reach 560.4411 over twenty years while their price indices range from 560.4411 down to exactly 100.0000 for the one that pays everything out, so a price index ranks identical investments strictly by payout policy. Applied to sectors that becomes an attribution error: on identical 9 per cent returns, technology takes 49.4449 per cent of the market’s price-index gain and utilities 9.6930, a ratio of 5.1011 to one, and because the bias is monotone in yield it never averages out. On the level, a price index captures 45.9932 per cent of total return over ten years and 19.8622 over forty, with the ratio between the two indices exactly the compounded yield ratio. The tax basis does something no other factor does: it makes the index investor-specific. Four investors in the same market reach levels from 862.3081 to 653.8363, a 24.1760 per cent spread, so a gross index is right for one of them and a net index at any rate for one, and none is right for the market. A manager taxed at 20 per cent against a gross benchmark starts 80.0000 basis points behind annually, 3.9365 per cent over five years, which can exceed the whole active fee. The reinvestment convention adds 3.2210 basis points of tracking difference over a sixty-day settlement gap, signed by market direction. So the two deferred factors generate three indices rather than adjusting one

The real families

  • builtLecture 4: equity indices. The summary lists SWIX and RAFI as parallel specialist indices and they are structurally different in a decisive way: SWIX adjusts the share count, so a SWIX holding drifts to exactly its new target weights after prices move to fifteen decimal places and needs no trade, while RAFI replaces the weighting rule and drifts away from targets prices never touch, at 0.052024 of one-way turnover after a single period. So an adjustment to the share count inherits cap weighting’s self-rebalancing property and an adjustment to the rule destroys it, which is the general test for any index innovation: free-float, SWIX and foreign-exclusion screens are free to track, and fundamental, equal and risk weighting are not. The summary also calls the Dow unweighted arithmetic; it is price weighted, a third construction, and a two-for-one split cuts a constituent’s price weight from 0.625000 to 0.454545 while leaving every cap weight unchanged, which is a sharper reason for the summary’s own verdict of unsuitable and one no divisor can fix. Its stated coverage figures over-determine a power law: fitting the FTSE 100’s 80 per cent needs an exponent of 1.090298, which gives the top 350 only 94.3122 against a stated 99, so the real tail is lighter than any fit to the top and lecture 1’s replication conclusion holds with room to spare. The tiers turn out to be lecture 1’s five purposes made concrete rather than a size taxonomy
  • builtLecture 5: bond and money market indices. Every bond index name conceals a duration nobody chose, which is the structural difference from an equity index: one names a set of companies and the other names a point on the yield curve. A clean-price index captures exactly 0.0000 per cent of a par bond’s return at an unchanged yield, against lecture 3’s 45.9932 per cent for equities over ten years, and price and income contribute equally only at a yield change equal to the coupon divided by the duration, 0.011429 here. The ALBI’s under-one-year exclusion imposes a duration floor, 6.872750 against 6.557381 if a three-month bond were kept, and forces 5.0000 per cent of constituents to be sold annually, half the original twenty within ten years. That is the one case where lecture 4’s inclusion-rules-are-free test fails, because a bond’s term falls with time whether or not any price moves, and equities have no maturity. The Credit Composite’s duration sits 2.125000 years from each of its own halves at an even split, so it benchmarks neither, and it drifts 0.425000 on a ten-point issuance shift with no yield movement, giving a credit tracker two sources of unchosen turnover. The four term buckets span 8.242127 years of duration, the longest 5.2795 times as rate-sensitive as the shortest, so naming a bucket is taking a rate view
  • builtLecture 6: unlisted and illiquid indices. The summary’s one word, subjectivity, is a systematic halving of a reported risk number: a valuer anchoring 60 per cent on the previous valuation reports 9.0000 per cent volatility against a true 18.0000, and the ratio is 0.500000 at every level of true volatility, so it is a property of the filter rather than the asset. A two-hundred-thousand-period simulation confirms the closed form at 0.498925 with an autocorrelation of 0.597702 against the predicted 0.6, which is both the signature of the smoothing and the recipe for undoing it. Fed to an optimiser the understated risk lifts the property weight from 0.3864 to 0.8254, and the investor believes a Sharpe ratio of 0.477552 for a portfolio delivering 0.267645, so smoothing costs twice, in the allocation and in the false confidence. Cost is not a separate problem but an amplifier: a triennial valuation cycle shows 0.1302 of the truth against a monthly cycle’s all of it, so the summary’s three words are one mechanism. Circularity differs in kind, delaying rather than destroying: at an echo share of 0.6 a 20 per cent shock shows 0.080000 at once and 0.184448 after five rounds, arriving in full only in the limit, which is why an unlisted index is still falling after the market has stopped and mechanical rebalancing sells into a recovery. Four of the five mechanisms this sub-module built fail outright here, so a valuation index is a different object rather than a price index with noise added
