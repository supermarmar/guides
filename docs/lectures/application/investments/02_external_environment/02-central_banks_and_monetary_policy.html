Step 1

The 30-second version

Lecture 1 found that every one of the summary's eleven transmission channels begins with a single policy rate. This lecture is about who sets it and with what. The summary lists seven tools across three headings, and two of its statements carry exact arithmetic it does not draw.

The first is forward guidance. The summary says it influences long bond yields "by expectation theory", and expectation theory makes an n-year yield the average of expected one-year rates. So guidance that covers the first two years moves a two-year yield by the full 1.0000 per cent, a ten-year yield by 0.2000 per cent, exactly one fifth, and a thirty-year yield by 0.0667 per cent, exactly one fifteenth. The dilution is precisely the ratio of the guided horizon to the bond's maturity, which is an identity and not an estimate. So the summary's claim is true and nearly empty at the long end: moving a thirty-year yield by a point requires guiding credibly for thirty years, which no central bank can do and none claims to.

The second is the fractional reserve system, which the summary describes in one clause and a bank run in the next without connecting them. A bank holding reserves of r creates money by a multiplier of exactly 1/r and can pay withdrawals from exactly r of its depositors. At a 2 per cent ratio the multiplier is 50.0000 and 2 per cent of depositors can be paid; at 100 per cent the multiplier is 1.0000 and everyone can. They are one fact with two names: raising the ratio to make the bank safe destroys the money creation that makes banking worth doing, which is why no regulator chooses 100 per cent and every regulator is uncomfortable with 2.

The reserve ratio is also a blunter instrument than the summary's presentation suggests. Moving it from 10 to 8 per cent adds 250.0000 of money supply on a 100 base, and moving it to 12 removes only 166.6667. The multiplier is convex in the ratio, so equal policy steps have unequal effects and the effect is largest where the ratio is already lowest. That is why reserve ratios are a crisis tool and the repo rate is the routine one.

Third, an interest rate ceiling does more than the summary says. It describes a ceiling as restricting the ability to compete for deposits, which is right and understated: a ceiling at 2 per cent against an 8 per cent market costs the depositor 6.0000 per cent a year and the money leaves for whatever is not capped. So a control intended to reduce bank lending relocates it, and the tool is dormant in easy conditions and binding in tight ones, which is exactly when a bank most needs to retain deposits.

Finally quantitative easing, which is the exact complement of forward guidance. Buying 50 of twenty-five-year bonds moves yields 0.5000 per cent and buying 50 of two-year bonds moves them 0.0400, a factor of 12.5 on the same money. Guidance is diluted at the long end and QE is amplified there, so the two tools are strongest at opposite ends of the curve and a central bank needs both.

Step 2

Why this matters

Lecture 1 reduced a macroeconomic view, for an investor, to a view on one number and its path. This lecture is about what determines that path, so it is where a macro view either becomes actionable or is revealed as untestable. The forward guidance result in section 4.1 is the sharpest instance: an investor who hears a central bank commit to low rates for two years now knows exactly what that implies for a ten-year bond, namely one fifth of the commitment, and can compare it with what the market has priced. That is a testable statement rather than a directional feeling.

It matters for liability-driven investors in particular. A pension fund's discount rate sits at the long end of the curve, and section 4.1 shows that the tool central banks talk about most, guidance, barely reaches it, while the tool they talk about least in normal times, asset purchases, reaches it hardest. So a scheme's funding level is far more exposed to the size of a central bank's balance sheet than to its rate rhetoric, which reverses the usual attention.

The reserve arithmetic in section 4.2 matters because it is the cleanest available statement of what a bank is. Every later banking lecture rests on the observation that a bank cannot pay all its depositors and is not supposed to be able to, and the multiplier identity makes that quantitative rather than rhetorical. It also explains why bank regulation is structurally difficult: the quantity a regulator would raise to reduce fragility is the same quantity that produces the credit the economy runs on.

And section 4.3's result on ceilings is a general lesson about price controls in finance dressed in a specific example. A cap on a price where the underlying can move elsewhere does not reduce the quantity, it relocates it, and the relocation is usually to a less supervised place. That is the mechanism behind shadow banking, behind money market funds in the 1970s, and behind every subsequent instance of a prudential rule producing an unregulated substitute.

Step 3

The mental model

A central bank has one instrument that works precisely and reaches nowhere, and one that reaches everywhere and works bluntly. Everything between them is a compromise between those two properties.

A ten-year interest rate is not a thing anyone sets. It is what you get if you average what people expect the one-year rate to be, over the next ten years. That single observation does most of the work here.

Now suppose a central bank says: we promise to keep rates low for the next two years. How much does the ten-year rate move? Two of the ten years have changed and eight have not, so the average moves by two tenths of the promise. Promise 1 per cent, move the ten-year by 0.2. Promise the same for a thirty-year bond and you move it by 2/30, which is almost nothing.

So talking cannot reach the long end. To move a thirty-year rate by talking, you would have to make a credible promise about thirty years, and nobody who will not be in the job can make one.

Which is why they buy bonds instead. Buying a bond takes it out of the market, and taking a long bond out removes far more interest rate risk than taking a short one out, because the long one carries more. So the same money spent at the long end moves yields much further. Talking is weak where buying is strong, exactly.

Then the other half of this lecture, which is about banks rather than markets. A bank keeps a small fraction of its deposits as cash and lends the rest. That is what lets a deposit become a loan become another deposit, and the money in the system ends up being your deposits divided by that fraction. Keep 10 per cent and there is ten times as much money as cash.

But notice what the same fraction means from the other direction. If 10 per cent of your depositors ask for their money, you can pay them. If 11 per cent ask, you cannot. The number that makes banking useful is the number that makes it fragile, and they are the same number. Nobody has ever found a way round that, which is why the whole apparatus of deposit insurance and lender-of-last-resort exists.

Formally, expectations theory gives the n-year yield as (1/n) x sum of expected one-year rates, so guidance shifting the first g of those by s moves the yield by s x min(g, n) / n. And in a fractional reserve system with ratio r, the money multiplier is 1/r and the fraction of deposits payable on demand is r, so the product of the two is exactly one whatever r is.

The detail

4.1 Forward guidance is diluted by exactly the ratio of the horizons

The summary says forward guidance influences long bond yields "by expectation theory" and stops. Expectation theory makes a long yield the average of expected short rates, and an average dilutes any change covering only part of the term. Take guidance shifting expected short rates by 100 basis points for two years.

Two years of guidance across five maturities

   guidance covers   the yield it moves   moved by   as a % of the   the dilution
   the next           (years)                         guidance        factor
   2 years                            1.0    1.0000%           100.00%          1.0000
   2 years                            2.0    1.0000%           100.00%          1.0000
   2 years                            5.0    0.4000%            40.00%          0.4000
   2 years                           10.0    0.2000%            20.00%          0.2000
   2 years                           30.0    0.0667%             6.67%          0.0667

The dilution factor is exactly the guided horizon divided by the maturity: 2/5, 2/10 and 2/30.

The ten-year yield moves 0.2000 per cent, exactly one fifth, because two of ten years are covered. The thirty-year moves 0.0667, exactly one fifteenth. The dilution is precisely the ratio of the guided horizon to the bond's maturity, which is an identity rather than an estimate and therefore does not need calibrating.

So the summary's claim is true and nearly empty at the long end. To move a thirty-year yield by a point, the central bank must guide credibly for thirty years, which no central bank can do and none claims to. The examinable position is the summary's, that guidance influences long rates through expectations; the complete answer supplies the dilution.

What it takes to move a ten-year yield

   guidance covers   move in the   as a % of the   what the bank must promise
   (years)           10-year       guidance
                 0.5        0.0500%             5.00%   a meeting or two
                 2.0        0.2000%            20.00%   a policy cycle
                 5.0        0.5000%            50.00%   beyond any mandate
                10.0        1.0000%           100.00%   beyond any mandate

Only guidance covering all ten years moves the ten-year yield fully, and a promise that long exceeds any governor's term.

The region is where the policy bites. Guidance over a meeting or two moves a ten-year yield by 5.00 per cent of the shift, which is negligible; extending it to five years reaches 50.00 per cent, which is material and requires a promise beyond any mandate. That is the honest limit of forward guidance as a long-rate tool, and it explains why central banks extended their guidance horizons from weeks to years after 2008 and why they still could not reach the long end. It is also why quantitative easing exists: it acts on long yields directly rather than through expectations, which section 4.4 quantifies.

4.2 The fractional reserve system and the run are one fact

The summary describes the fractional reserve system and a run on banks in adjacent clauses without saying they are the same arithmetic. Take deposits of 1000 at five reserve ratios.

The multiplier is exactly the reciprocal of the ratio, and the payable fraction is exactly the ratio. Their product is one.

The multiplier is exactly the reciprocal of the reserve ratio: 50.0000 at 2 per cent and 1.0000 at 100 per cent, where no money is created at all. And the fraction of depositors who can be paid before loans must be called is also exactly the reserve ratio. So the two facts the summary lists separately are one fact with two names: the same ratio setting how much money the system creates sets how few depositors it can pay, and their product is one whatever the ratio is.

Raising the ratio to make the bank safe therefore destroys the money creation that makes banking worth doing. That is why no regulator chooses 100 per cent and every regulator is uncomfortable with 2, and it is the structural reason bank regulation cannot be solved by choosing a better number: the objective function has two terms that move in exactly opposite directions with the same lever.

4.3 An interest rate ceiling causes the outflow it was meant to prevent

The summary says an interest rate ceiling restricts the ability to compete for deposits. That is right and understated: a ceiling below the market rate does not slow the inflow, it reverses it, because depositors can go elsewhere.

A ceiling at or above the market rate costs the depositor nothing and binds nothing. A ceiling at 2 per cent against an 8 per cent market costs 6.0000 per cent a year, and the money leaves for whatever is not capped. So a control intended to reduce bank lending relocates it rather than reducing it, which makes it self-defeating in a way the summary's own "lending type directives" are not: a directive names the lending and a ceiling names only the price, so the lending simply moves to where the price is free.

The ceiling is harmless while it sits above the market rate, so its damage depends entirely on where rates go after it is set. It is dormant in easy conditions and binds hardest in tight ones, which is precisely when a bank most needs to retain its deposits. The tool is procyclical by construction, and that generalises: any price cap in finance where the underlying can relocate produces its worst effects exactly when the stress it was meant to control arrives.

4.4 Quantitative easing acts where guidance cannot

The summary describes quantitative easing as crediting an account ex nihilo and buying government or corporate bonds or mortgage-backed securities. Its point of difference from section 4.1 is that it removes duration from the market directly rather than promising anything.

   the bank buys   at what duration   duration removed   yield fall   fall per 100
                                      (% of market)                   bought
          50.0000              2.0000               1.25%       0.0400%         0.0800%
          50.0000              8.0000               5.00%       0.1600%         0.3200%
          50.0000             25.0000              15.62%       0.5000%         1.0000%
         200.0000              8.0000              20.00%       0.6400%         0.3200%

The same 50 spent at twenty-five years moves yields 12.5 times as far as at two years.

Buying 50 of twenty-five-year bonds moves yields 0.5000 per cent and buying 50 of two-year bonds moves them 0.0400, a factor of 12.5 on the same money. So the maturity bought matters as much as the amount, and the summary's list of what may be purchased is therefore a list of different policies rather than one policy with three implementations: buying mortgage-backed securities and buying short government bills are not variations on a theme.

And that is the exact complement of section 4.1. Guidance is diluted at the long end and quantitative easing is amplified there, both for the same reason, which is that maturity enters one calculation as a denominator and the other as a multiplier. The two tools are strongest at opposite ends of the curve, which is why a central bank uses both and why the summary is right to list them under the same heading without saying why.

Precision and reach trade off throughout. The repo rate is precise and reaches only the short end. Quantitative easing reaches the long end and is blunt. The reserve ratio reaches everything and is so blunt it is reserved for crises. Nothing in the summary's list is both precise and far-reaching, and that is not an oversight in the list: it is the constraint monetary policy operates under, and it is why the policy debate is perpetually about which imperfect tool to use rather than about calibrating a good one.

The fourth item repays attention. Market integrity is not a separate public-spirited duty appended to the monetary mandate: a disorderly market breaks the transmission mechanism of section 4.1, so a central bank's interest in orderly markets is an interest in its own policy working. That is why it will intervene in a dysfunctional bond market even when inflation argues against it, and it is the cleanest available explanation of the 2022 gilt intervention that both the currency hedging and swaps lectures referred to from the fund's side. The intervention was not a rescue of pension schemes; it was a repair of the channel through which the policy rate reaches the economy.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

Three things a practitioner does with central bank policy that the announcements do not supply.

Convert every statement of guidance into its implied yield move before deciding whether it is news. Section 4.1's identity makes that a one-line calculation: the guided horizon over the maturity, times the shift. A committee that hears "rates on hold for eighteen months" and concludes that long bonds are attractive has skipped the step where eighteen months over thirty years is one twentieth. Doing the division is free, and it usually reveals that the announcement was priced at the short end and irrelevant at the long, which is where a liability-driven fund's exposure actually sits.

Watch the balance sheet rather than the rhetoric if your liabilities are long. The complementarity in section 4.4 means the tool that reaches a pension fund's discount rate is asset purchases, and the tool that gets discussed is the policy rate. So the informative signal for a long-dated investor is the announced pace and maturity composition of purchases, which is published and rarely headlined, rather than the tone of the accompanying statement. A fund monitoring the second and not the first is monitoring the part that does not reach it.

Expect every binding price control to produce a substitute, and look for it before it becomes systemic. Section 4.3 shows the mechanism arithmetically and it recurs constantly: a cap on deposit rates produced money market funds, a cap on bank leverage produced non-bank lenders, a cap on one form of risk-taking produces another. The professional habit is not to predict which substitute appears but to ask, whenever a new constraint binds, where the activity has gone and whether that place is supervised. The answer is usually available in flow data long before it is a problem.

On the summary, two observations are recorded, both of connection. Its statement that forward guidance works through expectation theory is correct and stops one step short of the dilution that makes it usable, which is an exact identity. And it describes the fractional reserve system and a bank run in adjacent clauses as though they were a mechanism and a risk, when they are one ratio read in two directions whose product is exactly one. Neither is an error. Both are places where a compressed list separates things that only make sense together.

Connections

What this assumes. The monetary theory is built in monetary economics and the crisis machinery in financial macroeconomics and crises. The transmission channels this lecture supplies the origin for are in the macroeconomic environment and the trade cycle, which found all eleven of them starting from one policy rate. The expectations theory of the term structure is derived in yield curves and bootstrapping, the duration arithmetic QE operates on is in fixed income pricing, and the gilt episode section 4.5 explains from the central bank's side is described from the fund's side in currency hedging and using swaps.

Where this goes next. Lecture 3 follows the policy rate into asset prices in detail, covering the transmission to banks, households, businesses and the exchange rate that lecture 1 enumerated and this lecture supplied the source of. Lecture 4 develops the sector taxonomy, where section 4.2's gearing arithmetic explains why financials are classified as they are. Lecture 5 turns from the central bank to the government, and the two are connected by the third of the summary's six other interests, since implementing government borrowing puts the central bank inside the fiscal operation it is supposed to be independent of.

Where the bank sits opposite. Everything in this lecture is done to a bank, and the bank's own view of it is the subject of the banking track. The reserve ratio and the multiplier of section 4.2 are the foundation of the banking business model, and the run they imply is why the liquidity coverage ratio exists, in liquidity risk. The repo rate's effect on the bank's margin, and the summary's own note that a rise raises loan interest faster than deposit interest, is the subject of interest rate risk in the banking book. And the lender-of-last-resort relationship that justifies the first of the six other interests is the reason bank capital is regulated at all, set out in capital management.
