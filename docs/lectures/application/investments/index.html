Application track

Investments

An ASSA F105 Finance and Investment Principles companion, ninety-nine lectures deep. The track answers one question in stages: given an investor with liabilities to meet, how do you choose what to hold, price it, build it into a portfolio, manage it, and then judge whether you did well. It follows the same two-folder shape as the banking track, so the internal environment holds everything the institution controls and the external environment holds everything it must react to.

How to use this

The track is built from a personal F105 summary, checked line by line against the official syllabus objectives (a) to (p). Where the summary is thin or silent, the lecture says so in a "what's beyond the source" callout and names the academic source it draws on instead. Status badges show what is built (built), planned, or a gap, meaning the source summary does not cover it and the lecture is written from external material. Every folder below carries its own landing page with the authoritative lecture list; this page is the map, not the detail.

Lectures assume the fundamentals rather than repeating them. Before the derivatives and valuation folders, read financial management and financial engineering. Before the external environment, read economics and the regulation track. Each lecture names its own prerequisite in the header.

Source material

Lectures follow a personal ASSA F105 summary, twelve chapters over thirty pages, cross-checked against the ASSA Subject F105 Finance and Investment Principles syllabus. The summary is a revision compression, so it is dense on structure and light on derivation; lectures rebuild the derivations in full.

Chapter 11 of the summary, portfolio construction, is a heading with nothing under it, while syllabus objective (f) requires mean-variance theory in full. That entire module is written from external sources: Markowitz (1952), Tobin (1958), Sharpe (1964), Ross (1976), Black and Litterman (1992), with Elton, Gruber, Brown and Goetzmann and Bodie, Kane and Marcus as the textbook spine. Other gap lectures name their sources in the same way. South African specifics come from Regulation 28 of the Pension Funds Act, the FAIS and FSR Acts, King IV, CRISA 2 and the JSE Listings Requirements.

Companion tracks: banking, which shares the internal and external environment split and covers credit, liquidity and operational risk from a regulatory capital angle rather than an investor angle.
