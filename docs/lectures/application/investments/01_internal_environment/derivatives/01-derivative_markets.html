Derivative markets: nothing is removed, everything is transformed

The source says the clearing house removes credit risk, and the arithmetic shows what actually happens. Novation replaces 19,900 bilateral relationships with 200. Netting removes 65.7 per cent of gross exposure before any margin is posted. And daily settlement cuts a five-year contract's potential exposure from 73.6 per cent of notional to 2.93 per cent, a factor of 25. The credit risk became a liquidity obligation, and the liquidity obligation arrives on the days you can least afford it.

Derivatives 1 of 7 Reading time, 45 minutes Sources: F105 summary chapter 8; the standard clearing house default waterfall
Step 1

The 30-second version

There are two places to trade a derivative and the source characterises both precisely. Exchange-traded contracts are standardised, cleared, very liquid, transactable only by members and therefore reached through a broker; the instruments are futures and options. Over the counter contracts are non-standardised, arranged with an investment bank, tailored, dearer to deal, less liquid, carrying higher credit risk, collateralised rather than margined, and without quoted market values; the instruments are forwards, options, contracts for difference, swaps, bond derivatives and credit derivatives.

The source lists five roles for the clearing house: counterparty to all trades, guarantor of all trades, registrar of deals, holder of margin, and facilitator of marking to market. Only the last of those does the economic work, and the arithmetic shows it. An unmargined five-year forward has a potential future exposure of 73.6 per cent of notional at a 20 per cent underlying volatility. The same position margined daily with a two-day close-out period has an exposure of 2.93 per cent, whatever its maturity. A factor of 25, produced by daily settlement rather than by the clearing house's own credit.

Before margin is posted at all, two structural features do a great deal. Novation replaces bilateral relationships with a hub: at 200 participants, 19,900 relationships become 200. And multilateral netting reduces exposure without reducing positions: on the four-participant example in step 4, R350 of gross bilateral exposure nets to R120, a 65.7 per cent reduction.

What is not removed is the risk itself. Initial margin of 4.93 per cent earning the collateral rate rather than the position's return costs 24.7 basis points a year, and a 99th percentile daily move on R100m notional is a R3.5m cash call with no notice. The credit risk was converted into a liquidity obligation, and price limits convert an inability to exit into a larger loss: four limit days at 5 per cent leave a trapped holder down 18.55 per cent rather than the 5 per cent it would have cost to leave on day one.

Feynman test Two people make a bet and worry the other will not pay. So they both hand their money to a third party every single day as the bet moves, which means neither is ever owed much. The third party has not made the bet safer. It has replaced "will he pay me in five years" with "can I find cash by tomorrow morning", and those are different problems that go wrong on different days.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that this lecture is the infrastructure the whole module runs on. Six lectures follow on the contracts themselves, and every one of them is traded in one of these two venues under one of these two credit arrangements. A forward and a future with identical payoffs are different instruments because of what is in this lecture and nothing else.

The second reason is that the margin arithmetic is the same calculation as the repo haircut from the money market lecture and the potential future exposure calculation from the banking track, and seeing that once means never having to learn it three times. In all three cases a collateral requirement is sized to a close-out period rather than to the term of the exposure, and the parameter that matters is how long liquidation is assumed to take.

The third reason is that the conversion of credit risk into liquidity risk is not a footnote. A margined position cannot default on you and can bankrupt you, because variation margin is due in cash on the day the position moves against you, and it moves against you when markets are disorderly. Several well-documented failures were of solvent institutions that could not meet margin calls, and the mechanism is entirely in step 4.

And a fourth reason about policy, which the source touches without developing. On the worked figures, bilateral uncollateralised trading is cheaper for the two parties involved until the counterparty's default probability exceeds 4.12 per cent. That is why central clearing had to be mandated rather than encouraged: the cost the private calculation omits falls on everybody else, and an externality is not internalised voluntarily.

Novation and netting, before any margin is posted

AND MULTILATERAL NETTING REDUCES EXPOSURE WITHOUT REDUCING POSITIONS. Four participants, six bilateral positions, positive meaning owed to the first named: A-B +100.00 A-C -60.00 A-D +40.00 B-C +70.00 B-D -30.00 C-D +50.00 gross bilateral exposure, summed 350.00 net position against the clearing house A +80.00 B -60.00 C +40.00 D -60.00 the clearing house's exposure is the sum of the POSITIVE net positions: 120.00 A 65.7% REDUCTION IN EXPOSURE, from netting alone, with every position unchanged. THE TWO EFFECTS ARE DISTINCT AND BOTH REQUIRE THE HUB. Netting bilaterally is possible under a master agreement, and it only nets A's positions against B. Only a central counterparty can net A's position with B against A's position with C, because only it is the counterparty to both. That is the structural reason clearing reduces exposure that bilateral netting cannot reach.

Initial margin is sized to a close-out period, not to the contract

Every parameter is a policy choice by the clearing house: how quickly it believes it can liquidate a defaulted book, and how much of the tail it will bear rather than mutualise. NOTE WHAT IS ABSENT FROM THE FORMULA: the term of the contract. A ten-year swap and a three-month future on the same underlying carry the same initial margin, because the exposure is reset to zero daily by variation margin. That is the most important consequence of daily settlement. THIS IS THE SAME CALCULATION AS THE REPO HAIRCUT. In the money market lecture a 2 per cent haircut against 0.5 per cent daily volatility was a four standard deviation cushion over one day, and the conclusion was that the haircut is sized to the margining interval rather than to the term of the repo. Identical logic, identical formula, different market. It is also the potential future exposure calculation the banking track applies to derivative counterparties, which is the next subsection.

SO THE SOURCE'S "CREDIT RISK REMOVED" IS ACHIEVED BY THE DAILY SETTLEMENT and not by the clearing house's own creditworthiness. A clearing house that collected margin annually would remove almost nothing, however well capitalised. WHICH REFRAMES THE SOURCE'S LIST OF FIVE ROLES. Counterparty to all trades, guarantor, registrar, holder of margin, and marking-to-market facilitator: only the last is doing the economic work. The other four are the machinery that makes daily settlement enforceable, which is a necessary condition and not the mechanism.

Small, real, and only the first cost. The second is the funding of variation margin, and it is the one that matters: a 95th percentile daily move on R100m R2,467,000 due tomorrow a 99th percentile daily move R3,489,000 a 99.9th percentile daily move R4,635,000 THOSE ARE CASH CALLS WITH NO NOTICE, and they arrive on precisely the days the position has lost money, which are the days when other things have also lost money and funding is least comfortable to raise. The source's phrase "variation margin paid/withdrawn" is describing a liquidity obligation rather than an accounting entry. WHICH IS THE MECHANISM BEHIND A WHOLE CLASS OF FAILURES. A margined position cannot default on you and it can bankrupt you: an institution that is solvent on a mark-to-market basis but cannot produce cash by the morning is closed out at whatever price the clearing house achieves. Lecture 12's argument about a fund's cash needs arriving at the worst moment is the same observation in a different wrapper, and the collateral rule in both cases is that liquidity is a separate resource from solvency.

BELOW THAT, BILATERAL UNCOLLATERALISED TRADING IS GENUINELY CHEAPER FOR THE TWO PARTIES INVOLVED, and 4.12 per cent is a distressed counterparty. So for almost any pair of good-quality institutions the private calculation favours staying bilateral. AND THAT IS THE ENTIRE ARGUMENT FOR A CLEARING MANDATE. The cost the private calculation omits is the one step 4's first subsection counted: the network of interlocking exposures that makes one failure everybody's problem. An externality is not priced by the parties creating it, so it does not get internalised voluntarily however sophisticated they are. The post-crisis response therefore did two things at once, and the source's summary gestures at both. Standardised contracts were MANDATED into central clearing. And contracts remaining bilateral attract higher capital and mandatory margin, which is the source's note that OTC trades "move to a clearing house after the deal is done" and involve "additional capital held". The policy is to change the private calculation until it agrees with the social one.

Open interest, and why it falls while volume rises

The source says open interest is the number of open contracts, that it decreases as delivery approaches, and that volumes increase. Both halves are correct and they look contradictory. THE RESOLUTION IS THAT A TRADE CAN DO ONE OF THREE THINGS: a new buyer meets a new seller open interest RISES by one an existing holder closes against a new entrant open interest UNCHANGED, transferred two existing holders close out against each other open interest FALLS by one Near delivery almost every trade is the third kind, because very few participants want physical delivery. So volume is high, because everybody is closing, and open interest collapses, because each closing trade removes a contract from existence. days to delivery open interest daily volume volume / open interest 60 10,000 1,200 0.12 30 9,500 1,800 0.19 10 7,000 3,500 0.50 3 2,500 4,000 1.60 1 400 1,500 3.75 THE RATIO IS THE USEFUL STATISTIC and it is not visible from the price. A volume-to-open-interest ratio well above one says the day's activity was position changes rather than new positions. A persistent value below 0.2 says a market where holders sit still, which is a liquidity warning for anybody who may need to move size. This also explains why the source pairs delivery with "closing out, physical delivery, cash settlement". Most contracts are closed rather than delivered, so the delivery mechanism matters less for what usually happens and enormously for the price convergence that makes the contract work at all.

Price limits protect the clearing house and trap the trader

The source lists "price limits, halt trading, prevents excess credit risk". A daily limit of 5 per cent caps the clearing house's one-day exposure at 5 per cent by construction, which makes a 4.93 per cent initial margin adequate almost by definition. It does nothing about the total move. A market repricing by a fifth simply takes four limit days to get there: day limit move cumulative can a holder exit? 1 -5% -5.00% NO: limit-down, there is no bid 2 -5% -9.75% NO 3 -5% -14.26% NO 4 -5% -18.55% NO 5 -5% -22.62% yes, once it stops SO THE LIMIT DOES EXACTLY WHAT THE SOURCE SAYS AND THE COST FALLS ELSEWHERE. The clearing house is protected because each day's move is bounded and margined. The participant who wanted out on day one cannot get out until day five, by which point the position has lost 18.55 per cent instead of the 5 per cent an immediate exit would have cost in a market with no limit. AND THE PARTICIPANT IS PAYING MARGIN THROUGHOUT. Each limit day generates a variation margin call of 5 per cent of notional, due the following morning, on a position that cannot be closed. That combination, a compulsory daily cash outflow on a position you are contractually prevented from exiting, is the most dangerous configuration in exchange-traded markets and it is produced by two safety mechanisms working exactly as designed.

What happens if the clearing house fails, and why the answer matters

Concentrating credit risk in one institution raises an obvious question, and the answer is a documented sequence rather than a hope. The default waterfall sets out whose money is used, in order, when a clearing member fails and its margin proves insufficient.

order resource whose money comment 1 the DEFAULTER'S initial margin the defaulter's sized to a 2-day close-out at 99% 2 the defaulter's DEFAULT FUND the defaulter's a further contribution contribution held against exactly this 3 the clearing house's own capital the CCP's "skin in the game", and tranche deliberately placed BEFORE the members' 4 the SURVIVING MEMBERS' default fund the other members' mutualisation begins here contributions 5 assessment rights: further calls the other members' capped, and the cap is on surviving members the real limit of the arrangement 6 tools of last resort, including the members WITH the losers' gains fund haircutting variation margin gains PROFITABLE positions the winners' losses and partial tear-up of contracts THE THIRD ROW IS THE DESIGN DECISION WORTH NOTICING. The clearing house's own capital sits ahead of the surviving members' mutualised fund, which gives it a direct financial incentive to set margin adequately. If it sat behind, the clearing house would be setting a margin level whose inadequacy other people paid for. AND THE FOURTH ROW IS THE ANSWER TO THE ORIGINAL QUESTION. Beyond the defaulter's own resources and the clearing house's capital tranche, losses are MUTUALISED across the surviving members. So the systemic risk was concentrated rather than eliminated, and the ultimate backstop is the same set of large institutions that were the bilateral counterparties before novation. THAT IS NOT AN ARGUMENT AGAINST CLEARING. Concentrating a risk in an institution designed to manage it, with a documented waterfall, daily margining and a published rulebook, is plainly better than leaving it distributed through an opaque network. It is an argument for knowing that a clearing house is systemically important by construction, which is why they are regulated as such.

The source's OTC feature list, examined

The summary gives six features of over-the-counter trading and every one is correct. Two of them repay a second look, because their consequences are larger than the phrases suggest.

"NO QUOTED MARKET VALUES" this is the deepest of the six. an OTC position is valued by a model, so: the value depends on the model, and both parties may use different ones a dispute about collateral is a dispute about a model output lecture 5's smoothing problem applies: a marked-to-model series understates volatility the accounting is a fair value hierarchy level 2 or 3 rather than level 1 and it interacts with the source's own "collateralising required": collateral calls are computed from the model value, so the mechanism that controls credit risk depends on the number that has no market. that is a real circularity and it is why OTC documentation specifies a valuation agent and a dispute procedure at length. "HIGHER CREDIT RISK" correct, and the size is step 4's PFE table: 73.57% of notional at five years unmargined against 2.93% margined. what the phrase understates is that the exposure is TWO-SIDED and changes sign. a swap that is an asset today is a liability tomorrow, so each party is alternately the creditor, and the credit assessment has to be symmetric. which is why the source's remark that OTC trades attract "additional capital held (IFRS 9)" points at the right place and names the wrong standard for the purpose. IFRS 9 governs the ACCOUNTING for expected credit losses on the exposure; the CAPITAL held against a derivative counterparty is set by the Basel counterparty credit risk framework, which computes an exposure at default and applies a risk weight. The two are related and they are different regimes with different numbers, and an examination answer should not conflate them.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The repo haircut arithmetic that the initial margin formula reproduces is from money market instruments, where the same conclusion appeared: collateral is sized to the margining interval rather than to the term. The marked-to-model valuation problem and its effect on measured volatility are from private debt. The collateral risk that a clearing house's own investment of margin introduces is the error identified in securitisation and insurance-linked securities and again in structured products. The liquidity obligation arriving at the worst moment is collective schemes and ETFs in a different wrapper.

Where this goes next. Lecture 2 covers forwards and futures, where the contract specification the source lists becomes the source of basis risk. Lecture 3 covers options, where the writer posts margin and the buyer does not, which follows directly from the asymmetry of the payoff. Lectures 4 and 5 cover swaps and swaptions, which are the largest over-the-counter market and therefore the main subject of the clearing mandate. Lecture 6 covers credit derivatives, where the reference entity's default and the counterparty's default can be the same event. Pricing all of them is valuation, and using them in a portfolio is portfolio management.

Where the bank's version of this lecture lives. The potential future exposure calculation in step 4 is the standardised approach for counterparty credit risk in counterparty credit risk, where a supervisor prescribes the multipliers this lecture derived from a volatility assumption. The credit valuation adjustment compared against margin cost in step 4 attracts its own capital charge there. The variation margin calls in step 4 are a modelled outflow in liquidity risk, and a bank's derivative book sits in the trading book under market risk. Central counterparties are supervised in South Africa under the financial markets regime administered alongside the South African Reserve Bank.

Numbers used here, and one correction. The venue characterisations, the five clearing house roles, the six OTC features, the three-step futures trading process, the contract specification list, the margin vocabulary (initial, variation, maintenance), marking to market, price limits, trading halts, open interest and the delivery options are all from the F105 summary chapter 8. The default waterfall is the standard published structure for a central counterparty. One correction to the source: it attributes the "additional capital held" on over-the-counter trades to IFRS 9. IFRS 9 governs the accounting for expected credit losses on the exposure; regulatory capital against a derivative counterparty is set by the Basel counterparty credit risk framework, which is a different regime with different numbers. Everything else is invented for illustration: the 1.50 per cent daily volatility, the 20 per cent annual volatility, the four-participant netting example, the 7 and 12 per cent return assumptions, the expected positive exposure and counterparty default probabilities, the open interest and volume series, and the 5 per cent price limit. The arithmetic on all of them is exact and none is a market observation.
