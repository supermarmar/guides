Investments, internal environment

Portfolio management

Thirteen lectures on running the portfolio once it has been built. Construction produces a target allocation; management is everything that happens between that target and reality, including the awkward fact that the manager doing the managing is a human being with predictable biases. The module ends on behavioural finance for that reason, not as an afterthought.

How to use this

Lecture 1 frames the whole module: whether to try to beat the market at all. Everything after it is a technique that presupposes an answer. The equity and bond arcs are independent of each other; the derivative arc, lectures 7 to 11, assumes both the derivatives and valuation modules. Status badges show what is built (built), planned, or a gap.

Tracking difference and tracking error separated as a mean and a volatility that rank funds differently, a full-replication fund with exactly zero sampling error still losing 0.6740 per cent a year of which withholding tax is 71.22 per cent, reconstitution impact rising with the square root of the indexed share, the multi-factor model's advantage peaking at 100 names, and commercial matching shown not to be dominated on tracking error. Value, growth, momentum, contrarian, rotational

The dealing cost paid twice so a ten-year switch needs a 6.63 basis point anomaly against 27.08 at two years, a half-year duration mismatch taking 33.17 per cent of the prize and the curve winning outright above 1.5075 years, the coupon effect shown to be a tax result whose direction flips with the tax rates rather than an anomaly, price ratios contaminated to 1.7081 by coupon differences alone, and "low risk" and "limited chances" shown to be the same fact at a required information coefficient of 0.3536.   • builtLecture 6: bond active management, policy switching and yield pick-up. Using derivatives

  • builtLecture 7: hedging with forwards and futures. The residual after hedging depending on the square of the correlation so 0.98 leaves 19.90 per cent of the risk, the minimum-variance ratio of 0.7364 beating one-for-one which leaves 54.21, the hedge accomplishing nothing at exactly twice the optimal ratio, a roll cost rising linearly to 5.4000 per cent while its uncertainty rises only as a square root, and a futures transition saving 2.2400 per cent of which 1.8000 is capital gains tax. Long and short hedges, close-out, basis risk
  • builtLecture 8: using options. The total gamma error over a year identical at 3.813878 whether rebalancing once or 250 times so continual adjustment buys predictability rather than cheapness, delta drifting from 0.7767 to 0.9860 with the price unchanged, the summary's naked put asymmetry verified with the bound at 27.7 times the premium, a straddle break-even that is a constant 0.797 standard deviations at every expiry, and a spread reward-to-risk ratio of 1.9897. Delta hedging, spreads, straddles, income enhancement and the naked-write trap
  • builtLecture 9: using swaps. The total gain from a comparative-advantage swap is exactly the quality spread differential, 0.80 per cent here, splitting 0.35 / 0.35 / 0.10 and summing exactly, with nothing more available to distribute; a 20 per cent chance of a 450 basis point downgrade widening accounts for the whole differential, so the gain may be correctly priced credit risk rather than an imperfection; the counterparty exposure peaks at exactly one third of the term, 3.3333 years on a ten-year swap, and its timing does not move with volatility while its size scales in proportion; a swap beats quarterly forward rolls above 0.3333 years and loses below it; a level swap against a liability amortising from 100 to 10 matches the present value exactly and mismatches every single year, worst at 83.05 per cent of the payment, so "large amounts only" and "need level income" are one constraint seen twice; includes the derivative reporting requirements
  • builtLecture 10: currency hedging. Covered interest parity means foreign bonds yielding 25.00 per cent and minus 0.50 both return exactly 9.0000 per cent once hedged, identical to machine precision, so a foreign yield advantage is not an opportunity and the break-even against an unhedged position is exactly the forward premium, 4.8077 per cent; currency is 86.21 per cent of a foreign government bond's variance and 38.78 per cent of developed equity's, crossing one half where the volatilities are equal; the variance-minimising hedge ratio is one only when the correlation is zero, wanting 1.6067 for emerging equity and 0.7600 for developed, and the mandate cap on over-hedging costs a fifth of what hedging is worth; the residual falls as the square root of rebalancing frequency, the opposite of lecture 8's delta result, because the error is linear rather than quadratic; a fund at the 45 per cent Regulation 28 limit needs 13.50 per cent of its value in cash after a 30 per cent rand move without having lost anything
  • builtLecture 11: transition management and overlays. Four of the summary's five costs are linear in trade size and market impact is not, so its share climbs from 7.33 to 71.43 per cent and overtakes the other four combined at 16.0000 times average daily volume, making the only unquoted cost the dominant one; "better to transact entire asset classes" is a statement about relative liquidity and reverses against a thin future; the summary's "no costs incurred" is false at 0.4814 per cent plus 0.8000 of basis risk and its conclusion survives at a 3.7186 per cent saving; the swap is cheapest at every horizon short of twenty years and the futures route never is, because quarterly rolls cost 0.3200 per cent a year against a 0.2500 fee; consolidating a stock selection profit LOWERS the return per unit of risk from 0.4480 to 0.4167, so the case is the 3.6000 per cent tax deferral and vanishes in a tax-exempt fund; the execution speed optimum is interior at five days

The manager as a source of risk

  • builtLecture 12: behavioural finance, individual biases. The same equity holding has a prospect value of minus 0.030440 judged quarterly and plus 1.277241 judged every twenty years, with the indifference period at 1.2548 years, which is Benartzi and Thaler's equity premium explanation; the summary's "less risk averse as frequency of choice / reporting period increases" contains two clauses pointing opposite ways and only the second is right; risk seeking in losses survives at a loss aversion coefficient of exactly one, so curvature and loss aversion are independent parameters; probability weighting, which the summary omits, makes a one per cent chance feel like 5.53 and explains insurance and lottery purchases by the same person; a manager with a true IC of 0.05 who believes it is 0.40 trades 1600 times and nets minus 6.0000 per cent; under a 1/n rule menu composition swings the equity weight 60 points
  • builtLecture 13: behavioural finance, market behaviour. A hundred thousand investors with independent errors price a security to within 0.0632 per cent and the same hundred thousand correlated at 0.10 are still 6.3248 per cent wrong however many arrive, the limit being the individual error times the square root of the correlation, so the summary's "enough investors are rational" names headcount where the binding condition is correlation, and all four of its market topics are correlating mechanisms; an arbitrageur right on every path is forced out on 82.25 per cent of them at eight times leverage against 21.12 unleveraged, so funding structure and not analysis limits arbitrage; the summary carries five of Baker and Wurgler's six proxies and drops the only demand measure; cross-sectional momentum is defined by its loser leg, the smaller at 42.86 per cent and the one a long-only fund cannot trade; overreaction and underreaction need different horizons; and four mood anomalies are exactly what chance yields from eighty candidate variables
