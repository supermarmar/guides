Five named styles, four distinct signals: contrarian correlates minus 1.0000 with momentum by definition

The summary defines contrarian as "the opposite view to the market" and momentum as continuation, so they are one signal with two signs and a fund holding both in equal size holds nothing. Applying lecture 1's breadth test to each style then separates them sharply: a whole-universe value screen needs an information coefficient of 0.0224 and clears it, while time series momentum needs 0.1443 and a concentrated growth mandate 0.0645, and neither is ever demonstrated. Value and momentum correlate at minus 0.4905, which is worth 69.03 per cent of information ratio when combined.

Step 1

The 30-second version

Lecture 1 established that breadth is the binding constraint on active management, and that breadth counts independent bets. This lecture takes the summary's five named styles and asks two questions of them: how many are actually distinct, and which of them can make enough independent bets to matter.

On the first question the answer is definitional. The summary defines contrarian funds as taking "the opposite view to the market" and cross-sectional momentum as losers continuing to lose. Those correlate at minus 1.0000, so a fund running both in equal size runs nothing. Selecting greedily from the five, four survive as distinct signals and contrarian is dropped as a duplicate. Value and momentum correlate at minus 0.4905, because a stock that has fallen is cheap by construction, which is an empirical overlap rather than a definitional one.

On the second question the styles separate sharply. A whole-universe value screen rebalanced annually makes 500 bets and needs an information coefficient of 0.0224, which is below the 0.05 anyone demonstrates, so it passes. Cross-sectional momentum on 500 names monthly makes 6,000 bets and needs 0.0065. But time series momentum, which is one market-direction call a month, needs 0.1443, and a concentrated growth mandate of thirty names reviewed twice a year needs 0.0645. Neither figure is ever demonstrated.

The negative correlation between value and momentum is not only a problem. Two styles of equal information ratio combine to IR × √(2/(1+ρ)), so at minus 0.30 the combination is 69.03 per cent better than either alone, equivalent to 2.86 times the breadth. That is why value and momentum are run together in practice despite being presented as rival styles. The gain collapses if the second style is weak, falling to 15.43 per cent when its information ratio is a quarter of the first's.

And the rotational style is market timing, which faces a hurdle rather than a breadth problem. On a 0.50 per cent round trip the break-even hit rate is 56.25 per cent for a four per cent move and 75.00 per cent for a one per cent move. So the hurdle is about the size of the move rather than about timing as such, and a style that rotates quarterly on small sector divergences cannot clear it.

Feynman test Five ways to pick shares, except two of them are the same way pointed in opposite directions, so a manager claiming both is claiming nothing. Of the rest, the ones that rank hundreds of shares at once get enough goes at it to work with a tiny edge, and the ones that make a few big calls need to be right far more often than anyone has ever managed. Buying cheap and buying what is going up are opposites often enough that doing both is better than either, which is why the two rival camps quietly run each other's strategy. And rotating between sectors only pays if the sectors move a long way, because the dealing costs are the same either way.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that a list of five styles reads as five sources of return and is four, one of which is the negative of another. A manager describing a process as "momentum with a contrarian overlay" is describing a position that partly cancels, and a fund of funds allocating to both is paying two fees for one net exposure.

The second is that the breadth test discriminates between styles in a way the summary's descriptions do not. Value and cross-sectional momentum pass because they rank whole universes; time series momentum and concentrated growth fail because they make few bets. That is a stronger basis for choosing a style than the qualitative characteristics the summary lists, and it applies to a specific manager's process rather than to the style label.

The third is that the negative correlation is a genuine free lunch of a size worth knowing. Sixty-nine per cent more information ratio for combining two styles that both work is the largest single improvement available in this module, and it requires no additional forecasting skill.

And the fourth is that the timing hurdle explains why tactical asset allocation disappoints without needing any claim about market efficiency. A 75 per cent hit rate on one per cent moves is not a demanding statistical standard to state and is an impossible one to meet, and the arithmetic is available before any performance data.

The mental model

Five names, four signals, one breadth test, and a timing hurdle set by the size of the move.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The first thing to carry is that a list of styles is a list of signals, and signals can be the same signal. Momentum and contrarian correlate at minus 1.0000 by the summary's own definitions, so of five named styles four are distinct. THE TEST TO APPLY TO ANY STYLE DESCRIPTION IS TO ASK WHAT SIGN OF WHAT SIGNAL IT IS, and a manager describing a process as momentum with a contrarian overlay is describing two positions that partly cancel.

THE FOUR DISTINCT SIGNALS, AND WHAT EACH IS A BET ON. value cheapness, however measured overlaps momentum at -0.4905 growth earnings or sales expansion nearly independent of value at 0.0308 momentum continuation of past returns contrarian is its negative size small against large nearly independent of everything THE VALUE-GROWTH CORRELATION OF 0.0308 IS THE INTERESTING ONE, because value and growth are usually presented as opposites. they are not: they are close to independent, and it is value and MOMENTUM that oppose each other.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The fundamental law and the 0.05 information coefficient benchmark are established in active versus passive, which also introduced the point that breadth counts independent bets. The correlated-bets arithmetic that makes "independent" the load-bearing word is measured in risk budgets, VaR and tracking error. The factor structure underlying the style signals is from arbitrage pricing theory and factors, and the value measures the summary describes are built in fundamental analysis and ratios. The 96.68 per cent of variance at stake in the mandate basis, and the load ratios that turn out looser than they appear, are both from benchmarks and comparators. The transaction costs in the timing hurdle are the execution arithmetic from liquidity risk.

Where this goes next. Lectures 4 to 6 take the bond equivalents, where the passive case is liability matching rather than index tracking and the active cases are the summary's anomaly switching, policy switching and yield pick-up trades. The breadth test applies there too and bites harder, because a bond manager has fewer independent instruments than an equity manager has names. Lectures 7 to 11 are implementation, and lecture 11 on transition management is where the style-switching cost implied by the timing hurdle is measured properly. Lectures 12 and 13 on behavioural finance are where the inefficiency that value and momentum both presuppose has to be argued for, and the summary's own herding and noise-trader material is the raw material for it.

Where the bank sits opposite. A bank's version of the style question is the choice of risk factors in a credit scorecard, and the same independence problem arises in the same form: twenty variables that all proxy for leverage are one variable, which is why variable clustering and correlation screening precede model fitting, as set out in credit risk. The breadth argument also has a direct analogue in portfolio construction for lending: a hundred exposures to one sector is one bet, which is the concentration result in the same folder. And the timing hurdle is the bank's version of a trading desk's break-even hit rate, where the bid-offer spread sets a minimum accuracy below which activity destroys value regardless of conviction, treated in market risk.
