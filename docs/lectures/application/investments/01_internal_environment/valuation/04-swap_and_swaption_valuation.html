Swap and swaption valuation: two decompositions that stopped being equivalent in 2008

The source gives two ways to value a swap and presents them as alternatives. On one curve they are the same sum grouped differently, agreeing to ten decimal places. On two curves, which is how every swap has been valued since 2008, the two-bond route is wrong by roughly the size of the basis: at 50 basis points the floating leg is worth 102.1057 rather than par, and the fair swap rate is 50.98 basis points higher. The strip-of-FRAs route survives untouched, and that is the real difference between them.

Step 1

The 30-second version

The source values a swap two ways, as the difference between a floating bond and a fixed bond, or as a strip of forward rate agreements, and lists them as separate methods. On a single curve they are not separate: route B is route A with the summation reordered, and both give −R1,773,004.63546224 on the worked example. Calling them two decompositions is only useful once you know where they part company.

They part company at the telescoping identity. With one curve the discounted floating cash flow for period i equals DF(i−1) minus DF(i), the sum collapses to 1 minus DF(n), and adding back the notional makes the floating bond worth exactly par. That step uses the same curve to project the forward and to discount it. Since 2008 the market projects off a term rate curve and discounts off an overnight collateral curve, the two no longer cancel, and the floating leg is no longer par.

The swaption formula the source gives is an annuity multiplied by a Black term, which is right and unexplained. The payoff is A(T) times the excess of the swap rate over the strike, and both factors are random, so Black cannot simply be applied. The justification is a change of numeraire: value everything in units of the annuity rather than in units of cash, under which the forward swap rate is a martingale and the annuity leaves the expectation as a constant. Without that step there is no reason to treat A as fixed.

Two results then follow. A payer swaption minus a receiver swaption equals the forward-starting swap at every strike, exactly, because it is an arbitrage relation rather than a model output. And cash-settled and physically settled swaptions are different contracts: the cash convention computes its annuity from the settlement rate itself, which on this curve makes the cash-settled payer worth 2.65 per cent more at the money, with a sign that depends on the curve rather than on the direction of the option.

Feynman test A swap is a swap of two streams of payments, and you can value it by valuing each stream or by valuing each exchange one at a time. Those used to be the same calculation. They stopped being the same when the market started using one interest rate to guess future payments and a different one to work out what a future payment is worth today. The option on a swap looks harder than it is, because the size of the thing you win and the amount you win by both move together, and the trick is to measure everything in units of the first so that only the second is left moving.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that the multi-curve break is the largest single valuation change of the past two decades and it is invisible in most syllabus material, which was written before it. A revision summary that gives the two-bond decomposition without qualification is teaching a formula that a bank's systems abandoned in about 2009. The formula is not wrong so much as historical, and knowing which of the two routes survived tells you something durable about why one construction is more robust than another.

The second is that the change of numeraire is the single most useful idea in derivative pricing and is usually presented as a technicality. It is what lets Black's formula be applied to a caplet in lecture 3, to a swaption here, and to a bond option in lecture 5, each time by choosing the numeraire that makes the relevant rate a martingale. Once the pattern is visible, three formulas that look unrelated become one formula applied three times.

The third is practical. The annuity is the swap desk's single risk number: it converts a rate move into money, it is the DV01, and at R41,305.10 per basis point on a five-year R100m swap it is what a trader quotes position size in. It is also concave in tenor, so a thirty-year swap carries only 12.38 times the risk of a one-year swap rather than thirty times, which is the kind of thing that surprises people sizing a hedge.

And a fourth. The cash settlement point is small in percentage terms and instructive out of proportion to its size, because it is a case where two contracts that everybody calls by the same name have measurably different payoffs. That happens more often than the naming suggests, and the discipline of asking what the settlement convention actually computes is worth more than the two-and-a-half per cent it recovers here.

Why the floating leg is worth par, and the step that does the work

Where the identity breaks: two curves

Since 2008 the market has projected forward rates off a term rate curve and discounted off an overnight collateral curve, on the reasoning that a collateralised derivative's cash flows are financed at the collateral rate rather than at an unsecured term rate. In South Africa that is a Jibar projection curve against a ZARONIA or overnight index swap discount curve. The two are separated by a basis.

The two routes are equivalent under an assumption that used to be automatic and is now false, and the route that never relied on the assumption is the one that survived. A NOTE ON WHY THE BASIS EXISTS AT ALL. Before 2008 the difference between an unsecured term rate and an overnight rate compounded was treated as negligible, because bank credit over three months was treated as negligible. It was not, and once counterparties began pricing the difference the two curves had to be built separately. Lecture 2's construction therefore runs twice, once for each curve, with the two bootstrapped jointly because the instruments observed depend on both.

The forward swap rate, and why the annuity is the numeraire

That product is the problem: Black's formula prices an expectation of max(X − K, 0) for a single lognormal X, and here there is a second random quantity multiplying it. Applying Black directly and multiplying by today's annuity is not obviously justified. THE FIX IS A CHANGE OF NUMERAIRE. Instead of measuring value in units of cash, measure it in units of the annuity: ask how many annuities the swaption is worth rather than how many rand. Under the probability measure that this choice induces: the forward swap rate S is a MARTINGALE, so its expected value at expiry is S(0) the annuity is the unit of account, so it is not random in those units which leaves V = A(0) x E[ max( S(T) - K , 0 ) ] with a single random variable inside the expectation, and Black applies to S alone. THAT IS WHY THE SOURCE'S FORMULA IS AN ANNUITY MULTIPLYING A BLACK TERM. The formula is right and the reason is absent, and the reason is the entire content: without the change of numeraire there is no justification for treating A as fixed. IT IS ALSO THE THIRD APPEARANCE OF THE SAME IDEA. Lecture 3 valued a caplet under the measure in which the T2-maturity bond is the numeraire, which makes the forward rate a martingale. THREE FORMULAS THAT LOOK UNRELATED ARE ONE TECHNIQUE APPLIED THREE TIMES, and the technique is always to pick the numeraire that makes the quantity you are optioning into a martingale.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The first thing to take is a general test for whether two derivations of the same result are really two derivations. Route A and route B agree to ten decimal places on one curve and disagree by two points of notional on two, which means they were never equivalent; they were equal under a condition that happened to hold. The way to tell in advance is to ask what each derivation uses that the other does not. Route A uses the telescoping identity, which needs one curve. Route B uses nothing. A derivation that consumes fewer assumptions is the one that survives, and it is worth identifying that before the assumption fails rather than afterwards.

Under multi-curve the forward swap rate is computed from the projection curve and the annuity from the discount curve, and a library written before 2008 will use one curve for both without saying so.

The second is that the change of numeraire is the technique the module has now used three times without naming it as one thing. Lecture 3 made the forward rate a martingale by taking the maturity bond as numeraire. This lecture makes the forward swap rate a martingale by taking the annuity. Lecture 5 will make the forward bond price a martingale by taking the forward measure. The recipe never changes: identify the quantity you are optioning on, find the asset whose price makes that quantity a ratio, and use it as the unit of account. Everything then reduces to Black. That is one idea rather than three formulas, and it is the difference between memorising a syllabus and understanding it.

The third is about what put-call parity is for. It has now appeared four times in this course, between calls and puts on equity, between caps and floors, between payer and receiver swaptions, and implicitly between the two swap legs. Each time it holds exactly, to the last decimal, under any volatility including a wrong one. That is precisely what makes it valuable as a control: it tests the arithmetic without testing the calibration, so a library that fails parity has a bug, while a library that passes it may still be badly calibrated. Knowing which of your checks are model-free and which are not is most of knowing what your checks mean.

That is not four facts. It is one fact about what an option pair spans.

The fourth is the cash settlement point, generalised. Two contracts sharing a name and differing in a settlement convention had payoffs that differ by two and a half per cent, in a direction determined by the curve rather than by anything about the option. Nothing in the source distinguishes them, and nothing in the name distinguishes them either. The general habit is to ask, of any quoted instrument, what the settlement mechanism actually computes rather than what it is supposed to represent. Derivatives lecture 6 found the same thing in credit, where auction settlement replaced two conventions that each had a defect, and the same question would have found it there too.

And the fifth, about the annuity. It is the swap's DV01, the CDS's risky annuity and the bond's duration, all the same construction with different weights, and it is concave in tenor in every one of them. A hedge sized by scaling notionals linearly with maturity is over-hedged at the long end by a factor that grows with the discount rate: 12.38 times the one-year risk at thirty years rather than thirty times. That is a large error available to anyone who reasons about interest rate risk in years rather than in money, and reasoning in years is the natural thing to do.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The proof that a floating leg is worth par, and the condition under which it holds, are from fixed income pricing, where the same result was reached by backward induction rather than by telescoping; this lecture supplies the second derivation and then breaks it. The discount factors, forward rates and the par yield formula that reappears here as the forward swap rate are from yield curves and bootstrapping, which under multi-curve must now be run twice. The change of numeraire, first used to justify Black's caplet formula, and the put-call parity check between caps and floors are from pricing interest rate derivatives. On the derivatives side, both swap decompositions and the exposure profile are from swaps, the swaption and its relation to a cap are from swaptions, caps, floors and collars, the risky annuity that this lecture's annuity generalises is from credit derivatives, and the collateral and clearing arrangements that made overnight discounting correct are from derivative markets.

Where this goes next. Lecture 5 applies the same change-of-numeraire technique a third time, to a bond option under the forward measure, and needs the conversion from yield volatility to price volatility that the duration relationship supplies; it then reads a firm's equity as a call on its assets under the Merton model. Lecture 7's optimal hedge ratio formalises the sizing question raised by the DV01 concavity here. The choice of discount curve for a liability stream, and whether it should be the collateral curve or something else, is the central question of portfolio construction for a liability-driven mandate, and the basis between projection and discount curves is itself a traded exposure that risk measurement must capture separately from the level of rates.

Where the bank sits opposite. The move to overnight discounting was a bank balance sheet event before it was a valuation one: it followed from collateral agreements making a derivative's funding cost the overnight rate rather than an unsecured term rate, which is a financial management question about how positions are funded. The projection-versus-discount basis is a live exposure in the trading book under market risk, and it is one of the reasons a funds transfer price separates a benchmark component from a funding component rather than quoting a single blended rate. A bank using swaps to manage the repricing mismatch between a floating loan book and differently-dated deposits is doing so under interest rate risk in the banking book, where the annuity computed here is the sensitivity that the economic value of equity measure aggregates. The counterparty exposure created by an uncollateralised swap, and its humped profile, is counterparty credit risk.

Numbers used here. Both swap decompositions, the put-call parity relation between caps, floors and swaps, and the swaption formula as an annuity multiplying a Black term are from the F105 summary chapter 9, stated there without derivation, which is why the module index flagged this lecture as a gap and why it is rebuilt from Hull, Options, Futures and Other Derivatives, chapter 29. Two things the source cannot state are supplied in step 4: the two-bond route depends on a single-curve telescoping identity that has been false since the market moved to overnight discounting after 2008, and the source's single swaption formula describes physical settlement, while the cash-settled convention computes its annuity from the settlement rate and is a different contract. Everything numerical is invented for illustration: the flat 7.50 per cent curve, the R100m notional, the 8.00 per cent fixed rate, the basis levels from 0 to 200 basis points, the 22 per cent swaption volatility and the one-into-four structure. The arithmetic is exact and internally checked three ways: the two routes agree to R0.0000000026, the telescoping identity reproduces the direct sum to R0.0000001788, payer minus receiver equals the forward swap to R0.00000000 at every strike, and the numerical integration used for cash settlement reproduces the Black price to a relative error of about three parts in ten million when the cash annuity is replaced by a constant.
