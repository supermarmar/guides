Investments, internal environment

Valuation

Thirteen lectures on what an individual holding is worth and how you would defend the number. The module runs in three arcs: fixed income and its derivatives, where the answer comes from no-arbitrage; asset pricing models, where it comes from a theory of risk; and analysis of a specific company or issuer, where it comes from judgement disciplined by accounts. The three arcs disagree with each other by design, and knowing when to trust which is most of the skill.

How to use this

The fixed income arc, lectures 1 to 7, builds cumulatively and should be read in order. Lectures 8 to 13 are more independent. Every formula is derived rather than quoted; the source summary lists results with no working, which is exactly the material an exam question probes. Status badges show what is built (built), planned, or a gap.

Source material

Chapter 9 of the F105 summary, covering syllabus objectives (d), (e) and part of (k). The summary states the interest rate derivative and bond option formulas without derivation or conditions of use, so lectures 3 to 5 are rebuilt from Hull's Options, Futures and Other Derivatives, chapters 28 and 29, and Fabozzi's Bond Markets, Analysis and Strategies. Lecture 13 covers three syllabus requirements the summary treats descriptively rather than as an evaluation method: rating an entity, evaluating a securitisation, and evaluating a credit derivative. Sources are S&P Global's corporate methodology, Fabozzi's Handbook of Mortgage-Backed Securities, Choudhry's Structured Credit Products and the ISDA credit derivatives definitions.

Assumes financial instruments for the yield curve, duration and convexity, and financial mathematics for discounting. Lecture 13 cross-links the banking track's probability of default lecture.
