A par swap has zero current exposure and a worst case of 20 per cent of notional

Potential future exposure on a ten-year swap peaks at exactly one third of its life, which is why a counterparty limit measured on current exposure authorises everything at inception. A BBB held for ten years is riskier than a BB held for one. And buying credit protection replaces an exposure with a correlated one: at a 0.70 default correlation the protection is 87.95 per cent effective, and no correlation makes it better than 75.00.

Step 1

The 30-second version

The source gives credit risk two control axes. Creditworthiness decides who may be dealt with, through a credit rating limit per duration, exchange trading, collateral and margin. Exposure decides how much, through single-counterparty limits, aggregate limits and credit derivatives. Every one of those controls leaves something uncontrolled, and two of them create a new exposure of a different kind.

Take the rating limit first, and note that the source says per duration. That qualifier is load-bearing. Fitting a constant hazard to one-year default rates, a single-A name is 0.080 per cent risky over one year and 2.372 per cent over thirty, and a BBB over ten years, at 2.472 per cent, is riskier than a BB over one, at 1.000. A limit expressed as a rating alone is therefore incoherent, and the coherent version is a table of terms: at a common 2.00 per cent default probability, an AAA may be held for 101 years, a BBB for 8.07 and a B for 0.49.

Then exposure, where single-name and aggregate limits turn out not to be substitutes. At a thousand equal exposures and zero default correlation the loss volatility is 0.3146 per cent, effectively nothing. At a 0.30 correlation it is 2.1582, 6.9 times larger, and it barely falls with the number of names. No single-name limit can control that, because every name is inside its limit at every correlation.

And counterparty exposure on a derivative behaves in a way no static limit captures. On a ten-year swap the potential future exposure is proportional to the remaining term times the square root of elapsed time, so it peaks at exactly one third of the life, verified numerically to 0.0003 years, at 20.0211 per cent of notional. A par swap's current exposure at inception is zero. The peak scales as maturity to the power 1.5, so doubling the maturity multiplies the worst case by 2.828.

Feynman test Two questions: who will you deal with, and how much. On the first, a rating alone says nothing, because a good name held for thirty years is worse than a poor one held for a year, so the limit has to pair quality with term. On the second, capping each name does nothing if they all fail together, and nothing in the standard list caps that. Then the awkward part. A swap you have just signed is worth nothing, so it uses none of your limit, and three years in it can be worth a fifth of its face value. And buying insurance against a default just swaps the risk for the risk that your insurer fails at the same moment.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that a rating limit without a term dimension is not a weaker control, it is an incoherent one, and the source is careful to say per duration where most practitioners are not. The iso-risk table computed here is what the control should look like: a single default probability, and a permitted term that falls from 101 years at AAA to under six months at B.

The second is that the standard control list omits the mechanism that actually produces credit losses. Single-name limits control idiosyncratic default and aggregate limits control the total, and neither touches correlation, which multiplies the loss volatility of a well-diversified book by 6.9 at a modest 0.30. The missing control is a sector or country limit, and it is absent from the source's list.

The third is that measuring counterparty exposure on current mark-to-market is not conservative, it is close to meaningless for a new derivative. A par swap consumes no limit on the day it is signed and reaches a fifth of its notional three and a third years later. Any limit framework that does not carry a potential-future-exposure measure will authorise an unbounded book of freshly-struck swaps.

And a fourth. Credit derivatives appear in the source as a control on credit exposure, and they are, but the residual is bounded below by the joint default probability. At a 0.70 correlation, protection is 87.95 per cent effective rather than complete, and as correlation approaches one the best achievable protection on this pair is 75.00 per cent. Buying protection on a bank from another bank in the same jurisdiction is the case where the hedge is least effective and most likely to be reached for.

Why the rating limit is "per duration"

So a limit table indexed on rating alone permits positions that a limit table indexed on default probability would forbid, and forbids positions it would permit. THE COHERENT FORM IS A TERM TABLE. Fix a common default probability and solve for the term: rating term at which cumulative default reaches 2.00% AAA 101.00 years AA 50.50 years A 25.24 years BBB 8.07 years BB 2.01 years B 0.49 years CCC 0.07 years Every row carries the same 2.00 per cent default probability. Under a constant hazard the permitted term is inversely proportional to the hazard, which the arithmetic confirms: the product of term and hazard is 0.020203 for all seven ratings, identically. A CAUTION ABOUT THE MODEL, because the elegance is an artefact of it. A constant hazard assumes the annual default probability never changes conditional on survival. Real cumulative default rates do not behave that way: high-yield curves FLATTEN, because the survivors of a CCC cohort are disproportionately the ones being upgraded, and investment-grade curves STEEPEN, because a AAA has nowhere to go but down. So the 0.07 year figure for CCC is not a usable number and the exact inverse proportionality would not survive a real rating transition matrix. THE SHAPE OF THE CONCLUSION SURVIVES AND THE PRECISION DOES NOT: a coherent limit must pair rating with term, and the pairing has to come from a transition matrix rather than from a one-parameter fit.

Counterparty exposure on a derivative

Counterparty exposure has two components. CURRENT EXPOSURE is the mark to market now, which for a par swap at inception is zero. POTENTIAL FUTURE EXPOSURE is how bad it could get, and it is not zero at all. The control that creates a new exposure

The source lists CREDIT DERIVATIVES among the exposure controls. Buying protection on issuer X from bank B removes the exposure to X and creates one to B, and the protection is worth nothing in the state where both default. Take X at a 2.00 per cent default probability and B at 0.50, joined by a one-factor Gaussian copula: correlation P(X defaults) P(B defaults) P(BOTH) independent ratio effective between X,B product protection 0.00 2.00% 0.50% 0.0100% 0.0100% 1.00 99.5000% 0.10 2.00% 0.50% 0.0191% 0.0100% 1.91 99.0464% 0.30 2.00% 0.50% 0.0539% 0.0100% 5.39 97.3040% 0.50 2.00% 0.50% 0.1221% 0.0100% 12.21 93.8946% 0.70 2.00% 0.50% 0.2410% 0.0100% 24.10 87.9492% THE JOINT DEFAULT PROBABILITY RISES BY A FACTOR OF 24.1 BETWEEN ZERO AND 0.70 CORRELATION, and the effectiveness of the protection falls from 99.5000 to 87.9492 per cent. THERE IS A FLOOR AND IT IS NOT ZERO RESIDUAL. As correlation approaches one, B defaults whenever X does up to the limit of B's own default probability, so the joint probability tends to the smaller of the two. Checking at a correlation of 0.9999 gives 0.5000 per cent against a minimum of 0.5000, so the best achievable protection is 1 - min(pX, pB) / pX = 1 - 0.0050/0.0200 = 75.0000 per cent NO PRICE AND NO CONTRACT IMPROVES ON THAT, because it is a property of the two default probabilities and nothing else. A protection seller weaker than the reference name cannot fully insure it. THAT IS WRONG-WAY RISK, and its practical form is uncomfortable: the case where credit protection is most attractive, hedging a large exposure to a financial institution, is the case where the seller is most likely to be another financial institution in the same jurisdiction, and therefore where the correlation is highest and the protection weakest.

Collateral, and what it converts the risk into

Collateral and margin cut the exposure to whatever can accrue between margin calls. For a margin period of risk of d days, using the peak-exposure duration: margin period exposure at 95% as % of the unmargined 10-year peak 1 d 0.6936% 3.46% 2 d 0.9808% 4.90% 5 d 1.5508% 7.75% 10 d 2.1932% 10.95% 20 d 3.1017% 15.49% 60 d 5.3722% 26.83% A TEN-DAY MARGIN PERIOD REDUCES THE EXPOSURE TO 2.1932 PER CENT OF NOTIONAL, WHICH IS 10.95 PER CENT OF THE UNMARGINED PEAK. That single figure is the entire case for central clearing and for the mandatory margining of bilateral derivatives. THE RESIDUAL SCALES AS THE SQUARE ROOT OF THE PERIOD AND NEVER REACHES ZERO. A counterparty that stops posting for sixty days leaves 5.3722 per cent, and the margin period of risk in a default is not the contractual call frequency but the time taken to notice, declare and close out. AND COLLATERAL DOES NOT REMOVE RISK, IT CONVERTS IT. The exposure the receiver loses becomes a liquidity obligation on the poster, which is precisely the mechanism measured in portfolio construction lecture 9: a fifteen per cent collateral buffer on a twenty-year duration overlay is exhausted by a 75 basis point move, and September 2022 supplied the move. CREDIT RISK CONTROL AND LIQUIDITY RISK CREATION ARE THE SAME OPERATION SEEN FROM THE TWO SIDES OF THE CONTRACT.

THE FOUR STEPS, APPLIED TO CREDIT RISK. STEP 1, MEASURING. The definition is the risk of counterparty default, and the source says the allowance is made INSIDE value at risk. That is a modelling choice with a consequence: lecture 10 of portfolio construction showed value at risk fails subadditivity precisely on lumpy credit losses, so folding credit into a VaR number uses the one measure the loss distribution breaks. STEP 2, MODELLING. A hazard or transition matrix for default timing, a copula or factor model for correlation, and a simulation for the exposure profile. Note that all three are needed: default probability, correlation and exposure are separate objects. STEP 3, REPORTING. The requirement that bites is aggregation across instrument types, since the same counterparty appears as a bond issuer, a swap counterparty and a protection seller, and a limit is only a limit if the three are added. STEP 4, DEPARTURE LIMITS. The two axes of this lecture, with the term dimension and the sector dimension both required and only the first of them in the source.
Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The first thing to carry is that the source's two words "per duration" are the whole of the creditworthiness control, and dropping them makes the limit incoherent rather than merely loose. A BBB at ten years is riskier than a BB at one. So a limit table indexed on rating alone simultaneously permits things a probability-based table would forbid and forbids things it would permit, which is worse than being too generous or too strict. THE COHERENT FORM IS A TERM TABLE AT A COMMON DEFAULT PROBABILITY, and it runs from 101 years at AAA to under six months at B.

THE CAVEAT ON THAT TABLE, STATED PLAINLY. assumption what it gives what it costs constant hazard per rating an exact inverse real curves flatten for high yield proportionality and steepen for investment grade THE SHAPE OF THE CONCLUSION SURVIVES AND THE PRECISION DOES NOT. a real limit table comes from a rating transition matrix, and the 0.07 year figure for CCC is an artefact rather than a number.

The second is that the exposure controls in the source address the risk that granularity already solves and omit the one it cannot. At a thousand names and zero correlation the loss volatility is 0.3146 per cent, which needs no limit at all. At a 0.50 correlation it is 3.4679, eleven times higher, with every name still inside its limit. THE MISSING CONTROL IS A SECTOR OR COUNTRY LIMIT and its absence from the list is a genuine gap, not a simplification, because correlated default is the mechanism by which credit books actually fail.

The third is the exposure profile, which is the most practically useful thing here. A par swap has zero current exposure and a worst case of 20.0211 per cent of notional reached at exactly one third of its life. The result t = T/3 falls out of maximising the remaining term against the square root of elapsed time, and it means the peak of a thirty-year swap arrives in year ten at 104.0326 per cent of notional. ANY LIMIT FRAMEWORK BUILT ON CURRENT MARK-TO-MARKET AUTHORISES AN UNBOUNDED BOOK OF NEW DERIVATIVES.

WHY THE PEAK IS AT ONE THIRD, IN ONE LINE. exposure ~ (T - t) . sqrt(t) the term falls linearly, the uncertainty grows as sqrt(t) d/dt = 0 gives -2t + (T - t) = 0 so t = T/3 and the peak value scales as T^1.5 so doubling the maturity multiplies it by 2.828 THE ONE-THIRD RESULT IS INDEPENDENT OF THE VOLATILITY AND OF THE CONFIDENCE LEVEL. only the magnitude depends on those.

The fourth is the floor on credit protection, which I had not expected to be so high. As the default correlation approaches one, the joint default probability tends to the smaller of the two default probabilities, so protection on a 2.00 per cent name bought from a 0.50 per cent seller cannot be better than 75.0000 per cent effective at any price. THAT IS A PROPERTY OF THE TWO DEFAULT PROBABILITIES AND NOTHING ELSE, and it means the quality of the protection seller relative to the reference name sets a hard ceiling on what the hedge can achieve.

And the fifth. Collateral is the one control in the list that works unambiguously, cutting the exposure to 10.95 per cent of the unmargined peak at a ten-day margin period, and it works by converting credit risk into liquidity risk on the other side of the contract. Portfolio construction lecture 9 measured that from the poster's seat: a fifteen per cent buffer on twenty-year duration is exhausted by 75 basis points. THE SAME OPERATION IS A CREDIT CONTROL FOR ONE PARTY AND A LIQUIDITY EXPOSURE FOR THE OTHER, and a risk framework that treats the two as separate disciplines will manage each without noticing the other.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The four-step control framework and the load ratios that departure limits use are from the risk control framework, and the choice of definition at step one is market risk. The failure of value at risk to be subadditive on lumpy credit losses, which is exactly what the source's decision to fold credit into VaR runs into, is demonstrated in risk budgets, value at risk and tracking error. The collateral mechanism and the September 2022 arithmetic that shows what it converts credit risk into are from liability hedging and dynamic liability benchmarks. Credit derivative mechanics, the ISDA credit event list and the correction that a credit default swap is not an option are in credit derivatives, and credit analysis of an issuer is credit analysis. The one-factor Gaussian copula used for the joint default probabilities is introduced in bond options and the Merton model, and the underlying probability from statistics.

Where this goes next. Lecture 5 develops liquidity risk, where the gap and elasticity of lecture 1 are worked out properly and where the collateral result of this lecture reappears as the poster's problem rather than the receiver's. Lecture 6 is operational risk, where the controls stop being quantitative. Lecture 8, stress testing, is where the correlation gap identified in step 4 is addressed, since a scenario can impose a common shock that no name-level limit expresses. In the external environment module, the counterparty framework becomes the clearing and margining regime that mandates most of what this lecture treats as a choice.

Where the bank sits opposite. Everything in this lecture is a simplified version of something a bank does under regulation. The rating-and-term structure is the maturity adjustment in the A-IRB capital formula, which exists for exactly the reason computed in step 4. The correlation gap is concentration risk, addressed under Pillar 2 precisely because Pillar 1's single-factor model assumes it away. Potential future exposure and the margin period of risk are the substance of counterparty credit risk, where SA-CCR replaces the analytic profile computed here with prescribed add-ons, and wrong-way risk carries an explicit multiplier. And the observation that collateral converts credit risk into liquidity risk is why the same institution's liquidity framework must model margin calls as an outflow.

Numbers used here. The two control axes and the seven listed mechanisms are from chapter 12 of the ASSA F105 summary in the user's notes, quoted as written, including the phrase "credit rating limit per duration" on which step 4 turns. The one-factor Gaussian copula follows Vasicek (1987) and the joint default calculation is standard. The margin period of risk framing follows the BCBS-IOSCO margin requirements for non-centrally-cleared derivatives, where ten days is the standard assumption for daily-margined portfolios. Everything numerical is invented for illustration: one-year default rates of 0.02 to 25 per cent across seven rating grades, chosen to be of a plausible order of magnitude rather than taken from any published table, default correlations from zero to 0.70, a 1.00 per cent rate volatility, a ten-year swap, and a protection pair at 2.00 and 0.50 per cent. The constant-hazard model is a deliberate simplification and its limits are stated in step 4: real cumulative default curves flatten for high yield and steepen for investment grade, so the exact inverse proportionality and the CCC figure are artefacts of the fit rather than results. Thirteen claims and counterexamples were encoded as assertions and every table was emitted by the computation and pasted without retyping. One claim failed on the first run, asserting that correlation would multiply the loss volatility by more than ten at a 0.30 correlation when the figure is 6.9; the threshold was wrong rather than the finding, and the corrected figure is used throughout.
