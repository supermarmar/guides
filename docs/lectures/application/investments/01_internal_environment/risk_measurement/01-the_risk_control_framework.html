Two mandates with the same risk budget, and one lets the smallest holding grow five and a half times

The syllabus offers two ways of writing a departure limit and does not say they differ. A load ratio is multiplicative, so its band is a constant 0.50 times the benchmark holding for every asset. A load difference is additive, so its band runs from 0.20 to 3.50 times, letting the smallest holding move 17.5 times more relative to itself than the largest. Calibrate both to the same 2.00 per cent tracking error and they still produce entirely different mandates.

Step 1

The 30-second version

Risk control is a four-step process, and every risk type in this module walks the same four steps. Measure the risk, which means choosing a definition. Model it, which means choosing a calculation. Report it, which means deciding who sees what and how often. And set departure limits, which means deciding how far a manager may deviate from the benchmark.

The first step carries more weight than it looks. For market risk the source offers two definitions, and they are not variants of each other. Actuarial risk is the risk of failing to meet liabilities as they fall due. Downside risk is the risk of failing to meet a specified target return. Neither is the variance of returns, and under actuarial risk cash is not a low-risk asset, which is the surplus result of portfolio construction lecture 8 arriving under a different name.

The fourth step is where the technical content sits. A load ratio caps the variation as a percentage of the benchmark holding; a load difference caps it as a range of percentage points. On a benchmark of 50, 30, 10, 8 and 2 per cent, a 25 per cent load ratio gives every asset a band of exactly 0.50 times its holding, while a 5 percentage point load difference gives bands from 0.20 to 3.50 times. Calibrated to the same 2.00 per cent tracking error budget, the ratio limit permits alternatives between 1.26 and 2.74 per cent and the difference limit permits 0.00 to 11.06.

And a source correction. The summary states that a liquidity risk elasticity of zero means no risk and a negative one means high risk. The first half is right; the second is incomplete. A negative elasticity means the gap shrinks when rates rise, a positive one means it shrinks when rates fall, and both are risk. The magnitude measures the exposure and the sign only names the direction that hurts. The elasticity turns out to equal the negative money-duration mismatch exactly.

Feynman test Say what you mean by risk, work out how to compute it, decide who gets told and how often, then write down how far the manager may stray. That last instruction can be phrased two ways that sound equivalent and are not. "Stay within a quarter of each holding" treats every holding alike. "Stay within five points of each holding" is generous to the tiny holdings and mean to the big ones, so the two-per-cent slice can quintuple while the fifty-per-cent slice barely moves. Both can be tuned to the same overall wobble, and they still describe different funds.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that this lecture is the template for the seven that follow. Market risk, asset-liability mismatching, credit, liquidity, operational, relative performance and basis risk each get the same four steps, and the repetition is the point: what changes between risk types is the definition chosen at step one and the model available at step two, not the structure.

The second is that the choice between a load ratio and a load difference is presented in the source as a pair of alternatives with no discussion, and it is one of the most consequential lines in a mandate. Neither form mentions tracking error, so neither tells the trustee how much risk has been authorised, and the two forms permit different amounts on the same benchmark. A mandate can be written in either and nobody involved need notice which.

The third is that the definition of risk chosen at step one determines whether the rest of the exercise is even measuring the right thing. Actuarial risk and downside risk give different rankings of the same assets, and both differ from variance. Portfolio construction lecture 8 showed cash moving from the safest asset to one of the riskiest when the objective changed from wealth to surplus; that is the same distinction, and here it is a choice made in a sentence at the start of a risk policy.

And a fourth, which is a small point of professional hygiene. The liquidity risk elasticity rule as stated in the summary would pass a scheme whose gap collapses when rates fall, because its elasticity is positive. Any sign-based rule for a two-sided exposure has this defect, and the correct statement is about magnitude with the sign as a separate piece of information.

Step 4

The detail

The four steps

step what it decides the choice that matters 1 MEASURING the definition of the risk absolute or relative 2 MODELLING how it is calculated MPT, ALM or scenario 3 REPORTING who sees it, how often, in what form independence of the reporter 4 DEPARTURE how far the manager may deviate load ratio or load difference LIMITS STEP 1, MEASURING, requires a definition, a choice between absolute and relative, and a timescale. The definition is not a formality, as the next subsection shows. STEP 2, MODELLING, is where the machinery of the previous module enters: modern portfolio theory if returns can be treated as normal, asset-liability modelling if the liability matters, and scenario analysis when neither holds. Different strategic allocations are compared under the chosen model. STEP 3, REPORTING, is the step most often treated as administration. The source's list of system requirements is worth reading as a set of failure modes rather than features: reporting must be regular, up to date, on standardised inputs, visual, escalated to senior management, understood at the risk-factor level, and INDEPENDENTLY MONITORED. The last one is the only one that cannot be bought with software. STEP 4, DEPARTURE LIMITS, must be clearly documented and expressed against risk-adjusted returns and benchmark or downside measures. The two available forms are the subject of the rest of this lecture.

What "risk" means at step one

risk type the definition chosen at step 1 market actuarial risk: failing to meet liabilities as they fall due downside risk: failing to meet a specified target return credit / counterparty the risk of counterparty default, allowed for inside VaR liquidity cash flows insufficient to meet liabilities, or an inability to raise funds in time operational loss from fraud, error or external events relative performance under-performing competitors basis futures and spot prices not moving together when hedging THE FIRST TWO ROWS DESERVE ATTENTION BECAUSE NEITHER IS THE VARIANCE OF RETURNS. ACTUARIAL RISK is the risk of failing to meet the liabilities as they fall due. That is a statement about the surplus, not about the assets, and it is precisely the framework of portfolio construction lecture 8. Under it, cash is not a low-risk asset: it has no covariance with the liability, so holding cash against a long-dated obligation leaves the whole liability unhedged. DOWNSIDE RISK is the risk of failing to meet a specified target return. That is a statement about the assets against a threshold, so it is asymmetric but not liability-relative. It ranks assets differently again. VARIANCE, the measure the whole of the previous module was built on, appears in neither definition. It is a modelling convenience adopted at step 2, and adopting it at step 2 after choosing actuarial risk at step 1 is a substitution that should be declared rather than assumed. THE PRACTICAL CONSEQUENCE IS THAT TWO FUNDS CAN RUN IDENTICAL PORTFOLIOS AND REPORT OPPOSITE RISK ASSESSMENTS, entirely because of a sentence at the top of the risk policy.

Load ratios and load differences

A LOAD RATIO caps the variation of an asset allocation as a PERCENTAGE OF THE BENCHMARK holding. A LOAD DIFFERENCE caps the RANGE the allocation may vary as a number of PERCENTAGE POINTS. THE DIFFERENCE COLUMN RUNS FROM 0.20 TO 3.50 TIMES, so a five percentage point limit lets the smallest holding move 17.5 times more, relative to itself, than the largest. WHICH IS RIGHT? Neither, in the abstract. A ratio limit says every position may be scaled by the same proportion, which suits a fund whose views are proportional to its holdings. A difference limit says every position may move by the same amount of the total fund, which suits a fund thinking in terms of contribution to the overall allocation. THE CHOICE IS A STATEMENT ABOUT HOW THE MANAGER IS EXPECTED TO THINK, and it is almost never discussed in those terms.

Neither form mentions risk

A departure limit is supposed to control risk and neither form contains a risk number. So compute the risk each permits. On equity, the ratio limit is the looser of the two, 31.44 to 68.56 against 40.94 to 59.06. On alternatives, the ratio limit is far tighter, 1.26 to 2.74 against 0.00 to 11.06, which is up to 5.53 times the benchmark holding. SAME RISK BUDGET, COMPLETELY DIFFERENT MANDATE. The ratio version authorises a large asset allocation bet and forbids any meaningful position in alternatives. The difference version does the reverse. A trustee approving either has approved 2.00 per cent of tracking error and has not been told which of those two funds they are getting. MECHANISM CHECK. If the divergence is caused by unequal benchmark weights, it should vanish when the weights are equal. At five equal weights of 20 per cent, a 25 per cent ratio and a 5 percentage point difference give identical bands and identical worst-case tracking error, to within machine precision. THE UNEQUAL WEIGHTS ARE THE WHOLE CAUSE.

The liquidity gap

The source gives two liquidity techniques, and the first is arithmetic. LIQUIDITY GAP ANALYSIS. On a six-month criterion, liquidity gap = liquid assets - volatile liabilities A positive gap is the desired position. The source's caveats are the important part: allow for dealing costs, since a liquid asset sold in haste is worth less than its marked value, and note that the measure contains NO IMPACT OF STRESS, because both sides are taken at current values. A correction to the source's sign rule

The summary states: "if LRE = 0 then no risk; if LRE < 0 then high risk". THE FIRST HALF IS RIGHT AND THE SECOND IS INCOMPLETE. Test three balance sheets: case DA DL money-dur LRE/100bp gap +200bp gap -200bp assets longer 1.5 0.8 100.0 -1.0000 18.0000 22.0000 equal money duration 1.0 1.2 0.0 -0.0000 20.0000 20.0000 liabilities longer 0.5 1.5 -90.0 +0.9000 21.8000 18.2000 ROW ONE has a negative elasticity and its gap falls from 20.00 to 18.00 when rates rise 200 basis points. The source's rule flags it correctly. ROW THREE has a POSITIVE elasticity and its gap falls from 20.00 to 18.20 when rates FALL 200 basis points. The source's rule does not flag it at all, and it is exposed by very nearly the same amount in the opposite direction. SO THE MAGNITUDE MEASURES THE EXPOSURE AND THE SIGN NAMES THE DIRECTION THAT HURTS. Only an elasticity of zero is genuinely immune, which row two confirms: its gap is 20.0000 at plus 200 basis points and 20.0000 at minus 200. WHY DOES THE SOURCE'S RULE LOOK RIGHT? Because in most real balance sheets the liquid assets are longer than the volatile liabilities, so the elasticity usually IS negative, and a rule that happens to be true of the common case reads as a general rule. THAT IS A COMMON SHAPE OF ERROR IN RISK MANAGEMENT RULES OF THUMB, and the test for it is always to construct the opposite case. NOTE ALSO WHAT ROW TWO SHOWS ABOUT IMMUNITY. Equal money duration is achieved with DA = 1.0 and DL = 1.2, which are NOT equal durations. Matching the DURATIONS of an unequal amount of assets and liabilities does not immunise the gap; matching the MONEY durations does, which is the distinction portfolio construction lecture 9 made about level and slope in a different setting.
Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The first thing to carry is that a departure limit written in either available form authorises an amount of risk that nobody has computed. The load ratio and load difference on the same benchmark permit 1.3471 and 1.1032 per cent of tracking error, and neither number appears in the mandate. Worse, the binding trade differs: the ratio limit's constraint is the large equity holding and the difference limit's is the small alternatives holding that the ratio limit had effectively frozen. A MANDATE IS A RISK BUDGET WHETHER OR NOT IT SAYS SO, and the only way to know what has been authorised is to compute the worst permitted position.

WHAT TO ASK OF ANY DEPARTURE LIMIT. question why what tracking error does it permit at worst? the mandate does not say which asset is the binding constraint? it is rarely the one intended which holdings has it effectively frozen? a ratio limit freezes small ones which could multiply? a difference limit lets them ALL FOUR ARE ANSWERABLE IN A FEW LINES OF ARITHMETIC AND NONE IS CONVENTIONALLY ASKED.

The second is that the two forms encode different theories of how a manager should think, and the choice is usually made without noticing there is one. A ratio limit says positions may be scaled proportionately, which suits a manager whose conviction is proportional to the existing holding. A difference limit says positions may move by the same share of the fund, which suits a manager thinking about contribution to the total allocation. Calibrated to the same 2.00 per cent budget, the ratio version authorises a large asset allocation bet and forbids any meaningful alternatives position; the difference version does the reverse, permitting up to 5.53 times the benchmark holding.

The third is the definition question at step one, which is easy to skim and determines everything downstream. Actuarial risk is failing to meet liabilities as they fall due. That is a surplus statement, and under it cash is not low risk, which portfolio construction lecture 8 measured as 0.50 per cent asset volatility against 17.47 per cent surplus volatility. Variance appears in neither of the source's two definitions; it enters at step two as a modelling convenience. SUBSTITUTING VARIANCE AT STEP TWO FOR ACTUARIAL RISK AT STEP ONE IS A REAL SUBSTITUTION and it should be declared.

THE SAME MACHINERY, THREE TIMES IN ONE LECTURE. appearance what it really is actuarial risk at step 1 the surplus objective of portfolio construction 8 liquidity duration analysis duration matching applied to the gap equal money duration immunising the gap the money-duration match, not equal durations THE F105 RISK CONTROL MATERIAL AND THE PORTFOLIO CONSTRUCTION MATERIAL ARE THE SAME SUBJECT IN TWO VOCABULARIES, and recognising that is worth more than memorising either list.

The fourth is the sign rule, which is a good example of a rule of thumb that is true of the common case and stated as though general. "A negative liquidity risk elasticity means high risk" flags a balance sheet whose liquid assets are longer than its volatile liabilities, which is most of them. It does not flag the reverse case, whose gap falls by 1.80 when rates fall 200 basis points against the first case's 2.00 when they rise. THE TEST FOR THIS SHAPE OF ERROR IS ALWAYS TO CONSTRUCT THE OPPOSITE CASE, and it takes three lines.

And the fifth. Step three, reporting, contains the only requirement in the whole framework that cannot be satisfied by buying something: independent monitoring. Regular, timely, standardised, visualised, escalated reporting is a systems procurement. A reporting line that does not run through the people whose positions are being reported is a governance decision, and it is the one that determines whether any of the other three steps produces information anybody acts on.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. Tracking error, the requirement that active weights sum to zero, and the risk budgeting that departure limits implicitly perform are from risk budgets, value at risk and tracking error. The actuarial definition of risk is the surplus objective of asset-liability modelling, which is also where the result that cash carries 17.47 per cent surplus volatility against 0.50 per cent asset volatility is established. The money-duration identity behind the liquidity elasticity, and the distinction between matching durations and matching money durations, are from liability hedging and dynamic liability benchmarks. Duration itself is from fixed income pricing, and the strategic allocation that a departure limit is written against is strategic asset allocation, whose tolerance ranges are these limits under another name.

Where this goes next. Lectures 2 to 8 walk these same four steps for each risk type in turn: market risk with its actuarial and downside definitions, asset-liability mismatching, credit and counterparty risk with creditworthiness and exposure limits, liquidity risk developing the gap and elasticity introduced here, operational risk where the controls are organisational rather than quantitative, relative performance and basis risk, and finally stress testing, which exists because of what value at risk cannot see. The repetition across those seven is deliberate and is what makes the material examinable.

Where the bank sits opposite. The four steps are recognisably the bank's risk management process, and the departure limit is the investment-side name for a risk appetite limit. The distinction this lecture draws between the two limit forms has a direct banking analogue in whether an exposure limit is set as a percentage of a portfolio or as an absolute amount, with the same consequence that one form freezes small books and the other lets them multiply. Liquidity gap analysis on a six-month criterion is the simplified ancestor of the liquidity coverage ratio, and the source's own caveat that the measure contains no impact of stress is exactly the gap the LCR was designed to close by applying prescribed runoff assumptions. The independence-of-reporting requirement at step three is the three-lines-of-defence model in a single clause.

Numbers used here. The four-step framework, both definitions of market risk, the load ratio and load difference definitions, the liquidity gap formula and the liquidity risk elasticity are taken from chapter 12 of the ASSA F105 summary held in the user's own notes, and the definitions are quoted as written there. The liquidity risk elasticity concept originates with Bierwag and Kaufman's work on duration gaps. Eleven claims and counterexamples were encoded as assertions and all eleven passed. One correction to the source is recorded: its rule that only a negative liquidity risk elasticity indicates high risk is incomplete, because a positive elasticity is exposed by very nearly the same amount in the opposite direction, and a counterexample is given in step 4.
