Investments, internal environment

Risk measurement

Eight lectures on monitoring and controlling exposure, one per risk type after an opening lecture on the control process itself. The framing is the investor's, not the regulator's: the question is not how much capital must be held against a position but whether the fund will still meet its liabilities, which is why actuarial risk and downside risk appear here alongside value at risk.

How to use this

Lecture 1 gives the four-step control process, measure, model, report, set departure limits, and every risk-type lecture afterwards walks the same four steps. Read it first; the repetition across lectures 2 to 8 is deliberate and is what makes the material examinable. Status badges show what is built (built), planned, or a gap.

Course sequence

The process

  • builtLecture 1: the risk control framework. The four steps, the actuarial definition of risk as failing to meet liabilities rather than as a variance, a load ratio giving every asset a band of exactly 0.50 times its holding against a load difference spanning 0.20 to 3.50 times, the two forms permitting 1.3471 and 1.1032 per cent of tracking error with different binding trades, and a correction to the source rule that only a negative liquidity risk elasticity is risky

Risks that come from the assets

  • builtLecture 2: market risk. Six measures on five portfolios naming three different ones safest, with all cash ranked first on absolute risk, fourth on actuarial and fifth on relative, semi-variance exactly half the variance under normality so it reorders nothing, a moment-matched pair where the skewed distribution has a 48.96 per cent higher 99 per cent VaR and an 11.64 per cent LOWER shortfall probability, and a balanced fund's VaR crossing zero at 2.8282 years
  • builtLecture 3: asset-liability mismatching risk. The mismatch measured as a difference of factor exposures, an Euler attribution in which two of four contributions are NEGATIVE so removing those mismatches raises total risk by 0.36 and 0.44 percentage points, the free-asset ratio setting the permitted duration mismatch from 2.15 to 21.49 years, a control loop running from buffer to mismatch to allocation to return that traps the weak fund at 7.5373 per cent, and a fully compliant gilt portfolio carrying a sixteen-year mismatch
  • builtLecture 4: credit and counterparty risk. Why a rating limit must pair quality with term, since a BBB at ten years beats a BB at one, an iso-risk term table from 101 years at AAA to 0.49 at B, correlation multiplying a thousand-name book's loss volatility by 6.9 with every name inside its limit, potential future exposure peaking at exactly one third of a swap's life at 20.02 per cent of notional, and credit protection bounded at 75.00 per cent effectiveness by the joint default probability
  • builtLecture 5: liquidity risk. A six-month net gap of plus 8.0 concealing a minus 29.0 cumulative position in month two, so the right statistic is the minimum cumulative gap, dealing costs making liquidity a property of the position rather than the asset, an execution optimum at 53.7348 days where impact and price risk are exactly equal, and a stressed gap that passes at plus 98.50 until the fund's own overlay is included

Risks that come from the institution and the peer group

  • builtLecture 6: operational risk. Nine of the source's thirteen sources falling outside its own definition, all thirteen mapped onto Basel's seven event types, segregation cutting fraud risk by a factor of one over the individual propensity but only 9.1 per cent of that surviving a 0.30 correlation in willingness, a tail year in which one event is 92.29 per cent of the loss, ten years of data reaching only the 99.50 per cent severity quantile, and halving the frequency cutting the capital number by just 30.9 per cent
  • builtLecture 7: relative performance and basis risk. Hedge effectiveness as the squared correlation against a residual volatility of 31.22 per cent at a 0.95 correlation, the source's three sources of basis risk resolving into one irreducible term and two that share a cause, forty quarterly rolls accumulating 6.32 times the basis risk of one, a one-point edge needing 43 years to demonstrate against a peer median lifted 0.79 points by survivorship, and the control raising the chance of universal failure 240,000-fold

Beyond the model

  • builtLecture 8: stress testing and scenario analysis. Two distributions with identical means and 99 per cent VaR differing by 2.08x at 99.9, the naive all-adverse three-sigma test sitting at 8.2796 Mahalanobis sigma on a matrix with two negative correlations, scenarios made comparable by their distance from the centre, reverse stress solved in closed form as the loss over the portfolio volatility with no factor moving three sigma, and a scenario set derived from the blind spots of lectures 2 to 7

Source material

Chapter 12 of the F105 summary, the risk control section, covering syllabus objective (f). Value at risk appears in the summary as a definition without a method, so lecture 2 and lecture 8 build the three standard approaches from Jorion's Value at Risk.

This folder deliberately shares its name with the banking track's risk measurement folder, and each lecture cross-links its banking counterpart. The two treatments differ in purpose: banking measures risk to size a regulatory capital requirement, investments measures it to decide whether a mandate is being honoured and liabilities will be met. Reading both is worthwhile precisely because the same risk name yields different machinery.
