The solvency buffer decides the mismatch, the mismatch decides the return, and the weak fund is trapped

A five per cent free-asset ratio permits a 2.15 year duration mismatch at a one per cent ruin probability and therefore an expected return of 7.5373 per cent. A thirty per cent ratio permits 12.90 years and 10.2239 per cent. The chain runs from the balance sheet to the strategy without passing through anybody's preferences, and it keeps the weak fund weak. Along the way, two of four mismatch exposures turn out to have negative risk contributions.

Step 1

The 30-second version

Asset-liability mismatching risk is the first risk type in syllabus objective (f), and it is the one the framework of lecture 1 handles least comfortably, because the mismatch is not a property of the assets or of the liabilities but of the difference between them.

Measure it by expressing both sides as factor exposures and subtracting. On a fund with a nine-year asset duration against an eighteen-year liability, and an inflation exposure of minus four against minus 15.3, the mismatch generates a surplus volatility of 21.7055 per cent against the 34.9642 per cent of holding no assets at all, so the portfolio removes 37.92 per cent of the liability's risk and leaves the rest.

Attribute it with Euler and the parts sum exactly, to 2.8 parts in ten thousand trillion. The result contains a surprise: two of the four contributions are negative. The currency mismatch has a standalone volatility of 2.5000 per cent and a contribution of minus 0.2177; the credit spread mismatch, 2.0000 and minus 0.3518. Setting either to zero raises total risk, by 0.3587 and 0.4394 percentage points. Those mismatches are hedges, and an instruction to reduce them would make the fund riskier.

Then the control question, which is where this lecture earns its place beside the hedging lectures. Free assets are the buffer that absorbs a mismatch loss, so a free-asset ratio of f permits a surplus volatility of f divided by the ruin-probability z-score. At one per cent, a five per cent ratio permits 2.1493 per cent of surplus volatility and therefore a 2.15 year duration mismatch; a thirty per cent ratio permits 12.90 years. Since matching costs return, the buffer determines the achievable return: 7.5373 per cent against 10.2239.

Feynman test The danger is not in what you own or in what you owe but in the difference. Work out how much spare money you have above your debts, because that is what absorbs a bad year, and it tells you exactly how far apart your assets and debts may drift. Thin cover means matching closely, matching closely means owning safe things, and safe things earn less, so the fund with the least spare money is forced into the strategy least likely to build any. Also worth knowing: a couple of your mismatches are quietly cancelling the others, so tidying them up would make things worse.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that the control loop this lecture computes is the actual mechanism by which a weak balance sheet becomes a permanently weak one. The buffer sets the permitted mismatch, the permitted mismatch sets the allocation, and the allocation sets the return. A fund with five per cent free assets earns 7.5373 per cent and one with thirty per cent earns 10.2239, and the difference compounds. No preference, risk appetite or investment view enters anywhere in that chain.

The second is that risk attribution can assign a negative contribution to a real exposure, and a governance process that instructs managers to reduce every mismatch will therefore increase risk. Two of the four exposures here are in that position, and removing them raises total surplus volatility by 0.3587 and 0.4394 percentage points. The test is arithmetic and it takes one line, and it is not conventionally performed.

The third is that reverse stress testing is the more useful framing for a board than a volatility number. A fund with a ten per cent buffer and an eighteen-year mismatch is exhausted by a 56 basis point move; with a two-year mismatch it needs 500. The first of those is a question a board can answer from memory, and September 2022 answers it.

And a fourth. The regulatory controls the source lists, admissible assets, localisation, currency matching, self-investment prohibitions and concentration limits, mostly do not control the mismatch. A portfolio of 100 per cent domestic government bonds satisfies every one of them and can carry a sixteen-year duration mismatch requiring a 37.22 per cent free-asset ratio to support. Compliance and matching are close to orthogonal.

Step 3

The mental model

Measure the mismatch, attribute it, and then let the balance sheet decide how much of it is permitted.

The free-asset constraint

Now the control question, which the hedging lectures did not ask: how much mismatch is PERMITTED? FREE ASSETS are assets less liabilities, the buffer that absorbs a mismatch loss. For a free-asset ratio f, expressed as a fraction of liabilities, and a surplus volatility s, the one-year probability that the buffer is exhausted is approximately the normal probability of a move worse than f divided by s. A five per cent fund earns 7.5373 per cent and a thirty per cent fund earns 10.2239, a gap of 2.69 percentage points a year. AND THAT IS A TRAP RATHER THAN MERELY A CONSTRAINT. The fund with the thinnest buffer is forced into the strategy least able to build one, and the fund with a comfortable buffer is permitted the strategy that widens the gap. Nothing in the chain is anybody's decision: no preference, no risk appetite and no investment view appears anywhere in it. TWO ESCAPES EXIST AND BOTH SIT OUTSIDE THE INVESTMENT DECISION. Raise the buffer by contribution, which moves the fund up the table, or raise the ruin tolerance, which moves it rightward within the first table. A weak fund's investment strategy is therefore determined by its funding plan, which is the conclusion portfolio construction lecture 9 reached about the hedge ratio by a completely different route.

Reverse stress

Invert the question. Rather than asking what volatility the buffer supports, ask what single move exhausts it: duration yield move that exhausts a free-asset ratio of mismatch 5% 10% 15% 20% 30% 1.0y 500bp 1000bp 1500bp 2000bp 3000bp 2.0y 250bp 500bp 750bp 1000bp 1500bp 4.0y 125bp 250bp 375bp 500bp 750bp 8.0y 62bp 125bp 188bp 250bp 375bp 12.0y 42bp 83bp 125bp 167bp 250bp 18.0y 28bp 56bp 83bp 111bp 167bp The exhausting move is the free-asset ratio divided by the mismatch, so it is inversely proportional to the mismatch: a ten per cent buffer survives 500 basis points on a two-year mismatch and 56 on an eighteen-year one. REVERSE STRESS IS THE BETTER FRAMING FOR A BOARD because it produces a number that can be judged against experience rather than against a distributional assumption. "Our surplus volatility is 4.30 per cent" invites no reaction. "A 56 basis point move wipes out our buffer" invites the correct question, which is whether that has happened recently. Portfolio construction lecture 9 supplies the answer: long gilt yields moved far more than that in September 2022. NOTE THAT THE REVERSE STRESS NUMBER CONTAINS NO PROBABILITY. That is its strength and its weakness: it cannot be wrong about the distribution because it makes no distributional claim, and it cannot tell you how worried to be.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The first thing to carry is the control loop, because it is the mechanism by which a funding problem becomes a permanent one and it contains no decisions. The buffer sets the permitted mismatch, the permitted mismatch sets the allocation, the allocation sets the return. Five per cent free assets gives 7.5373 per cent; thirty gives 10.2239. THE FUND LEAST ABLE TO AFFORD A LOW RETURN IS THE ONE COMPELLED TO ACCEPT IT, and the only escapes, more contributions or a higher ruin tolerance, both sit outside the investment decision entirely.

WHERE THE INVESTMENT DECISION ACTUALLY GETS MADE. step who decides is it an investment decision? the free-asset ratio the funding plan NO the ruin tolerance the board, once NO the permitted mismatch arithmetic NO the allocation follows from the above barely security selection the manager yes THE FIRST THREE ROWS DETERMINE ALMOST THE WHOLE ANSWER AND NONE OF THEM IS TAKEN BY AN INVESTOR. that is the same conclusion portfolio construction lecture 9 reached about the hedge ratio, arrived at from the solvency side rather than the return side.

The second is that risk attribution can be negative and that this is not a curiosity. Two of the four mismatches here have negative contributions, and removing either raises total surplus volatility by more than a third of a percentage point. A risk policy that instructs managers to minimise identified mismatches, which is how such policies are almost always worded, would make this fund riskier. THE TEST IS ONE LINE OF ARITHMETIC PER EXPOSURE and it is not conventionally performed, because the intuition that a mismatch is bad is very strong.

The third is that reverse stress is the framing that gets a board to engage, and the reason is that it makes no distributional claim. A surplus volatility of 4.30 per cent invites no reaction from anybody. "A 56 basis point move exhausts our buffer" invites the only useful question, which is whether that has happened lately. Its weakness is the mirror of its strength: it cannot be wrong about the distribution and it cannot tell you how worried to be.

TWO WAYS OF SAYING THE SAME THING TO A BOARD. framing what it invites "surplus volatility is 4.30%" nothing "a 56bp move exhausts the buffer" "has that happened?" BOTH ARE THE SAME NUMBER DIVIDED DIFFERENTLY. only one of them is a question somebody can answer from memory, and September 2022 answers it.

The fourth is the orthogonality of compliance and matching, which is worth stating bluntly because the two are routinely conflated. A portfolio of 100 per cent domestic government bonds at a two-year duration satisfies admissibility, localisation, currency matching, self-investment prohibitions and every concentration limit, and carries a sixteen-year mismatch needing a 37.22 per cent buffer to support. Of the seven regulatory controls the source lists, exactly one addresses a mismatch. A COMPLIANCE REPORT IS NOT A MATCHING REPORT and no amount of the first substitutes for the second.

And the fifth, about the source. The summary develops six risk types in its risk control section and passes over mismatching in a single line, despite it being the first bullet of syllabus objective (f). That is worth noticing rather than papering over: the gap is in the summary and not in the syllabus, and a candidate revising from the summary alone would arrive at the exam having read one sentence on the topic the examiner listed first. THE MATERIAL EXISTS, it is the surplus framework of the previous module, and it has to be brought across rather than found.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The surplus framework, the treatment of a liability as a negative-weight asset and the result that duration matching minimises surplus variance are all from asset-liability modelling. The four degrees of matching and the twist ranges quoted for each are measured in liability hedging and dynamic liability benchmarks, which also supplies the September 2022 arithmetic that makes the reverse stress numbers concrete and reaches the same conclusion about the hedge ratio being a funding decision. Euler's decomposition and the fact that contributions sum exactly are from risk budgets, value at risk and tracking error. The four-step control framework and the regulatory control vocabulary are from the risk control framework, and the distinction between measurement bases that makes the standalone asset volatility the wrong comparison is market risk.

Where this goes next. Lecture 4 turns to credit and counterparty risk, where creditworthiness and exposure limits do the work that duration matching does here. Lecture 5 develops the liquidity gap and elasticity that lecture 1 introduced, and the free-asset logic of this lecture reappears there as the question of how much illiquidity a buffer can support. Lecture 8, stress testing, generalises the reverse stress framing used in step 4 from a single yield move to a full scenario. In the external environment module, the regulatory controls listed here are treated as the regime they belong to rather than as a checklist.

Where the bank sits opposite. The free-asset ratio is the investment-side name for a bank's capital ratio, and the control loop computed in step 4 is exactly the mechanism by which a thinly-capitalised bank is forced into low-return lending, which is why the countercyclical buffer in capital management is built to be released rather than held. Reverse stress testing is a formal supervisory requirement rather than an option for a bank, and the yield-move framing in step 4 is how it is conducted. The negative risk contribution result has a direct analogue in concentration risk, where an exposure that diversifies the book can carry a negative marginal capital charge, and the same governance trap follows. And the orthogonality of compliance and matching is why IRRBB is supervised through economic value sensitivity rather than through asset eligibility rules.

Everything numerical is invented for illustration: four factors at 1.00, 1.50, 10.00 and 0.80 per cent standard deviations with the stated correlations, a nine-year asset duration against an eighteen-year liability with a 0.85 inflation linkage, free-asset ratios from 5 to 50 per cent, ruin probabilities from 5 to 0.1 per cent, and a 7.00 against 11.50 per cent return spread. Fourteen claims and counterexamples were encoded as assertions and every table was emitted by the computation and pasted without retyping. Two claims failed on the first run: that the mismatch volatility would be smaller than either side alone, which is false because it exceeds the standalone asset volatility and the comparison was badly posed, and that the standalone and contribution orderings would differ, which they do not on this universe. Correcting the second led to the negative-contribution finding reported in step 4, which is stronger than the reordering originally claimed.
