Lectures / Investments / Internal environment / Performance measurement

At one year of monthly data, the sign of a company's economic value added is statistically undetermined

A company earning 18 on capital of 100 breaks even on the security market line at a beta of 2.1667. Estimating that beta from twelve monthly observations gives a 95 per cent interval of 0.79 to 2.98, which contains the break-even point, so the answer to "does this company create value" has no sign. Twenty years of the same history closes the interval to 1.31 to 1.56 and settles it. Along the way: the summary's cryptic CAPM line turns out to mean that a persistent excess return proves the existence of 47.5410 of capital nobody recorded.

Step 1

The 30-second version

Lecture 2 measured one asset. This lecture measures the company behind it, and the summary sets out a four-rung ladder: profit, which charges for nothing; return on capital, which charges for capital as a ratio; economic value added, which charges for capital at a price; and the capital asset pricing model, which charges for the risk of that capital. Each rung fixes exactly one blindness, and the last one replaces what you ignored with what you had to estimate.

Take two companies both earning 18. Profit cannot separate them at all. On capital of 100 and 200 their returns on capital are 18 and 9 per cent, and a flat 12 per cent charge makes the first look 12.00 better. Charging for risk at betas of 1.80 and 0.60 cuts that gap to 1.40, so the flat rate overstates the difference by a factor of 8.5714, and searching for the boundary, the ranking reverses outright at a beta of 2.0333.

Then the capital figure itself, which is where accounting standards intervene. IAS 38 permits purchased goodwill on the balance sheet and prohibits internally generated goodwill, so a company that bought its customer base for 40 reports capital of 100 while an identical company that built the same base reports 60. Their returns on capital are 18 and 30 per cent on identical economics, and under IFRS nothing unwinds it, because goodwill is impaired rather than amortised. A regime that does amortise closes the gap to zero by year ten, and does so by writing the acquirer down to the organic builder's understated figure. It agrees by becoming equally wrong.

The summary's hardest line says that a risk-adjusted return should leave no excess, and any excess means intangible assets have been created. Read as arithmetic that is a claim you can invert: a company earning 18 where the security market line requires 12.20 per cent has an excess of 5.80, and capitalising it at the required return gives 47.5410 of capital nobody recorded, 47.54 per cent of the reported figure. Add it back and the excess is exactly zero. The same perpetuity formula that explained a discount to net asset value in lecture 2, used in reverse.

And the banking version. Risk-adjusted return on capital is a ratio, and the identity EVA = (RAROC − hurdle) × EC holds exactly, which shows what the ratio discards: the multiplier. A retail unit at 18 per cent on 100 of economic capital and a wholesale unit at 15 per cent on 500 both clear a 12 per cent hurdle, and closing the wholesale unit raises group RAROC from 15.50 to 18.00 per cent while destroying 15.00 of value. That is the internal-capital twin of the internal rate of return defect from lecture 2.

Feynman test Four ways to say whether a company is any good, each charging for one more thing than the last. Profit ignores the money tied up. Return on capital notices the money but not what it costs. Economic value added charges rent on it at one flat rate. And the capital asset pricing model charges more rent for riskier money, which is right in principle and moves the whole argument onto an estimate of riskiness that a year of data cannot pin down well enough to give the answer a sign. Meanwhile the money tied up is whatever the accounts happen to admit, and they admit a customer base you bought and refuse one you built.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that the beta result is not a caveat, it is the answer. A company breaking even at a beta of 2.1667 and estimated from twelve monthly observations has a 95 per cent interval of 0.7911 to 2.9782. The interval contains the break-even point, so the honest statement is not "this company creates value" but "the data cannot tell you". Anybody who reports a risk-adjusted economic value added from a year of monthly data is reporting the midpoint of an interval that spans both verdicts.

The second is that the forward-looking adjustment moves the verdict on its own. The standard shrinkage toward the market, 0.67 × beta + 0.33, means any raw beta between 2.1667 and 2.7413 destroys value unadjusted and creates it adjusted. That is a band 0.5746 wide in which a defensible statistical convention, rather than anything about the company, decides the sign.

The third is that the goodwill asymmetry biases every cross-company comparison in a single direction. An acquisitive company reports more capital than an organically grown one with the same economics, so it reports a lower return on capital and a lower economic value added, permanently. Comparing a serial acquirer against an organic grower on either measure is not noisy, it is systematically wrong, and the 12.00 percentage point gap here is larger than any difference in operating skill a screen is looking for.

And the fourth, which is why this lecture matters to a bank rather than only to an analyst. Risk-adjusted return on capital is the measure most large banks use to allocate capital between business lines, and the identity above shows it cannot rank units of different sizes. The pathology is not hypothetical: a group optimising the ratio shrinks toward its smallest high-return business, and the arithmetic here destroys 15.00 of value, two and a half times the value of the business retained.

What a persistent excess return actually proves

The summary's hardest line is the CAPM one, and it repays being read as arithmetic rather than as a slogan: the risk-adjusted return should equal the required return, extra return should come only from extra risk, and if there is still an excess then INTANGIBLE ASSETS HAVE BEEN CREATED. The logic is that in a competitive market a genuine excess return on capital cannot persist. So if it appears to, either the market is inefficient or you have measured the capital too low. And if the capital is too low, the amount by which it is too low can be recovered by capitalising the excess. At a beta of 1.20 the SML requires 5% + 1.20 x 6% = 12.20%. Adding it back gives a risk-adjusted economic value added of exactly zero, verified to 0.00e+00. AND THE ARITHMETIC IS THE PERPETUITY FORMULA FROM LECTURE 2, USED IN REVERSE. There, a perpetual management fee of 1 per cent at an 8 per cent discount rate was worth 12.50 per cent of net asset value, explaining a discount. Here, a perpetual excess of 5.80 at a 12.20 per cent required return is worth 47.5410 of capital, explaining an apparently impossible return. SAME FORMULA, A FLOW DIVIDED BY A RATE, ONCE FOR A COST AND ONCE FOR A BENEFIT. WHAT THIS DOES TO THE INTERPRETATION IS THE POINT. A HIGH AND PERSISTENT RISK-ADJUSTED RETURN ON CAPITAL IS NOT EVIDENCE OF MANAGEMENT SKILL, IT IS EVIDENCE OF AN ASSET NOT ON THE BALANCE SHEET. Usually a brand, a licence, a distribution network or a set of customer relationships. The measure does not identify skill, it identifies unrecorded capital, and treating the two as the same thing is how a company with a valuable brand gets mistaken for a company with a talented board.

The beta, and where the sign of the answer goes

Once the required return comes from the security market line, the beta decides the verdict. Start by finding the beta at which the verdict changes: beta* = (profit/capital - rf) / MRP = (0.1800 - 0.0500) / 0.0600 = 2.1667 A company earning 18.00 on capital of 100.00 creates value below that beta and destroys it above, and at exactly beta* the risk-adjusted economic value added is zero. NOW ESTIMATE THE BETA. The summary flags three choices: the CALCULATION, the CHOICE OF DATA AND TIME, and the FORWARD-LOOKING ADJUSTMENT. At twenty years it does not, and the standard error has fallen by a factor of 7.9. NOTE WHAT DOES AND DOES NOT IMPROVE. The standard error falls monotonically with the window. THE POINT ESTIMATE DOES NOT: it runs 1.8847, 1.3513, 1.4404, 1.5043, 1.5837, 1.4365, wandering either side of the true 1.50. So a longer window buys you a tighter interval and not a closer estimate in any particular case, which is the same non-monotone convergence found for the linked internal rate in lecture 1. WHAT IS SIMULATED HERE IS ONLY SAMPLING ERROR. Two further sources of beta error are real and not modelled: the intervalling effect, whereby betas estimated from daily data are biased towards zero for thinly traded shares because the price does not move every day, and genuine time variation in the underlying beta, which makes a long window an average of something that has changed. BOTH WIDEN THE PROBLEM RATHER THAN NARROWING IT.
AND THE FORWARD-LOOKING ADJUSTMENT CAN FLIP THE VERDICT ON ITS OWN. Because betas revert towards the market over time, the standard practice is to shrink the raw estimate towards one: beta_adjusted = 0.67 x beta_raw + 0.33 Solving for the raw beta that lands exactly on beta* after adjustment gives 2.7413: raw beta adjusted beta raw verdict adjusted verdict 1.5000 1.3350 creates value creates value 2.0000 1.6700 creates value creates value 2.1667 1.7817 break-even creates value 2.4000 1.9380 destroys value creates value 2.7413 2.1667 destroys value break-even 3.0000 2.3400 destroys value destroys value ANY RAW BETA BETWEEN 2.1667 AND 2.7413 DESTROYS VALUE UNADJUSTED AND CREATES IT ADJUSTED. That is a band 0.5746 wide in which a statistical convention, and nothing about the company, decides the sign of the answer. The adjustment is not wrong. Shrinkage towards the mean genuinely improves out-of-sample prediction, which is the same result that made shrinkage beat the sample covariance matrix in portfolio construction lecture 6. THE POINT IS THAT A DEFENSIBLE STATISTICAL IMPROVEMENT AND A CHANGE OF VERDICT ARE THE SAME OPERATION HERE, so the verdict has to be reported with the convention attached. CHECKING THE LIMIT OF THE EFFECT: the shrinkage cannot flip a company whose raw beta is already comfortably below beta*. At a raw beta of 1.20 the adjusted figure is 1.1340, and both are below 2.1667, so the verdict is unchanged. THE BAND IS NARROW AND IT SITS EXACTLY WHERE THE INTERESTING CASES ARE.

RAROC, and why a ratio cannot rank

Syllabus objective (l) names risk-adjusted return on capital, which is the internal version of the same idea and the measure most large banks use to allocate capital between business lines. Its numerator is a risk-adjusted return, usually profit after an expected-loss charge, and its denominator is economic capital rather than accounting equity. unit risk-adjusted return economic capital RAROC EVA at a 12% hurdle RETAIL 18.00 100.00 18.00% 6.00 WHOLESALE 75.00 500.00 15.00% 15.00 GROUP 93.00 600.00 15.50% 21.00 BOTH UNITS CLEAR THE HURDLE, SO BOTH ADD VALUE. The ratio prefers RETAIL and the value added prefers WHOLESALE by a factor of 2.5. THE IDENTITY THAT EXPLAINS IT, VERIFIED FOR BOTH UNITS TO MACHINE PRECISION: EVA = (RAROC - hurdle) x economic capital SO RAROC IS THE MARGIN AND EVA IS THE MARGIN TIMES THE SIZE. The ratio drops the multiplier, which is precisely the internal rate of return's defect from lecture 2, where five par bonds shared an internal rate of return of exactly 8.0000 per cent and differed 3.7-fold in net present value. THE STRUCTURAL PARALLEL IS EXACT: RAROC IS TO EVA AS THE IRR IS TO THE NPV. AND IT PRODUCES A REAL PATHOLOGY, because RAROC is what managers are paid on: decision group EC group RAROC group EVA keep both units 600.00 15.50% 21.00 close WHOLESALE 100.00 18.00% 6.00 close RETAIL 500.00 15.00% 15.00 CLOSING THE LOWER-RAROC UNIT RAISES GROUP RAROC FROM 15.50 TO 18.00 PER CENT AND DESTROYS 15.00 OF VALUE, which is two and a half times the value of the business retained. A group that maximises the ratio shrinks towards its smallest high-margin business and reports an improving headline the whole way down. MECHANISM TEST: scaling WHOLESALE down to the same economic capital as RETAIL gives RAROC 15.00 per cent against 18.00 and EVA 3.0000 against 6.0000. AT EQUAL SIZE THE TWO MEASURES AGREE, which confirms that size, and only size, is what the ratio discards.
AND THE DENOMINATOR IS NOT ADDITIVE, WHICH IS THE PROBLEM WITH NO CLEAN SOLUTION. Economic capital combines across units by the same square-root rule as any other standard deviation, so the group figure is less than the sum of the parts: correlation sum of standalone EC group EC diversification benefit group RAROC 0.00 600.00 509.90 15.02% 18.24% 0.30 600.00 538.52 10.25% 17.27% 0.50 600.00 556.78 7.20% 16.70% 0.80 600.00 583.10 2.82% 15.95% 1.00 600.00 600.00 0.00% 15.50% At a correlation of 0.30 the standalone figures sum to 600.00 and the group needs only 538.52, so there is 61.48 OF DIVERSIFICATION BENEFIT THAT BELONGS TO NO UNIT IN PARTICULAR. At a correlation of one the benefit vanishes exactly, which confirms the mechanism. SO A UNIT'S RAROC DEPENDS ON WHAT ELSE THE GROUP OWNS, and the allocation of that 61.48 decides each unit's verdict. Allocate it in proportion to standalone capital and every unit looks better by the same ratio. Allocate it by marginal contribution, which is the Euler decomposition used in portfolio construction lecture 10, and the unit that diversifies the group best gets most of the benefit, which is the economically right answer and the one that makes a unit's reported performance depend on its colleagues' positions. THERE IS NO ALLOCATION THAT IS BOTH ADDITIVE AND FAIR TO EACH UNIT IN ISOLATION, which is why capital allocation is a permanent negotiation inside a bank rather than a calculation.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The first thing to carry is that the top of the ladder is not the end of the argument, it is a change of subject. A flat-rate economic value added is wrong in a known direction, biased towards the riskier company by a factor of 8.5714 in the case above. The risk-adjusted version removes that bias and hands you a confidence interval of 0.7911 to 2.9782 on the input that decides the sign. SO THE PROGRESSION FROM PROFIT TO A RISK-ADJUSTED CHARGE TRADES A BIAS YOU CAN SIGN FOR A VARIANCE YOU CANNOT, and which of those you prefer depends on whether you are ranking companies or valuing one.

THE HONEST WAY TO REPORT A RISK-ADJUSTED EVA. the wrong way "risk-adjusted EVA is 2.20, so the company creates value" the right way "the company breaks even at a beta of 2.1667; our estimate is 1.8847 with a 95% interval of 0.7911 to 2.9782, so on one year of monthly data the sign is undetermined" THE SECOND FORM IS LONGER AND IT IS THE ONLY ONE THAT SURVIVES CONTACT WITH THE DATA. reporting the break-even beta alongside the estimate costs one line and turns a false precision into a real one.

The second is the goodwill asymmetry, which is the largest single distortion in this lecture and the one least often adjusted for. IAS 38's prohibition on internally generated goodwill produces a 12.00 percentage point gap in return on capital between two companies with identical economics, permanently, because IFRS impairs goodwill rather than amortising it. The amortising alternative closes the gap by year ten and does so by writing the acquirer down to the organic builder's understated figure, so IT REACHES COMPARABILITY BY BECOMING EQUALLY WRONG. Neither regime gives you the number the measure needs, which is why the summary insists the adjustment be made by hand.

The third is what the summary's CAPM line actually says, which took inverting to see. A persistent risk-adjusted excess of 5.80 capitalises to 47.5410 of capital nobody recorded, 47.54 per cent of the reported figure, and adding it back drives the excess to exactly zero. THAT REFRAMES THE MEASURE ENTIRELY: A HIGH PERSISTENT RISK-ADJUSTED RETURN ON CAPITAL IS EVIDENCE OF AN UNRECORDED ASSET, NOT OF MANAGEMENT SKILL. A brand, a licence, a distribution network. The measure locates missing capital, and reading it as a verdict on the board is the standard mistake.

THE SAME PERPETUITY FORMULA, TWICE, IN OPPOSITE DIRECTIONS. lecture 2 a perpetual COST of 1.00% at an 8.00% rate is 12.50% of NAV -> explains a DISCOUNT to net asset value lecture 3 a perpetual EXCESS of 5.80 at a 12.20% rate is 47.5410 of capital -> explains an apparently impossible RETURN on capital A FLOW DIVIDED BY A RATE, ONCE FOR SOMETHING THE OWNER PAYS AND ONCE FOR SOMETHING THE OWNER RECEIVES. worth noticing because it is the same arithmetic doing the explanatory work in both places, and in both places the alternative explanation on offer was behavioural.

And the fourth, which is the transferable rule. Every measure in this lecture that is a ratio fails to rank, and every measure that is a currency amount succeeds. The identity EVA = (RAROC − hurdle) × EC makes the reason explicit: the ratio is the margin and the value is the margin times the size, so the ratio discards size and nothing else. That is the same defect the internal rate of return had in lecture 2, where five par bonds shared a rate of 8.0000 per cent and differed 3.7-fold in value. RATIOS COMPARE, CURRENCY AMOUNTS RANK, AND A BANK PAYING ITS MANAGERS ON A RATIO WILL SHRINK, here by 15.00 of destroyed value against 6.00 retained.

And a closing note on what the rest of this module inherits. Lectures 4 to 7 all measure a portfolio rather than a company, and they inherit the beta problem intact: the Treynor ratio and Jensen's alpha both divide by or subtract a beta estimated exactly the way it was estimated here. The 0.5746-wide band in which the shrinkage convention decides the verdict does not shrink when the subject changes from a company to a fund. WHAT DOES CHANGE IS THAT A FUND'S BETA CAN BE ESTIMATED FROM ITS HOLDINGS RATHER THAN ITS RETURNS, which is the one route out of the problem and is available to a trustee and not to an outside analyst.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The security market line and the beta come from the capital market line and CAPM, and the shrinkage that flips the verdict here is the same estimator that beat the sample covariance matrix in limitations and Black-Litterman, which is why the adjustment cannot simply be dropped. The perpetuity capitalisation used to size the unrecorded capital is the formula that explained a discount to net asset value in individual asset performance, and the ranking failure of RAROC is the internal rate of return defect from the same lecture. Reading published accounts for the capital figure draws on fundamental analysis and ratios. And the marginal capital allocation named at the end of step 4 is the Euler decomposition built in risk budgets, VaR and tracking error.

Where this goes next. Lecture 4 turns to the portfolio and asks why performance is measured at all and what measuring it does to manager behaviour, which is the same incentive problem the RAROC pathology illustrates at business-unit level. Lecture 5 covers the three benchmark choices and lecture 6 the attribution of a return into sector and stock selection. Lecture 7 is where the beta problem returns in force, since the Treynor ratio and Jensen's alpha both depend on an estimated beta and inherit the confidence interval measured here unchanged. Lecture 8 collects the limitations, and the goodwill asymmetry and the advertising split both belong there as cases where the measure is precise and the input is a judgement nobody can falsify.

Where the bank sits opposite. A bank uses the top of this ladder as its central management tool rather than as an analyst's technique. Economic capital, the denominator of RAROC, is constructed in economic capital, and the allocation problem left unresolved at the end of step 4 is what the internal capital adequacy assessment process in ICAAP has to settle before any business line can be assessed. The hurdle rate itself is the cost of equity set in capital management. And the goodwill question has a sharper form for a bank than for an industrial company, because supervisors deduct goodwill and other intangibles from common equity tier one entirely, so the regulatory capital measure takes the opposite view from the performance measure: the accounts admit purchased goodwill as capital and the capital rules refuse it.

Numbers used here. The four-rung ladder, the goodwill and advertising adjustments, the CAPM line about intangible assets and the security market line are from the "company performance" section of chapter 10 of the ASSA F105 summary in the user's notes, and risk-adjusted return on capital is named in syllabus objective (l). The accounting treatments are externally verifiable: IAS 38 prohibits the recognition of internally generated goodwill, IFRS 3 and IAS 36 require goodwill to be impairment-tested rather than amortised, and the ten-year straight-line amortisation used as the contrast is the US GAAP private-company election under ASU 2014-02. Everything numerical is invented for illustration: a five per cent risk-free rate, a six per cent market risk premium, a flat twelve per cent cost of capital, profits of 18 on capital of 100 and 200, betas from 0.60 to 3.00, an acquisition of 40 against 60 of tangible assets, a 5.00 advertising budget, and two business units at 18 on 100 and 75 on 500 of economic capital. The beta estimates come from 240 months of simulated returns with a true beta of 1.50, a 4.50 per cent monthly market volatility and a 4.67 per cent monthly idiosyncratic volatility, fitted by ordinary least squares with Student's t critical values; only sampling error is simulated, and the intervalling effect and genuine time variation in beta are named in step 4 rather than modelled.
