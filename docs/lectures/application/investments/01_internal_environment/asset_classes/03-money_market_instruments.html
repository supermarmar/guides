Money market instruments: where eight per cent is three different numbers

Under a year to maturity, minimal credit risk, and a reputation for being the dull end of the market. The dullness is misleading in three specific ways: the quotation conventions are mutually incompatible, so a stated rate is not a return until you convert it; the dominant risk is rollover rather than default, which is a liquidity risk wearing a credit risk's clothes; and a benchmark called capital stable carries 164 days of interest rate exposure.

Asset classes 3 of 14 Reading time, 40 minutes Sources: F105 summary chapter 6; Commercial Paper Regulations 1994; SARB market notices on ZARONIA
Step 1

The 30-second version

The money market is the market in debt of under a year, and the source organises it by who is borrowing: governments issue treasury bills and enter repurchase agreements, banks take call, notice and term deposits and issue negotiable certificates of deposit, and companies issue commercial paper and eligible bills and draw on credit facilities and term loans. That taxonomy is correct and it is the wrong first cut, because it groups instruments the investor cannot exit alongside instruments the investor can sell in an afternoon.

The first thing to fix is arithmetic. A 91-day bill quoted at a discount rate of 8.00 per cent has a simple yield of 8.1628 per cent and an effective annual return of 8.4163 per cent. Those are the same instrument, and the gap between the first and last figures is 41.6 basis points, which is larger than the credit spread most money market instruments carry. Compare a bill at "8 per cent" with a certificate of deposit at "8 per cent" and you have compared nothing.

The second thing to fix is the risk. A 91-day claim on an investment grade bank has an expected credit loss too small to see. The borrower must nonetheless refinance it four times a year, and a market that declines to refinance is not making a credit judgement, it is simply absent. Asset-backed commercial paper conduits in 2007 held assets that were mostly fine and funding that vanished, and the same shape appears in every money market accident since.

And the third is the comfortable label. The STeFI Composite, as the source describes it, is 15 per cent call deposits, 30 per cent three-month certificates, 35 per cent six-month and 20 per cent twelve-month, which is a weighted average maturity of 164 days. A 100 basis point rise in rates costs 0.45 per cent of capital, about three weeks of the portfolio's own income. Capital stability is a statement about amortised cost accounting, not about risk.

Feynman test Lending for three months to a government or a large bank is about as safe as lending gets, and almost nothing in this lecture is about whether you get paid. It is about the fact that dealers quote the price three incompatible ways, that you have to do the whole thing again next quarter, and that the index everyone calls cash is really a short bond fund.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that the money market is where an institution's liquidity actually lives, so the framework's marketability dimension stops being a comparative judgement and becomes an operational constraint. A pension fund holding a bond portfolio has a view about marketability; a bank holding a liquid asset buffer has a regulator with an opinion about it. Everything in this lecture reappears in the banking track's treatment of liquidity risk, with the same instruments and much less discretion.

The second reason is the quotation arithmetic, which is the most reliably examined thing in the module and the easiest to get wrong under time pressure. Discount rate, simple yield, effective annual yield and day-count basis are four separate conversions, and each of them changes the answer by tens of basis points on instruments whose entire risk premium is tens of basis points. A candidate who can move between them fluently has an advantage that compounds through the bond and valuation modules.

The third reason is that the money market is where the framework's security dimension is least informative and its term dimension is most. Almost every instrument here is a claim on a government or a large bank, so security discriminates weakly. What discriminates is whether the instrument is negotiable, and for how long the holder is committed. Reorganising the source's taxonomy along that axis, which step 5 does, is the single most useful thing to take from this lecture.

And a fourth reason, about the reference rate underneath all of it. The floating instruments in this market price off an interbank benchmark, and South Africa is part-way through replacing Jibar with ZARONIA, an overnight rate compiled from actual transactions rather than from quoted offers. The source predates the transition entirely. Anyone valuing a floating rate note or a swap over the next few years has to know which rate the contract references and what happens when the old one stops.

Step 4

The detail

What defines the money market, and why the definition is about the lender

The conventional boundary is an original maturity of one year, and the STeFI benchmark the source describes uses a portfolio term of under twelve months. The boundary is arbitrary as boundaries go, and it survives because it marks a genuine change in what the lender is doing. Above a year the lender is taking a view on credit and on the path of rates. Below a year the lender is parking money, and the questions that matter are whether it comes back on the day promised and what it costs to get it back sooner.

That is why the instruments cluster the way they do. Every one of them is a claim on a government, a bank or a large company, because a lender parking money will not do credit work for a 91-day exposure. The compensation for taking any credit risk at all over three months is too small to pay for the analysis, so the market solves the problem by only lending to borrowers who do not need analysing. When that solution fails, it fails suddenly, which is the subject of step 5.

Quotation: the conversions, and the size of the mistake

A discount instrument is sold below face value and redeems at face. The discount rate expresses the discount as a proportion of the face value; the yield expresses the same money as a proportion of the price actually paid. Since the price is lower than the face, the yield is always higher than the discount rate, and the gap widens with both the rate and the term.

A 91-day bill, face 100, quoted at a DISCOUNT RATE of 8.00% on an actual/365 basis. price = 100 x (1 - d t/B) = 100 x (1 - 0.08 x 91/365) = 100 x (1 - 0.019945) = 98.00548 simple yield = (100 - P)/P x B/t = (1.99452/98.00548) x (365/91) = 0.020351 x 4.010989 = 8.1628% effective ann. = (1 + y t/B)^(B/t) - 1 = (1.020351)^4.010989 - 1 = 8.4163% discount rate 8.0000% what the dealer said simple yield 8.1628% what you earn over the 91 days, annualised, +16.3 bp effective annual 8.4163% what it compounds to if rolled four times, +41.6 bp GOING THE OTHER WAY. An NCD quoted 8.00% SIMPLE ADD-ON pays 100 plus interest on 100, so as a discount rate it is only 7.8436%. Therefore: 8.00% quoted as a discount = 8.1628% as an add-on yield 8.00% quoted as an add-on = 7.8436% as a discount rate A bill at "8%" and a certificate at "8%" differ by 16.3 basis points of actual return, and the bill is the better one. Treasury bills and the auction: two ways to want the same thing

Treasury bills are issued by tender, and the source names the two bid types. A competitive bid states both a quantity and a rate, and is filled only if the rate is low enough to be accepted. A non-competitive bid states a quantity and accepts whatever average the auction produces. The two are not merely administrative options; they are opposite trades in certainty.

The repurchase agreement: one loan, documented as two sales

A repo is a sale of securities with an agreement to buy them back at a fixed price on a fixed date, and the source's description is exactly right as far as it goes. Economically it is a secured loan: the difference between the two prices is interest, and the securities are collateral. The legal form matters anyway, because a sale gives the cash lender title, so it can sell the collateral immediately on default rather than joining a queue. That difference in remedy is why repo funding is cheaper than unsecured funding for the same borrower and the same term.

Against a bond with 0.5% daily volatility, a 2% haircut is a 4.0 standard deviation cushion over ONE day and only 1.8 over five. WHICH IS THE WHOLE ARGUMENT FOR DAILY MARGINING. The haircut is not sized to cover the term of the repo; it is sized to cover the time between margin calls. A weekly repo margined weekly is a materially different instrument from a weekly repo margined daily, and the documentation, not the rate, tells you which one you have. The source also names OPEN REPO, with no fixed maturity and notice from either side. That converts a known exposure into a rollover exposure, which is cheaper on the day and is exactly the risk step 5 is about.

Bank deposits, and the one problem the NCD was invented to solve

The bank instruments form a ladder in which the investor is paid for giving up access. A call deposit is withdrawable on demand and pays least. A notice deposit requires a stated period of notice. A term deposit is fixed for a term and imposes a penalty if broken early. Each step up the ladder buys the bank more certain funding, and the bank pays for it, which is the same trade the framework's term dimension describes from the other side.

A negotiable certificate of deposit then breaks the ladder. It is a term deposit that the holder can sell to somebody else, so the bank keeps its fixed-term funding while the investor keeps an exit. Both sides are better off, which is unusual enough to be worth noticing: the improvement comes from separating the bank's funding term from the investor's holding period, and nothing else changed. The source's note that certificates are bearer documents, so the bank pays whoever presents them at maturity, is the mechanism that makes transfer cheap and also the reason a lost certificate is a genuine loss rather than an administrative inconvenience.

Corporate paper, eligible bills, and the regulation that shapes them

Commercial paper is an unsecured promissory note issued by a company, typically to fund working capital, and the source notes that issuers can repurchase their own paper. Being unsecured and short, it is available only to borrowers whose name is good enough to make credit analysis unnecessary, which is why the commercial paper market is small in issuer count and large in value. In South Africa the instrument is shaped by the Commercial Paper Regulations of 1994, issued under the Banks Act, which carve commercial paper out of the prohibition on taking deposits and in exchange impose disclosure requirements and a minimum subscription per investor. The effect is to make it a wholesale instrument by construction rather than by convention.

An eligible bill is a trade bill that a bank has accepted, meaning the bank has added its own name to the obligation. The buyer of the bill is then relying on the bank rather than on the trading company, which is what makes the paper eligible for discount. The source's distinction between recourse and non-recourse factoring is the same question asked of the underlying receivable: does the seller of the invoice remain liable if the debtor fails? The answer is priced, and it is priced steeply.

Credit facilities: paying for an option you hope not to exercise

The source distinguishes an evergreen credit facility, which has no fixed maturity and can be ended by notice from either side, from a revolving credit facility, which has a fixed maturity. Both have a stated limit, a commitment fee, and minimum payments, and the commitment fee is the part worth understanding properly, because it is an option premium and its effect on the borrower's cost depends entirely on utilisation.

The benchmark, and the duration hiding inside a word

The source describes the STeFI Composite as a money market benchmark with a portfolio term under twelve months, built from a call deposit component at 15 per cent, three-month certificates at 30 per cent, six-month at 35 per cent and twelve-month at 20 per cent. Those weights have an arithmetic consequence the label obscures.

That is small and it is not zero, and the accounting is what decides whether anyone ever sees it: amortised cost carries the instruments at their accreted value and reports nothing, right up until redemptions force a sale into the market that moved. Which is why "capital stable" is a statement about a measurement basis and a holding assumption, not about risk. Both parts of it fail together, and they fail on the same day, because the rate rise and the redemption request have a common cause.
What the source predates Floating rate instruments in this market reference an interbank benchmark, and the benchmark is changing. Jibar, the Johannesburg Interbank Average Rate, is compiled from NCD quotes, which means it rests on offers rather than on trades. The South African Reserve Bank has designated ZARONIA, the South African Rand Overnight Index Average, as its successor, computed from actual overnight deposit transactions, and a Market Practitioners Group is running the transition with cessation announced for the end of 2026. Two consequences follow for anything in this lecture. An overnight rate compounded over three months is not the same object as a three-month term rate, because it contains no forward-looking term premium, so contracts have to be rewritten rather than merely re-pointed. And the exact dates in a benchmark transition move, so check the SARB's current market notices rather than trusting a date in a lecture.
Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The money market's risk is rollover, and rollover risk is not a small version of credit risk

Every instrument in this lecture has an expected credit loss too small to be the point. A ninety-one day claim on an investment grade bank, at a one-year default probability of say 0.10 per cent and a forty-five per cent loss given default, has an expected loss of about one basis point over the term. Nobody builds a money market strategy around one basis point. What they are exposed to instead is the requirement to do the transaction again, four times a year, and the fact that the counterparty on the other side is under no obligation to be there.

The distinction matters because the two risks behave differently under stress. Credit risk is idiosyncratic first and correlated second; a diversified book of short-term claims genuinely diversifies it. Rollover risk is correlated by construction, because the event that stops one lender rolling paper is usually the event that stops all of them, and diversifying across borrowers does not help when the problem is on the lenders' side of the market. A portfolio of a hundred issuers' commercial paper is a hundred exposures to one funding market.

The 2007 asset-backed commercial paper episode is the clean illustration. Conduits held long-dated assets funded with paper of thirty to ninety days, and when investors declined to roll, the conduits had to either sell the assets or draw on bank liquidity lines. Most of the assets were eventually money-good. The instrument still failed, because the maturity transformation was the product rather than an incidental feature of it. South Africa has its own version in the 2014 curatorship of African Bank, where a wholesale-funded lender lost access to its funding market and senior wholesale claims took a haircut while retail depositors were protected. The lesson in both cases is the same and it is not about credit analysis: an instrument whose repayment depends on the existence of a market is exposed to that market's existence.

Reorganise the source's taxonomy, and the framework's answers fall out

Grouping by issuer puts a term deposit next to a negotiable certificate, which are the same credit, the same term and completely different assets from the holder's point of view. Grouping by negotiability puts the certificate next to the treasury bill, which is where it belongs, because both give the holder an exit that does not require the borrower's consent.

Money market return is interest, taxed as income, with nothing deferred and no inclusion rate. For the top-rate individual from lecture 1 that turns 8.00% gross into 4.40% net, which is why cash is held for liquidity and for matching, and almost never for return.

Where the source is thin, and what a good answer supplies

Three omissions are worth naming. The source lists the instruments and their features and does not give the quotation conventions, which is the one piece of money market knowledge that is purely technical and purely examinable. It predates the Jibar to ZARONIA transition, as the callout above sets out. And it treats the credit facilities as instruments alongside the tradable paper, when from an investor's point of view a facility is not an asset at all: it is a commitment the lender has written, whose exposure is contingent, and whose risk is that it is drawn precisely when the borrower has become worse. A bank's undrawn facilities behave like written options on its own balance sheet, which is why they carry a capital charge through a credit conversion factor and appear in the liquidity coverage ratio as an outflow assumption rather than as an asset.

The one place the source's framing is actively misleading is the description of a repo as "sell stock with agreement to repurchase at agreed price". That is the legal form and it invites the conclusion that the seller has disposed of the asset. For accounting and for risk the seller has not: the securities stay on the seller's balance sheet, because it retains substantially all the risks and rewards, and the cash received is recorded as a liability. Getting that the wrong way round in an examination answer costs more than the mark, because it propagates into a wrong answer about the seller's leverage.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The eight dimensions and the argument that three of them describe a pairing rather than an asset come from the asset class framework, and the tax row in step 5 uses its figures directly. Discounting, compounding and the difference between simple and effective rates are from time value of money. Instrument mechanics, including the accrued interest conventions that make the repo break-even 1.859 rather than 2.000 per cent, are in financial instruments.

Where this goes next. Lecture 4 extends the same conversions past a year, where the term premium and reinvestment stop being negligible and the discount-versus-yield question becomes the yield curve. Lecture 5 takes the private lending instruments named here, term loans and bridging loans and direct facilities, and asks what changes when there is no market price at all. The benchmark arithmetic in step 4 is the entry point to indices, where the STeFI construction is set out properly alongside the bond and equity index families.

Where the same instruments reappear with less discretion. A bank holds most of this lecture's negotiable half as its liquid asset buffer, and the regulatory treatment is in liquidity risk: the same treasury bills and certificates, with haircuts and outflow assumptions imposed rather than chosen. The repo appears there as the funding instrument it is, and in liquidity management as the mechanism by which a bank turns unencumbered securities into cash without selling them. The credit conversion factors that price undrawn facilities sit in credit risk. The repo rate that anchors the whole market is a policy instrument, described in the South African Reserve Bank.

Numbers used here, and where they came from. The instrument taxonomy and its sub-features, and the STeFI component weights of 15, 30, 35 and 20 per cent, are from the F105 summary chapter 6; index methodologies change, so treat the weights as the source's statement rather than as current JSE methodology. The Commercial Paper Regulations of 1994 under the Banks Act, and the SARB's designation of ZARONIA as Jibar's successor with cessation announced for end-2026, are matters of public record and the transition dates should be checked against current market notices. Everything else is invented for illustration: the 8.00 per cent quoted rate, the auction bids and sizes, the R100m repo with its 2 per cent haircut and 0.5 per cent daily volatility, the facility terms, the invoice and the factoring advances. The arithmetic on all of them is exact, and none of them is a market observation.
