Structured products: a bond, an option, and a fee, sold as one thing

The source gives the recipe in six words: a note guaranteeing return, plus a derivative for upside. Take it apart and every term of the offer becomes arithmetic. The participation rate is an output, not a promise: 114 per cent at an 8 per cent risk-free rate and 40 per cent at 2 per cent, on identical products. The guarantee is an unsecured claim on the issuer. And a range accrual paying a headline 12 per cent has an expected coupon of 7.65 per cent, which is less than the plain bond it was sold against.

Step 1

The 30-second version

A structured product has two components, and the source names them exactly: a note that guarantees a return, and a derivative that provides the upside. Three varieties follow. An equity-linked note pays the greater of the principal and an equity index's performance, built from a zero-coupon bond and a call option, aimed at a risk-averse investor with a bullish view, and paying nothing until maturity. An interest rate-linked note pays a high coupon while a reference rate stays inside a range and a low one or nothing otherwise. A credit-linked note passes a credit default swap premium through a vehicle to an investor.

Decompose the first and every marketing term becomes a calculation. On R100 for five years at an 8 per cent risk-free rate, the zero-coupon bond guaranteeing the principal costs R68.06 and a 4 per cent fee takes R4.00, leaving R27.94 to buy an at-the-money five-year call costing R24.44. The participation rate is therefore 114.33 per cent, and it was never a choice. Each rand of fee costs 4.09 percentage points of participation.

The product is a function of the yield curve rather than of investor appetite. At a 2 per cent risk-free rate the bond costs R90.57, the fee still takes R4.00, and R5.43 buys 40.27 per cent participation. That is why capital-guaranteed notes vanish from low-rate markets and return when rates rise, and it is why every design device (guarantee 90 per cent instead of 100, cap the upside, lengthen the term, average the index) exists to relax the same constraint.

Two costs are invisible in the brochure. Forgoing the index's dividends for five years at a 3 per cent yield costs 15.93 per cent of the investment, half the entire option budget, and it is not a fee. And the guarantee is an unsecured claim on the issuer: at a 1 per cent annual default probability its expected cost is 1.96 per cent of principal, which is comparable to the whole fee load.

Feynman test Give a bank R100 for five years. It puts R68 in a bond so it can hand your R100 back, keeps R4, and spends R28 on a call option. You get whatever the option pays. Everything the salesperson can offer you was decided by the price of those two things and the size of the R4, and if interest rates are low there is nothing left to spend.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that this is the one asset class in the module you can price exactly, from published inputs, in about five lines. Everything else has required an unobservable: a correlation, a growth rate, a valuer's yield, a smoothing parameter. Here the bond price and the option price are observable and the arithmetic closes. That makes it the best available exercise in reading a product's terms as a set of prices.

The second reason is that all three varieties are the investor selling something they were not told they were selling. In the equity-linked note it is the dividends. In the range accrual it is a strip of digital options on the reference rate, so the investor is short volatility. In the credit-linked note it is credit protection on a reference name. A candidate who can identify what has been sold in each case has understood the class.

The third reason is the word "guaranteed", which describes the payoff formula and not the certainty of payment. The 2008 failure of a large investment bank left retail holders of its capital-guaranteed notes as unsecured creditors, which is precisely what the arithmetic says they always were. This is a framework point rather than a scandal: the security dimension asks who owes you the money, and for a structured note the answer is the issuer, whatever the product is called.

And a fourth reason, in the source's favour. Its credit-linked note cash flow description is quantitatively detailed and internally consistent, and checking it is instructive: the numbers imply a fairly priced credit default swap, in which the protection buyer converts a risky 4.5 per cent into a riskless 4.0 per cent for exactly 50 basis points. Verifying a source's arithmetic and finding it correct is worth as much as finding it wrong.

The mental model

One recipe, three variants, and in each of them something the investor has sold.

The credit-linked note, with the source's own figures checked

The source's description of the credit-linked note is the most quantitatively detailed passage in its asset classes chapters, and its numbers hang together. A vehicle is created by the holder of an asset-backed security rather than by the issuer. That holder buys protection from the vehicle by paying a credit default swap premium of 0.5 per cent, while keeping its 4.5 per cent asset income. The vehicle issues a note to investors, invests the proceeds in government bonds at 4 per cent, and pays the investors the government yield plus the premium.

A version collateralised with anything else has smuggled in a second exposure, which is the identical error lecture 10 identified in 2008 catastrophe bond collateral, and it is the reason both markets now specify sovereign collateral explicitly.

Self-assembly, and what the wrapper is actually worth

An investor with access to a bond market and an options market can build the equity-linked note themselves. The comparison is arithmetic, on the same R100, the same term and the same guarantee. route bond cost option cost fee participation packaged retail note R68.0583 R24.4385 4.0% 114.33% private bank version R68.0583 R24.4385 2.0% 122.52% self-assembled R68.0583 R24.4385 0.3% 129.47% THE SELF-ASSEMBLED VERSION GETS 129.47% PARTICIPATION AGAINST 114.33%, a difference of 15.1 percentage points of index exposure for the same money and the same guarantee. That difference is what the wrapper costs. WHAT THE WRAPPER GENUINELY PROVIDES, and it is not nothing: a single instrument, so one line on a statement and one tax treatment no options account, no margin calls, no expiry management access for an investor whose mandate permits notes and prohibits derivatives exactly the payoff promised, with no basis, rounding or rebalancing risk a government-bond leg replaced by the issuer's, which is a cost not a benefit SO THE HONEST QUESTION IS NOT WHETHER STRUCTURED PRODUCTS ARE EXPENSIVE, WHICH THEY PLAINLY ARE, BUT WHETHER THIS INVESTOR COULD HAVE BOUGHT THE PARTS. An institution with a derivatives desk is paying 15 percentage points of participation for convenience it does not need, and should not. A retail investor with no options account is paying for access it cannot otherwise obtain, and for that the price may well be reasonable. The question a trustee should ask is therefore not "is this good value" but "can we replicate it, and what would that cost us". Both halves are answerable and the second one puts a number on the first.

The eight dimensions, for an instrument that is three instruments

SECURITY the ISSUER, and only the issuer. this is the dimension the product's name most obscures: a "capital-guaranteed" note is an unsecured claim, worth 1.96% of principal in expected loss at a 1% issuer PD. YIELD for the equity-linked note, NIL until maturity, which is the source's "no income" and costs 15.93% of the investment in forgone dividends. for the range accrual, a contingent coupon with an expected value of 7.65% against a headline 12%. SPREAD the payoff is a non-linear function of the underlying, so the (variability) distribution is skewed by construction: a floor at the guarantee and participation above it. that is the product's selling point and it is bought with everything in the yield row. TERM fixed, and effectively unbreakable: secondary markets in structured notes are thin and the issuer's bid is the only reliable one, so early exit is at a price the issuer sets. EXPENSES 4% embedded here, which is 12.5% of the risk budget and 15.1 percentage points of participation. the most expensive wrapper in the module relative to what it does. MARKETABILITY poor. one buyer, who is also the issuer and the calculation agent. TAX depends entirely on the wrapper's legal form, which is one genuine advantage: a single instrument with one tax treatment can beat holding a bond and an option separately, and for some investors that alone justifies the structure. CURRENCY whatever the note is denominated in, but the reference index may be foreign, in which case the note is quanto or not and the term sheet must say which. an unhedged reference index inside a domestic-currency note is a currency position the investor did not choose. THE ROW THAT MATTERS MOST IS SECURITY, and it is the one the product's marketing is designed to make you skip. Every other feature of a structured product can be replicated. The issuer credit exposure is the part you cannot avoid by understanding it, only by choosing a different issuer or a different structure.
Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

In every structured product the investor is the seller of something

The recipe the source gives, a note plus a derivative, is accurate and it describes only the half of the trade the investor is shown. Every one of these products requires the investor to give something up, because a derivative has two sides and the issuer is on the other one. Collecting the three cases makes the pattern impossible to miss.

Find it, price it, and the product is understood. AND THE CORRESPONDING WARNING SIGN: a product whose illustration shows only the favourable path is showing the leg the investor bought and hiding the leg it sold. In the range accrual the sold leg is invisible in year one (99.23% inside the range) and dominant by year five (44.90%), so a one-year illustration is not merely optimistic, it is structurally misleading.

Where structured products are genuinely the right answer

Two lectures of arithmetic showing that a product is expensive can slide into an assumption that it is never appropriate, and that would be wrong. The framework's own logic identifies the cases where the wrapper earns its cost, and they are specific rather than rhetorical.

the case why the wrapper wins A MANDATE CONSTRAINT a fund permitted to hold notes and prohibited from holding derivatives can obtain an option payoff only inside a note. the alternative is not a cheaper option; it is no option. A GUARANTEE THE INVESTOR MUST a with-profits fund or a capital-protected DEMONSTRATE, not merely have retail product may need a contractual floor it can point to, which a replicating portfolio does not provide even if it delivers the same payoff. A SINGLE TAX TREATMENT holding a bond and an option separately can produce interest, a capital gain and a derivative outcome with three treatments. one instrument can be materially better after tax, and lecture 1's arithmetic says the difference can reach 2.16 percentage points a year. A PAYOFF THAT CANNOT BE BOUGHT a bespoke basket, an unusual averaging, a IN LISTED FORM long-dated option on an index with no listed expiry beyond two years. the wrapper is the only route. OPERATIONAL SIMPLICITY AT SCALE a small pension fund with no derivatives operations is not paying 15 percentage points for laziness. it is paying to avoid a collateral, margin and expiry process it cannot staff. WHAT IS NEVER A GOOD REASON: a headline rate. The arithmetic in step 4 shows the headline is determined by the yield curve and the fee, so a high headline in a low-rate market is evidence that something has been sold, not evidence of value.

What the source gets right, and where it stops short

Its two-component definition is exactly the right way to hold the whole class in mind, and it generalises to every product invented since. Its equity-linked note description is complete: the maximum formula, the zero-coupon bond, the call option, the risk-averse and bullish investor, and the absence of income. Its range accrual description names the mechanism, two coupons and a range, in a single line. And its credit-linked note cash flows are quantitatively consistent, which is more than most summaries manage.

Three things it stops short of, all of them the same omission in different clothes. It does not say that the participation rate is determined rather than chosen, so it cannot explain why these products come and go with the interest rate cycle. It does not identify what the investor sells in each case, so the range accrual reads as an opportunity rather than as a short volatility position. And it does not note that the guarantee is issuer credit, which is the single most consequential fact about the class and the one that turned academic in 2008.

One small thing worth flagging rather than correcting. The source says the equity-linked note pays "the max of 100 per cent principal amount or performance of equity index". Read literally that is a 100 per cent participation product, and real notes participate at some rate above or below 100 per cent, which is the output computed in step 4. The formula in the source is the special case where participation happens to be one, and a candidate who quotes it without the participation rate has described one product rather than the family.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The eight dimensions, and the tax arithmetic that makes a single wrapper's treatment worth up to 2.16 percentage points a year, are from the asset class framework. The zero-coupon bond pricing and the yield conventions are from government and corporate bonds, and the convertible bond decomposed there is the same exercise as this lecture's equity-linked note, from the issuer's side rather than the buyer's. The special purpose vehicle, the two-way non-recourse and the collateral risk raised again in step 4 are from securitisation and insurance-linked securities. Black-Scholes, the dividend adjustment and digital option payoffs are in derivatives.

Where this goes next. Lectures 12 to 14 cover the pooled vehicles and the newer routes into old asset classes, where the same fee arithmetic recurs in a different wrapper. The derivatives module covers the source's chapter 8 properly, including the option pricing this lecture uses and the credit default swap the credit-linked note is built on. Valuing a structured note in the absence of a market price, which is what an investor needs for a monthly statement, belongs to valuation, and the question of how much of a portfolio should sit in instruments with one buyer is portfolio construction.

Where the bank is on the other side. Every product in this lecture is manufactured by a bank, which holds the opposite position and must hedge it, capitalise it and account for it. The option book sits in the trading book under market risk, where the volatility the range accrual investor sold is a risk factor with a capital charge. The note is unsecured funding for the issuer, which is why structured note issuance appears in liquidity management rather than in its product range by accident. The credit default swap inside the credit-linked note creates the exposure measured in counterparty credit risk. And selling a complex principal-at-risk product to a retail investor is a conduct matter, governed in South Africa by the market conduct regime in the FSCA.
