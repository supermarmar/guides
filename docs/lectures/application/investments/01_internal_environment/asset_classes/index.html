Investments, internal environment

Asset classes

Fourteen lectures on the investable universe. The first lecture builds the comparison framework, eight dimensions along which any holding can be described, and every lecture afterwards applies it to one class. The discipline matters more than the list: an examiner asking about infrastructure wants the same eight answers as one asking about commercial paper, and the framework is what stops the answer becoming a collection of remembered facts.

How to use this

Read lecture 1 first and keep it open. Lectures 2 to 14 each work the framework across one class, so they can be read in any order once the framework is fixed. All fourteen are built. Four of them were built without any source text at all, because the summary leaves the heading empty (index funds and ETFs, hedge funds) or has no section for the class (conventional bonds, listed equity), and each of those says so in its own source note.

Course sequence

The framework and the benchmark

  • builtLecture 1: the asset class framework. Security, yield, spread, term, expenses, marketability, tax, currency. Five dimensions belong to the asset and three to the pairing of asset with investor, which is why the ranking of assets is not stable across holders
  • builtLecture 2: the risk-free rate. Three conditions no instrument meets, reinvestment risk quantified over an 18-year liability, whether a government bond is default-free, constructing a local rate, the real rate and its three contaminations, and what a 100 basis point error costs in five different applications. Built externally

Lending: short and long

  • builtLecture 3: money market instruments. Treasury bills and the auction, repos and haircuts, deposits and NCDs, commercial paper, eligible bills and factoring, credit facilities as written options, term and bridging loans. Includes the quotation conversions the source omits and the duration hiding in a capital-stable benchmark
  • builtLecture 4: government and corporate bonds. The four yields and bootstrapping, index-linked bonds and the indexation lag, the seniority waterfall, covenant headroom, convertibles as volatility positions, callable bonds and yield to worst. Chapter 6 has no bond section, so most of this is external
  • builtLecture 5: private debt. Direct lending, mezzanine, venture, infrastructure and distressed debt. The spread decomposed into cost and premium, prepayment as a borrower-held option, the equity kicker priced, and why appraisal smoothing halves the reported volatility

Owning

  • builtLecture 6: listed equity. The residual claim, preference shares, the leverage cascade from sales to earnings, four index weighting schemes giving four answers, chain-linking through a rights issue, and the implied equity risk premium. Chapter 6 has no equity section either, so most of this is external
  • builtLecture 7: private equity and the fund lifecycle. Venture, development and restructuring capital, leveraged buyouts, the seven-stage cycle, and the arithmetic the source omits: the J-curve, the catch-up band where the manager takes 100 per cent, undrawn commitments at 0.48 times NAV, and survivorship bias worth 36 percentage points

Real assets

  • builtLecture 8: property and infrastructure. The capitalisation identity and property's 22-year duration, term and reversion, hardcore and froth, voids and the number of buildings needed for a stable income, why a NAV discount is not an asset discount, the regulated asset base, and availability against demand-based infrastructure
  • builtLecture 9: commodities and currency. Physical types, the three-part futures return and what contango costs, the convenience yield, production weighting, covered interest parity and what a hedge really costs, the carry trade as a written option, and the break-even correlation that "low correlation" has to clear

Repackaged claims

  • builtLecture 10: securitisation, ABS and insurance-linked securities. Tranching computed from the single-factor model, the correlation sensitivity that decides who bears the loss, thinness as leverage, over-collateralisation against excess spread, the SPV and two-way non-recourse, and catastrophe bonds priced at a multiple of expected loss
  • builtLecture 11: structured products. Equity-linked, interest rate-linked and credit-linked notes, each priced from its parts: the participation rate as an output of the yield curve, forgone dividends, the guarantee as issuer credit, the range accrual as sold volatility, and the source's own CLN cash flows verified

Buying it through someone else

  • builtLecture 12: collective schemes, index funds and ETFs. The three legal forms and their exit mechanisms, partial replication tracking error, the ETF arbitrage band, the closed-end discount as part of the return, the total cost cascade, securities lending, and an open-ended fund as a bank. Built externally: the source leaves the heading empty
  • builtLecture 13: hedge funds. Global macro, market-neutral, event-driven, multi-strategy and contrarian, each labelled with its real exposure; why zero net exposure is not zero risk, carry as a call option on volatility, the path dependence of the high-water mark, the index bias stack, and how much of the alpha reaches the investor. Built externally: the source leaves Features empty
  • builtLecture 14: new ways of investing in old asset classes. The framework used as a test for innovation: secondaries against the J-curve, co-investment, synthetic against physical exposure, factor indices, fractionalisation, listed private assets and new collateral, each classified by the dimension it changes and the one it degrades

Source material

Chapters 6 and 7 of the F105 summary, covering syllabus objective (i). Note that those chapters have no section on conventional government and corporate bonds: they run from money market instruments straight to private debt. Lecture 4 is therefore built from the index families in chapter 5, the yield taxonomy and pricing in chapter 9, and external material for seniority, covenants, callability and convertibles. Chapter 7 leaves hedge funds, index funds and ETFs as bare headings with no content beneath, so lectures 12 and 13 are written from Lhabitant's Handbook of Hedge Funds, the CFA Institute alternative investments readings, JSE ETF listing requirements and ASISA collective scheme statistics. The risk-free rate is absent from the summary entirely and is built from Damodaran's NYU Stern working paper, Bodie, Kane and Marcus chapter 5, and SARB bond market data.

Assumes financial instruments, which already covers bond structure, the yield curve, duration and convexity. This module covers the investor's selection angle, not the mechanics.
