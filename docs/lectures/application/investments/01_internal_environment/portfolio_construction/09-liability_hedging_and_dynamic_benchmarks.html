Step 1

The 30-second version

Lecture 8 established that duration matching minimises surplus variance and leaves an exposure to the curve changing shape. This lecture closes that gap, and then finds a larger problem underneath it.

The fix is to stop matching one number. Bump seven points on the curve separately with tent-shaped shocks, measure the money sensitivity to each, and match all seven. That requires an eighth condition and an eighth instrument, because matching sensitivities pins the slope and leaves the level free: seven slope conditions alone leave a present value excess of 0.3453 per cent. With eight instruments the twist range falls from 1,061.20 on a duration-matched barbell to 14.59, and a full forty-instrument cash flow match takes it to exactly zero.

Then the hedge ratio, where the standard justification is wrong. Adding an unhedgeable component to the liability does not lower the optimal hedge ratio: the surplus volatility minimum sits at exactly 1.00 and the residual there is exactly the unhedgeable volatility, 3.00 per cent. Unhedgeable risk sets a floor on the residual, which is a different statement. Schemes hedge below 100 per cent because they need return, and a scheme requiring 2.00 per cent of surplus return cannot hedge above 0.5556. That is a funding decision wearing investment clothes.

And the implementation. A swap overlay hedges the whole liability using 15 per cent collateral, freeing 85 per cent of assets to earn an equity return. A 130 basis point rise costs the swap 26 per cent of assets and reduces the liability by the same 26, so the surplus is unchanged at every yield level tested. The collateral shortfall is 11 per cent, payable in cash within days. Economically hedged and operationally short of cash, which is the arithmetic of the UK episode of September 2022.

Feynman test Matching one summary number protects you against the whole interest rate picture moving together. Match seven points along the curve instead and almost everything goes; match every single payment and all of it goes, at the cost of owning nothing but bonds. Then the practical trap. You can rent the protection instead of buying it, which frees your money to earn more, but renting means posting a deposit and topping it up whenever the position moves against you. When rates jumped in 2022 the protection did exactly its job, the debt shrank by precisely what the hedge lost, and schemes still had to find large sums in cash within days.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that the residual risk after hedging is a choice about how many instruments to hold, and the schedule is steep at the start and flat later. Going from one instrument to eight removes 98.6 per cent of the twist range; going from eight to forty removes the remaining 1.4 per cent and costs the ability to hold any other asset class. Almost every real mandate sits at the first step and not the second, and that is the correct place to sit.

The second is that the level-versus-slope distinction is a live source of error rather than a technicality. Matching seven money sensitivities exactly still leaves the portfolio 0.3453 per cent richer than the liability, which shows up as a permanent 5.19 surplus that has nothing to do with rates. On a 1.5 billion liability that is 5 million of unexplained surplus, and a reconciliation that cannot account for it will attribute it to something else.

The third is the hedge ratio argument, which is stated wrongly in most trustee papers I have seen. Unhedgeable longevity or inflation basis risk does not justify hedging less of the rate risk, and the arithmetic is unambiguous: the minimum sits at 1.00 and moving away from it in either direction raises the residual symmetrically. The honest reason for hedging less is that the scheme needs the equity premium to close a deficit, which is a statement about funding that should be made by the people responsible for funding.

And a fourth. September 2022 is the most instructive recent event in this whole module because nothing failed except the plumbing. The hedges performed exactly as designed, surpluses were protected, and schemes were nonetheless forced sellers into a falling market because a collateral mechanism converts market risk into liquidity risk. Market risk and liquidity risk are managed by different people against different limits, and the interface between them is where the loss occurred.

Step 3

The mental model

Close the twist gap with more instruments, get the hedge ratio argument the right way round, and then meet the collateral problem that no amount of hedging solves.

Implementation: physical bonds against a swap overlay

A scheme with assets of 100, liabilities of 100 and a liability duration of 20: liability PV01 = L x D x 0.0001 = 0.2000 per basis point ROUTE ONE, PHYSICAL BONDS. Buy twenty-year duration outright. This uses the entire 100 of assets, achieves a 100 per cent hedge ratio, earns the bond return of 7.00 per cent, and leaves nothing for return-seeking. From the previous section, a scheme needing any surplus return at all cannot do this. ROUTE TWO, A SWAP OVERLAY. The liability is a moving target

A static hedge assumes a static liability. If the discount rate is set by regulation or by an accounting standard rather than by the market, the liability can move without any market variable moving, and no market instrument hedges it. A change in the prescribed basis is a pure unhedgeable shock. QUANTIFY THE TWO THAT GET THE MOST ATTENTION, on the same forty-year liability: a 100 basis point FALL in rates PV rises 211.2789 (14.0419%) one extra year of life expectancy PV rises 9.1719 ( 0.6096%) ratio 23.0 times RATES DOMINATE LONGEVITY BY TWENTY-THREE TIMES. The reason is discounting: an extra year of payments falls forty years out and is worth very little today, while a rate move revalues every payment at once. LONGEVITY IS GENUINELY UNHEDGEABLE AND GENUINELY SMALL. Both halves of that matter. It deserves attention because there is no market solution at scale, and it does not deserve priority over rates, because it is one twenty-third of the size. A SCHEME THAT ARRANGES A LONGEVITY SWAP BEFORE HEDGING ITS RATE EXPOSURE HAS MIS-ORDERED ITS PRIORITIES, and the commentary on pension risk routinely inverts these two.
Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The first thing to carry is the shape of the residual risk schedule, because it decides how a mandate should be written. One instrument to eight removes 98.6 per cent of the twist range; eight to forty removes the remaining 1.4 and forfeits every other asset class. Marginal instruments are enormously valuable up to about eight and nearly worthless afterwards. THAT IS WHY A REAL LIABILITY-DRIVEN MANDATE SPECIFIES KEY-RATE BUCKETS RATHER THAN A DURATION NUMBER OR A CASH FLOW SCHEDULE, and a mandate written either of the other two ways is on the wrong part of the curve.

WHAT EACH ADDITIONAL INSTRUMENT BUYS. from -> to twist range removed cost 1 -> 8 1061.20 -> 14.59 98.6% seven more bonds, still an overlay 8 -> 40 14.59 -> 0.00 1.4% every other asset class THE SECOND ROW IS WHERE THE ARGUMENT ABOUT FULL CASH FLOW MATCHING SHOULD BE HAD, and it should be had in those terms rather than as a debate about whether hedging is a good idea.

The second is the level-versus-slope error, which I would not have found without checking it. Matching seven money sensitivities exactly leaves the portfolio 0.3453 per cent richer than the liability, because sensitivities are derivatives and the present value is the function; pinning every derivative leaves the constant free. The general rule is that N sensitivity constraints plus the level need N plus one instruments. A MANDATE WRITTEN TO MATCH SIX KEY RATES WITH SIX BONDS CARRIES A LEVEL MISMATCH NOBODY HAS BUDGETED FOR, and on a large scheme it is millions of pounds of surplus attributed to whatever else is convenient.

The third is that the hedge ratio argument is made backwards in most trustee papers. Unhedgeable longevity does not justify hedging less of the rate risk: the minimum sits at exactly 1.00, the residual there is exactly the unhedgeable volatility, and the curve is symmetric so over-hedging costs the same as under-hedging by the same amount. The real reason to hedge less is that the scheme needs the equity premium, and a scheme requiring 2.00 per cent of surplus return cannot hedge above 0.5556. NAMING IT AS A FUNDING DECISION CHANGES WHO SHOULD MAKE IT.

TWO ARGUMENTS FOR HEDGING BELOW 100 PER CENT. argument status "we cannot hedge longevity anyway" FALSE: the optimum is still 1.00 "we need return to close the deficit" TRUE, and it caps h at 1 - req/(EQ-BD) ONLY THE SECOND IS AN ARGUMENT. the first is a description of the residual, which is a different quantity from the optimum and gets confused with it constantly.

The fourth is September 2022, and the reason it belongs in a lecture on portfolio construction rather than one on operations. Every hedge performed exactly as designed. The surplus column is zero at a 50 basis point rise and zero at 300. The schemes were nonetheless forced sellers into a falling market, because a 15 per cent collateral buffer on twenty-year duration is exhausted by 75 basis points and the only quickly saleable assets were the ones causing the problem. A COLLATERAL MECHANISM CONVERTS MARKET RISK INTO LIQUIDITY RISK, and no amount of getting the hedge right addresses that, because the hedge being right is what generates the call.

And the fifth. Rates dominate longevity by twenty-three times on this liability, and the commentary on pension risk routinely inverts them, because longevity is interesting and rates are not. The discipline worth taking from the whole module is to size a risk before deciding how much attention it deserves, and to be suspicious when the risk receiving the most discussion turns out to be one twenty-third of the size of the one receiving least. THAT IS ALSO TRUE OF THE DISCOUNT BASIS, which can move the liability with no market variable moving at all and has no hedge whatsoever.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The surplus objective, the result that duration matching minimises surplus variance, and the twist exposure this lecture closes are all from asset-liability modelling, whose Redington barbell reappears here as the worst of the four approaches on both curve measures. Present values, Macaulay and modified duration and convexity are from fixed income pricing, and the curve whose seven points are bumped separately is built in yield curves and bootstrapping. The interest rate swap used as the overlay instrument is priced in swap and swaption valuation, and the collateral and margin mechanics that turn the hedge into a liquidity problem are from derivatives. The equity premium that the hedge ratio trades away is measured in the capital market line and CAPM.

Where this goes next. Lecture 10 measures what is left after hedging as a risk budget, and the natural unit becomes tracking error against the liability rather than against a market index, which is the reporting change lecture 8 argued was necessary. The collateral result feeds directly into the portfolio management module, where the liquidity of the return-seeking portfolio stops being a secondary consideration and becomes the binding constraint on how much leverage the overlay can carry. In performance measurement, a liability-driven fund's attribution has to be run against the dynamic benchmark described here, since an attribution against a market index would report the hedge as a large negative contribution in every year rates rose.

Where the bank sits opposite. This lecture is the closest correspondence in the whole track. A bank's key-rate bucketing under IRRBB is the same construction as step 4, and the BCBS 368 requirement to run non-parallel scenarios exists for exactly the reason measured here. The collateral result is the investment-side statement of what a bank calls the interaction between market and liquidity risk, and the September 2022 sequence, a correct hedge generating a cash call that forces sales that worsen the price, is the same mechanism the liquidity coverage ratio was designed to survive. A bank holds a buffer of high quality liquid assets against precisely this; a pension scheme running an overlay generally does not, and the arithmetic in step 4 shows how large that buffer would need to be.
