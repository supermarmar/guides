The capital market line: a benchmark 98.9 per cent as efficient as the true one manufactures a full point of alpha

Adding cash straightens the frontier into a line and collapses ten thousand portfolios into one. Every investor holds the same risky sleeve, 15.10 equity, 70.31 bonds, 14.60 property, and varies only the cash. Then two things break it: the borrowing rate, which fails one-fund separation for every risk aversion between 2.89 and 7.36, and the market proxy, which reproduces the entire empirical failure of CAPM with no mispricing whatsoever.

Portfolio construction 4 of 10 Reading time, 60 minutes Sources: Tobin (1958); Sharpe (1964); Black, Jensen and Scholes (1972); Roll (1977)
Step 1

The 30-second version

Add a risk-free asset to the three risky ones and the efficient frontier stops being a curve. It becomes the straight line from the risk-free rate through a single point of tangency, the capital market line, and every point on it is a mix of cash and that one risky portfolio.

The tangency portfolio has a closed form, the inverse covariance matrix applied to the excess returns and normalised to sum to one, which at a 5 per cent risk-free rate gives 15.0990 per cent equity, 70.3051 bonds and 14.5959 property, a return of 8.7474 and a Sharpe ratio of 0.5250. It maximises the Sharpe ratio over the whole frontier, confirmed against a grid search to within 9.4 parts in a trillion. The consequence is one-fund separation, stronger than lecture 2's two-fund version: risk aversion now determines only the cash weight, so every investor holds identical risky weights.

Push that to equilibrium and the tangency portfolio must be the market portfolio, which delivers the security market line. Expected return is the risk-free rate plus beta times the market premium, and it holds exactly for all three assets, to within 2.8 times ten to the minus seventeen, because it is the tangency first-order condition rearranged rather than an empirical claim.

Two things then break it, and the second is the serious one. Borrowing dearer than lending puts a kink in the line and destroys one-fund separation for every investor with a risk aversion between 2.8906 and 7.3561, who holds neither tangency portfolio. And using the wrong market proxy, an 80/10/10 index rather than the true tangency, fits a security market line with an intercept 2.7954 percentage points too high and a slope 0.5593 too flat, which is the exact shape Black, Jensen and Scholes reported in 1972, produced here with every true alpha equal to zero by construction.

Feynman test Once you can hold cash, the whole menu of risky mixes collapses to one. Find the single risky portfolio with the best reward-per-wobble, then decide how much of your money to put in it and how much to leave in the bank. Timid people leave more in the bank; bold people borrow to put in more than they have. Nobody needs a different set of shares. And if everybody does this, the one portfolio everybody holds must be the whole market, which tells you what each share should return. The catch is that you cannot see the whole market, and using a nearly-right stand-in makes perfectly fairly-priced things look like bargains.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that this is where portfolio theory becomes an asset pricing theory. Lectures 1 to 3 took expected returns as given and asked what to hold. This one runs the logic backwards: if everybody optimises and markets clear, the expected returns cannot be arbitrary, because they must be exactly what makes the market portfolio the tangency portfolio. Beta is not a new idea bolted on, it is the same first-order condition read from the other end.

The second is that one-fund separation is the intellectual justification for the entire index fund industry, and this lecture shows precisely how fragile it is. It requires a single rate for borrowing and lending, which no investor faces. Introduce a 200 basis point spread and separation survives only for the very cautious and the very aggressive, with everybody in a wide middle band holding a bespoke portfolio.

The third is Roll's critique, which is usually stated as a philosophical objection and is in fact a quantitative one. Because the true market portfolio is unobservable, every test of CAPM is a joint test of the model and the proxy. This lecture measures the damage: a proxy with 98.9 per cent of the tangency's Sharpe ratio already manufactures 0.9697 per cent of spurious annual alpha, and the asset with the lowest proxy beta shows the largest positive one. The low-beta anomaly and Roll's critique are the same arithmetic.

And a fourth, which matters for anybody who has to explain a performance report. If alpha is measured against a benchmark that is not mean-variance efficient, alpha is not evidence of skill. The 2.4677 per cent alpha the bonds allocation shows here is entirely an artefact, and no amount of statistical significance would help, because the number is not noisy, it is wrong.

The mental model

Step 4

The detail

The tangency portfolio

Add a fourth asset that is risk free: it returns rf with certainty, so its variance and all its covariances are zero. Any mix of cash and a risky portfolio P sits at m = rf + a (m_P - rf) sigma = a . sigma_P with a the weight in P, so eliminating a gives a STRAIGHT LINE from (0, rf) through P with slope (m_P - rf)/sigma_P, which is the SHARPE RATIO of P. The investor wants the steepest such line, so the problem reduces to finding the risky portfolio of MAXIMUM SHARPE RATIO. That portfolio is the TANGENCY PORTFOLIO: w_T = V^-1 (mu - rf.1) / 1' V^-1 (mu - rf.1) The numerator is the inverse covariance matrix applied to the excess returns; the denominator just makes the weights sum to one. What is supposed to happen to a mispriced asset

Suppose property is expected to return one percentage point more than the SML says, 10.5000 rather than 9.5000 per cent, so it has an alpha of +1.00. Recomputing the tangency: property weight 14.5959% -> 25.6276% a move of +11.03 percentage points equity weight 15.0990% -> 10.3811% bonds weight 70.3051% -> 63.9914% Sharpe ratio 0.5250 -> 0.5532 A ONE PERCENTAGE POINT ALPHA MOVES THE OPTIMAL WEIGHT BY 11.03 PERCENTAGE POINTS. In the theory that is the mechanism by which prices correct: everybody wants more property, the price rises, the expected return falls, and equilibrium is restored when the alpha is gone. IT IS ALSO LECTURE 2's INSTABILITY WEARING A DIFFERENT HAT. The same sensitivity that makes the optimiser useless in practice is what makes the equilibrium argument work in theory. You cannot have one without the other: an optimiser that ignored a 1 per cent alpha would not push prices back.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The first thing to carry is that the security market line is not a discovery. It holds to 2.8 times ten to the minus seventeen on all three assets here, and it would hold to machine precision on any inputs whatsoever, because it is the tangency first-order condition with both sides divided by the market variance. Anybody who presents the exactness of the SML in a worked example as evidence for CAPM has misunderstood what they computed. THE CONTENT OF CAPM IS NOT THE SML, IT IS THE CLAIM THAT THE OBSERVED MARKET INDEX IS THE TANGENCY PORTFOLIO, and that claim is the one Roll showed cannot be tested.

WHAT IS AN IDENTITY HERE AND WHAT IS AN ASSUMPTION. statement status the CML is a straight line IDENTITY, from mixing with a zero-variance asset the tangency maximises the Sharpe ratio IDENTITY, it is the definition the SML holds exactly against the tangency IDENTITY, the first-order condition rearranged every investor holds the tangency ASSUMPTION: one rate, homogeneous beliefs the tangency IS the observable market index ASSUMPTION, and untestable THE FIRST THREE ARE FREE AND THE LAST TWO CARRY EVERYTHING. Most of what is taught as CAPM is the free part.

The second is the leverage row, which is the hinge of the whole lecture and is usually skipped. At a risk aversion of 1 the model recommends 735.61 per cent in the tangency, financed by borrowing 635.61 per cent of wealth. No investor can do that. So an investor who wants CML-like returns and cannot borrow does the only thing left: they tilt towards high-beta assets instead. That bids up high-beta prices, depresses their expected returns, and flattens the security market line. Frazzini and Pedersen's betting-against-beta mechanism is not an add-on to CAPM, it is the direct consequence of the one row of the model's own output that nobody can act on.

The third is the zero-gain result, which is the most elegant thing in the lecture and the least practically important. The value of having cash available is exactly zero for exactly one investor, at a risk aversion of 7.356129, and the discriminant of the quadratic vanishes identically rather than approximately. It is worth doing because it is a check: an algebraic identity that survives to eighteen decimal places is strong evidence the rest of the machinery is wired correctly. Treat it as a test of the arithmetic rather than as a result about investing.

THE SAME NUMBER, 7.356129, ARRIVING THREE WAYS. route value the risk aversion minimising the gain from cash 7.356129 solving m*(lambda) = m_T directly, D/(A.m_T - B) 7.356129 the upper boundary of the curved segment under a spread 7.3561 THEY MUST AGREE, because all three ask when the no-cash optimum coincides with the tangency. Confirming that they do, to 2.3e-14, is how you find out whether the code is right.

The fourth is the benchmark result, which is the one to remember in a professional setting. A proxy retaining 98.88 per cent of the true portfolio's Sharpe ratio already manufactures 0.9697 per cent of annual alpha, and pushing the proxy much further wrong adds surprisingly little on top. The damage is nearly all in the first few per cent of inefficiency. So "our benchmark is approximately right" is worth almost nothing, and the alpha in a performance report is a statement about the benchmark at least as much as about the manager. NO SAMPLE SIZE FIXES THIS, because the error is not sampling error.

And the fifth. Notice which assumption did the least work. CAPM is usually attacked on homogeneous beliefs, which is obviously false and turns out to matter less than it should, because heterogeneous investors still hold portfolios that aggregate to the market. It is the borrowing rate and the unobservable market that do the damage, and both are mundane institutional facts rather than deep epistemological problems. THE MODEL IS BROKEN BY PLUMBING, NOT BY PHILOSOPHY.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The closed-form frontier, the A, B, C and D scalars and two-fund separation are from the opportunity set and the efficient frontier, whose weight-instability result reappears here as the equilibrium mechanism. The mean-variance utility maximised along the line, the certainty equivalent used to price the risk-free asset, and the risk aversions tabulated throughout are from indifference curves and the optimum portfolio, and the covariance algebra from portfolio risk and return. CAPM as one member of the asset pricing family, alongside the consumption model and the Hansen-Jagannathan bound, is in asset pricing models and factors, and the risk-free rate the CML starts from is the short end of the curve built in yield curves and bootstrapping.

Where this goes next. Lecture 5 takes the empirical failure measured here as its starting point: if a single beta does not explain the cross-section, add factors, which is arbitrage pricing theory and then the Fama-French family. Lecture 6 attacks the input instability that both this lecture and lecture 2 keep running into, and Black-Litterman's central trick is to invert the CAPM logic of this lecture, reading implied expected returns out of observed market weights rather than optimising weights from forecast returns. Lecture 7 uses the market portfolio as the anchor for strategic asset allocation, and lecture 10 returns to beta as a risk-budgeting tool. In the performance measurement module, every appraisal ratio inherits the benchmark problem quantified in step 4.

Where the bank sits opposite. A bank meets beta as the cost of equity rather than as an allocation input, since the CAPM discount rate is what sets the hurdle a transaction must clear on its allocated regulatory capital. The unobservable-market problem has a direct analogue in the bank's own capital models: an A-IRB single-factor model posits exactly one systematic driver, unobservable in the same way and proxied in the same way, with the asset correlation prescribed by the regulator precisely because it cannot be estimated reliably. And the leverage constraint that breaks the CML here is a formal ratio for a bank, so the betting-against-beta mechanism is not a market curiosity but a description of what a leverage-constrained balance sheet is forced to do.

The separation theorem is Tobin (1958), "Liquidity Preference as Behavior Towards Risk", Review of Economic Studies 25(2); CAPM is Sharpe (1964), "Capital Asset Prices", Journal of Finance 19(3), with Lintner (1965) and Mossin (1966); the zero-beta form is Black (1972), "Capital Market Equilibrium with Restricted Borrowing", Journal of Business 45(3). The three-asset universe carried from lectures 2 and 3 is invented for illustration: expected returns of 11.5, 8.0 and 9.5 per cent, volatilities of 19, 7 and 13, correlations of 0.15, 0.55 and 0.25, a 5 per cent risk-free rate, a 7 per cent borrowing rate and an 80/10/10 market proxy, all chosen to make the mechanisms visible rather than to represent any market.
