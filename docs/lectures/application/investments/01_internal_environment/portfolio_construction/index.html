Portfolio construction

Ten lectures on the step between knowing what each asset is worth and knowing what to hold. Valuation prices assets one at a time; construction is where they stop being independent, because what matters to a portfolio is not an asset's own risk but how it moves against everything else already held. That single shift, from variance to covariance, is what the whole module is about, and it is where the exam concentrates.

How to use this

Read this module in order. It is the one place in the track where each lecture is a strict prerequisite for the next: risk and return gives the covariance algebra, the opportunity set uses it, the efficient frontier is the boundary of the opportunity set, indifference curves choose a point on it, and CAPM is what happens when everybody does this at once. Skipping ahead does not work.

The whole module is a provenance gap rather than a quality one. Chapter 11 of the source summary is a heading with nothing beneath it, so every lecture here is written from primary academic literature and the standard textbooks rather than from the summary, and each names what it drew on in its closing section. Unbuilt lectures carry a gap badge for the same reason.

Course sequence

Mean-variance theory

  • builtLecture 1: portfolio risk and return. Return linear in the weights and variance not, the diversification floor at the single-asset volatility times the square root of the correlation, 93.63 per cent of a fifty-asset variance coming from covariance, the condition that an asset helps if and only if its correlation times its volatility is below the portfolio's, and a 111.06 per cent volatility rise when a 0.15 correlation is stressed to 0.85
  • builtLecture 2: the opportunity set and the efficient frontier. The closed form verified against direct computation to 7e-17, why only the upper half is efficient, two-fund separation confirmed to 2e-15 including levered mixes, a fourth asset worth 3.37 to 9.79 per cent of volatility, a no-shorting constraint costing exactly nothing until it binds, and the global minimum variance weights unchanged across a plus or minus 4.25 per cent swing in the equity expected return
  • builtLecture 3: indifference curves and the optimum portfolio. The optimal return as the minimum-variance return plus D over lambda A, the tangency verified against the frontier slope to 5.5e-12, a factor-of-two error in risk aversion costing 0.4163 per cent of certainty equivalent against lecture 2's swing of a whole portfolio from one standard error, quadratic utility satiating at a 25 per cent return, and the equity premium puzzle's risk aversion implying a 7.41 per cent equity weight

Equilibrium and factors

  • builtLecture 4: the capital market line and CAPM. The tangency in closed form confirmed against a grid search to 9.4e-13, one-fund separation collapsing every investor onto the same 15.10/70.31/14.60 risky sleeve, the security market line exact to 2.8e-17 because it is the first-order condition rearranged, the risk-free asset worth exactly nothing at exactly one risk aversion, and an 80/10/10 proxy manufacturing the whole Black-Jensen-Scholes shape with every true alpha zero
  • builtLecture 5: arbitrage pricing theory and factor construction. The arbitrage portfolio earning 2 per cent for zero cost and zero exposure, and its information ratio of 0.0408 needing 16,200 assets to reach 3.0 against the JSE's 300, equicorrelated residuals helping rather than hurting because a zero-cost portfolio cancels anything common, block correlation stalling the volatility at 24.49 per cent, a covariance matrix singular at 500 assets on twenty years of data, and 316 published factors implying 16 false positives at the 5 per cent level
  • builtLecture 6: limitations of mean-variance and the Black-Litterman response. A simulation in which the optimiser beats equal weighting on 54.07 per cent of samples and loses to it on the mean, the weight linear in the estimated mean with a slope of 9.9310 so the one-sigma swing of 0.4219 exceeds the correct weight of 0.2777, gross leverage of 22.31 on a universe whose true optimum is long-only, optimal shrinkage falling from 0.8 to 0.4 as data accumulate, and Black-Litterman moving the portfolio along the view portfolio and nowhere else

From theory to a real allocation

  • builtLecture 7: strategic asset allocation. Terminal dispersion growing as the square root of the horizon while annualised dispersion shrinks, so both sides of the time-diversification argument are right, Samuelson verified against simulation at every horizon with a spread equal to the grid step, the rebalancing return claim at 1.07 standard errors against a real 9.81 per cent volatility reduction, a five per cent band capturing 94.89 per cent of that at 63.20 per cent of the turnover, and an unrebalanced fund breaching a 75 per cent cap on 56.59 per cent of paths while its mean drift reaches only 67.77
  • builtLecture 8: asset-liability modelling. Cash at 0.50 per cent asset volatility and 17.47 per cent surplus volatility while index-linked runs 14.47 and 3.45, so the two almost swap places, a surplus-variance minimisation reproducing a duration of 17.80 against the liability's 18.00 with no notion of duration in the code, the asset-optimal and surplus-optimal portfolios differing by 18 years of duration at the same target return, and Redington's barbell immune to every parallel shift while carrying 3.16 times the twist range it was built to improve on
  • builtLecture 9: liability hedging and dynamic liability benchmarks. Key-rate matching cutting the twist range from 1,061 to 14.59 and cash flow matching to exactly zero, the discovery that N sensitivity conditions plus the level need N plus one instruments so seven slopes leave a 0.3453 per cent present-value excess, the surplus-volatility minimum sitting at exactly 1.00 so unhedgeable risk does not lower the optimal hedge ratio, and a 15 per cent collateral buffer exhausted by 75 basis points while the surplus stays unchanged at every yield level
  • builtLecture 10: risk budgets, value at risk and tracking error. Euler contributions summing exactly to 3.5e-18 so a risk budget can actually be allocated, the risk-versus-weight gap surviving statistically identical sleeves and turning out to be a concentration effect that vanishes only when weights are equal, effective breadth of 35.7 out of 100 at a pairwise correlation of 0.99 rather than the collapse to one usually claimed, VaR failing subadditivity on two four per cent default bonds where diversifying raised the measured risk from zero to fifty, and a model wrong by a factor of two passing a 250-day backtest 76.37 per cent of the time
