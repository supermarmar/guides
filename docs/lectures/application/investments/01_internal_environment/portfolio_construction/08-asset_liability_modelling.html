Cash has 0.50 per cent asset volatility and 17.47 per cent surplus volatility

Introduce a liability and the risk ranking inverts. Cash, the least volatile asset, becomes one of the riskiest holdings, and index-linked bonds swap places with it: 14.47 per cent asset volatility against 3.45 per cent surplus volatility. The portfolio that minimises asset risk has a duration of minus 0.32 and the one that minimises surplus risk has 17.68, and an optimiser given nothing but a covariance matrix reproduces duration matching on its own.

Portfolio construction 8 of 10 Reading time, 60 minutes Sources: Redington (1952); Sharpe and Tint (1990); Leibowitz
Step 1

The 30-second version

Every lecture so far has maximised the utility of wealth. A pension fund, an insurer and a bank do not care about wealth, they care about surplus, which is assets minus the present value of what they owe. Changing the objective from wealth to surplus changes almost every answer in the module.

The liability is an asset with a negative weight. Model it as a set of factor exposures like anything else, and an eighteen-year pension liability against a one per cent yield volatility has a 17.46 per cent volatility of its own, higher than long bonds and higher than index-linked. Surplus variance is then the asset variance, minus twice the covariance with the liability, plus the liability variance, and it is the middle term that overturns everything.

Cash has an asset volatility of 0.50 per cent and a surplus volatility of 17.47, because it has no covariance with the liability at all. Index-linked bonds have 14.47 and 3.45. The two almost exactly swap places. The risk-free asset of lecture 4 is not risk free here, and it is not a subtlety: the numeraire changed, so the asset that is certain in wealth terms is maximally uncertain in surplus terms.

Minimise surplus variance and the optimiser returns a portfolio with a duration of 17.80 against the liability's 18.00, without ever being told what duration is. Duration matching is surplus variance minimisation when rates are the dominant factor. Then Redington's immunisation delivers exactly what it promises and no more: a duration-matched barbell with more convexity than the liability has a non-negative surplus for every parallel shift tested, and loses 130.22 on a curve twist, with 3.16 times the twist sensitivity of the single zero it was built to improve on.

Feynman test Stop asking how much your savings wobble and start asking how much the gap wobbles between what you have and what you owe. Now the bank deposit is the dangerous holding, because the debt grows and moves and the deposit does not, so the gap swings by the full amount of the debt. The safe holding is whatever moves in step with the debt, which for a pension promise means very long bonds. And matching the timing protects you against rates all rising or falling together, which is the one thing it was designed for, while leaving you exposed to short rates going up as long rates go down.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that almost every institution that matters has liabilities, so the wealth-maximising framework of lectures 1 to 7 is the special case rather than the general one. A defined benefit pension fund, a life insurer, a general insurer and a bank all run surplus, and for all four the asset-only efficient frontier is measuring the wrong thing.

The second is the governance failure this creates, which is entirely predictable and extremely common. At an eight per cent target return the asset-optimal portfolio here has an asset volatility of 3.92 per cent and a surplus volatility of 17.34; the surplus-optimal portfolio has 17.66 and 2.00. A committee monitoring asset volatility will conclude the liability-aware portfolio is four times riskier, and will be wrong about the only risk the fund actually bears.

The third is that the classical actuarial machinery and the modern portfolio machinery turn out to be the same thing, which is worth knowing because they are usually taught in separate courses by separate people. Redington wrote down immunisation in 1952 in terms of present values and durations; a surplus variance minimisation using a covariance matrix arrives at a duration of 17.80 against a liability duration of 18.00 with no notion of duration anywhere in the code. They are one result in two vocabularies.

And a fourth. Immunisation protects against precisely the risk it models and creates exposure to the risk it does not. The convexity that makes a barbell immune to parallel shifts comes from spreading cash flows apart, and spreading cash flows apart is exactly what makes a portfolio sensitive to the curve changing shape. That is not a flaw to be patched, it is a structural feature, and it is why real liability-driven mandates hedge multiple points on the curve rather than one duration number.

THE MECHANISM IS STRUCTURAL AND NOT A DEFECT TO BE PATCHED. Convexity comes from spreading cash flows apart in time. A portfolio with cash flows at two years and thirty years has enormous convexity and, for exactly the same reason, enormous exposure to the two-year and thirty-year rates moving differently. YOU CANNOT BUY THE CONVEXITY WITHOUT BUYING THE TWIST RISK. THAT IS WHY REAL LIABILITY-DRIVEN MANDATES DO NOT HEDGE ONE DURATION NUMBER. They hedge the sensitivity to several points on the curve separately, which is key-rate duration or PV01 bucketing, and a full cash flow match removes the problem entirely at the cost of giving up every other asset class. The choice between them is the subject of lecture 9.

Funding level, and why the surplus is the wrong variable on its own

measure definition what it is used for SURPLUS A - L the balance sheet, in money FUNDING RATIO A / L solvency reporting, scale free surplus volatility sd(r_A - r_L) the risk to be managed contribution risk sd of required inflow what the sponsor actually feels THE FUNDING RATIO IS THE MORE USEFUL VARIABLE FOR A REAL SCHEME, because a 100 million surplus means something different on a 200 million liability than on a 20 billion one, and because a scheme's obligations are proportional to its size. BUT NOTICE WHAT NEITHER MEASURE CAPTURES. The sponsor does not experience the surplus, it experiences the CONTRIBUTIONS required to repair a deficit, and those are a function of the surplus, the recovery period agreed with the trustees and the sponsor's own ability to pay. A scheme can be well hedged in surplus terms and still deliver unacceptable contribution volatility if the recovery period is short. SO THE OBJECTIVE IN A REAL ASSET-LIABILITY STUDY IS USUALLY NOT MINIMUM SURPLUS VARIANCE. It is something closer to minimising the probability that contributions exceed what the sponsor can afford, subject to a target funding level by a target date, which is a multi-period problem with a path-dependent objective and no closed form. EVERYTHING IN THIS LECTURE IS THE SINGLE-PERIOD APPROXIMATION TO THAT, and it is used because it is tractable and because it gets the direction of every important decision right.
Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The first thing to carry is that the risk-free asset is a property of the numeraire and not of the instrument. Lecture 4 built an entire equilibrium theory on cash being risk free, and cash is risk free only if the objective is wealth. Change the objective to surplus and cash acquires a 17.47 per cent volatility while a fifteen-year index-linked bond drops to 3.45. NOTHING ABOUT EITHER INSTRUMENT CHANGED. The question changed, and the answer to "which asset is safe" changed with it.

ASKING WHETHER AN ASSET IS RISKY IS INCOMPLETE WITHOUT NAMING THE OBLIGATION IT IS HELD AGAINST.

The second is that Redington and Markowitz are the same result, and I would not have believed how exactly until the optimiser produced a duration of 17.8046 against a liability duration of 18.0000 with no notion of duration in the code. Actuarial immunisation and mean-variance optimisation were developed independently in the same year in different literatures, and the reason they converge is that duration is a first-order sensitivity to the dominant factor, and matching a first-order sensitivity is what minimising variance does when one factor dominates. THE TWO VOCABULARIES OBSCURE A SINGLE PIECE OF MATHEMATICS.

The third is the governance point, and it is the one most likely to matter in a professional setting. The liability-aware portfolio has four and a half times the asset volatility and one eighth of the surplus volatility. Any monitoring framework that reports asset volatility, tracking error against a market index, or peer-group comparison will flag it as the riskier choice. Those are the three most common things reported to trustees. A CORRECT DECISION WILL LOOK WRONG IN EVERY STANDARD REPORT, and defending it requires changing the report rather than winning the argument.

The fourth is that immunisation is honest about its own scope in a way its users often are not. Redington claimed protection against a small parallel shift and delivered it exactly: the barbell's surplus is non-negative for every parallel shift tested, up to three percentage points. He did not claim protection against the curve changing shape, and there is none, with the barbell carrying 3.16 times the twist range of the portfolio it improved on. The convexity that buys the immunity is the same property that creates the exposure, because both come from spreading cash flows apart. IT IS NOT A LOOPHOLE, IT IS THE PRICE.

And the fifth. Everything in this lecture is single-period, and the real problem is not. A sponsor does not experience the surplus, it experiences the contributions demanded when the surplus is negative, and those depend on the recovery period the trustees agree and the sponsor's capacity to pay. A scheme can be well hedged in surplus variance and still deliver unacceptable contribution volatility. THE SINGLE-PERIOD FRAMEWORK EARNS ITS PLACE BY GETTING THE DIRECTION OF EVERY IMPORTANT DECISION RIGHT, not by being the right model, and lecture 9 takes the next step towards the problem as it is actually posed.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The covariance algebra and the observation that an asset helps a portfolio through its covariance rather than its own volatility are from portfolio risk and return, and this lecture is that observation applied with the liability inside the matrix. The constrained minimisation and the closed-form frontier are from the opportunity set and the efficient frontier, and the extreme unconstrained weights are the corner solutions measured in limitations of mean-variance and the Black-Litterman response. The claim that cash is the risk-free asset, which this lecture overturns, is the capital market line and CAPM, and the factor structure used to build the covariance matrix is from arbitrage pricing theory and factor construction. Duration, convexity and present value are from fixed income pricing, and the curve whose parallel shifts and twists are applied here is built in yield curves and bootstrapping. The horizon question that lecture 7 could not settle from preferences alone is answered here by the term of the obligations.

Where this goes next. Lecture 9 takes up the twist exposure this lecture ends on, replacing a single duration match with key-rate hedging and cash flow matching, and introduces the dynamic liability benchmark that makes the hedge a moving target rather than a fixed portfolio. Lecture 10 measures the residual against a risk budget, where the tracking error is now relative to the liability rather than to a market index, which is the reporting change step 5 argues is necessary. The performance measurement module inherits the same problem: an attribution against a market benchmark tells a liability-driven fund almost nothing.

Where the bank sits opposite. This lecture is the investment-side statement of what a bank calls interest rate risk in the banking book, and the correspondence is close enough to be worth reading in both directions. A bank's economic value of equity is exactly the surplus of this lecture, its repricing gap analysis is duration matching, and its non-parallel-shift scenarios under BCBS 368 exist precisely because of the twist result in step 4. The bank's version of the governance trap is that a duration-matched balance sheet can show volatile net interest income while a stable-income balance sheet carries economic value risk, which is the same two-numeraire problem with earnings and value in place of assets and surplus. The liquidity side adds the constraint this lecture ignores, that the hedge must be fundable through the period it is held.
