Arbitrage pricing theory: the arbitrage needs 16,200 assets, and the JSE has about 300

APT prices assets without a market portfolio, without utility and without normality, which is why it is the theoretically cleaner model. Its arbitrage is not an arbitrage: on a 20 per cent residual volatility and a one point mispricing, the trade has an information ratio of 0.0408 across three assets and needs 16,200 to reach 3.0. The reason factor models are used anyway has nothing to do with pricing.

Step 1

The 30-second version

CAPM failed its empirical tests in lecture 4, and one response is to blame the single factor rather than the framework. Arbitrage pricing theory assumes returns are generated by some set of common factors plus asset-specific noise, then prices assets by ruling out arbitrage rather than by aggregating optimising investors.

The argument is elegant. Build a portfolio whose weights sum to zero, so it costs nothing, and whose factor exposures also sum to zero, so it carries no systematic risk. If expected returns are not linear in the factor loadings, that portfolio earns something from nothing. On three assets with loadings of 0.5, 1.0 and 1.5, the weights 1, minus 2 and 1 do it, and a one percentage point mispricing on the middle asset yields exactly 2.00 per cent for zero cost and zero exposure.

It is not an arbitrage. The portfolio still holds residual risk, and at a 20 per cent residual volatility that supposedly riskless 2 per cent has a standard deviation of 48.99 per cent, an information ratio of 0.0408. The risk falls as one over the square root of the number of assets, so reaching an information ratio of 3.0 needs 16,200 assets. The JSE lists roughly 300, on which the same trade scores 0.41. APT is an asymptotic statement, and it says nothing about any individual asset at any finite size.

And the assumption everybody quotes is the wrong one. Equicorrelated residuals do not break the argument, because a zero-cost portfolio is immune to anything common to all assets: the variance actually falls by one minus the correlation. What breaks it is correlation structured so that the weights do not sum to zero within a correlated block, which stalls the residual volatility at 24.49 per cent however many assets are added. So the requirement is not uncorrelated residuals but no omitted factor aligned with the trade, and only the second of those is unfalsifiable.

Feynman test Suppose a few big forces push all share prices around, and each share also wobbles on its own. Buy and sell in proportions that cancel both your outlay and your exposure to every big force. What is left should be worth nothing, so whatever it does earn tells you how the market prices those forces. The snag is that the private wobbles never quite cancel. With three shares they barely cancel at all, and you would need more shares than exist in most countries before the leftover risk was small enough to call the trade a sure thing. The theory is right in a world with infinitely many shares, and we do not live there.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that the practical case for factor models has almost nothing to do with APT. A full covariance matrix on 500 assets needs 125,250 parameters and a three-factor model needs 2,006, a reduction of 62 times. More decisively, estimating a covariance matrix from T monthly observations gives a matrix of rank at most T, so with 500 assets and even twenty years of monthly data the sample matrix is singular and cannot be inverted at all. Every optimisation in lectures 2 to 4 would fail. A factor model always produces an invertible matrix. That, not the pricing theory, is why the industry runs on factors.

The second is that APT and CAPM fail in opposite directions and neither failure is comfortable. CAPM makes a strong claim, that expected returns depend on one specific portfolio, which Roll showed cannot be tested. APT makes a claim so weak it cannot be falsified, because it never says what the factors are or how many. Choosing between them is choosing which kind of untestability to prefer.

The third is that the freedom to choose factors has been used, extensively. Harvey, Liu and Zhu counted 316 published factors by 2012, and testing that many at the conventional 5 per cent level should throw up about 16 successes by chance alone. Their recommended correction, a t-statistic above 3.0 rather than 1.96, is not free: a genuine factor with a true t of 3 is detected 85 per cent of the time at the old hurdle and exactly 50 per cent at the new one.

And a fourth, which matters for anybody signing off a risk system. The best rank-K approximation minimises the error on the matrix as a whole, and this lecture shows it can make an individual covariance pair worse while doing so: the largest off-diagonal error rises from 0.1681 at one factor to 0.2416 at two. Since one badly-fitted covariance can dominate an optimisation, an aggregate goodness-of-fit statistic is the wrong validation test.

The detail

The factor model and the arbitrage

APT starts from a RETURN-GENERATING MODEL rather than from investor behaviour: r_i = E[r_i] + b_i1.f_1 + ... + b_iK.f_K + e_i with E[f_k] = 0, E[e_i] = 0, cov(f_k, e_i) = 0 and the e_i uncorrelated across assets. The b_ik are FACTOR LOADINGS. Note what is absent: no market portfolio, no utility function, no distributional assumption. Take one factor, three assets, and a factor premium of 4.00 per cent over a 5.00 per cent risk-free rate: asset loading APT price actual alpha A 0.50 7.0000% 7.0000% 0.0000% B 1.00 9.0000% 8.0000% -1.0000% C 1.50 11.0000% 11.0000% 0.0000% BUILD A PORTFOLIO THAT COSTS NOTHING AND CARRIES NO FACTOR RISK. Two conditions, three unknowns, so a one-parameter family of solutions. Take w = (1, -2, 1): cost sum w = +0.000000000000000 factor exposure sum w.b = +0.000000000000000 expected profit sum w.E[r] = +2.000000% ZERO COST, ZERO SYSTEMATIC RISK, AND 2.00 PER CENT OF RETURN. On the FAIR returns the same portfolio earns 1.388e-17, which is zero. The whole of APT is that observation run backwards: if no such opportunity exists, expected returns MUST be linear in the loadings. E[r_i] = rf + b_i1.lambda_1 + ... + b_iK.lambda_K The weights are not unique, and the direction matters: w = (1, -2, 1) cost 0 exposure 0 profit +2.0000% w = (2, -4, 2) cost 0 exposure 0 profit +4.0000% w = (-1, 2, -1) cost 0 exposure 0 profit -2.0000% w = (3, -5, 2) cost 0 exposure -0.5 NOT a valid arbitrage portfolio Doubling the weights doubles the profit, which is why any violation implies an UNBOUNDED opportunity and why the equilibrium argument has teeth. Reversing the signs reverses the trade, so the direction depends on the sign of the mispricing.

Why it is not an arbitrage

The portfolio has no factor exposure. It still holds RESIDUAL risk. With a 20 per cent residual volatility on each asset and uncorrelated residuals: residual variance sum w^2 . se^2 = 6 x 0.04 = 0.240000 residual volatility = 48.9898% expected profit = 2.0000% information ratio = 0.0408 THAT IS NOT A RISKLESS PROFIT. IT IS A POOR TRADE. The assumption the textbooks get wrong

The standard statement is that APT requires UNCORRELATED residuals. Test it. Under EQUICORRELATION at rho, for any weights w, var = se^2 [ rho.(sum w)^2 + (1 - rho).sum w^2 ] and the arbitrage portfolio has sum w = 0 BY CONSTRUCTION, so the first term vanishes identically and the variance FALLS by a factor of (1 - rho): N rho=0 rho=0.05 rho=0.25 rho=0.50 30 15.4919% 15.0997% 13.4164% 10.9545% 300 4.8990% 4.7749% 4.2426% 3.4641% 3000 1.5492% 1.5100% 1.3416% 1.0954% EQUICORRELATED RESIDUALS DO NOT BREAK THE ARGUMENT. THEY HELP IT. The reason is worth stating plainly: a component common to every asset IS a factor, by definition, and the portfolio has no factor exposure. APT already handles factors, so calling one a residual changes nothing. WHAT DOES BREAK IT is correlation STRUCTURED so that the weights fail to sum to zero within a correlated block. Put the residuals in blocks by loading group, correlated at rho inside a group and uncorrelated across: N rho=0 rho=0.05 rho=0.25 rho=0.50 30 15.4919% 18.6548% 27.9285% 36.3318% 300 4.8990% 11.9499% 24.8596% 34.8138% 3000 1.5492% 11.0580% 24.5316% 34.6583% NOW IT STALLS. At rho = 0.25 within blocks the residual volatility converges to 24.4949 per cent however many assets are added, because each block behaves as a single asset with weight equal to its group total, and the group totals are not individually zero, only their sum is. limiting residual volatility, rho = 0.05 10.9545% limiting residual volatility, rho = 0.25 24.4949% limiting residual volatility, rho = 0.50 34.6410% SO THE CONDITION APT ACTUALLY NEEDS IS "NO OMITTED FACTOR ALIGNED WITH THE TRADE", NOT "UNCORRELATED RESIDUALS". Those are different requirements, and the difference matters: the first can in principle be checked by testing whether residuals cluster, and the second is a statement about an unspecified set of unobserved variables and cannot be checked at all.

CAPM MAKES A STRONG CLAIM THAT CANNOT BE TESTED. APT MAKES A WEAK CLAIM THAT CANNOT BE FALSIFIED. Those are different failures and neither is a good position, but the second is worse in one specific way: it does not constrain anything, so it cannot be wrong, so it cannot help.

Routes (a) and (c) find them in the data, which is where the next section's problem starts.

The factor zoo

APT does not say how many factors there are or what they are. The profession has taken the invitation. HARVEY, LIU AND ZHU (2016) counted 316 published factors claiming to price the cross-section by 2012. Testing that many candidates at the conventional 5 per cent level: expected false positives at alpha = 0.05 15.8 expected false positives at alpha = 0.01 3.2 SIXTEEN "DISCOVERIES" BY CHANCE ALONE. Their proposed correction is a Bonferroni adjustment: per-test p = 0.05 / 316 = 1.582e-04, which is t = 3.7776 and their headline recommendation is a minimum hurdle of t greater than 3.0 rather than 1.96. THE CORRECTION IS NOT FREE. For a factor whose TRUE t-statistic is t0, the power of the test: true t power at 1.96 power at 3.00 power lost 1.0 0.1685 0.0228 0.1458 2.0 0.5160 0.1587 0.3573 3.0 0.8508 0.5000 0.3508 4.0 0.9793 0.8413 0.1380 5.0 0.9988 0.9772 0.0216 A GENUINE FACTOR WITH A TRUE t OF 3 IS FOUND 85 PER CENT OF THE TIME AT THE OLD HURDLE AND EXACTLY 50 PER CENT AT THE NEW ONE. Raising the bar trades false positives for false negatives, and there is no setting that avoids both. HOU, XUE AND ZHANG (2020) retested 452 anomalies: 65 per cent fail to replicate at t greater than 1.96 about 158 survive 82 per cent fail at t greater than 3.00 about 81 survive AND THE PROBLEM IS LARGER THAN MULTIPLE TESTING. The Bonferroni calculation assumes 316 independent tests on independent data. They are not independent: the profession has reused the same CRSP and Compustat histories for forty years, so the effective number of independent trials is unknown and the true false-discovery rate is uncomputable rather than merely large. THAT IS WHY OUT-OF-SAMPLE AND OUT-OF-MARKET EVIDENCE CARRIES MORE WEIGHT THAN ANY t-STATISTIC.
Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The first thing to carry is that the case for factor models and the case for APT are two different cases, and only one of them is strong. APT is an asymptotic pricing result whose arbitrage needs 16,200 assets to become convincing on this calibration. Factor models are used because a sample covariance matrix on 500 assets is singular at any realistic sample length, and B.F.B' plus a positive diagonal is not. A practitioner who defends a factor risk model by appealing to Ross has picked the weaker of the two available arguments.

THE TWO ARGUMENTS, SEPARATED. claim strength returns are linear in factor loadings asymptotic only; IR 0.41 on 300 assets factors reduce 125,250 parameters to 2,006 true, and only at large N factor matrices are always invertible TRUE ALWAYS, and decisive the factors have economic meaning depends entirely on the construction route THE THIRD ROW IS THE ONE THAT SURVIVES CONTACT WITH A REAL PORTFOLIO. It is also the one never taught as the reason.

The second is the residual correlation result, which is the part of this lecture I would most want a candidate to have understood. The textbook condition, uncorrelated residuals, is neither necessary nor the real requirement. Equicorrelation makes the trade safer, not riskier, because a zero-cost portfolio is immune to anything common to all assets, and anything common to all assets is a factor by definition. What kills the argument is residual correlation clustered so that the weights fail to cancel within a cluster, which stalls the volatility at 24.49 per cent no matter how many assets are added. THE CONDITION IS ABOUT THE ALIGNMENT BETWEEN THE OMITTED STRUCTURE AND THE TRADE, NOT ABOUT CORRELATION AS SUCH.

The third is that APT's generality is bought at a price nobody names at the time. Dropping the market portfolio, the utility function and normality is a genuine gain, and the cost is that the theory constrains nothing until the factors are specified. A theory that can absorb any failure by respecifying its inputs is not falsifiable, and unfalsifiable is not the same as true. Between CAPM's untestable strong claim and APT's unfalsifiable weak one, the honest position is that neither delivers what an asset pricing theory is supposed to deliver.

HOW EACH THEORY ANSWERS A FAILED TEST. theory the failure the available answer CAPM betas do not explain returns "your index is not the market" (Roll) APT the factors do not price assets "you used the wrong factors" BOTH ANSWERS ARE ALWAYS AVAILABLE AND NEITHER CAN BE REFUTED. that symmetry is not usually pointed out, because the two theories are normally taught by different people.

The fourth is the reconstruction result, which is a small technical point with a large practical consequence. Eckart-Young guarantees the best rank-K approximation and guarantees it in the Frobenius norm, which is a statement about the sum of squared errors over the whole matrix. Going from one factor to two here reduces that sum and raises the worst single off-diagonal error from 0.1681 to 0.2416. Since portfolio optimisation is exquisitely sensitive to individual covariances, a risk model validated on an aggregate fit statistic can be worse where it matters while looking better overall.

And the fifth. The factor zoo is usually presented as a multiple-testing problem with a multiple-testing solution, and it is worse than that. Bonferroni assumes independent tests on independent data. Forty years of the same two databases means the effective number of independent trials is unknown, so the false-discovery rate is not merely large, it is uncomputable. NO t-STATISTIC HURDLE FIXES A DATA-REUSE PROBLEM, which is why evidence from a market or a period nobody was mining carries weight that no amount of significance in the original sample can match.

Step 7

Connections

What this assumes. The empirical failure of the single-factor model that motivates APT is measured in the capital market line and CAPM, whose Roll critique this lecture sets against APT's own unfalsifiability. The covariance matrix whose parameter count and rank drive the practical argument is from portfolio risk and return, and the optimisation that requires its inverse and is destabilised by any single badly-fitted entry is the opportunity set and the efficient frontier. The asset pricing family this belongs to is surveyed in asset pricing models and factors. Eigenvalues, eigenvectors and the rank of an estimated matrix are from mathematics; multiple testing, statistical power and the false discovery rate from statistics.

Where this goes next. Lecture 6 takes the estimation problem that both this lecture and lecture 2 keep returning to and addresses it directly, including shrinkage of the covariance matrix and Black-Litterman for the expected returns. The factor structure built here is the input to the risk budgeting of lecture 10, where a portfolio's risk is decomposed by factor rather than by holding, and to the attribution machinery of the performance measurement module, where the benchmark problem from lecture 4 and the factor problem from this one compound. In portfolio management, factor tilts are the language in which an active mandate is written.

Where the bank sits opposite. A bank's regulatory credit capital rests on a single-factor model, and lecture 4's parallel deepens here: the A-IRB formula assumes exactly one systematic driver with all residual default risk diversified away, which is precisely the asymptotic argument tested in step 4, and the regulator's response to the finite-portfolio problem is the granularity adjustment and the concentration risk add-on under Pillar 2. The rank problem is why market risk models cannot estimate a full covariance matrix across thousands of risk factors and use prescribed correlations instead. And the factor zoo has a direct supervisory analogue in model risk governance, where a scorecard selected from hundreds of candidate variables faces exactly the multiple-testing problem Harvey, Liu and Zhu describe, which is why out-of-time validation samples are mandatory rather than advisory.
