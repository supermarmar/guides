Visualisation design

The sixth sub-module of the data analysis course, and the first to leave the plotting library behind. Lecture 5's visualisation lecture answered "which seaborn call produces the right chart"; this sub-module answers the questions underneath that one: why a human eye reads certain shapes and colours more accurately than others, what a chart's history teaches about the choices still being made today, and how to build a chart, and a narrative around it, that survives a sceptical audience's first question. Six lectures move from the history of the discipline, through the perceptual science of why some encodings simply work better than others, through colour and accessibility, audience and storytelling, the taxonomy-versus-grammar debate that structures every modern charting library, and close with the plain, practical discipline of a figure that earns its place in a report.

How to use this

Read in order, though lectures 2 and 3 (perception, colour) can be swapped without much loss. Lecture 1's history is not decoration: several of the specific mistakes flagged in lectures 2 and 4 were first documented, and first fixed, generations before either of this section's authors were born, and knowing that lineage is part of taking the discipline seriously. Lecture 5's taxonomy-versus-grammar material assumes lecture 4's audience material and lecture 2's perceptual material as background. Lecture 6 closes the course with the specific, practical rules that turn everything before it into a figure fit for print. This sub-module sits alongside, not beneath, lecture 5's visualisation lecture: that lecture is the API reference, this one is the reasoning that should decide which API call to make. Status badges show what is built (built) or planned.
