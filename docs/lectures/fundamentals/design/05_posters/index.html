Module 5 · Design track

Posters and one-pagers

Two lectures on a single sheet with no speaker, no scroll and no second page. A conference poster read by someone walking past, or an executive one-pager that has to survive being the only thing anyone reads. The constraint is absolute and it does most of the design work.

What this module is

Everything else in this track has an escape hatch. A report has a next page, a deck has an appendix, a web page has a link and a speaker can be asked a question. A single sheet has none of these. Whatever the reader takes away has to be on it, visible from wherever they are standing, in the time they are prepared to give it.

That produces a design problem the other modules do not have: you are serving three different readers who are the same person at three distances. Someone deciding from two metres whether to stop. Someone who has stopped and is giving you thirty seconds. Someone genuinely interested who will spend three minutes. A sheet built for only the third reaches none of them, and that describes almost every conference poster ever printed.

The two lectures divide by problem. The first is distance and hierarchy: how big, how few words, and how to serve all three readers at once. The second is encoding: Isotype's rules for showing quantity with symbols, why the modern infographic breaks them constantly, and the structure of a business one-pager.

Prerequisites

All eleven foundations lectures. Lecture 9 (hierarchy) and lecture 10 (data graphics) do most of the work; lecture 8 (whitespace) supplies the budget argument.

Module sequence

Distance, then encoding

  • builtLecture 22: designing for distance. The three-depth model, the legibility table, Mike Morrison's Better Poster and what it actually redistributes, the word budget as a design token, and whitespace allocated before anything is placed.
  • builtLecture 23: Isotype and the one-pager. Neurath and Arntz's rule that quantity is shown by repetition and never by scaling, why scaling fails in two directions at once, Minard as the standard nobody meets, and the one-pager's block structure.

Token specification

The building blocks are in 05_posters/DESIGN.md: A-series sheet geometry with print resolutions, the distance-legibility table, the Better Poster zones, pictogram sizing, and the one-pager block proportions.

A gap this module is honest about The distance-legibility table is practitioner consensus from institutional poster guidance rather than a measured standard, and two commonly quoted rules of thumb disagree with it and with each other. Lecture 22 uses the table, says why, and tells you not to use the formulae.

Where this reappears

The three-depth model
Lecture 22. The same idea as the blogs module's layered page and the presentation module's ghost deck: let the reader stop at any depth and leave with something coherent.
Repeat, never scale
Lecture 23. The most-violated rule in modern data graphics, and a direct consequence of area sitting fifth in the Cleveland and McGill ranking.
Word budget
A hard token here, 800 words on a poster and 400 on a one-pager. The presentation module's 75 words per slide is the same instrument on a different sheet.
