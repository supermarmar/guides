Module 0 · Design track

Foundations

How perception, type, colour and layout actually work. Eleven lectures that supply the vocabulary and the evidence every medium module then assumes. Take them in order; the sequence is cumulative and each one is used by the next.

What this module is

Everything in the seven medium modules is stated in the terms established here. You cannot be told to fix the measure until you know what a measure is, you cannot be told that a chart encodes badly until you know the perceptual ranking it encodes against, and you cannot judge a palette without knowing what contrast ratio a body-size word actually needs. This module supplies that apparatus.

It is also where the course makes its central claim: a surprising amount of what looks like taste is not. Four independent authorities converge on the same line-length band. Cleveland and McGill ranked visual encodings by measured accuracy in 1984 and the ranking has held. Contrast has a formula. Where the evidence exists these lectures use it and cite it; where a convention is only a convention, they say so rather than dressing it as law.

Prerequisites

None. This is the entry point to the track. No drawing ability is assumed and none is taught.

Module sequence

Part 1 · The premise (lectures 1 to 2)

What design is doing, and what the reader's visual system does with it before they have consciously read a word.

  • builtLecture 1: what design is. Design as the assignment of meaning to visual difference. Why "undesigned" is not available, only designed by default, and the two errors every design failure reduces to.
  • builtLecture 2: perception and Gestalt. Preattentive processing, the grouping principles, and the experimental result that proximity overrides every other grouping cue including colour.

Part 2 · Type (lectures 3 to 4)

The system that carries almost all the meaning in almost every document, and the one where amateur instinct is most reliably wrong.

  • builtLecture 3: typography I, anatomy and choosing a face. Legibility against readability, the five measurements that predict how a face behaves, the serif and sans question answered honestly, and a five-step selection procedure.
  • builtLecture 4: typography II, measure, leading and the scale. The return sweep and the 66-character target, why leading and measure are locked together, why geometric scales fail at both ends, and fluid sizing with clamp.

Part 3 · Colour (lectures 5 to 6)

The channel most used and least understood. Two lectures: what colour does to perception, then how to build a palette that survives contact with reality.

  • builtLecture 5: colour I, why colour deceives. The four independent reasons the specified value is not the perceived one, why lightness is the dimension that survives, and why colour-blind safe is not the same as greyscale safe.
  • builtLecture 6: colour II, building a palette. The build order, the twelve-step ramp with its contrast recorded, the three data families, and complying with a contrast standard known to be imperfect.

Part 4 · Arrangement (lectures 7 to 9)

Where things go, how far apart, and which one the reader sees first.

  • builtLecture 7: the grid. Field, module and grid; why twelve columns; the Van de Graaf canon and why it suits a book and not a PDF; and the honest answer about the golden ratio.
  • builtLecture 8: whitespace. Space as relative rather than absolute, the spacing scale, the nesting rule, and how to defend whitespace to someone who wants it removed.
  • builtLecture 9: hierarchy and contrast. The squint test, the tools ranked with colour fifth, three levels as the working budget, and hierarchy as an editorial argument rather than a formatting choice.

Part 5 · Evidence and diagnosis (lectures 10 to 11)

The most transferable lecture in the course, then the capstone that turns all of it into a repeatable procedure.

  • builtLecture 10: data graphics. The Cleveland and McGill ranking demonstrated, the pie chart and zero-baseline questions settled precisely, data-ink worked through, and the experimental evidence against Tufte given a fair hearing.
  • builtLecture 11: a diagnostic protocol. Ten checks in order, five content-blind and five content-aware, with a worked pass on a validation report and an explicit repair triage.

Where this reappears

Proximity
Established as the dominant grouping cue in lecture 2. Becomes the whitespace rule in lecture 8, the block structure in the poster spec, and the reason a dashboard tile has more internal padding than external gap.
Measure
Defined in lecture 4. Sets the reading column in the blog module, the page margins in the report module, and is the first thing lecture 11 checks.
The perceptual ranking
Cleveland and McGill, lecture 10. Decides chart type in the dashboard module, bar geometry in the report module, and why the presentation module forbids a legend.
Contrast ratio
Lecture 6. Every colour token in all eight specifications carries one, and the split between fill values and text-safe values exists because of it.
