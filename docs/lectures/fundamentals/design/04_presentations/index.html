Module 4 · Design track

Presentations

Three lectures on the medium with the most advice, the least evidence, and one substantial exception to both. Slide design is mostly folklore, and then there is a body of experimental work on multimedia learning that gives measured effect sizes for a handful of decisions and contradicts how nearly every professional deck is built.

What this module is

A slide deck has a property nothing else in this track has: a speaker attached. The artefact is not the communication, it is one channel of a two-channel communication, and almost every mistake in slide design comes from forgetting that. A slide carrying everything the speaker will say is not a thorough slide, it is a slide competing with its own presenter for the audience's attention, and the research says the audience loses.

The module opens with the evidence rather than the craft, which inverts the usual order and is deliberate. Richard Mayer's multimedia learning work provides the only measured effect sizes in this entire course, and they point somewhere uncomfortable. Edward Tufte's critique goes further and argues the medium itself is defective. Both deserve to be taken seriously before anyone starts choosing fonts.

The second lecture is the craft: canvas, type at distance, the layout set, and what a chart needs when it is read from four metres rather than forty centimetres. The third is the action title, which is the consulting tradition's genuine contribution and the single habit most likely to change how you work.

Prerequisites

All eleven foundations lectures. Lecture 9 (hierarchy) and lecture 10 (data graphics) do most of the work here, and lecture 5 (colour perception) supplies the projector argument.

Module sequence

Evidence, then craft, then argument

Take lecture 19 first. The constraints in lecture 20 look arbitrary without it.

  • builtLecture 19: why most decks fail. Tufte's critique and the Columbia case, then Mayer's twelve principles with their effect sizes. Coherence, redundancy and modality are the three largest and all three say the same thing: put less on the slide.
  • builtLecture 20: the slide as a designed object. The 16:9 canvas and its zones, two type scales for two rooms, the seven-layout master set, and why a chart exported at report settings is unreadable from the fourth row.
  • builtLecture 21: the action title. Minto's pyramid applied to a deck, the ghost-deck test, horizontal and vertical logic, Zelazny's chart selection, and the honest question of when the deck should have been a report.

Token specification

The building blocks are in 04_presentations/DESIGN.md: the 16:9 canvas with its title, content and footer zones, the two-column type scale for projected against shared delivery, the seven layouts, and the chart overrides.

The finding this module will not soften Presenting the same words as both on-screen text and spoken narration is worse than either alone. The effect is among the largest in the multimedia learning literature. This directly contradicts the common practice of building decks that also work as read-alone documents, and lecture 19 treats that practice as a request for a different artefact rather than as a counterexample.

Where this reappears

The action title
Lecture 21. Already met as the exhibit title in the reports module and the informative heading in the blogs module. Three mediums, one habit.
Direct labelling
A preference in the reports module and a hard rule here, because at projection distance the cost of the eye travelling to a legend is much higher than on paper.
De-emphasis
Grey everything except the series under discussion. Introduced here as the most useful technique in the set, and the same rule governs the dashboard module's default state.
