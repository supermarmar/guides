Module 6 · Design track

Dashboards

Three lectures on a screen someone returns to. Not a report that happens to be interactive and not a chart gallery: a display of the information needed to achieve specific objectives, on a single screen, monitored at a glance. Every constraint in this module comes out of that definition.

What this module is

Dashboards are the newest medium in this track and the worst served by convention, because the tools arrived before the discipline did. Power BI, Tableau and their equivalents make it trivially easy to put a chart on a screen and offer no opinion about whether it should be there. The result is that most dashboards are collections of available charts rather than instruments for a decision, and the failure is invisible because everything on them is technically correct.

Stephen Few is the authority here and his contribution is a definition sharp enough to exclude things. Single screen, at a glance, in service of specific objectives. Each clause does work: a dashboard you scroll is not one, a dashboard you study is not one, and a dashboard nobody can name the objective of is not one either.

The three lectures run from purpose to encoding to construction. The first asks what the thing is for and catalogues how that goes wrong. The second applies the perceptual ranking to real chart choices and settles the arguments about pie charts and truncated axes. The third builds the screen: tiles, axes, number formatting and the dark variant.

Prerequisites

All eleven foundations lectures, and lecture 10 in particular, which this module applies rather than restates. Lecture 16 supplies the IBCS notation that reappears here for a screen that refreshes.

Module sequence

Purpose, encoding, construction

  • builtLecture 24: what a dashboard is for. Few's definition and what it excludes, his catalogue of failures, and designing backwards from the decision rather than forwards from the available data.
  • builtLecture 25: choosing the encoding. The perceptual ranking applied, the FT's nine relationship families as a selection tool, the pie chart and truncated axis questions settled, and the underrated case for discrete encodings.
  • builtLecture 26: building the screen. KPI tile anatomy, the bullet graph against the gauge, gridlines and axes, number formatting, and why a dark variant is a re-pick rather than an inversion.

Token specification

The building blocks are in 06_dashboards/DESIGN.md: the twelve-column modular grid, the compressed type scale, the data palette kept strictly separate from the interface palette, the KPI tile and bullet graph components, number formatting rules and the dark variant.

The rule that shapes the whole module Grey is the default and colour is spent only on meaning. Most dashboards colour every series because the tool does it by default, which exhausts the colour channel before anything meaningful arrives. A dashboard where six things are coloured has no highlight at all.
