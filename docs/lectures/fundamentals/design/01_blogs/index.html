Module 1 · Design track

Blogs and long-form web

Two lectures on the least constrained medium in this track. A web page imposes almost nothing: no page size, no print budget, no client rendering engine worth fearing. Every decision is available, which means every failure is yours. That is why it comes first.

What this module is

The governing claim belongs to Oliver Reichenstein: the web is overwhelmingly written language, so web design is mostly typography. His figure of 95 per cent is rhetoric rather than measurement, and the conclusion survives the exaggeration. On a page that is text, there is almost nothing to design except the text.

So the module is short and its two lectures divide cleanly. The first is about the type layer: how large, how long the line, how far apart, and what changes when the page is emitted by a backlit panel rather than absorbed by paper. The second is about the page layer: how readers actually consume a long page, which is by scanning rather than reading, and what that demands of headings, widths, figures and depth.

Almost everything in both is a direct application of foundations. The module's job is to say what the screen changes, and the honest answer is: less than people assume, but the changes it does make are not negotiable.

Prerequisites

All eleven foundations lectures. Lectures 4 (measure and leading), 5 (colour perception) and 9 (hierarchy) are load-bearing here and this module does not restate them.

Module sequence

The two layers

  • builtLecture 12: reading on a screen. What actually changes between paper and a backlit panel, the reading column as the decision that fixes every other one, why the body size does not scale fluidly, and dark mode as a designed palette rather than an inversion.
  • builtLecture 13: the long-form page. The scanning patterns and what they demand of headings, the three-width system, sidenotes against footnotes, code and tables that must escape the column, and gwern's layered depth as a model for letting the reader choose.

Token specification

The module's building blocks are in 01_blogs/DESIGN.md: the 66ch reading column, body at 18px on 1.65 leading, the dark palette with its accent re-picked at #A99BFF, and the three widths a blog layout is allowed to use.

The one override worth knowing The house body size is 16px; this module raises it to 18px and its leading to 1.65. Screen reading happens at a greater distance than print, and Butterick's screen range runs from 15 to 25 pixels. Sixteen is the accessible floor rather than the target, and long-form is the one context where the difference between floor and target is worth taking.

Where this reappears

The reading column
Fixed at 66ch here. The report module derives its A4 margins backwards from the same target, and the poster module abandons it entirely because a poster is not read in lines.
Designed dark mode
Established in lecture 12 as a separate palette rather than an inversion. The dashboard module reuses the argument, and the email module has to give it up because two of the three major clients ignore what you declare.
Scanning
Lecture 13's F-pattern, layer-cake and spotted patterns. The same finding drives the action-title rule in the presentation module and the three-depth model in the poster module.
