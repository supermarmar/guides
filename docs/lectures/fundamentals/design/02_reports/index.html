Module 2 · Design track

Reports

Three lectures on the medium with the highest professional stakes in this collection. A validation report, a board paper, an ICAAP submission: A4, print-first, delivered as PDF, read by someone senior and sceptical who will look at the summary and the exhibits before deciding whether to read anything else.

What this module is

A report is the hardest medium in this track to get right, for a reason that is easy to miss. It has no interactivity to lean on, no scrolling, no collapsible sections and no hover. It is a fixed sequence of fixed pages, and everything the reader will ever get from it has to be visible on one of them. The web page in module 1 could offer the reader a choice of depth; a report has to make that choice on their behalf.

It is also the medium where the professional cost of failure is highest. A blog post that loses a reader costs a reader. A validation report whose caveat is invisible, or whose exhibit misleads, ends up in a decision, and then in a file, and occasionally in front of a regulator asking why the committee understood it the way they did.

The three lectures divide by object. The first is the page: geometry, rhythm, and the type settings that follow from a 66-character target. The second is tables, which are the most misdesigned object in professional writing and the one most reports contain most of. The third is exhibits and business notation, where the IBCS standard turns a set of ad hoc chart conventions into a vocabulary a reader learns once.

Prerequisites

All eleven foundations lectures. Lecture 4 (measure and leading), 7 (the grid), 9 (hierarchy) and 10 (data graphics) are load-bearing throughout and are not restated.

One inherited decision worth flagging This module uses symmetric page margins rather than the classical Van de Graaf canon, and lecture 7 is the argument for that choice. The short version: the canon's asymmetry only resolves across a spread, and a report is read one page at a time on a laptop.

Module sequence

The page, then what goes on it

Take them in order. The table and exhibit specifications both assume the page geometry and the baseline established in the first lecture.

  • builtLecture 14: the report page. Deriving A4 margins backwards from the 66-character target, the 15.4pt baseline and what vertical rhythm buys in print, the serif body with sans headings, and running heads, folios and paragraph treatment.
  • builtLecture 15: tables. Why vertical rules and zebra striping are both wrong, alignment by data type, decimal alignment and tabular figures, marking the total, and the question of when a table should have been a chart.
  • builtLecture 16: exhibits and business notation. The exhibit as an object with a title, a source and a claim; IBCS scenario notation and why it encodes by fill rather than hue; variance charts; and the greyscale test as a shipping gate.

Token specification

The building blocks are in 02_reports/DESIGN.md: A4 page geometry, the point-based type scale anchored to a 15.4pt baseline, the table specification, the exhibit anatomy, and the IBCS scenario tokens.
