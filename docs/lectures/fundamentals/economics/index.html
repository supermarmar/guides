Course companion

Economics

A bachelor's-level economics course, written in the LSE / Cambridge tradition. Twenty-five lectures, theory first, with British policy and banking applications throughout. Assumes you already have calculus, probability, and regression from the maths, statistics and risk-modelling tracks.

How to use this

Lectures are sequenced bottom up and each one is a prerequisite for the next. The micro half builds the analytics of individual choice and market structure; the macro half scales that up to the whole economy; the econometrics part teaches you how to interrogate the resulting theory with data; the field part applies all three to specific policy problems. Status badges show what is built (built), planned, or a gap.

Source material

Each lecture is built from its own research rather than a single source PDF. Where a lecture covers ground that a canonical textbook covers more deeply, it says so in a "what's beyond the syllabus" callout near the top and points the reader to the relevant chapter (typically Varian for micro, Mankiw or Blanchard for macro, Mas-Colell for advanced theory, Wooldridge or Angrist and Pischke for econometrics).
