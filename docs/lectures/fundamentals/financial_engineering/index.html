Course companion

Financial engineering

An intuition-first walk through the mathematical theory of derivatives pricing, built from the University of Pretoria WTW 364 and WTW 365 course and extended with the depth a working quant or actuary expects. Thirteen lectures, sequenced from the simplest derivative (the forward) through stochastic calculus to applied topics in interest rate and credit risk.

How to use this

Each lecture takes one source PDF and rebuilds it as a teaching session, with intuition first, formal derivations second, and worked examples throughout. The sequencing reorganises the source order slightly so the conceptual scaffolding builds cleanly: discrete-time arbitrage first, then the stochastic calculus, then the continuous-time pricing machinery that depends on both. Status badges show what is built (built) or planned.

Course sequence

Group 1: derivatives foundations

The two simplest derivatives, priced by the simplest argument (no arbitrage). Everything later is a generalisation of these.

  • builtForward contracts: the prototype derivative and the cash-and-carry argument
  • builtOptions: payoffs, put-call parity and the no-arbitrage bounds

Group 2: discrete-time arbitrage pricing

Binomial trees: the cleanest possible model of a stochastic asset, where every pricing argument reduces to solving two equations in two unknowns. Calibration sits here because it is the practical bridge from a tree to a market.

  • builtBinomial model: single period, replication and the risk-neutral measure
  • builtBinomial model: multi-period, recursion and the CRR tree
  • builtCalibration of the binomial tree to market data

Group 3: stochastic calculus foundations

The mathematical machinery that lets discrete-time pricing arguments survive the passage to continuous time. Brownian motion, Ito calculus, SDEs, and the Feynman-Kac bridge that translates between PDEs and expectations.

  • builtBrownian motion and stochastic integrals
  • builtStochastic differential equations and Ito's lemma
  • builtThe Feynman-Kac theorem

Group 4: continuous-time pricing

The Black-Scholes machinery: the PDE, its closed-form solution, the dividend extension, and the hedging Greeks that connect theory to risk management practice.

  • builtThe Black-Scholes PDE and the risk-neutral solution
  • builtDividends and other adjustments
  • builtHedging and the Greeks

Group 5: applied topics

Two settings where the standard machinery needs significant extension: interest rate derivatives (where the underlying and the discount rate are not independent) and credit risk (where the default event is a discontinuous jump).

  • builtInterest rate derivatives: short-rate models and the term structure
  • builtCredit risk: structural and reduced-form models

Source material

Each lecture cites its primary source PDF from the WTW 364 / WTW 365 module at the University of Pretoria, and every lecture in the sequence maps to exactly one of the thirteen source PDFs. Two of the supporting files are narrower than their names suggest, so it is worth knowing which is which. WTW 364 Summary.pdf is the formula sheet for this course, organised in six units (arbitrage portfolios and the single-step binomial model, binomial trees, stochastic calculus, Black-Scholes theory, interest rates and bonds, credit risk), and it is the right cross-reference for any lecture here. Notes.pdf is a companion to Luenberger, Investment Science, chapters 6 to 9, and covers portfolio theory, CAPM and general pricing principles: it contains nothing on forwards or binomial models, so it is cited only where the general no-arbitrage and risk-neutral-pricing material genuinely applies. WTW 365 Formulas.pdf is a portfolio-theory and CAPM formula sheet, and is not a source for the derivatives material at all.

Where a lecture goes beyond its source, it says so in the callout near the top and accounts for the difference in the "reported gaps in source" block at the foot of the page, in both directions: several of the source PDFs cover material these lectures omit, notably the exotic-option and combination-strategy surveys in the options and multi-period binomial chapters. Public references (Hull, Björk, Shreve, and the original Cox-Ross-Rubinstein, Jarrow-Rudd and Derman-Kani papers) are cited where a lecture extends past the source.
