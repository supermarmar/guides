Actuarial risk management

An ASSA A311 companion, forty lectures deep, built around a single idea: the actuarial control cycle. Specify a problem, produce a solution, live with it, monitor it, and go round again. This course runs that loop once, generically, across every practice area an actuary might work in: life, health and general insurance, pensions, banking, investments. It is the pre-course, meant to be read after the foundation subjects (financial mathematics, actuarial mathematics, contingencies, risk modelling) and before any of the specialised application tracks (F107 banking, F105 investments, F102 life insurance), because those tracks assume the loop this course teaches and spend all their depth on one practice area at a time.

What this course is

Chapter 0 of the source is the actuarial control cycle itself: a general economic environment feeds a problem specification, which a solution addresses, which is then lived with and monitored, all under a standing duty of professionalism. Every one of the source's other thirty-nine chapters is pre-tagged, in the source itself, to one of those stages. That tagging is what makes this course wide rather than deep: chapter 6 (life insurance products) and chapter 40 (banking) sit inside the same "stakeholders and products" stage as chapter 9 (bond markets), because from the ACC's point of view a with-profits endowment, a current account and a government bond are all just instances of "a product a stakeholder needs," and the cycle treats them identically until the risk-management stages get more specific.

That is also why this course overlaps so heavily with the others. Its stakeholder, product and market chapters cover exactly the ground F102, F105 and F107 cover, and its risk-governance chapters are the generic version of the loop F107's risk management track already specialises for banking. The overlap is intentional, not a mistake to fix: this course is the shared vocabulary the other three tracks assume, so it stays at survey depth wherever another track will eventually go deep, and only goes deep itself where no other track owns the material.

How this track is organised

Seven parts, sequenced by ACC stage rather than by source chapter number or by practice area:

  • Part 0, the control cycle itself (2 lectures): the loop and the professional duty that sits above it.
  • Part 1, the general environment (11 lectures): the external world, then a tour of every stakeholder and every product and market it trades in. Survey depth throughout, because F102, F105 and F107 each own one slice of this territory and will eventually go far deeper. Every lecture here that has a future home says so.
  • Part 2, problem specification (4 lectures): data, contract design, and the two risk-identification chapters that set up everything from Part 5.
  • Part 3, producing the solution (12 lectures): modelling, assumptions, and the technical machinery of pricing, valuation and provisioning. This is the largest part because it is where the ACC's "solution development" stage does most of its work.
  • Part 4, living with the solution (5 lectures): capital, surplus, reporting and insolvency, the consequences of having sold the product.
  • Part 5, risk governance and response (5 lectures): full depth, not survey. This is the course's own material: no other track teaches the generic risk-management loop (identify, measure, respond, govern) that F107 later specialises for banking specifically.
  • Part 6, monitoring (1 lecture): closing the loop, and a short synthesis back to Part 0.

Where this reappears

The ACC is generic; the machinery it runs is not. A short map of which future module deepens which part.

F107 risk management (built)
Specialises Part 5's generic governance/identification/measurement/response loop for banking: three lines of defence, risk appetite, the eight banking risk types, economic capital, ICAAP, SREP. Read Part 5 first to see the loop in general form before F107 names it for banking.
Risk modelling for non-life insurance (built)
Deepens Part 1's general insurance survey (lecture 8) and Part 5's reinsurance and risk-measurement lectures (37, 38) into the full frequency-severity, compound Poisson, ruin theory and credibility machinery.
F105 investments (planned)
Deepens Part 1's market survey (lectures 10 to 13) into ninety-nine lectures of asset classes, derivatives, valuation, portfolio construction and management, and performance measurement. Also deepens Part 3's investment lectures (23 to 27) from an investing-institution's perspective rather than a generic stakeholder's.
F102 life insurance (planned)
Deepens Part 1's product survey (lectures 6, 7) into the full product, pricing, risk-management and risk-measurement treatment across thirty-six F102 chapters. Also deepens Part 3's pricing and provisioning lectures (19 to 22, 28, 29) into the life-office-specific asset share, reserving and capital machinery.

Source material

Two personal ASSA A311 summaries: A311 Concept Summary (96 pages) and A311 List Summary (103 pages, and the source for the table of contents this chapter map is built from). Both are dense bullet-point and mnemonic revision compressions rather than worked prose, in the same register as the F105 summary: structure is thick, derivation is thin. Lectures will need to expand every definition and worked example rather than transcribe the source directly.

The source also carries an unnumbered "Frameworks" appendix: template income statements and balance sheets for an insurer and for a bank, and a general product-cycle diagram. This is reference material rather than a lecture, in the same spirit as formulae and tables, and will be folded into whichever lecture (or a short standalone reference page) makes most sense once Parts 3 and 4 are actually written.
