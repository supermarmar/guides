Course companion

Actuarial mathematics

The maths of life insurance: how to put a price on a contract whose cash flows depend on when someone dies. Six lectures rebuilt from the University of Pretoria course summary, with intuition first and every formula derived.

How to use this

Each lecture rebuilds one block of the source summary as a teaching session, with intuition first and formulae fully derived where the source skipped steps. The lectures are sequenced so each one depends on the previous: survival probabilities feed life tables, life tables feed annuities and assurances, and those feed premiums and reserves. Status badges show what is built (built) or planned.

Course sequence

1. Foundations

  • builtSurvival models: how long does this person have left?
  • builtLife tables: the tabulated form, and why "select" lives are different

2. Cash flows on a single life

  • builtLife annuities: pricing a stream of payments that ends at death
  • builtLife assurances: pricing a lump sum that arrives at death
  • builtVariable benefits: pensions that escalate, sums assured that grow

3. Pricing and valuation

  • builtPremiums and reserves: the equation of value, and the money on the balance sheet

Source material

Every lecture cites the University of Pretoria Actuarial Mathematics / Summary.pdf as its source. Where a lecture goes beyond the source (intuition, derivation, worked example, exam tip), it says so explicitly in the "what is beyond the source" callout near the top.
