Life assurances

An assurance pays a lump sum at death. Same EPV machinery as annuities, but applied to a one-off payment at a random time rather than a stream.

Lecture 4 of 6 Reading time, 25 minutes Source: UP / Actuarial Mathematics Summary.pdf §6
Step 1

The 30-second version

A life assurance pays a lump sum on a single event: death (whole life, term, endowment cover) or survival (pure endowment). The expected present value at age $x$ is the discount factor at the time of payment, multiplied by the probability the event happens, summed over all possible times. For death benefits paid at the end of the year of death, the discount factor is $v^{k+1}$ and the probability is ${}_{k|}q_x$, the deferred one-year mortality probability.

Four contracts cover the syllabus. Whole life pays on death whenever it happens. Term pays on death within $n$ years. Pure endowment pays on survival to $n$. Endowment pays on whichever happens first. The first three are mutually exclusive; the fourth is the sum of the second and third.

Feynman test A 45-year-old buys a 10-year term assurance with sum assured £100,000. If he dies in the second year, the insurer pays £100,000 at the end of year 2. The present value of that £100,000, looked at from today, is $100{,}000 \cdot v^2$. But the insurer does not know in advance whether he will die in year 2; the probability is $p_{45} \cdot q_{46} = {}_{1|}q_{45}$. So the expected cost today of "paying out for a year-2 death" is $100{,}000 \cdot v^2 \cdot {}_{1|}q_{45}$. Sum across years 1 to 10 and you have the EPV of the contract. That sum is $100{,}000 \cdot A^1_{45:\overline{10|}}$.
What is beyond the source The source PDF lists the formulas without much commentary. This lecture adds the recursion $A_x = v(q_x + p_x \cdot A_{x+1})$ as the cleanest computational identity, and derives the continuous-to-discrete relationship $\bar{A}_x \approx (1+i)^{1/2}\, A_x$ from a UDD argument rather than asserting it.
Step 2

Why this matters

Life assurance pricing is the other half of the life-insurance equation of value (annuities paid the premium in; this side pays the benefit out). Every life assurance product on the UK retail shelf reduces to one of these four contracts or a linear combination. A "whole life with cash-back" rider is a whole life plus a pure endowment. A "term to 65 with conversion option" is a term assurance with a contingent option whose value is itself an EPV.

The endowment assurance was the workhorse of British life insurance for most of the twentieth century, often sold as a mortgage repayment vehicle. The contract structure is unchanged today; what collapsed in the late 1990s was the assumed investment return on the unit-linked variants, which turned out far lower than the projections fed to consumers. The actuarial mathematics in this lecture is robust; the marketing in the 1990s was not.

The continuous-to-discrete adjustment $(1+i)^{1/2}$ matters in modern practice because life-assurance contracts pay promptly on death (within weeks of notification), not at the end of the year. The pricing basis therefore properly uses $\bar{A}^1_{x:\overline{n|}}$, with the discrete formula and the $(1+i)^{1/2}$ multiplier as a standard approximation.

Step 3

The mental model

Four basic contracts arranged by what triggers the payout:

Whole life
Pays 1 on death, whenever it happens. Symbol $A_x$ (end of year) or $\bar{A}_x$ (continuous).
Term assurance
Pays 1 on death within $n$ years; nothing on survival. Symbol $A^1_{x:\overline{n|}}$.
Pure endowment
Pays 1 on survival to $n$; nothing on death. Symbol $A_{x:\overline{n|}}^{\,1}$. The "1" is over the $n$ this time.
Endowment
Pays 1 on whichever event happens first. Symbol $A_{x:\overline{n|}}$ (no "1"). Equals term plus pure endowment.

The two decomposition identities that tie them together:

$$ A_{x:\overline{n|}} \;=\; A^1_{x:\overline{n|}} + A_{x:\overline{n|}}^{\,1}, \qquad A_x \;=\; A^1_{x:\overline{n|}} + {}_{n|}A_x. $$

And the size ordering:

$$ A^1_{x:\overline{n|}} \;\le\; A_x \;\le\; A_{x:\overline{n|}}. $$

The term is cheaper than the whole (because the whole pays out for sure, the term only sometimes). The whole is cheaper than the endowment (because the endowment also pays on survival).

Step 4

The detail

Whole-life assurance

Death benefit of 1 paid at the end of the year of death:

$$ A_x \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{\omega - x - 1} v^{k+1} \cdot {}_k p_x \cdot q_{x+k} \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{\omega - x - 1} v^{k+1} \cdot {}_{k|}q_x. $$

The two forms are identical: ${}_k p_x \cdot q_{x+k} = {}_{k|}q_x$ is the probability of dying in the $(k+1)$-th year. Payment at the end of that year, hence the $v^{k+1}$ discount.

Continuous version, payment on death:

$$ \bar{A}_x \;=\; \int_0^{\omega - x} v^t \cdot {}_t p_x \cdot \mu_{x+t}\, dt. $$

The integrand is the discount factor at the instant of death, multiplied by the density of dying then.

Term assurance

Death benefit paid only if death is within $n$ years:

$$ A^1_{x:\overline{n|}} \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{n-1} v^{k+1} \cdot {}_{k|}q_x, \qquad \bar{A}^1_{x:\overline{n|}} \;=\; \int_0^{n} v^t \cdot {}_t p_x \cdot \mu_{x+t}\, dt. $$

Same summands as $A_x$ truncated at $n$. The little "1" over the $x$ marks "pays on death".

Pure endowment

A single payment of 1 at time $n$, conditional on survival:

$$ A_{x:\overline{n|}}^{\,1} \;=\; v^n \cdot {}_n p_x \;=\; \frac{D_{x+n}}{D_x}. $$

No integral needed: only one possible payment time. Continuous and discrete coincide because the timing is fixed.

Endowment

By construction:

$$ A_{x:\overline{n|}} \;=\; A^1_{x:\overline{n|}} + A_{x:\overline{n|}}^{\,1}, \qquad \bar{A}_{x:\overline{n|}} \;=\; \bar{A}^1_{x:\overline{n|}} + A_{x:\overline{n|}}^{\,1}. $$

Deferred whole life

Cover that does not begin until age $x + n$:

$$ {}_{n|}A_x \;=\; v^n \cdot {}_n p_x \cdot A_{x+n} \;=\; \frac{D_{x+n}}{D_x} \cdot A_{x+n}, \qquad {}_{n|}\bar{A}_x \;=\; v^n \cdot {}_n p_x \cdot \bar{A}_{x+n}. $$

Logic: discount the future EPV back $n$ years, weighted by the probability of surviving to be exposed to it.

The recursion identity

The single most useful identity in the chapter. Condition on what happens in the first year. Either the life dies (probability $q_x$, payment of 1 at end of year, present value $v$) or survives (probability $p_x$, contract continues at age $x + 1$, present value $v \cdot A_{x+1}$):

$$ A_x \;=\; v \cdot q_x + v \cdot p_x \cdot A_{x+1} \;=\; v\!\left(q_x + p_x \cdot A_{x+1}\right). $$

This is how actuarial software computes $A_x$ in practice: start at the ultimate age with $A_{\omega - 1} = v \cdot q_{\omega - 1}$ and unwind backwards. The same recursion is the foundation of the reserve recursion in lecture 6.

Continuous-to-discrete relationship

Under UDD, $\bar{A}_x \approx (1+i)^{1/2} \cdot A_x$. The derivation makes the assumption explicit. Split the integral by year of death:

$$ \bar{A}_x \;=\; \sum_{k=0}^{\omega - x - 1} \int_k^{k+1} v^t \cdot {}_t p_x \cdot \mu_{x+t}\, dt. $$

Under UDD the integrand is constant in $t$ within the year, equal to ${}_{k|}q_x$. So the inner integral is

$$ {}_{k|}q_x \int_k^{k+1} v^t\, dt \;=\; {}_{k|}q_x \cdot \frac{v^k - v^{k+1}}{\delta} \;=\; {}_{k|}q_x \cdot v^k \cdot \frac{i}{\delta}, $$

using $\delta = \ln(1+i)$ and $1 - v = iv$. Summing over $k$ and noting $v^k = (1+i) v^{k+1}$:

$$ \bar{A}_x \;=\; \frac{i(1+i)}{\delta} \cdot A_x. $$

The factor $i(1+i)/\delta$ approximates $(1+i)^{1/2}$ at typical interest rates by Taylor expansion.

The intuition: under UDD, deaths within a year happen on average at mid-year, so the continuous death benefit is paid on average half a year earlier than the year-end discrete benefit. Half a year of interest is $(1+i)^{1/2}$.

Consequences for term and endowment:

$$ \bar{A}^1_{x:\overline{n|}} \;\approx\; (1+i)^{1/2} \cdot A^1_{x:\overline{n|}}, \qquad \bar{A}_{x:\overline{n|}} \;\approx\; (1+i)^{1/2} \cdot A^1_{x:\overline{n|}} + A_{x:\overline{n|}}^{\,1}. $$

The pure endowment is not multiplied by $(1+i)^{1/2}$, because the survival benefit is paid at the fixed time $n$, not at a random time during a year.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The recursion is the workhorse

Every actuarial system computes $A_x$ by working backwards from the ultimate age using $A_x = v(q_x + p_x A_{x+1})$. The same recursion, repurposed, computes reserves in lecture 6. Once you see this pattern, you stop memorising the closed-form sums and start writing the recursion directly.

Term assurance is the cheapest cover at every age

Because most lives survive most terms, the pure premium for term assurance is a fraction of the pure premium for whole-life or endowment. UK term assurance is sold profitably at retail margins of 100 to 200 per cent over pure premium because the consumer is, in expectation, almost certain to pay premiums and not claim. This is why term is the most price-competitive of all life products.

Endowment assurance has fallen out of fashion

Endowment products dominated UK retail life insurance until the late 1990s mis-selling scandals. The contract structure is the same; what changed was the realism of the assumed investment return. The pure-endowment piece in unit-linked endowments depended on a return that did not materialise. The actuarial mathematics in this lecture is unchanged; the projections marketing departments asked it to deliver were not.

A common F107 trap

Watch out The "1" position in $A^1_{x:\overline{n|}}$ (term) versus $A_{x:\overline{n|}}^{\,1}$ (pure endowment) is one of the most common notation errors. Above the $x$: pays on death. Above the $n$: pays on survival. No "1": pays on whichever happens first. Drill this until the symbol is visually unambiguous.

Modern pricing pays promptly

Practitioner insight Modern UK term-assurance contracts pay on death within weeks of notification, not at the end of the calendar year. The pricing basis therefore properly uses $\bar{A}^1_{x:\overline{n|}}$, not $A^1_{x:\overline{n|}}$. In practice many priced bases still use the discrete formula with an explicit $(1+i)^{1/2}$ multiplier; some sophisticated bases use a continuous mortality model directly.

Test yourself

Try to answer each prompt out loud, in plain English, as if to a curious finance graduate. Do not look back at the lecture until you have tried.

  1. State the four basic assurance contracts and the two decomposition identities that link them.
  2. Why is the discount factor on $A_x$ given as $v^{k+1}$ rather than $v^k$? What event happens in year $k+1$?
  3. Derive the recursion $A_x = v(q_x + p_x A_{x+1})$ by conditioning on whether the life survives the first year. Explain why every actuarial system uses this rather than the closed-form sum.
  4. Derive $\bar{A}_x \approx (1+i)^{1/2}\, A_x$ from UDD. In one sentence, explain why the pure endowment is not multiplied by $(1+i)^{1/2}$.
  5. Justify each of $A^1_{x:\overline{n|}} \le A_x \le A_{x:\overline{n|}}$ in one line.
  6. For the table on this page, compute $A^1_{60:\overline{5|}}$, $A_{60:\overline{5|}}^{\,1}$, and $A_{60:\overline{5|}}$ from scratch. Verify the endowment equals the sum of the other two.
