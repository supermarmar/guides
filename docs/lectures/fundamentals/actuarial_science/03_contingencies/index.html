Course companion

Contingencies

An eight-lecture rebuild of the University of Pretoria Contingencies course, picking up where the actuarial mathematics single-life foundations leave off. The contracts and the populations both get richer: lives that can be in more than two states, lives that can leave for more than one cause, contracts written on two lives, pension schemes, with-profits and unit-linked policies, and the profit-testing apparatus that decides whether any of it makes money. Every identity derived, every notation collision resolved, every accompanying Excel example reproduced in the lecture.

How to use this

Each lecture rebuilds one block of the source material as a teaching session, with intuition first, formulae fully derived where the source went straight to the answer, and a worked example at the end. The lectures are sequenced so each one depends on the previous: the multi-state framework introduced in lecture 1 specialises to multi-decrement (lecture 2) and multi-life (lecture 3), feeds pension funds (lecture 5), and then sits underneath the profit-testing apparatus (lectures 6 to 8) that values modern insurance contracts. Status badges show what is built (built) or planned.

Notation cheat sheet

The contingencies course brings several new pieces of notation on top of the single-life foundation. The three traps that catch new readers:

${}_t p_x^{ij}$ in the multi-state world
In the multi-state framework ${}_t p_x^{ij}$ is the probability that a life aged $x$ who is in state $i$ now is in state $j$ at time $t$. The single-life ${}_t p_x$ is the special case where $i = j$ = alive and the only other state is dead. The Kolmogorov forward equations describe how these probabilities evolve. Lecture 1 derives them from scratch.
$q_x^{(d)}$ vs $\,_{\!}aq_x^{(d)}$ vs $q_x^{*(d)}$
In the multiple-decrement world there are three different mortality rates for cause $d$ and none of them are interchangeable. $q_x^{(d)}$ is the dependent probability of leaving by cause $d$ in the presence of the other decrements. $q_x^{*(d)}$ (sometimes written $aq_x^{(d)}$) is the independent probability you would observe if cause $d$ were the only decrement. The two coincide only when the other decrements have zero rate. Lecture 2 explains when each applies.
$A_{xy}$ vs $A_{\overline{xy}}$ vs $A_{x|y}$
Three different two-life assurances. $A_{xy}$ pays on the first death (joint-life status). $A_{\overline{xy}}$ pays on the last death (last-survivor status). $A_{x|y}$ pays on the death of $y$ provided $x$ has already died (a contingent assurance). The identity $A_{xy} + A_{\overline{xy}} = A_x + A_y$ falls out of the fact that one of "first death" and "last death" must be each of $x$ and $y$. Lecture 3 makes this concrete.
Unit fund vs non-unit fund
A unit-linked contract has two parallel accounts. The unit fund holds the policyholder's investment after charges and is paid out on maturity or surrender. The non-unit fund (also called the sterling fund) holds the insurer's own cashflows: charges received, expenses, death benefits less unit-fund payout, and the reserves needed to cover negative future cashflows. Profit testing is the arithmetic of the non-unit fund. Lecture 6 sets up both, lecture 8 reserves them.
