Stochastic processes

Markov jump processes, Brownian motion, and Monte Carlo simulation primitives.

Section 7 of 10 Source: Curtin §7, IFoA orange book §7

7.1 Markov jump processes

Kolmogorov forward equation
For transition probability $p_{ij}(s, t)$ and transition rate $\sigma_{ij}(t)$ from $i$ to $j \neq i$ at time $t$: $$\frac{\partial}{\partial t} p_{ij}(s, t) = \sum_{k \in S} p_{ik}(s, t) \sigma_{kj}(t),$$ with $\sigma_{ii} = -\sum_{j \neq i} \sigma_{ij}$.
Kolmogorov backward equation
$$\frac{\partial}{\partial s} p_{ij}(s, t) = -\sum_{k \in S} \sigma_{ik}(s) p_{kj}(s, t).$$
Expected time to reach state $k$ from $i$
$$m_i = \frac{1}{\lambda_i} + \sum_{j \neq i, j \neq k} \frac{\sigma_{ij}}{\lambda_i} m_j, \qquad \lambda_i = \sum_{j \neq i} \sigma_{ij}.$$
Holding-time distribution
Time spent in state $i$ before any jump is exponential with rate $\lambda_i$. Conditional on leaving, the destination is $j$ with probability $\sigma_{ij}/\lambda_i$.

7.2 Brownian motion

Standard Brownian motion
$\{B_t, t \geq 0\}$ with $B_0 = 0$, independent and stationary increments, $B_t - B_s \sim N(0, t - s)$ for $s < t$, and continuous sample paths.
Martingales from $B_t$
Each of the following is a martingale with respect to the natural filtration of $B$: $$B_t, \quad B_t^2 - t, \quad \exp\!\left(\lambda B_t - \tfrac{1}{2} \lambda^2 t\right).$$
Ornstein-Uhlenbeck process
$dX_t = -\gamma X_t\,dt + \sigma\,dB_t$, $\gamma > 0$. Mean-reverting to zero with reversion rate $\gamma$.

7.3 Monte Carlo methods

Box-Muller
If $U_1, U_2 \sim U(0, 1)$ independent, then $$Z_1 = \sqrt{-2 \log U_1} \cos(2 \pi U_2), \qquad Z_2 = \sqrt{-2 \log U_1} \sin(2 \pi U_2)$$ are independent $N(0, 1)$.
Polar method
If $V_1, V_2 \sim U(-1, 1)$ independent and $S = V_1^2 + V_2^2$, then conditional on $0 < S \leq 1$, $$Z_1 = V_1 \sqrt{-2 \log S / S}, \qquad Z_2 = V_2 \sqrt{-2 \log S / S}$$ are independent $N(0, 1)$.
Inverse-CDF method
If $U \sim U(0, 1)$ and $F$ is the target CDF, then $X = F^{-1}(U)$ has distribution $F$. Used for exponential ($X = -\log U / \lambda$), Weibull, Pareto.
