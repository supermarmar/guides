Fundamentals

Actuarial science

Six companion courses built from the University of Pretoria curriculum, the IFoA CS/CM syllabuses and an ASSA A311 summary, sequenced from time-value-of-money foundations through single-life actuarial machinery and general-insurance risk modelling to advanced life contingencies and the generic actuarial control cycle, closing with a synthesis course on AI for actuaries that draws together the machine-learning track's deep-learning, generative-modelling, large-language-model, and reinforcement-learning material into a specifically actuarial frame, plus a formulae and tables reference that doubles as a revision aid.

How the courses fit together

The dependency chain runs left to right. Financial mathematics comes first because everything else discounts cashflows, and the rate-conversion grid and equation-of-value machinery it builds are assumed silently by every later course. Actuarial mathematics adds mortality to those cashflows: survival models, life tables, and the annuity and assurance functions that turn a survival distribution into a price. Contingencies then generalises that machinery to multiple states, multiple decrements, and multiple lives, and applies it to pensions, unit-linked business, and profit testing.

Risk modelling sits to one side of that chain rather than inside it. It is the general-insurance counterpart: instead of a single life whose death time is uncertain, the object of study is an aggregate claims process whose frequency and severity are both random. It depends on the statistics course rather than on the life-contingencies courses, so it can be read early. Actuarial risk management sits above all four: it is the ASSA pre-course, read after these foundations and before any application track, and it runs the actuarial control cycle once, generically, across every practice area rather than specialising in one. The formulae and tables reference is not a course at all; it is the lookup layer, organised the way the exam tables are, and worth skimming once before using as a reference.

Courses

Foundations

  • Built Financial mathematics (7 lectures) — cashflows, accumulation and force of interest, the rate-conversion grid, inflation, level and increasing annuities, equations of value and project appraisal

Life contingencies

  • Built Actuarial mathematics (6 lectures) — survival models, life tables, life annuities, life assurances, variable benefits, premiums and reserves
  • Built Contingencies (8 lectures) — multiple-state models, multiple decrements, joint lives, variable benefits, pensions, unit-linked business, profit testing, reserves

General insurance

  • Built Risk modelling for non-life insurance (8 lectures) — loss distributions, aggregate claims models, reinsurance and parameter uncertainty, ruin theory, Bayesian estimation, credibility theory, empirical Bayes credibility

Synthesis

  • Built AI for actuaries (7 lectures): why actuarial modelling and machine learning share a method, covariate engineering, actuarial network architectures (CANN, LocalGLMnet, ICEnet), the credibility transformer, generative and LLM applications, reinforcement learning for pricing, governance and validation

Reference

  • Built Formulae and tables (10 sections) — mathematical methods, statistical distributions, statistical methods, compound interest, survival models, annuities and assurances, stochastic processes, time series, economic models, financial derivatives
  • Planned Actuarial risk management (40 lectures across 7 parts) — the actuarial control cycle, run once across every practice area: general environment, problem specification, producing and living with a solution, risk governance, monitoring. An ASSA A311 companion; sequenced, none built

Source material

Lectures are built from the University of Pretoria course material, the IFoA CS1 / CS2 / CM1 core reading, and the Formulae and Tables for Examinations booklet. Where a source treatment is thin, the lectures extend it with the standard results from Bowers et al., Dickson, Hardy and Waters, and Klugman, Panjer and Willmot. The AI for actuaries course additionally adapts Wüthrich, Richman, Avanzi, Lindholm, Maggi, Mayer, Schelldorfer and Scognamiglio, AI Tools for Actuaries (version 20 January 2026, https://ssrn.com/abstract=5162304, CC BY-NC); its own landing page carries the full attribution.
