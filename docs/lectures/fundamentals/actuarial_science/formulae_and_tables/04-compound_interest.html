Compound interest

Rates of interest and discount, the force of interest, annuities-certain, and equations of value. The mechanical foundation of every later life and finance section.

4.1 Rates of interest and discount

Effective rate of interest
$i$ is the proportional growth of one unit over one period: $1 + i$ is the accumulation factor.
Discount factor
$v = (1 + i)^{-1}$.
Effective rate of discount
$d = i v = 1 - v = i / (1 + i)$. Equivalently $1 - d = v$.
Nominal rates convertible $m$thly
$i^{(m)}$ satisfies $\left(1 + \dfrac{i^{(m)}}{m}\right)^{\!m} = 1 + i$. Similarly $d^{(m)}$ satisfies $\left(1 - \dfrac{d^{(m)}}{m}\right)^{\!m} = 1 - d = v$.
Force of interest
$\delta = \log(1 + i)$, the continuously-compounded equivalent. Then $1 + i = e^{\delta}$, $v = e^{-\delta}$, and $\delta = \lim_{m \to \infty} i^{(m)} = \lim_{m \to \infty} d^{(m)}$.
Ordering
For $i > 0$: $d < d^{(2)} < d^{(4)} < \cdots < \delta < \cdots < i^{(4)} < i^{(2)} < i$.

4.2 Variable force of interest

Accumulation factor
For force of interest $\delta(t)$ varying over time, $$A(t_1, t_2) = \exp\!\left(\int_{t_1}^{t_2} \delta(t)\,dt\right).$$
Present value
$\text{PV} = \dfrac{1}{A(0, t)} = \exp\!\left(-\int_0^t \delta(s)\,ds\right)$.

4.3 Level annuities-certain

All identities assume a constant effective rate $i$ per period and unit payments.

Present values

In arrears
$$a_{\overline{n}|} = v + v^2 + \cdots + v^n = \frac{1 - v^n}{i}$$
In advance (due)
$$\ddot{a}_{\overline{n}|} = 1 + v + \cdots + v^{n-1} = \frac{1 - v^n}{d} = (1 + i)\, a_{\overline{n}|}$$
Continuous
$$\bar{a}_{\overline{n}|} = \int_0^n v^t\,dt = \frac{1 - v^n}{\delta}$$
Payable $m$thly
$$a_{\overline{n}|}^{(m)} = \frac{1 - v^n}{i^{(m)}}, \qquad \ddot{a}_{\overline{n}|}^{(m)} = \frac{1 - v^n}{d^{(m)}}$$
Perpetuity
$a_{\overline{\infty}|} = 1/i$, $\ddot{a}_{\overline{\infty}|} = 1/d$, $\bar{a}_{\overline{\infty}|} = 1/\delta$.
Deferred
$_{m|}a_{\overline{n}|} = v^m a_{\overline{n}|}$, deferred $m$ periods then paid for $n$.

Accumulated values

In arrears, in advance
$$s_{\overline{n}|} = (1 + i)^n a_{\overline{n}|} = \frac{(1 + i)^n - 1}{i}, \qquad \ddot{s}_{\overline{n}|} = (1 + i)^n \ddot{a}_{\overline{n}|} = \frac{(1 + i)^n - 1}{d}$$
Continuous
$\bar{s}_{\overline{n}|} = e^{n \delta} \bar{a}_{\overline{n}|} = \dfrac{e^{n \delta} - 1}{\delta}$.

4.4 Varying annuities-certain

Increasing

In arrears
$$(Ia)_{\overline{n}|} = \frac{\ddot{a}_{\overline{n}|} - n v^n}{i}$$
In advance
$$(I\ddot{a})_{\overline{n}|} = \frac{\ddot{a}_{\overline{n}|} - n v^n}{d}$$
Continuously increasing
$$(\bar{I} \bar{a})_{\overline{n}|} = \frac{\bar{a}_{\overline{n}|} - n v^n}{\delta}$$

Decreasing

In arrears
$$(Da)_{\overline{n}|} = \frac{n - a_{\overline{n}|}}{i}$$

Geometric escalation at rate $g$

Present value
An annuity in arrears paying $1, (1+g), (1+g)^2, \ldots, (1+g)^{n-1}$ has present value $\dfrac{1 - \left[(1+g)/(1+i)\right]^n}{i - g}$ for $i \neq g$.

4.5 Equation of value and yield

Equation of value
A transaction balances when discounted cash inflows equal discounted cash outflows at some rate of interest $i$: $$\sum_t \text{outflow}_t \cdot v^t = \sum_t \text{inflow}_t \cdot v^t.$$
Net present value
$\text{NPV}(i) = \sum_t C_t v^t$, where $C_t$ is the net cashflow at time $t$.
Internal rate of return
The yield $i^*$ is the solution of $\text{NPV}(i^*) = 0$, found by interpolation or numerical methods.
Discounted payback
The smallest $t$ such that $\sum_{s \leq t} C_s v^s \geq 0$.
