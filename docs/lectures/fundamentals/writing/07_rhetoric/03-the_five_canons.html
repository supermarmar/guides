The five canons

Invention, arrangement, style, memory and delivery. A decomposition of the task that has survived two thousand years because it is a workflow in order, and most professional writers start at the third canon because it is the one that feels like writing.

Lecture 3 of 9 Reading time, 30 minutes Source: Cicero, De Inventione and De Oratore; Quintilian, Institutio Oratoria
Step 1

The 30-second version

The classical tradition divides the rhetorical task into five parts. Invention is finding what to say. Arrangement is ordering it. Style is putting it into words. Memory is holding it. Delivery is presenting it. The division is codified in Cicero and transmitted through Quintilian, and it survived because it is a decomposition of an activity rather than a theory about one.

Mapped onto professional writing, the canons are a workflow whose order matters and which almost nobody follows. Invention is the analysis plus the choice of framing that lecture 1 showed determines the audience. Arrangement is module 4. Style is modules 2, 5 and 6. Most writers begin at style, because style is what looks like writing, and the result is prose that is well made and about the wrong thing, arranged badly.

Memory and delivery are usually described as having died with oratory, and in a banking context they have not. Memory survives as knowing your material well enough to state a finding without reading it, which matters because papers get discussed in meetings. Delivery survives as formatting, typography and the actual act of presenting. A finding you cannot state from memory in a committee is a finding that will be characterised by whoever can.

Feynman test Five jobs, in order. Work out what to say. Decide what order to say it in. Choose the words. Learn it. Say it. Notice that most writing advice, including four modules of this course, is entirely about job three, and that jobs one and two determine whether job three is worth doing.
What is beyond the source The canons are ancient and the mapping is standard. Two things here are not. The first is the argument in step 5 that the canons are the classical statement of a principle this course has now derived three times independently, that structural work precedes local work, which is worth noticing because it suggests the principle is about the task rather than about writing. The second is the claim that memory and delivery are live rather than vestigial for anyone whose documents are discussed in meetings, which most modern treatments concede only as a curiosity.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is diagnostic. If you are dissatisfied with a document, the canons tell you which of five distinct problems you have, and they have different remedies. Nothing to say is an invention failure and no amount of restructuring helps. Wrong order is arrangement. Clumsy sentences are style. Being unable to defend it in the room is memory. Looking like a wall of text is delivery. Writers conflate all five into "it needs work".

The second reason is the ordering, which is the same argument module 3's lecture 7 made about diagnostic sequence and module 4's lecture 7 made about structure-first. Local work is wasted on material that will be cut or reordered. The canons put that in the strongest form available: style is the third of five jobs, and doing it first is doing the third job first.

The third reason is that invention is the canon nobody teaches and the one that determines everything else. Modules 2 to 6 of this course are style, module 4 is arrangement, and until this module nothing addressed how you find something to say. Lecture 4 supplies the classical apparatus for it, and it is the most practically useful material in the module for anyone who has ever stared at a blank analysis section.

And a reason specific to this collection's readers. A validation paper's life does not end when it is circulated; it continues in a committee where somebody will summarise it aloud, someone will challenge it, and the author will answer from memory. Treating the document as the deliverable and the meeting as an afterthought is a canon-four and canon-five failure, and it is where a great deal of carefully written analysis is lost.

Step 3

The mental model

Five canons, what each is, where this course covers it, and what survives of the last two.

Memory deserves more than the figure gives it, because the professional version is concrete. If you cannot state your finding, its magnitude and its mechanism in three sentences without looking, then in the meeting where the paper is discussed you will read from it, and reading from a paper in a committee cedes the framing to whoever speaks fluently. That is a canon-four failure with a canon-four remedy, which is rehearsal, and it is not a writing problem at all.

Invention
Finding what to say: the analysis, and the choice of which exigence to treat. The canon that determines all the others and the one no part of this course addressed before now.
Memory
Holding the material. Survives in two forms: your ability to state the finding without reading, and the document's retrievability through its heading layer. Not obsolete for anyone whose papers are discussed.
Delivery
Presenting it. In writing, the typographic and visual layer; in person, the summary you give aloud. Both are prepared or not, and usually not.

The detail

The canons as a diagnostic

SymptomCanonRemedy, and why the obvious one fails
Five different problems that all present as "it needs work"
The document is competent and pointless Invention Go back to the exigence. Editing cannot supply a point, and module 3's lecture 1 established that coherence is not repairable by technique.
Every section is sound and the sequence is confusing Arrangement Redraw the structure. Rewriting sentences in a badly ordered document is the waste module 3's lecture 7 warned about.
The reader has to work at individual sentences Style Modules 2, 3 and 5. This is the only symptom for which sentence-level work is the correct response.
You read aloud from the paper in the meeting Memory Rehearse three sentences. No amount of rewriting the paper fixes it, and the paper may already be excellent.
Nobody read past page two Delivery, or arrangement Check the visual layer first, because it is cheaper: headings, spacing, a table where a table belongs. If that is sound, the problem is that the point is on page six.

The fourth row is the one professionals resist, because it locates a failure outside the document. A paper can be the best thing you have written and still lose the meeting because you read from it while the model owner spoke from memory. That is not unfair and it is not a communication skills problem in the vague sense: it is the fourth canon, it was taught for two thousand years, and it takes twenty minutes to prepare.

Memory, in the form that survives

THE THREE-SENTENCE REHEARSAL Before any meeting where your paper will be discussed, prepare three sentences and learn them. 1. THE FINDING, with its magnitude. "The downturn LGD calibration understates the peak loss rate by about 40 basis points." 2. THE MECHANISM, in one clause. "Its stress window runs from 2011 to 2023, so it contains no severe downturn at all." 3. THE ASK, with an owner and a date. "The model owner needs to rebuild it on a window that includes 2008, before the next capital return." WHY THESE THREE They are the apex of module 4's pyramid, the question its parent raises, and the action. Anyone who hears them has received the paper. If you can say only one thing, say the first; if two, the first and third. WHAT TO ALSO HOLD, WITHOUT SCRIPTING The two numbers you will be asked for: 1.4 per cent arrears in the window, 3.1 per cent in 2008. The strongest objection and your answer to it, which is lecture 9's steelmanning prepared in advance. Where in the paper the evidence sits, so you can point rather than search. THE TEST Close the paper. Say the three sentences. If you reach for the document, you are not ready, and the failure is not in the document.

Delivery, and what it means on paper

Module 4's lecture 8 argued that the visual layer carries meaning rather than presenting it, and the fifth canon is where that claim comes from. Classical delivery was voice and gesture; the written analogue is everything about a document that the reader processes before or instead of the prose: heading hierarchy, spacing, whether a table exists, whether the page looks navigable.

Two consequences follow that are easy to act on. A document whose delivery is poor will not have its style noticed, because nobody reaches it, which means canon five gates canon three in practice. And delivery is the cheapest canon to fix: converting label headings to assertions, adding a table, and breaking a wall of text take an hour and change what proportion of readers reach your argument.

The in-person half is worth one further note. Delivery in a committee is not performance; it is the two minutes in which the paper is characterised for people who have skimmed it. Whoever speaks first and most fluently sets the frame, and lecture 1's constraints analysis applies: if the model owner is more fluent about your finding than you are, the finding becomes theirs to describe.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

The same ordering principle, derived three times

This course has now arrived at the same conclusion by three independent routes, and the convergence is worth noticing because it suggests the principle is about the structure of the task rather than about writing specifically.

Module 3's lecture 7 argued that the passage-level checks must run before the sentence-level ones, because structural repairs invalidate local work and local repairs do not propagate. Module 4's lecture 7 argued via McPhee that structure should be settled before drafting, because restructuring destroys prose and structuring does not. And the canons put style third of five, after invention and arrangement, which is the same claim made two thousand years earlier and without either argument.

Three derivations, one conclusion: work from the largest unit inwards. That is a stronger reason to believe it than any of the three arguments alone, and it is the practical content of this lecture. If you take nothing else from the canons, take the number: style is job three.

The honest complication is module 5's lecture 7, where Klinkenborg denies the whole sequence and argues that you find your material in the sentences. That objection stands, and the resolution given there also stands: draft to discover if you must, then settle invention and arrangement, then write the document anyone will read. Klinkenborg is disputing whether the canons describe how writing happens; he is not disputing that the deliverable should be built in that order.

Why the canons survived

Most rhetorical apparatus from antiquity is of historical interest. The canons are in active use in every rhetoric department and in no productivity system, which is odd given that they are a better decomposition of knowledge work than most of what has replaced them.

The reason they survived is that they are a decomposition rather than a theory. They make no claim about how persuasion works, what truth is, or what writing is for; they simply divide a task into parts that can be worked on separately and in sequence. That makes them almost unfalsifiable and therefore not very interesting intellectually, and extremely durable practically, which is a trade most frameworks would take.

It also means they are content-free in a specific way. The canons tell you that invention comes first and say nothing about how to do it, which is why lecture 4 exists and why the topics and stasis theory are the substantive part of the classical inheritance. A writer who knows the five canons and has no method of invention has a good filing scheme for a problem they cannot solve.

The two canons this course will not teach

Memory and delivery get a lecture section each and no more, and that is a limit worth declaring. Memory in its classical form was a set of mnemonic techniques, the method of loci among them, developed for orators delivering hour-long speeches without notes. Those techniques work and they are not what a professional needs, which is the ability to hold three sentences and two numbers.

Delivery in its classical form was voice, pace, gesture and stance, and it is a genuine discipline with its own literature. This course covers its written analogue in module 4's lecture 8 and does not cover speaking, which is a real gap for readers whose papers are presented. If your findings are routinely lost in meetings rather than in documents, the material you need is not in this course, and knowing that is more useful than a paragraph pretending otherwise.

Common failure Using the canons as a stage-gate process. They are an ordering of concerns rather than a sequence of phases with sign-offs, and treating them as phases reproduces exactly the failure module 5's lecture 7 identified: a plan settled before you understood the material. The canons say style is job three, not that invention must be complete before a word is written. Invention continues, and it should be settled before the deliverable rather than before the first draft.
Step 6

Test yourself

  1. Imitate before you compose. Prepare the three-sentence rehearsal from step 4 for a real paper of yours: finding with magnitude, mechanism in one clause, ask with owner and date. Close the paper and say them. Report whether you reached for it.
  2. Name the five canons in order and say what each is. Which does four fifths of this course cover?
  3. Give the five symptoms in step 4 and the canon each belongs to. Which is the only one for which sentence-level work is the right response?
  4. In what two forms does memory survive? Why does the first matter in a committee?
  5. Why does delivery gate style in practice? What makes it the cheapest canon to fix?
  6. State the ordering principle this course has derived three times, and name the three routes. Why is the convergence better evidence than any one route?
  7. Why did the canons survive when most ancient rhetorical apparatus did not? What does that make them content-free about?
  8. Distinguish the canons as an ordering of concerns from the canons as a stage-gate process. What failure does the second reproduce?
