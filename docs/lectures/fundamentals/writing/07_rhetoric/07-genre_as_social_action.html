Genre as social action

A document type is a typified response to a recurring situation, not a template. Which means you can reverse-engineer any unfamiliar genre from three good examples of it, and that the procedure for doing so is the most portable professional skill in this module.

Lecture 7 of 9 Reading time, 34 minutes Source: Miller (1984); Swales, Genre Analysis (1990)
Step 1

The 30-second version

Carolyn Miller's argument, in a 1984 paper that reoriented the field, is that a genre is defined by the action it accomplishes rather than by its formal features. A validation report is not a shape with sections; it is a typified response to a situation that keeps recurring, in which a model has been built, somebody independent must judge it, and a supervisor requires evidence that the judging happened.

Two things follow. First, you cannot learn a genre from its form, because the form is downstream of the action, and copying the form produces a document that looks right and does nothing. Second, the genre carries obligations you did not choose. If the recurring situation includes independent challenge, a report exhibiting no challenge fails the genre however well written it is.

John Swales turned this into a method. A genre is a sequence of moves, each a functional unit doing one job, and you identify them by collecting three to five exemplars and asking what each part of each one is for. His CARS model does this for research article introductions: establish a territory, establish a gap in it, occupy the gap.

That procedure is the practical payload. It is how you walk into an unfamiliar institution, obtain three credit papers that were well received, and work out in an afternoon what a credit paper is supposed to be there. Nobody teaches it and it transfers to every document type you will ever meet.

Feynman test A document type exists because a situation keeps happening and people keep needing to do something about it. So work out what the document has to accomplish, not what it has to look like. Then get three good examples, label what each paragraph is doing, and you have the recipe.
What is beyond the source Miller and Swales are cited accurately and move analysis is a standard research method. Three things here are not standard. The first is step 4's move analysis of a model validation report, which is constructed for this lecture rather than taken from published work. The second is the claim that its gap move fails exactly as CARS Move 2 fails, so that a report describing a model and reporting test results without stating a gap has produced a document with no finding, which is the commonest structural failure in the genre. The third is step 5's argument that move analysis reverse-engineers the genre as practised rather than as it should be, so three exemplars of a bad house style teach you the bad house style faithfully.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that professional writers change institutions and every institution's document types differ. The generic advice, which is to read a few examples and imitate them, is what everyone already does and it fails in a predictable way: you absorb the surface, the heading names and the register, and miss the functional obligations, so your first paper reads correctly and gets sent back. Move analysis is the same activity done deliberately, and the difference in yield is large.

The second reason is that genre explains a class of document failure that looks like a writing failure and is not. A report can be clear, well structured and correctly toned, and still fail because it did not perform an action the genre requires. Naming that as a genre failure rather than a quality failure tells you what to fix, and the remedy is usually a section rather than a rewrite.

The third reason is Miller's harder point, which is that genres carry obligations reflecting what an institution believes the situation to be. Reading the genre therefore tells you something about the organisation you have joined: whether the model owner's response is an obligatory move tells you a great deal about how challenge is understood there, and it takes three documents to find out rather than a year.

And a reason for exam candidates, since the ASSA papers this collection supports are themselves a genre. A past paper answer that scores well performs specific moves in a specific order, and marking schemes reward them explicitly. Move analysis on three model answers is a more efficient use of an evening than re-reading the syllabus.

Step 3

The mental model

Genre as action rather than form, with the recurring situation underneath it, and Swales's CARS as the worked instance.

Genre
In Miller's sense, a typified rhetorical action recurring in a typified situation. Defined by what it accomplishes, not by the shape it takes.
Move
A functional unit within a genre, doing one job towards the genre's action. Moves may be obligatory or optional, and their order may be fixed or free; both are empirical questions about a given genre.
Move analysis
Swales's method: take three to five exemplars, label what each part is doing functionally, then compare across the set to find which moves are obligatory, which are optional and how each is linguistically realised.
Step 4

The detail

Move analysis of a model validation report

Constructed here rather than borrowed, so treat it as a demonstration of the method rather than an authority on the genre. The point is the labelling discipline: every part gets a functional label, and the labels are verbs.

GENRE: model validation report ACTION: judge a model against its own claims, on evidence, and record the judgement auditably. M1 ESTABLISH THE MANDATE obligatory What was reviewed, under whose authority, against what standard, over what period. Realised as: a scope section, a policy reference. Test: could a supervisor tell whether you were entitled to conclude what you concluded? M2 ESTABLISH THE MODEL'S CLAIM obligatory What the model asserts about itself: documented purpose, design intent, the representativeness it claims for its data. Realised as: a model description section, usually too long and usually copied from the model documentation. Why it exists: you cannot find a gap without a claim to measure against. This move is the setup for M3 and is almost never written as such. M3 ESTABLISH THE GAP obligatory Where the evidence does not support the claim. Realised as: the finding statement. >>> THIS IS CARS MOVE 2 AND IT IS THE MOVE THAT GETS SKIPPED. A report that does M1, M2, M4 and stops has described a model and reported some tests. It contains no finding. It will be read as a clean opinion whatever the numbers say. M4 EVIDENCE THE GAP obligatory Tests performed, results, method, limitations of the testing itself. Realised as: results tables, appendices. M5 GRADE THE GAP obligatory Severity, materiality, and against what threshold. Realised as: a rating from a house scale. Note: the scale is institution-specific and its categories tell you what the institution thinks severity means. Read it before using it. M6 ASSIGN AND DATE REMEDIATION obligatory Owner, action, deadline. Realised as: an action table. Test: lecture 1's audience criterion. Is the named owner capable of mediating the change? M7 RECORD THE OWNER'S RESPONSE VARIES Whether this move is obligatory is the single most diagnostic feature of the local genre. Obligatory -> challenge is understood as a dialogue, and your finding will be read alongside a rebuttal. Absent -> challenge is understood as a verdict, and the rebuttal will happen in the meeting instead, unrecorded. Either is workable. Not knowing which you are in is not.

Two things to take from this. M3 is the load-bearing move and the one that vanishes, for the same reason CARS Move 2 vanishes: stating a gap is a social act with a cost, and describing things is not. And M7's variability is what makes move analysis worth doing rather than assuming, because the answer is not derivable from first principles and it changes what your document has to anticipate.

Running the analysis yourself

THE PROCEDURE, IN AN AFTERNOON 1 GET THREE TO FIVE EXEMPLARS Not any three. Three that were well received, which means asking someone rather than taking what is in the shared drive. A rejected paper in your sample teaches you the failure mode as if it were the genre. 2 LABEL EVERY PART WITH A VERB Go paragraph by paragraph and write what it is doing, not what it is about. "Establishes the mandate", not "scope". If you cannot write a verb, the paragraph may be doing nothing, which is itself a finding about the genre. 3 BUILD THE MOVE TABLE Rows are moves, columns are exemplars. Mark present or absent. Present in all -> obligatory Present in some -> optional, and ask what the presence correlates with Present in one -> possibly that author's habit rather than the genre 4 NOTE ORDER AND WHETHER IT VARIES Fixed order is a strong constraint and worth obeying even where it seems arbitrary. Free order means you have a choice, and lecture 5 tells you how to use it. 5 NOTE THE REALISATIONS How is each move actually worded? Which verbs, which hedges, what person? This is where you find that the house genre requires "the review noted" rather than "we found", which is module 6's register question answered empirically instead of guessed. 6 ASK WHAT THE MOVE SET IMPLIES ABOUT THE SITUATION Miller's step, and the one people skip. If M7 is obligatory, the institution treats challenge as dialogue. If a section on limitations of the testing is obligatory, it expects its validators to be fallible. Read the genre for what it assumes.

Cases where the genre beats the principle
A scope section restating the mandate the reader wrote Cut it. Module 5's lecture 1 calls this redundancy. Genre wins. The move is not for this reader; it is for the third party auditing the file in three years. Redundancy for the present reader is evidence for the future one.
Impersonal realisation: "the review noted" Name the actor. Module 2's lecture 2 is emphatic. Genre usually wins, and understand why. The impersonal form asserts that the finding belongs to the function rather than the individual, which is the point of an independent review. Use it for findings and name actors everywhere else.
A fixed heading set you may not alter Assertion headings. Module 4's lecture 8. Genre wins on the heading text, and you can still put the assertion in the first sentence under it. The constraint is narrower than it looks.
No move for stating objections Write a refutatio. Lecture 5. The principle wins. An absent move is not a prohibition, and adding a section the genre does not require is how genres change. Expect to justify it once.

The last row is the distinction that makes this workable. A genre obligation tells you what must be present; it rarely tells you what may not be. Most of what looks like constraint is convention nobody has tested, and the way to find out is to add the section and see whether anyone objects.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

Move analysis reproduces the genre as practised

The honest limit of the method, and it is a serious one. Move analysis is descriptive. It tells you what three well-received documents did, which is the local genre as practised, and it has no way of telling you whether the local genre is any good.

So the procedure faithfully teaches you a bad house style. If the three exemplars all bury the finding on page nine, open with four pages of model description and omit the gap statement, your analysis will report those as obligatory moves and your first paper will do the same. You will have successfully joined a dysfunctional genre, and you will have done it faster than someone who did no analysis at all.

The correction is to run the analysis and then ask Miller's question, which is what situation this move set is a response to. A genre whose gap move is optional is responding to a situation in which challenge is uncomfortable, and that is a fact about the institution rather than about validation. Knowing it lets you make a choice: conform, conform while doing the omitted move anyway, or make the case for changing the genre. All three are available. Not seeing the choice is what the method risks.

Ritual, and the sections nobody reads

Miller's account has a consequence she draws out and that professionals feel daily. If a genre is a response to a recurring situation, then when the situation changes and the form persists, the form becomes ritual: a move that is still obligatory and no longer does anything.

Every institution has these. The executive summary that duplicates the findings table because both were introduced for readers who no longer exist. The data-quality appendix that was a genuine finding in 2016 and is now a template with the dates changed. The declaration of independence that everyone signs and nobody reads. These are not writing failures and cutting them is not a writing decision, because the move is obligatory in the genre even though it is empty.

What you can do is diagnose them, which is worth more than it sounds. A move whose action you cannot name is a candidate ritual, and the test is to ask who reads it and what they do differently as a result. If nobody can answer, you have found something an institution can retire, and the case for retiring it is a genre argument rather than a style preference, which makes it winnable.

Why the gap move is hard everywhere

CARS Move 2 is the move students omit, and M3 is the move validators omit, and the parallel is not a coincidence. Both require asserting that something is missing or wrong in work that somebody else did, and both sit in documents where the surrounding moves are descriptive and safe.

Swales's observation about the research article is that Move 2 is where the writer must claim a deficiency in the literature, and that inexperienced writers either skip it or hedge it into invisibility, producing an introduction that reviews a field and then announces a study with no connection between the two. The professional version is identical. A report that describes a model at length, reports its test results accurately, and never states that the evidence contradicts the model's own claim of downturn representativeness, will be filed as a clean opinion.

Which gives a specific revision check worth more than most of this module. Find the sentence in your document that states the gap. If you cannot point at one sentence, the move is missing, regardless of how much evidence surrounds the place where it should be. Evidence is Move 4. It does not perform Move 3, and readers do not infer findings from tables.

Common failure Treating a genre as a template and filling it in. The move set tells you what actions the document must perform; it does not tell you that a section exists to be populated. A scope section written to satisfy the move rather than to establish the mandate produces the characteristic dead paragraph that repeats the policy reference and defines nothing, and a reader who audits the file in three years cannot tell from it what you were entitled to conclude. Every move has a test in step 4. Apply the test rather than the label.
Step 6

Test yourself

  1. Imitate before you compose. Run the full six-step procedure from step 4 on a real genre you write: three exemplars, verb labels, move table, order, realisations, and Miller's question. Report which move you discovered was obligatory that you had not known about.
  2. Then compose. In a recent document of yours, point at the single sentence that performs the gap move. If there is not one, write it.
  3. State Miller's definition of genre, and say what follows from defining it by action rather than form.
  4. Why is copying a document's form reasoning in the wrong direction? What does it produce?
  5. Name the three CARS moves and say which one gets skipped. What does an introduction with Moves 1 and 3 only actually do?
  6. Give the seven moves of the validation report from step 4. Which is load-bearing and which one's presence or absence is diagnostic of the institution?
  7. Why must you use exemplars that were well received rather than any three from the drive?
  8. Give two cases where a genre obligation should beat a principle taught earlier in this course, and one where the principle should win. What distinguishes them?
  9. What is the descriptive limit of move analysis, and what question corrects for it?
  10. What is a ritual move, and what is the test for identifying one?
