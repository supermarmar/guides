Concreteness and the specific detail

A named thing beats a category, and the reason is mechanical rather than aesthetic. Concrete language gets two encoding routes instead of one. It is also persuasive partly by exploiting a known bias, which is why every specific case needs its base rate beside it.

Lecture 1 of 7 Reading time, 32 minutes Source: Paivio; Hayakawa; Sadoski; Small and Loewenstein
Step 1

The 30-second version

Professional prose lives too high on what Hayakawa called the abstraction ladder. "Data quality issues" is three rungs above "1,847 accounts with no collateral valuation recorded since 2019", and the higher rung is easier to write, harder to remember, and impossible to act on.

The mechanism is Paivio's dual coding: concrete words are encoded both verbally and as imagery, giving two retrieval routes where an abstract word gives one. That makes the concreteness advantage in recall one of the more reliable findings in verbal learning, and it is a claim about memory rather than about taste, so it applies to a validation report exactly as it applies to an essay.

The professional discipline is a question you can ask of every abstract noun: what would you point at? If the answer is a number, a named field, a count of accounts or a date, that is what belongs on the page. If there is no answer, you may not have a finding.

And a warning that belongs with the technique rather than after it. A single vivid case moves people more than a statistic covering a thousand cases, which is a documented bias rather than a feature of good writing. So a specific example must always be accompanied by its representativeness, and using a striking case to carry an unrepresentative finding is a misuse of a mechanism this lecture is teaching you to operate.

Feynman test Your brain files a picture and a word separately, so a thing you can see gets stored twice and a category gets stored once. "Control deficiencies" is stored once. "The benchmark control is run by the team that built the model" is stored twice, and the reader can still repeat it a week later.
What is beyond the source Dual coding, the abstraction ladder and the identifiable-victim work are all cited accurately, and step 5 is explicit about where the evidence is strong and where it thins. Two arguments are this lecture's own. The first is the base-rate pairing rule in step 4, which treats the identifiable-victim effect as a professional hazard and gives a mechanical guard against it. The second is step 5's claim that concreteness cannot be added in revision, because the specifics have to have been recorded during the analysis, which makes it a data-collection discipline that only looks like a writing one.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that abstraction is the default failure of institutional prose, and it is a different failure from the ones modules 2 to 5 addressed. A sentence can have a character as its subject, an action as its verb, no nominalisations and perfect cohesion, and still say nothing, because every noun in it names a category rather than a thing. Williams's machinery does not catch this, and the reader experiences it as prose that is easy to read and impossible to hold.

The second reason is that in a professional setting the specific detail is not decoration, it is the difference between a finding and an impression. "Governance weaknesses in the benchmarking process" cannot be remediated, because nobody can tell what to change. "The benchmark model is built and run by the same team that owns the production model" names something a person can act on this week, and it is shorter.

The third reason is retention across time, which matters more in this field than in most. A finding raised in March is discussed in June and audited in 2029, and what survives that journey is what a reader can restate. Abstract findings degrade into their category on each retelling: the specific becomes "a benchmarking issue", then "some governance findings", then nothing. Concreteness is how a finding keeps its shape when passed between people.

And a reason for exam candidates. Markers reward the specific instance, the named regulation, the real magnitude, because those are what demonstrate knowledge rather than familiarity. An answer that says liquidity risk is managed through appropriate metrics scores nothing; one that names the LCR's 30-day horizon and its 100 per cent minimum scores. Concreteness is not a stylistic upgrade there, it is the marked content.

Step 3

The mental model

The ladder, the mechanism that makes lower rungs stick, and the pointing test that gets you down it.

Abstraction ladder
Hayakawa's image, after Korzybski, for the levels of generality at which the same referent can be named. Every rung is accurate; only the lower ones are informative.
Dual coding
Paivio's account of why concrete language is retained better: it is encoded verbally and imagistically, so recall has two independent routes rather than one.
Base-rate pairing
The discipline of stating a specific case together with what proportion of the population it represents, so that vividness cannot do persuasive work the evidence has not earned.
Step 4

The detail

Descending the ladder, in practice

FOUR ABSTRACT FINDINGS, DESCENDED BEFORE "There are data quality issues in the collateral data." POINT AT WHAT? A count of records, and a rule they break. AFTER "1,847 accounts, 3.2 per cent of the portfolio, carry no collateral valuation dated after 2019 while the exposure is current." BEFORE "Model documentation is not fully adequate." POINT AT WHAT? A section that is missing, and the standard requiring it. AFTER "The documentation has no section on the choice of stress window, which the model policy requires at section 4.3." BEFORE "Monitoring thresholds may not be appropriately calibrated." POINT AT WHAT? A threshold, its value, and what it failed to catch. AFTER "The PD stability threshold is set at a 20 per cent relative shift, which the observed 2023 deterioration of 14 per cent did not breach." BEFORE "There is insufficient challenge in the validation process." POINT AT WHAT? A count of findings, or of rejections. AFTER "Of 23 findings raised in 2024, none was graded above 'low', and none required a model change." NOTE WHAT DESCENDING DID IN EACH CASE It forced a claim that can be checked and therefore can be wrong. That is why the abstract version was comfortable. Rung 5 findings are safe because there is nothing in them to falsify, and a finding that cannot be falsified cannot be acted on either.

The last note is the honest account of why abstraction persists. It is not that writers cannot descend the ladder; it is that the lower rungs commit you. "Governance weaknesses" cannot be shown to be wrong, and "all four benchmark runs in 2024 were executed by the model's own developer" can be shown to be wrong by a single email. That exposure is the cost of saying something, and it is the whole cost.

Which specifics earn their place

Kind of specificWhat it buysWhen it becomes clutter
Not all detail is worth its space
A magnitude with units Everything. It converts an assertion into a measurement and lets the reader form their own view of severity. Almost never. If you have a number, use it.
A count with a denominator Representativeness, which is what makes a count interpretable at all. 1,847 means nothing without the portfolio size. Never omit the denominator. A bare count is a rhetorical device masquerading as evidence.
A named field, table or document section Checkability. The reader can verify it without asking you, which is worth more than any amount of assertion. When the name is internal and the reader is external, in which case describe the field and name it in a footnote.
One illustrative case Comprehension. A reader who has seen one instance understands the class, which no amount of definition achieves. When it stands alone. Paired with its base rate it illuminates; unpaired it exploits the identifiable-victim effect and is a misuse.
A date Auditability, and it is nearly free. "Since 2019" answers a question the reader would otherwise raise. When you are stacking dates for their own sake. Three dates in a sentence is a table trying to escape.
Sense detail: what a screen showed, what a file looked like Little, in this genre. Literary nonfiction runs on it and a validation report does not. Usually. The exception is process findings, where "the reviewer's sign-off box was pre-populated" is a sense detail doing real work.

The second row is the one that gets violated most and it is worth stating as a rule. A count without a denominator is not a specific, it is a number chosen for its size, and a reader who has been handed "1,847 accounts" without being told the portfolio has 57,000 has been managed rather than informed. That is the same fault as the unpaired illustrative case, in numerical form.

The exception: when abstraction is right

Descending the ladder is not always correct, and two cases matter. The first is genuine generalisation, where the claim really is about a class. If four unrelated models all lack stress-window documentation, the finding is about the documentation standard rather than about four models, and writing it four times at rung 1 conceals the pattern. State the general finding and then instance it.

The second is the summary layer. Module 4's pyramid puts the answer first, and the answer is usually one rung above the evidence, because a reader who wanted rung 1 would have read the appendix. The structure that works is abstract at the apex and concrete immediately beneath, so the first sentence names the class and the second points at the thing. What fails is abstraction all the way down, where every level restates the category and the reader never reaches anything countable.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

What the evidence supports, and where it thins

The concreteness effect is real and it is worth being precise about its scope, because this course has claimed elsewhere that its principles are testable and that claim only holds if the limits are stated too.

For individual words, the finding is about as solid as anything in verbal learning. Paivio and colleagues produced imagery and concreteness norms in the late 1960s and the recall advantage for concrete over abstract nouns has replicated widely; dual coding is the standard explanation and it makes the right predictions. For individual sentences, Begg and others found the same direction. So far, so firm.

For connected prose the picture is more complicated, and honesty requires saying so. Sadoski and colleagues found concreteness the strongest predictor among the text variables they examined for comprehension, interest and recall, which is a real result about real passages. But concreteness in natural text correlates with familiarity, with interest and with imagery, and disentangling them is hard, so the clean word-list mechanism does not straightforwardly license claims about a forty-page report. The defensible position is that the direction is well supported and the magnitude in a professional document is unmeasured.

Which is enough, because the professional argument does not rest on the memory research anyway. A rung-1 finding is better than a rung-5 finding because it can be checked, acted on and falsified, and that argument holds even if the recall advantage in connected text turned out to be small.

Concreteness cannot be added in revision

This is the practical claim in the lecture that most changes how you work, and it is easy to miss because concreteness looks like a style property.

You cannot descend the abstraction ladder in editing if you did not record the specifics. If your analysis produced the conclusion that collateral data are stale, and you did not keep the count, the proportion, the date cutoff or an example account reference, then the rung-1 sentence is not available to you at the writing stage. You will write "data quality issues" not because you prefer abstraction but because it is the only accurate thing you can say, and going back for the numbers costs a day you have not got.

So concreteness is a data-collection discipline that presents as a writing discipline. The remedy sits in the analysis rather than the draft: when a result emerges, record what you would point at. The count and its denominator, the field name, the date, one example, the threshold and its value. That takes a minute at the point of discovery and it is unavailable three weeks later, which is the same structure as lecture 3 of module 7 put on the canons: invention comes first, and no amount of skill at canon three recovers what canon one did not gather.

The vividness problem, stated fairly

The identifiable-victim work should make anyone teaching concreteness uncomfortable, and the discomfort is the right response rather than something to write around.

Small, Loewenstein and Slovic found not only that a named individual draws more response than statistics, but that inducing deliberative thinking suppresses the response to the individual without lifting the response to the statistics. The natural reading is that the vivid case produces a reaction the numbers do not, and that the reaction is not a better-calibrated judgement, it is a stronger one. A validator who opens with a single striking account and lets the committee's reaction to it carry a portfolio-level conclusion is using that.

The guard is the base-rate pairing rule and it is genuinely mechanical, which is why it is worth having: state the case, state what proportion it represents, in adjacent sentences, always. That does not eliminate the effect and it makes it visible, so a reader who wants to discount the vividness has what they need to do it. The stronger discipline is a question about your own draft: would this finding survive if I removed the example? If not, the example is doing the arguing, and module 9's lecture 7 is where that stops being a craft question.

Common failure Descending the ladder on the wrong noun. A sentence usually has one noun that carries the finding and several that carry scaffolding, and specificity spent on the scaffolding produces a document dense with irrelevant precision: exact dates for routine meetings, full system names for uncontested processes, counts of things nobody disputes. That reads as thoroughness and consumes the reader's attention before it reaches the noun that mattered. Run the pointing test on the finding, not on every noun in the sentence.
Step 6

Test yourself

  1. Imitate before you compose. Take four abstract findings from your own recent work and descend each one, in the before / point-at-what / after format of step 4. Report which one you could not descend because you had not recorded the specific.
  2. Then compose. For any finding of yours that rests on an illustrative case, add the base rate in the adjacent sentence. Then answer: would the finding survive without the case?
  3. What is the abstraction ladder, and who is it from? Why is every rung accurate?
  4. State dual coding and say what it predicts about recall.
  5. Give the pointing test and its most useful outcome.
  6. Why do writers stay on rung 5? Give the honest reason rather than the flattering one.
  7. Why is a count without a denominator not a specific at all?
  8. Give the two cases where abstraction is the right choice, and say what fails in the second.
  9. Where is the concreteness evidence strong, and where does it thin? Why does the professional argument not depend on the answer?
  10. Why can concreteness not be added in revision, and what does that make it a discipline of?
  11. State the identifiable-victim finding fairly, including the deliberative-thinking result, and give the guard against it.
