Module 8 · Writing track

Craft

Image, narrative and the music of prose. The preceding modules produce writing that is competent. Seven lectures on what makes it memorable, drawn from the people who write for readers who could simply stop.

What this module is

A validation report and a piece of literary nonfiction have less distance between them than either profession likes to admit. Both are read by someone with other demands on their attention, both fail if the reader stops, and both are improved by the same handful of techniques: the specific detail rather than the category, the metaphor that carries an idea the reader can hold, the passage that moves rather than accumulates.

The module takes those techniques from writers rather than from theorists. Le Guin on crowding and leaping, McPhee on when to slow a scene down, King on the toolbox and on adverbs as a signal that the writer fears he has not been clear. Where there is research behind a technique, and for concreteness and metaphor there is a great deal, the lecture cites it.

The last lecture is about reading, and it is the one most likely to be skipped. It should not be. Every serious account of how writing skill develops, from the progymnasmata to King, puts wide reading and deliberate imitation at the centre rather than at the periphery, and the empirical work in module 9 supports them.

Prerequisites

  • Module 5: concision, rhythm and grace. Craft here builds on the sentence-shaping and sound work rather than repeating it.
  • Module 7, lecture 6: figures. Lecture 2 argues that metaphor is misclassified as a figure, which only lands if you have seen it classified that way.

Module sequence

Part 1 · The concrete and the figurative (lectures 1 to 2)

Two techniques with real evidence behind them, and the largest single improvement available to abstract professional prose.

  • builtLecture 1: concreteness and the specific detail. Why a named thing beats a category. Sense data, real magnitudes, and the recall evidence that concrete language is retained better than abstract language expressing the same content.
  • builtLecture 2: metaphor as thought, not ornament. Lakoff and Johnson on conceptual metaphor: the figure is not decoration on an idea but often the only form the idea has. Fresh, dying and dead metaphors, Orwell on the prefabricated phrase, and the test that catches a mixed metaphor before a reader does.

Part 2 · Movement (lectures 3 to 5)

Making prose go somewhere, and getting explanatory material into it without stopping the motion.

  • builtLecture 3: narrative structure in nonfiction. Scene against summary, chronology, flashback. Le Guin's crowding and leaping: crowd the moments that matter so time slows and awareness sharpens, leap over everything that does not.
  • builtLecture 4: point of view, person and tense. What each grammatical person licenses and forbids, including the first person plural that institutional writing hides behind. Tense consistency, deliberate tense shift, and how both control distance.
  • builtLecture 5: exposition without tedium. Weaving explanatory material into prose that is going somewhere. The information dump as a recognisable failure, and braided structures as the standard remedy.

Part 3 · Word choice and apprenticeship (lectures 6 to 7)

The plain-fancy decision made deliberately, and the practice that underwrites everything else.

  • builtLecture 6: the plain-fancy axis. The shorter word by default and the longer one on purpose. Jargon as both a barrier to outsiders and a legitimate compression among specialists, and the rule that a technical term gets defined once, in a sentence of its own.
  • builtLecture 7: reading as apprenticeship. King's insistence that reading widely is not optional. Deliberate imitation and pastiche as exercises rather than as cheating, and the progymnasmata tradition of copying models before attempting composition.

Where this reappears

Imitation
Recommended in lecture 7 on the authority of the tradition. Module 9, lecture 6 supplies the deliberate-practice framework that explains why it works.
The dead metaphor
A craft failure in lecture 2. Module 9, lecture 7 follows Orwell in treating the prefabricated phrase as a political problem rather than merely an aesthetic one.
Concreteness
Lecture 1 argues it as a technique. Module 9, lecture 8 argues that knowing which detail to keep, out of the hundred available, is judgement rather than technique, and that the difference is the whole subject.
