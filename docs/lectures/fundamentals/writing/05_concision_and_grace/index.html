Module 5 · Writing track

Concision, rhythm and grace

Prose is now clear and coherent. What remains is subtraction and shaping, which is where correct becomes good. Seven lectures, ending with a writer who thinks the whole preceding order of this course is backwards.

What this module is

Two operations run through everything here. The first is cutting, and it is more systematic than it looks: Williams identifies six recognisable kinds of redundancy, each with its own signature, so a concision pass becomes six searches rather than one vague instruction to tighten. The second is shaping, which covers the internal architecture of a long sentence, the variation of length across a paragraph, and the sound of the thing when read aloud.

Sound is the part most professional writers skip, on the reasonable-sounding grounds that a validation report is not read aloud. Le Guin's answer is that sound is not ornament added to prose but the foundation of it, and that a reader subvocalises whether or not anyone intends them to. A sentence with an accidental metrical thump or an unpronounceable consonant cluster costs the reader attention they would rather spend on your argument.

The module closes on a genuine disagreement. Verlyn Klinkenborg rejects the split between sentence work and the discovery of material, distrusts outlines, is hostile to the whole concept of flow, and argues that the writer finds her material in the struggle to perfect each sentence. Set against Minto in module 4 and McPhee in module 4, lecture 7, that is a real argument with real consequences for how you work. The lecture does not resolve it for you.

Prerequisites

  • Module 2, lecture 8: the Paramedic Method. Lecture 1 supplies the taxonomy of what the lard factor was measuring.
  • Module 2, lecture 7: the stress position. Lectures 3 and 6 both build on it.
  • Module 4: architecture. Lecture 7 only lands as a disagreement if you have taken the position it disagrees with.

Module sequence

Part 1 · Subtraction (lectures 1 to 2)

Cutting as a systematic operation rather than an instinct.

  • builtLecture 1: six kinds of redundancy. Meaningless modifiers, doubled words, implied words, redundant categories, obvious statements and metadiscourse. Each with its signature, so a concision pass becomes six targeted searches.
  • builtLecture 2: metadiscourse and hedging. Writing about your writing. The line between useful signposting and throat-clearing, and how to calibrate a hedge to uncertainty that is actually there rather than to institutional nervousness.

Part 2 · Shaping (lectures 3 to 4)

The internal architecture of a long sentence, and the variation of length across a passage.

  • builtLecture 3: the shape of the long sentence. Reach the subject and verb quickly, then extend rightward with resumptive, summative and free modifiers. Coordinating balanced elements, and why a hundred-word sentence can be easier to read than a thirty-word one.
  • builtLecture 4: sentence variety and rhythm. Provost's demonstration passage, which teaches more in two paragraphs than a chapter of explanation. Le Guin's Short and Long exercise. Parataxis against hypotaxis, and how length variation signals emphasis and controls pace.

Part 3 · Sound, elegance, and a dissent (lectures 5 to 7)

The auditory layer, the figures that produce grace, and an argument against everything above.

  • builtLecture 5: sound, cadence and reading aloud. Le Guin's claim that sound is foundation rather than ornament. Accidental metre, alliteration you did not intend, clumsy consonant clusters, and the closing cadence that tells a reader a paragraph has finished.
  • builtLecture 6: balance, antithesis, climax and artful repetition. Parallelism and chiasmus, the tricolon, deliberate repetition against accidental repetition, and the short closing sentence that lands because everything before it was long.
  • builtLecture 7: the counter-position. Klinkenborg on the sentence as the unit of composition, his distrust of outlines, his hostility to flow, and his claim that thinking happens in revision rather than before it. Set directly against Minto and McPhee, with the case for each side put as strongly as it can be put.
