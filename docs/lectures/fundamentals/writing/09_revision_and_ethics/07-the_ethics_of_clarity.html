Lectures / Writing / Revision and ethics

The ethics of clarity

Obscurity is what evasion looks like when the writer will not lie. Every diagnostic in this course is also an instrument of concealment used in reverse, which means these sixty-seven lectures have equipped you for both and there is no technical defence against the second.

Lecture 7 of 8 Reading time, 32 minutes Source: Williams; Orwell (1946); Lanham
Step 1

The 30-second version

Williams closes Style on ethics, and his argument is that obscurity is frequently a strategy rather than an accident. The strategy has specific grammatical instruments, and this course has taught you to detect every one of them: the agentless passive that hides who acted, the nominalisation that hides that an act occurred, the euphemism that hides both, the abstraction that hides a magnitude.

The sharpest way to put the professional case is that obscurity is what evasion looks like when the writer will not lie. "The model understates downturn LGD by forty basis points" is falsifiable and can be wrong. "Certain aspects of the downturn calibration approach may benefit from further consideration" contains nothing false, says nothing, and satisfies the obligation to disclose.

Which produces the uncomfortable observation this lecture is built around. Eight techniques taught earlier as diagnostics are concealment instruments read backwards, so a reader who has finished this course is better equipped to obscure than they were when they started, and no way of teaching the material avoids that.

One distinction does most of the ethical work. Withholding is not obscuring. You may decline to say something, for confidentiality or because it is not yours to disclose, and saying nothing is honest. Writing something in a form that appears to disclose while not disclosing is a different act, and it is the one the instruments are for.

Feynman test If you will not lie but do not want to be understood, you write vaguely. Every trick for doing that is a trick this course taught you to spot, run backwards. And there is a clean line: staying silent is fine, and sounding like you said it when you did not is not.
What is beyond the source Williams's ethical lesson, Orwell's argument and Pullum's criticism are all reported as they stand. Three things are the lecture's own. The first is the dual-use inventory in step 3, which lists eight of this course's diagnostics as concealment instruments and states plainly that the course cannot be taught in a way that prevents it. The second is the formulation that obscurity is honesty-constrained evasion, which explains why it is the technique of choice for people who would not falsify. The third is step 5's argument that the individual ethical frame is inadequate, because obscurity is usually an accommodation to institutional incentives, which makes it a question about professional courage rather than about writing.
Step 2

Why this matters

The first reason is that this course would be dishonest without it. Sixty-seven lectures have argued that clarity is a technical achievement with testable mechanisms, and the same mechanisms run in reverse. Teaching the first half and omitting the second would leave a reader believing that clear writing is a competence question, when a large part of the obscurity they encounter professionally is a competence they are looking at from the wrong side.

The second reason is that the documents this collection concerns are exactly where the stakes sit. A model limitation disclosed in a form that satisfies a requirement and informs nobody, a finding written to be technically true and practically unnoticed, a regulatory response that answers a question adjacent to the one asked: these are common, they are not lies, and they have consequences measured in capital.

The third reason is that clarity is a professional obligation for this collection's reader rather than a preference. The IFoA's Actuaries' Code makes communication one of its principles, and the FRC's technical actuarial standards require actuarial communications to be clear and comprehensible to their users. Obscurity in an actuarial communication is therefore a standards matter, not a stylistic lapse, which changes what is at stake in the choices this lecture describes.

And a fourth reason, which is why the lecture ends where it does. Most obscurity is not chosen by an individual acting alone; it is an accommodation to a setting in which the plain version creates conflict and the hedged version does not. That reframing matters because it locates the problem where it can actually be addressed, and it also removes the comfortable conclusion that obscure writers are bad people.

Step 3

The mental model

The inventory, read in both directions, and the line between withholding and obscuring.

Honesty-constrained evasion
Obscurity understood as the technique available to someone who will not state a falsehood. Nothing in the document is untrue; nothing in it can be acted on either.
Withholding
Declining to address something and saying so. Honest, because the reader knows what they have not been told and can ask.
The actionability test
Asking whether a formulation exists that is equally true and more actionable, and if you did not choose it, requiring yourself to name why. Not a prohibition, since good reasons exist, but a demand that the reason be stated.
Step 4

The detail

One finding, five degrees of concealment

EVERY VERSION IS TRUE. NONE IS A LIE. LEVEL 0 · actionable "The downturn LGD understates loss by 40 basis points because the estimation window contains no downturn. The model owner should rebuild it on a sample including 2008, before the next capital return." A reader knows what is wrong, by how much, why, who acts, and by when. Falsifiable on every clause. LEVEL 1 · agent removed "The downturn LGD understates loss by 40 basis points. The estimation window should be rebuilt on a sample including 2008." Still quantified. Nobody owns the action, so it enters a register and waits. LEVEL 2 · magnitude removed "The downturn LGD is understated because the estimation window is not downturn-representative. Remediation is recommended." Ladder ascended. No number, so no materiality argument is possible and no one can rank it. LEVEL 3 · act nominalised, claim softened by litotes "The representativeness of the estimation window is not beyond question, and further consideration of the calibration approach is not unwarranted." Two litotes, two nominalisations, no actor, no act, no magnitude. Deniable in both directions: it can be read as raising a concern or as clearing the model. LEVEL 4 · apparent disclosure "Downturn representativeness has been considered as part of the review of the calibration approach." Reads as though the matter was addressed. Reports only that somebody thought about it. Satisfies a requirement to have covered the topic. WHAT MOVED BETWEEN 0 AND 4 Not truth. Every version survives a challenge on accuracy. What moved is whether a reader can do anything, and whether the author can be held to anything, and those two moved together, which is the point. THE DIAGNOSTIC Ask of any finding: could a reader act on this without asking me a question? If not, list the questions they would have to ask. Each one is a thing you knew and did not write.

Levels 3 and 4 are the ones worth dwelling on, because they are the register of a great deal of real professional writing and they are not produced by cynics. They are produced by people who have been told to raise a concern, who do not want a fight, and who have found a form of words that discharges the obligation. That is the institutional mechanism step 5 takes up.

When obscurity is the right choice

SituationLegitimate?What it requires
The counter-cases, given fairly, because a rule with no exceptions is not being tested
Genuine uncertainty about a magnitude Yes Say the uncertainty rather than hedging the claim. "Between 30 and 50 basis points, the range reflecting the 2008 reconstruction" is precise about being imprecise. Module 5's lecture 2 has the discipline.
Confidential material you may not disclose Yes Withhold and say you are withholding. Never write a form of words that reads as disclosure. The distinction in step 3 is the whole answer here.
A finding whose cause is a process, not a person Yes, if true Locate the cause accurately, which module 8's lecture 4 argued is accuracy rather than tact. And accept that sometimes the honest account is about a person, in which case write it without adjectives.
Legal exposure, litigation, privilege Not your call This is counsel's decision and not a writing decision. What you can insist on is knowing that the constraint is legal rather than presentational, because those get conflated.
Softening bad news for the person it concerns Usually not Williams treats compassionate obscurity as a real category and still a failure, because the reader needs the information most when it is unwelcome. Adjust the route, never the claim, which is module 7's lecture 8.
"The house style requires it" Partly Module 7's lecture 7 distinguishes an obligatory move from a ritual one. A required impersonal register is workable; a required vagueness about magnitudes is a genre problem worth naming as one.

The first row carries a point that recurs throughout this course. Precision about uncertainty is not hedging, and the two are constantly confused. A stated range with a stated reason is a strong claim; "may not be fully representative" is a weak one, and the weak one is what gets written when the writer has not done the work to produce the range.

Step 5

The grandmaster's perspective

Orwell, fairly

Orwell's essay is the standard citation here and it needs handling rather than quoting. His argument has a strong version and a weak one, and only the weak one survives.

The strong version is that the decay of language causes the decay of politics, that bad prose and bad thinking are mutually reinforcing, and that improving prose is therefore a political act. That claim is unfalsifiable as stated and probably false in the direction it asserts: political evasion produces evasive language rather than the reverse, and states with excellent prose traditions have not been noticeably better governed.

The weak version is defensible and is what this lecture uses. Certain constructions are unusually useful for concealing agency and act; their concentration in a document is therefore evidence about what the document is for; and a reader who can identify them is harder to manage. That is a claim about instruments rather than about causation, and step 3's inventory is its content.

On the rules themselves, Pullum's criticism stands: they are unfollowable as written, Orwell's own prose violates them repeatedly, and the injunction against any metaphor familiar from print would ban most good writing. It is worth noticing that Orwell effectively conceded this in his sixth rule, "break any of these rules sooner than say anything outright barbarous", which subordinates the other five to a judgement the rules cannot specify. That is an admission that they are heuristics, and heuristics are what this course has been careful to call them.

The individual frame is inadequate

The natural way to read this lecture is as an exhortation to personal honesty, and that reading is comfortable and mostly wrong. Level 3 and level 4 findings are not written by people who have decided to deceive. They are written by people responding to a setting.

Consider the incentives on a validator writing a level 0 finding. It names a colleague, quantifies a capital impact, sets a deadline, and creates a conflict that will be conducted in a committee where the model owner is more fluent about their own model. The level 3 version discharges the obligation to raise a concern, cannot be shown to be wrong, offends nobody, and is entirely defensible if challenged later, because the words are there on the page. A rational actor optimising for a quiet quarter writes level 3, and most people are not writing level 3 because they lack integrity.

Which means the honest diagnosis is that obscurity is usually a signal about an institution rather than about an author. A function whose findings are all level 2 or above is telling you something about how challenge is received there, and this is the same observation module 7's lecture 7 made about genre: the move set encodes what the institution believes the situation to be.

And it means the remedy is not primarily a writing remedy. Writing the level 0 version requires being willing to have the conversation the level 3 version avoids, and that is a question about professional courage and about whether the institution makes courage affordable. This course can tell you which version you have written. It cannot make the first one cost less than the second.

Where the obligation is not optional

For this collection's reader there is a further consideration, and it changes the register of the discussion from ethics to compliance.

Clear communication is a professional requirement for actuaries rather than a virtue. The Institute and Faculty of Actuaries' Actuaries' Code makes communication one of its principles, requiring members to communicate appropriately, and the Financial Reporting Council's technical actuarial standards require that actuarial communications be clear and comprehensible to their intended users. A level 3 finding in an actuarial communication is not merely weak writing; it is arguably a failure against a standard the author is bound by.

That does not resolve the courage problem and it changes the calculation. A writer weighing the discomfort of a level 0 finding against the safety of a level 3 one is not choosing between candour and prudence; they are choosing between a difficult conversation and a documented professional obligation. Knowing that the second option is not actually the safe one is the single most useful thing this lecture can offer to a professional in that position.

Common failure Using this lecture to diagnose other people. It is much easier to identify level 3 prose in a document somebody else wrote, and doing so is nearly costless, which is why it happens. The inventory in step 3 is worth almost nothing applied to colleagues and a great deal applied to your own last finding, where the relevant question is not whether the words are defensible but whether you chose them because they were the truest available or because they were the least troublesome.
Step 6

Test yourself

  1. Imitate before you compose. Take your most recent finding and write it at all five levels from step 4. Report which level you actually wrote, and answer the actionability test: was there a formulation equally true and more actionable, and why did you not choose it?
  2. Then compose. Rewrite it at level 0, with actor, magnitude and date. Do not send it. Report what you now expect the conversation to be about.
  3. State the formulation that obscurity is honesty-constrained evasion, and explain why it is the technique of choice for someone who will not falsify.
  4. Give four of the eight instruments from step 3 and say what each hides.
  5. State the dual-use problem. What defence is available?
  6. Distinguish withholding from apparent disclosure, with an example of each.
  7. Give the actionability test. Why is it a demand to name a reason rather than a prohibition?
  8. What moved between level 0 and level 4, and what did not? Why do the two things that moved move together?
  9. Give three situations in which obscurity is legitimate and one in which it is not your decision.
  10. Why is precision about uncertainty not hedging? What gets written instead when the work has not been done?
  11. Separate the strong and weak versions of Orwell's argument. Which survives, and what does his sixth rule concede?
  12. Why is the individual ethical frame inadequate? What does a function whose findings are all level 2 tell you?
  13. Why is the level 3 option not actually the safe one for an actuary?
