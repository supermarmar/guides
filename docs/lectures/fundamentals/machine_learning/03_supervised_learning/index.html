Supervised learning

The third sub-module of the machine learning course and the largest part of stage 3 (fit a model). Seven lectures take you through the canonical supervised algorithms, then extend into two topics that the practitioner literature treats as essential but most introductory courses skip: causal modelling and conformal prediction. Each algorithm is a different choice of function class, a different point on the bias-variance axis, and a different trade between interpretability and raw fit. Read them as a tour of inductive biases rather than as a checklist of techniques.

How to use this

The five core algorithms (KNN, decision trees, SVM, naive Bayes, ensembles) are independent of each other and can be read in any order, but the suggested sequence below mirrors the way most modellers learn them: instance-based first (intuition without parameters), then tree-based (the workhorse of credit), then margin-based (a different geometric philosophy), then probabilistic, then ensemble methods that combine all of the above. The last two lectures (causal models, conformal prediction) build on the ensemble methods lecture and should be read after it. Source material

Lectures 1 to 5 follow the wiki notes at docs/wiki/fundamentals/06_machine_learning/03_supervised_learning/. Lectures 6 and 7 are built out from the wiki's stub files (each was a single-reference notebook in the source) into substantive lectures, because causal interpretation and predictive uncertainty are both increasingly central to credit risk practice under regulatory expectations on model defensibility.
