Course companion

Deep learning

The fifth sub-module of the machine learning course and the third algorithmic family at stage 3. Nine lectures. The first two lay the foundation and its limits: the training loop, and what the universal approximation theorem does and does not promise on finite data. Lecture 3 turns that limit into a variance-reduction tool. Lectures 4 and 5 cover representation learning for categorical and text data. Lectures 6 through 8 develop the three major architecture families, convolutional, recurrent, and attention-based, and lecture 9 closes with the tabular adaptation of attention that competes directly with gradient boosting on actuarial and credit data. The aim throughout is not to compete with a full deep-learning degree course but to give the credit-risk modeller the working vocabulary to read a neural-network paper, evaluate a vendor model, and place every architecture on the bias-variance axis built up across the rest of the course.

How to use this

Read the sub-module in order after the ensemble learning lecture in sub-module 03; each lecture builds on the one before. The narrative arc is that neural networks are what happens when you stack non-linear function approximators deep enough that the resulting model class can represent any continuous function, and pay for that flexibility in interpretability, data hunger, and a fitting problem whose finite-sample solution is not identified the way a GLM's is. Course sequence

Foundations and their limits

  • builtNeural networks: perceptrons, MLPs, activations, forward and backward propagation
  • builtUniversality, non-identifiability, and Kolmogorov-Arnold networks
  • builtEnsembling and nagging: variance reduction across independently seeded fits

Representation learning

  • builtTensors and entity embedding: high-cardinality categorical covariates
  • builtWord embeddings: word2vec, negative sampling, GloVe

Architecture families

  • builtConvolutional networks: kernels, stride, padding, pooling
  • builtRecurrent networks: RNN, LSTM, GRU, and why attention displaced them
  • builtTransformers: scaled dot-product attention, multi-head attention, positional encoding
  • builtTabular transformers: the feature tokeniser transformation and the CLS token

Source material

Lecture 1 follows the wiki notes at docs/wiki/fundamentals/06_machine_learning/05_deep_learning/01-neural-nets.ipynb. Lectures 2 through 9 adapt material from AI Tools for Actuaries (Wüthrich, Richman, Avanzi, Lindholm, Maggi, Mayer, Schelldorfer, Scognamiglio; version 20 January 2026; https://ssrn.com/abstract=5162304, CC BY-NC); the prose, structure, worked examples, and figures are written fresh for this course. The black-box framing that recurs throughout is the natural bridge into the model interpretation sub-module, where SHAP and LIME are the standard tools for explaining what a neural network learned.
