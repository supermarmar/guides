Model interpretation

The eighth and final sub-module of the machine learning course and the sixth stage of the pipeline. Three lectures move the question from "how well does the model score" to "why does it score that way". White-box and black-box framing first, then the model-specific tools (coefficients, odds ratios, tree importance) that come for free with interpretable models, then the model-agnostic tools (permutation importance, partial dependence plots, SHAP, LIME) that work on anything including the neural networks of sub-module 05. In credit risk the regulatory expectation is no longer optional: every production model has to be explainable to a supervisor.

How to use this

Read the three lectures in order. The framing lecture sets the white-box vs black-box distinction and motivates why interpretability is the constraint that flows backwards through the whole pipeline. The model-specific lecture covers what you can read directly from a fitted linear model, a tree, or an ensemble. The model-agnostic lecture covers the post-hoc tools applied after the model is fitted, and is the longest of the three because the toolset has grown substantially in the last decade. Status badges show what is built (built) or planned.
