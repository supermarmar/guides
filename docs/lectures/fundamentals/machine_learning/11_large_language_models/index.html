Large language models

The eleventh sub-module of the machine learning course, and a direct scaling-up of the previous sub-module's closing lecture. A decoder transformer trained on internet-scale text turns out to exhibit behaviours a smaller model of the identical architecture does not, most strikingly the ability to perform a new task from a handful of examples given only at inference time, with no further training at all. Four lectures trace the GPT lineage and the scaling laws behind it, the pretraining recipe that produces a foundation model, the prompting and reasoning techniques that get useful work out of one, and close with the governance discipline a regulated actuarial user must apply before trusting any of it.

How to use this

Read in order. Lecture 1 establishes what changed, in kind and not just in degree, as decoder transformers scaled up, and previews the competing explanations for in-context learning that lecture 3 returns to properly. Lecture 2 covers how a foundation model is actually built, before any of the prompting techniques in lecture 3 are usable. Lecture 4 is not optional reading for a regulated actuarial user: every governance point it raises is a direct, load-bearing constraint on how any of the previous three lectures' techniques may responsibly be used in practice. Status badges show what is built (built) or planned.

Source material

This sub-module adapts material from AI Tools for Actuaries (Wüthrich, Richman, Avanzi, Lindholm, Maggi, Mayer, Schelldorfer, Scognamiglio; version 20 January 2026; https://ssrn.com/abstract=5162304, CC BY-NC), specifically the chapter on large language models. The prose, structure, worked examples, and figures throughout are written fresh for this course and do not reproduce the source text; readers wanting the original mathematical derivations and the companion notebooks should consult the source directly and aitools4actuaries.com. Companion track: deep generative modelling's closing lecture on decoder transformers is the direct architectural prerequisite for this entire sub-module.

Applied continuation: the language engineering track picks these four lectures up at the point where theory stops and construction starts. Where this sub-module explains why in-context learning happens, that track catalogues the prompting techniques that exploit it; where this one names fine-tuning, that one covers LoRA, QLoRA and direct preference optimisation in detail; and it adds the whole apparatus of tokenisers, embedding models, retrieval-augmented generation and agents that a working system needs and this sub-module does not treat.
