Deep generative modelling

The tenth sub-module of the machine learning course, and the first to leave supervised prediction behind entirely. Every model in sub-modules 3 and 5 answers "what is the single best-guess output for this input"; this sub-module answers a different question, "what does the entire distribution of plausible outputs look like, and can a model draw fresh samples from it". Four lectures move from why a full generative model earns its computational cost in actuarial practice (synthetic portfolios, capital simulation, anomaly detection), through the variational autoencoder's tractable approximation to that distribution, through the generative adversarial network and diffusion model's two competing answers to the same problem, and close with the decoder transformer, the autoregressive architecture that, in the next sub-module, becomes the engine behind large language models.

How to use this

Read in order. Lecture 1 sets out why a full predictive distribution matters and previews the state-space and ARMA/GARCH parallel from the statistics track, useful context before either architecture below feels like anything more than mathematical machinery. Lecture 2 (VAEs) and lecture 3 (GANs and diffusion) are two independent, competing solutions to the identical problem and can be read in either order, though the ELBO derivation in lecture 2 is the gentler introduction to the idea of an intractable objective replaced by a tractable bound. Lecture 4 (decoder transformers) assumes the attention mechanism from sub-module 5's transformer lecture and is the direct bridge into sub-module 11. Status badges show what is built (built) or planned.

Source material

This sub-module adapts material from AI Tools for Actuaries (Wüthrich, Richman, Avanzi, Lindholm, Maggi, Mayer, Schelldorfer, Scognamiglio; version 20 January 2026; https://ssrn.com/abstract=5162304, CC BY-NC), specifically the chapter on deep generative modelling. The prose, structure, worked examples, and figures throughout are written fresh for this course and do not reproduce the source text; readers wanting the original mathematical derivations and the companion notebooks should consult the source directly and aitools4actuaries.com. Companion track: deep learning covers the feed-forward and attention building blocks this sub-module assumes; large language models is the direct continuation of lecture 4.
