Calibration and forecast evaluation

The ninth sub-module of the machine learning course. Sub-module 7 (model evaluation) answered "is this model any good, on average, over the whole dataset". This sub-module answers a sharper question that a single RMSE or a single AUC number cannot: is the model's predicted price level correct at every price point, does it rank risks in the right order, and how do you show both of these things to a validator who has seen a plausible-looking model fail before. Six lectures move from the bias every fitted model can carry, through the property (auto-calibration) that a defensible pricing model must have, through the non-parametric tools that diagnose and repair miscalibration, to the discrimination and lift statistics that quantify ranking quality, and close with the resampling toolkit that makes all of the above trustworthy on the small samples credit and insurance data actually supply.

How to use this

Read in order; each lecture depends on the one before it. Lecture 1 (bias and the balance property) is the foundation everything else corrects for. Lecture 2 (auto-calibration) is the property a validator will ask about first. Lectures 3 to 5 are the diagnostic and scoring toolkit built on top of that property. Lecture 6 (resampling) is the validation discipline that makes every earlier number trustworthy at the sample sizes actuarial data actually comes in. Status badges show what is built (built) or planned.

Course sequence

  • builtStatistical bias and the balance property: in-sample bias, global unbiasedness, and why the intercept is not free to leave alone
  • builtAuto-calibration: the property a defensible pricing model must have, and the actual-versus-predicted plot that tests for it
  • builtNon-parametric recalibration: local regression, isotonic regression, and the pool-adjacent-violators algorithm

Discrimination and model-agnostic scoring

  • builtDiscrimination and the Gini score: the Lorenz curve, the cumulative accuracy profile, and why Gini needs calibration to mean anything
  • builtScore decomposition and lift charts: Murphy's uncertainty-discrimination-miscalibration split, lift charts, and double lift charts

Resampling validation

  • builtResampling validation: stratified cross-validation, the bootstrap, out-of-bag validation, and where AIC and BIC stop applying

Source material

This sub-module adapts material from AI Tools for Actuaries (Wüthrich, Richman, Avanzi, Lindholm, Maggi, Mayer, Schelldorfer, Scognamiglio; version 20 January 2026; https://ssrn.com/abstract=5162304, CC BY-NC), specifically the interlude chapter on model calibration and analysis and the chapter 1 discussion of model validation. The prose, structure, worked examples, and figures throughout are written fresh for this course and do not reproduce the source text; readers wanting the original mathematical derivations and the companion notebooks should consult the source directly. Companion track: model evaluation covers the average-performance metrics (AIC, RMSE, AUC) this sub-module assumes as background; model interpretation covers why a model scores the way it does, a different question from whether its scores are calibrated.
