# Regulatory Capital (Trading Book)

Most of the credit risk that has been discussed in the chapter thus far originates in the banking book, where assets are held to maturity in order for the bank to make a profit and credit risk entails the risk of a client defaulting within this term. The trading book, on the other hand, consists of mainly market risk, but credit risk is originated in this part of a bank’s activities as well. The trading book is primarily made up of shorter-term trades, for outcomes such as shortterm gains, hedging risk, and placing trades on the market for clients. This includes trading in derivatives.

Credit risk may occur as the risk of default of the issuer of the securities, as well as in the form of counterparty credit risk – where the counterparty could default before settlement with the position needing to be replaced at a worse price. However, most securities trade on a deliveryvs- payment basis (DVP) or via Central Clearing Parties (CCPs), so there is little or no settlement risk in exchanging securities and cash.
