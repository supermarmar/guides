# Global systemically important banks (G-SIBs)

<https://www.fsb.org/uploads/P111120.pdf>

1. The [[fsb|Financial Stability Board]] ([[fsb|FSB]]), in consultation with [[bis|Basel Committee on Banking Supervision]] ([[bis|BCBS]]) and national authorities, has identified the 2020 list of global systemically important banks (G-SIBs). The list is based on end-2019 data and the updated assessment methodology published by the [[bis|BCBS]] in July 2013. The overall number of G-SIBs remains 30 (see Annex). 
3.  [[fsb|FSB]] member authorities apply the following requirements to G-SIBs:

- Higher capital buffer: Since the November 2012 update, the G-SIBs have been allocated to buckets corresponding to higher capital buffers that they are required to hold by national authorities in accordance with international standards.
- [[tlac|Total Loss-Absorbing Capacity]] ([[tlac|TLAC]]): G-SIBs are required to meet the [[tlac|TLAC]] standard, alongside the regulatory capital requirements set out in the [[basel_framework|Basel III]] framework. 
- Resolvability: These include group-wide resolution planning and regular resolvability assessments. The resolvability of each G-SIB is also reviewed in a high-level [[fsb|FSB]] Resolvability Assessment Process (RAP) by senior regulators within the firms’ Crisis Management Groups.
- Higher supervisory expectations: These include supervisory expectations for [[01-risk_management|risk management]] functions, risk data aggregation capabilities, risk governance and internal controls.

4. The methodology for G-SIB identification is described in the technical summary published by the [[bis|BCBS]] in November 2014.3. The numbers in parentheses are the required level of additional common equity loss absorbency as a percentage of risk-weighted assets that each G-SIB will be required to hold in 2022.
