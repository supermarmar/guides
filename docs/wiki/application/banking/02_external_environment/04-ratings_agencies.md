# Ratings Agencies

The largest rating agencies have been performing analyses and collecting
data for well over a century. Rating agencies have large teams and resources, and regular contact with the entities they rate. However, while external ratings are undoubtedly useful, they are no more than opinions, and are not a substitute for analysis and informed decisionmaking. Banks with borrower relationships may have insights that lead them to assign different ratings from those of the agencies.

Rating agencies serve a wide range of constituencies that use ratings for differing purposes. As such, there is a range of opinions on their processes. Some want ratings to be adjusted quickly to give signals of possible deterioration, while others want them to be more stable and uninfluenced by short-term developments. The role of the economic cycle in ratings is often debated.

Rating agencies rely on the accuracy and completeness of information supplied by borrowers, and do not search for or expose fraud. If the analysts feel that the available data (both from the rating agencies as well as internal rating information) raises some questions, they can either assign more conservative ratings, fail to assign, or withdraw the ratings, but users cannot rely on rating agencies for more information than the agencies already provide in their ratings.
