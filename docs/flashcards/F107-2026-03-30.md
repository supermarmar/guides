## Card 3 — Credit Losses · Medium

Explain the relationship between EL, UL, and regulatory capital under the IRB approach.
?
Expected Loss (EL) = PD × LGD × EAD, summed across all obligors. EL represents the average cost of doing business and should be covered by provisions and pricing spreads. Unexpected Loss (UL) is the deviation of actual losses from EL — measured by portfolio standard deviation and driven by individual facility volatility and inter-facility correlations. Under Basel II/III IRB, regulatory capital is calibrated to cover UL only (not EL), set at a 99.9% VaR confidence level (i.e. the bank should only breach capital once in 1,000 years). If provisions fall short of EL, the shortfall is deducted from Tier 1 capital; an excess may be partially included in Tier 2. #flashcard #topic/credit-risk #difficulty/unknown  #date/2026-03-30

## Card 4 — Loan Pricing · Medium

(a) Define loan-to-value (LTV) and explain its role in mortgage pricing. (b) Describe the margin management problem created by fixed-rate mortgages and how UK banks address it.
?
(a) LTV = Loan outstanding ÷ Market value of property. It is the primary variable in mortgage pricing models: higher LTV means the bank needs a larger property price fall to suffer a loss on repossession. Higher LTV bands attract higher spreads and higher regulatory capital requirements.
(b) Fixed-rate mortgages funded by short-term variable-rate deposits create NIM compression when rates rise. They also embed an asymmetric prepayment option: borrowers prepay when rates fall (shortening duration) but stay when rates rise (extending duration at below-market rates). UK banks address this through deal periods (2- or 5-year fixed rates) that revert to the Standard Variable Rate (SVR) — a bank-controlled rate that restores repricing capability. The US S&L crisis illustrates the risk when this structure is absent. #flashcard #topic/loan-pricing #topic/credit-risk #difficulty/unknown  #date/2026-03-30

## Card 5 — Climate Risk · Medium

(a) List the four TCFD disclosure areas. (b) Distinguish Scope 1, 2, and 3 emissions under the WBCSD framework, and explain why banks' emissions are primarily Scope 3 downstream.
?
(a) TCFD four areas: (1) Governance — board and management oversight of climate risks; (2) Strategy — actual and potential impacts on business strategy; (3) Risk Management — how climate risks are identified, assessed, and managed; (4) Metrics and Targets — quantitative measures of climate exposure and reduction commitments.
(b) Scope 1: direct emissions from assets/processes owned or controlled by the firm. Scope 2: emissions from purchased electricity generation. Scope 3: indirect emissions — upstream (supply chain) and downstream (clients). Banks' primary climate exposure is Scope 3 downstream (funded emissions) because banks themselves have small direct footprints; the bulk of emissions arise from the activities their lending and investment make possible. Managing funded emissions requires understanding counterparty-level climate risk channelled through known risk types (credit, market, operational). #flashcard #topic/climate-risk #difficulty/unknown  #date/2026-03-30
